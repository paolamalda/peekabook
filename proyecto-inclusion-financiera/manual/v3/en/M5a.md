# M5 U01 | How do I turn a wish into a goal?
objetivo: Measure your net worth and set goals with an amount, a date and a priority, without counting the same money twice.
gancho: Luis wants to study English, help his mom fix her house and someday buy a car. He's eager, but every month the money goes and no goal moves forward. In this lesson you'll learn to turn wishes into goals you can actually reach.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-star | A wish is not a goal
"I want a house" is a wish. "I want to save 15,000 dollars for the down payment and buying costs in five years" is a goal.

The difference is that the goal has numbers and a date. That way you know how much to set aside each month.

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | A goal has four parts
* fa-money | Amount | How much it costs. | 600 dollars. | si
* fa-calendar | Date | By when. | In 12 months. | si
* fa-globe | Currency | What currency it's paid in. | Dollars or pesos. | si
* fa-sort-amount-desc | Priority | What comes first if there isn't enough for everything. | 1, 2 or 3. | si

--- ecuacion | fa-calculator | How much to set aside each month
You want to pay for something that costs 120 in 6 months and you have nothing set aside.
= 120 | What it costs
- 0 | What you already have for that goal
= 120 | What's missing: 120 ÷ 6 = 20 a month
Check whether those 20 fit in your budget.

--- paso | fa-ban | The same dollar doesn't pay for two goals
If you have 80 left over a month and your goals need 150, you can't assign the same 80 to each one.

You have to change amounts, deadlines or priorities. It's better to move forward on one goal than on none.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Luis has 150 left over a month. His goals: a 600 English course in 12 months (50 a month), his mom's 1,200 roof in 12 months (100 a month) and a 3,000 car in 24 months (125 a month). That adds up to 275.

He decides to start with the course and the roof (150) and leave the car for when he finishes the course.

> **Key idea:** a goal has an amount, a date, a currency and a priority.

--- pasos | fa-list-ol | How to order your goals
1. **First, your savings cushion:** a small emergency goal protects all the others. Without it, any surprise forces you to use another goal's money or go into debt.
2. **Next, what avoids costs:** paying off an expensive debt usually "earns" more than any savings.
3. **Then, what improves your life:** a course, a move, a car.
4. **Last, the wishes that aren't urgent:** you can move their date with no problem.

This order is a guide, not a rule. Your family decides what comes first.

--- comprueba
1. You want 600 in 12 months and already have 180 set aside. How much do you set aside each month? || 35, because 420 is missing and 420 ÷ 12 = 35.
2. Can you assign the same 80 to three goals? || No. Each dollar has only one job.

--- recuerda
- Turn each wish into an amount, date, currency and priority.
- Calculate how much to set aside each month.
- If it isn't enough, change deadlines or priorities.

== profundiza

--- tabla | fa-balance-scale | Your net worth
Your **{{net worth|What is really yours: everything you have minus everything you owe.}}** is what you have minus what you owe, on a given date.

| What you have | | What you owe | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash | 800 | Car loan | 5,500 |
| Savings | 1,200 | Credit card | 1,000 |
| Car (estimated value) | 7,000 | | |
| **Total** | **9,000** | **Total** | **6,500** |

Net worth: 2,500. But the money you could use tomorrow is only 2,000.

--- tema | fa-tint | Liquidity
Not everything you own turns into cash quickly. A house, a car and a savings account have different **{{liquidity|How quickly you can turn something into money without losing value.}}**.

That's why, besides your net worth, check how much money you could use in an emergency.

--- tema | fa-heart | Your net worth is not your worth
Use realistic values and mark the estimates. Sentimental value isn't a sale price.

A negative net worth describes a situation, not a person's worth. Many people start with a negative net worth because of student or car debts, and improve it over time.

--- tema | fa-clock-o | Short-term and long-term goals
- **Short term** (less than 2 years): the money should be safe and available, for example in a savings account.
- **Long term** (5 years or more): you can consider options that grow more, with more risk (M5 U02).

Separating your goals by time frame helps you decide where to keep the money for each one.

> **Good to know:** according to Federal Reserve research, people who write down their goals and set money aside automatically save more than those who decide each month.

--- tema | fa-globe | Goals in two currencies
If your goal is in Mexico, like your mom's roof, the exchange rate can change the amount you need in dollars.

If today 20,000 pesos equal about 1,100 dollars, in a year it could be more or less. Review your goal every three months and adjust what you set aside.

A simple way to protect yourself is to set aside a little more than you calculate, for example 10%, to cover changes in the exchange rate and the cost of sending money (M2 U08).

--- casos
### Case 1. Daniela's course
Daniela wants a 600 baking course in 12 months, with no debt. She already has 180 set aside just for that.
? How much does she set aside each month? || 420 is missing: 35 a month.

### Case 2. Andrés's net worth
Andrés has a net worth of 2,500, but if a 2,300 bill arrives tomorrow, he can't use his car to pay it.
? What does he still need to check? || His liquidity: how much money he can use right away.

### Case 3. Luis's goals
Luis has three goals that add up to 275 a month and only 150 left over.
? What does he do? || He prioritizes two and puts off the third. He doesn't assign the same money to all three.

--- errores
* Having wishes with no numbers | You never know if you're making progress | Give it an amount and a date
* Assigning the same money to several goals | You think you have more | Every dollar, one job
* Confusing net worth with available money | You can't pay for an emergency | Check your liquidity
* Wanting every goal at the same time | None move forward | Prioritize

# M5 U02 | Does having more money mean my investment made money?
objetivo: Choose between saving and investing based on time frame, risk and access, and calculate returns without fooling yourself.
gancho: Andrés saw that his retirement account went from 2,000 to 2,300 in a year and thought he had earned 300. But he had put in 250 from his pay. In reality, the investment only earned 50. In this lesson you'll learn to measure what your money really earns.

== esencial

--- ecuacion | fa-calculator | What you put in isn't a gain
You start with 100, add 20 from your own pocket and end with 125.
= 125 | What you have at the end
- 120 | What you put in (100 + 20)
= 5 | What the investment earned
What you put in isn't a gain from the investment.

--- tarjetas | fa-balance-scale | Saving and investing are not the same
* fa-shield | Saving | Keeping money in a safe, available place. | It grows a little, but doesn't go down. | si
* fa-line-chart | Investing | Putting money in something that can go up or down. | It can grow more, but it can also lose. | igual

--- paso | fa-calendar | The time frame decides
If you'll need the money in less than one or two years, access and stability matter more than trying to earn a lot.

If you won't need it for many years, you can ride out ups and downs.

--- paso | fa-arrows-v | Ups and downs don't cancel out
If an investment goes up 20% one year and down 20% the next, you don't end up the same.

1,000 goes up to 1,200 and then down to 960. You lost 4%. To recover from a 20% drop, you need to go up 25%.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Rosa needs 1,000 in three months for a trip to Guadalajara. Someone recommends an investment that "could go up 15%."

Rosa asks: "And if it goes down?" It could drop 20% and leave her with 800. Since she needs the money soon, she keeps it in a savings account.

> **Key idea:** having more dollars doesn't always mean being able to buy more. Check inflation.

--- tarjetas | fa-list | Three questions before investing
* fa-calendar | When do I need it? | If it's in less than two years, saving is better. | The time frame rules. | si
* fa-life-ring | Do I have my savings cushion? | Without it, an emergency forces you to sell at a bad time. | Cushion first. | si
* fa-credit-card | Do I have expensive debts? | Paying off a card at 25% "earns" more than almost any investment. | Pay the expensive debt first. | si

If you answer all three well, you can start investing with a little, calmly.

> **Before you act, check:** who manages the investment, how much it charges in total, how you get your money out and whether it's registered with the SEC or FINRA.

--- comprueba
1. Your account went from 2,000 to 2,300 and you put in 250. How much did the investment earn? || 50, because 2,300 − 2,000 − 250 = 50.
2. An investment goes up 10% and then down 10%. Starting from 1,000, how much is left? || 990, because 1,000 × 1.10 × 0.90 = 990.

--- recuerda
- Separate what you put in from what the investment earned.
- If you need the money soon, don't invest it in something that can go down.
- Check fees and inflation.

== profundiza

--- tabla | fa-table | Where a gain comes from
You buy an investment for 1,000. In a year it pays you 40 and at the end it's worth 1,030.

| Item | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Return from payments | 40 ÷ 1,000 | 4% |
| Change in price | 30 ÷ 1,000 | 3% |
| Total return | 70 ÷ 1,000 | 7% |
| With 15 in fees and 10 in taxes | 45 ÷ 1,000 | 4.5% net |

--- tema | fa-percent | Real return
With a 4.5% net return and 3% inflation: 1.045 ÷ 1.03 − 1 = 1.46%. That's the real increase in your buying power.

Getting payments doesn't guarantee a gain. If the investment ends up worth 900 and paid 40, you lost 60.

--- tema | fa-calendar | Compare equal periods
2% in three months isn't 2% a year.

From 1,000 to 1,210 in two years is 21% in total, but 10% a year compounded.

--- tema | fa-pie-chart | Diversifying
**{{Diversifying|Spreading your money across different investments so you don't depend on just one.}}** means spreading your money across different investments. It spreads the risk, but it doesn't eliminate losses.

Having many investments in the same sector isn't diversifying.

> **Good to know:** a 1% yearly fee seems small, but over 30 years it can eat up almost a quarter of what you would have built up. Always compare fees.

--- tema | fa-heartbeat | Riding out a drop
Being able to handle a drop emotionally isn't the same as being able to pay the rent if it happens.

Before investing, make sure you have your emergency fund and won't need that money soon.

--- tema | fa-money | Compound interest: time on your side
**{{Compound interest|When what you earn also starts to earn. Over time, growth speeds up.}}** makes what you earn start earning too.

1,000 at 5% for 10 years becomes about 1,629. For 30 years, about 4,322. Time matters more than the amount.

That's why starting early, even with a little, helps more than waiting to have a lot. Remember that 5% is an example: nobody can guarantee that return.

--- casos
### Case 1. Rosa's trip
Rosa needs 1,000 in three months.
? Does she invest in something that can go up or down? || No. If it drops 20%, she wouldn't have enough for her trip. She uses a savings account.

### Case 2. Andrés's balance
Andrés's retirement account went from 2,000 to 2,300, but he put in 250.
? How much did the investment earn? || 50.

### Case 3. Luis's "average"
A friend tells Luis that his investment "averaged 0%": it went up 20% and down 20%.
? Did it stay the same? || No. From 1,000 it went to 960: it lost 4%.

--- errores
* Counting your contributions as gains | You think you earned more | Subtract what you put in
* Investing money you need soon | You can lose it when you need it | Use savings for near-term goals
* Ignoring fees | They eat your gains | Always compare them
* Averaging ups and downs | You miscalculate | Multiply the changes

# M5 U03 | Does opening an account mean I already invested?
objetivo: Tell apart the account (the container) from the investment (what's inside) and verify who sells it to you.
gancho: Andrés opened a retirement account a year ago and deposits money every month. When he checked, his money hadn't grown: it was sitting in cash, because he never chose what to invest in. In this lesson you'll learn the difference between opening an account and investing.

== esencial

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | The bag and what you keep inside
* fa-shopping-bag | The account (the container) | An investment or retirement account, like an IRA or a 401(k). | It has rules, but doesn't grow on its own. | igual
* fa-cubes | What's inside | What you buy inside: cash, stocks, bonds or funds. | This is what goes up or down. | igual

--- paso | fa-exclamation-circle | Opening the account isn't investing
A retirement account is like a bag. If you put money in and don't choose what to buy, it stays in cash.

The name "retirement account" doesn't say what your money is invested in.

--- pasos | fa-list-ol | Three questions about your account
1. **Which account do I use?** IRA, 401(k), CalSavers, brokerage account.
2. **What product did I really buy?** Ask for the exact name.
3. **How does it gain or lose value, and how do I take my money out?**

--- paso | fa-search | Verify who sells to you
Before investing, look up the company and the person on FINRA's **{{BrokerCheck|A no-cost website where you check whether a broker or investment adviser is registered and has complaints.}}** or in the SEC's adviser database.

Ask whether your money goes to the institution or to the seller's personal account. If it goes to a personal account, it's a red flag.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Daniela got a message from a social media "adviser" promising 20% a month in cryptocurrency.

She looked up his name on BrokerCheck and he wasn't there. She didn't invest. A month later, several people in the group reported they had lost their money.

> **Key idea:** nobody can guarantee you a high return with no risk.

--- tabla | fa-table | Types of accounts
| Account | What it's for | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Brokerage account | Buying and selling investments | Fees and custody |
| IRA | Saving for retirement with tax advantages | You need work income |
| 401(k) | Employer retirement plan | Employer contribution |
| CalSavers | State retirement plan | How much is deducted |

Inside each one, you choose what to buy. If you don't choose, the money may stay in cash.

> **Before you act, check:** the product's exact name, where your money is held and whether the person appears on BrokerCheck.

--- comprueba
1. Is an IRA the same as a stock? || No. The IRA is the account; a stock is something you can buy inside it.
2. Where do you check a broker? || On FINRA's BrokerCheck or in the SEC's database.

--- recuerda
- The account is the container; the investment is what's inside.
- Check what your money is invested in.
- Verify who sells to you before investing.

--- tabla | fa-table | Basic products
| Product | What it is | Main risks |
|---|---|---|

--- tema | fa-lock | Who holds your investments
**{{Custody|Who holds your investments and keeps the record that they're yours.}}** is who holds your investments and keeps the record that they're yours.

SIPC protection covers certain cases if the brokerage firm fails, but it does **not** protect against market drops.

--- tema | fa-exclamation-triangle | Red flags
- "High guaranteed return."
- Gains people show off on social media.
- Pressure to decide quickly.
- Payments only in cryptocurrency or to personal accounts.
- "Invite your friends and earn more."

> **Good to know:** cryptocurrency investment scams were among the ones that cost people in the U.S. the most money in recent years, according to the FTC.

--- tema | fa-pie-chart | Really diversifying
Having two "technology" funds with almost the same companies isn't diversifying: it doubles your risk.

A broad index fund, which holds hundreds of companies in different sectors, is usually a simple, low-cost way to diversify.

--- tema | fa-clock-o | Target date funds
Many retirement plans offer **{{target date funds|Funds that adjust their own risk as the year of your retirement gets closer.}}**, with a year in the name, like "2055."

They adjust their risk on their own: more stocks when there's a long way to go and more bonds as retirement gets closer. They're a simple option if you don't want to choose fund by fund.

Even so, check their yearly fee and that the fund's year is close to the year you plan to retire.

* Believing in guaranteed returns | A scam | Nobody guarantees without risk
* Investing with someone without verifying | You lose your money | Look them up on BrokerCheck
* Believing having many funds is diversifying | Concentrated risk | Check what's inside each one

# M5 U04 | Rent or buy? What rights do I have as a tenant?
objetivo: Know your basic rights as a tenant in California and the real costs of buying a home, even with an ITIN.
gancho: Andrés dreams of buying a house in Sacramento. Carmen, his wife, would rather keep renting for a few years. While they decide, the landlord announces a 15% rent increase. In this lesson you'll learn your rights as a tenant and what buying really costs.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-home | Renting isn't "throwing money away"
Paying rent isn't throwing money away: you pay for a safe place to live and the flexibility to move.

Buying isn't always better either. It depends on how long you'll stay, your stability and all the costs.

--- tarjetas | fa-shield | Your rights as a tenant in California
* fa-line-chart | Limit on rent increases | Many homes have a yearly cap: 5% plus local inflation, with a maximum of 10%. | Some are exempt. | si
* fa-gavel | Just-cause eviction | In many homes, the landlord needs a valid reason to end the lease. | Ask for the reason in writing. | si
* fa-user-secret | Your immigration status | The landlord can't ask about it or use it to rent to you or evict you. | It's the law in California. | si
* fa-wrench | A livable home | The landlord must make the necessary repairs. | Ask in writing. | si

--- paso | fa-exclamation-triangle | If you get eviction papers
If you get eviction papers from a court, the deadline to respond is **only a few days**.

Get legal help right away. Don't ignore the papers, even if you think they're wrong.

--- paso | fa-calculator | Buying costs more than the mortgage
A rent of 1,800 and a **{{mortgage|A loan to buy a house. The house backs the loan.}}** of 1,800 don't cost the same.

When you buy, you also pay property tax, homeowners insurance, maintenance and, sometimes, association fees.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
The landlord announces a 15% increase to Andrés. Andrés checks whether his building has a rent cap: it does.

He answers in writing that the increase allowed this year is lower, citing the law. The landlord adjusts the increase.

> **Key idea:** keep your lease, your receipts, photos of the home when you move in and all your messages with the landlord.

--- tema | fa-folder-open | What to keep as a tenant
- Your signed lease and any change in writing.
- Rent receipts, especially if you pay in cash. Always ask for a receipt with the date and amount.
- Photos and videos of the home when you move in and when you move out.
- Messages and letters with the landlord about repairs or increases.

With these papers you can defend yourself if there's a disagreement about the rent, the deposit or an eviction.

--- comprueba
1. Can the landlord ask about your immigration status in California? || No. The law prohibits it.
2. Does an 1,800 mortgage cost the same as 1,800 in rent? || No. Buying has more costs: taxes, insurance, maintenance.

--- recuerda
- Know the rent cap and your rights.
- Keep everything in writing.
- If court papers arrive, get legal help now.

== profundiza

--- tema | fa-money | Security deposit
In California, since 2024, the security deposit generally can't be more than one month's rent. Some small landlords can ask for up to two.

When you move out, the landlord must return the deposit or explain in writing what was deducted, within 21 days.

--- tabla | fa-table | Buying: the full costs
| Cost | What it is |
|---|---|
| Down payment | The part of the price you pay upfront |
| Closing costs | Paperwork, appraisal, insurance, fees |
| Mortgage | Principal and interest |
| Property tax | Yearly, based on the value |
| Homeowners insurance | Required with a mortgage |
| Maintenance | Worth setting aside for each month |
| Association fees (HOA) | In some developments |

--- tema | fa-id-card | Buying with an ITIN
Some lenders, like certain credit unions and community development financial institutions (CDFIs), offer mortgages with an ITIN. They usually require a bigger down payment and have higher rates.

Compare at least two options and check the total cost.

--- tema | fa-handshake-o | Homebuying assistance programs
In California there are state programs, like those of the California Housing Finance Agency (**CalHFA**), and local programs that help first-time buyers with the down payment and closing costs. Each program has its own requirements, and some may ask for documents an ITIN doesn't meet. [POR CONFIRMAR]

A **HUD-certified housing counselor** can help you at no cost or at low cost to review which programs apply to your case, whether you qualify for a mortgage with an ITIN and how much you need to save.

> **Good to know:** the counselor can also help if you already own a home and fall behind on the mortgage, so you don't lose it.

--- tema | fa-line-chart | Reporting rent to your credit history
Some landlords and services report your rent payments to the credit agencies. In California, some owners of certain buildings must offer that option to their tenants. [POR CONFIRMAR]

If you pay on time, this can help you build a credit history for a future mortgage (M3 U03).

Before accepting, ask whether it has a cost, whether they also report late payments and how to cancel the service.

# M5 U05 | Is the advertised price all it costs?
objetivo: Compare a car or a course by its total cost and its flexibility.
gancho: Luis saw a car ad for "only 280 a month." It seemed perfect. When he added insurance, gas and maintenance, the car cost him 650 a month. In this lesson you'll learn to see everything something costs before you buy it.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-tag | The advertised price isn't everything
A car may ask for a 200 monthly payment and also require insurance, gas and maintenance.

The **{{total cost of ownership|Everything you pay to own and use something: the price, plus insurance, maintenance, gas and more.}}** includes everything you pay to own and use something.

--- tabla | fa-car | Luis's two cars
| | Car A | Car B |
|---|---|---|
| Payment | 280 | 340 |
| Insurance | 160 | 120 |
| Gas | 140 | 80 |
| Maintenance | 70 | 60 |
| **Total per month** | **650** | **600** |

The car with the lower payment costs more each month.

--- pasos | fa-list-ol | Three steps to compare
1. **Write down the advertised payment.**
2. **Add the other costs:** insurance, gas, maintenance, registration.
3. **Compare** the options with the same list, including the option of waiting.

--- paso | fa-graduation-cap | Education too
A course has tuition, but also materials, transportation, child care and the income you give up while you study.

Check that the provider is real and recognized. An ad promising a "guaranteed job" isn't guaranteed income.

> **Key idea:** always compare with the same list of costs.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Daniela wants a baking course. Tuition is 1,200, materials 300, and she'd give up 500 in earnings because of class hours.

The total cost is 2,000, not 1,200. She decides to take it anyway, because she expects to raise her prices afterward. Now she knows how much she needs to save.

--- tarjetas | fa-question-circle | Questions before you sign
* fa-money | What's the total price? | Not just the monthly payment. | Ask for it in writing. | si
* fa-percent | What's the yearly rate (APR)? | Compare with your bank or credit union. | Bring an offer beforehand. | si
* fa-plus-square | What extras are included? | Warranties, insurance and optional services. | You can say no. | si

If you're pressured to sign today, that's a good reason to wait.

--- paso | fa-clock-o | Waiting is also an option
Sometimes the best purchase is waiting a few months. With a bigger down payment, the loan is smaller and you pay less interest.

If your current car still works, calculate how much you'd save in six months and compare it with the cost of buying today.

--- comprueba

--- recuerda
- Add up all the costs, not just the payment.
- Compare with the same list.
- Check that a course is real before paying.

== profundiza

--- tema | fa-car | Car: total cost
Price, financing, insurance, gas or charging, maintenance, repairs, parking, registration and loss of value.

A longer loan lowers the payment, but it can leave you owing more than the car is worth. If you sell it or it's totaled, you still owe the difference.

--- tema | fa-file-text | Leasing
Leasing has mileage limits, return conditions and charges if you return the car damaged.

Don't compare only the monthly payment: at the end you don't own the car.

--- tema | fa-line-chart | {{Depreciation|The value something loses over time, like a new car.}}
A new car loses a significant part of its value in the first years. A used car in good condition can be a better option for many families.

Have a trusted mechanic inspect a used car before you buy it.

--- tema | fa-graduation-cap | Education: check before you pay
- Check that the school or program is recognized.
- Ask how many students finish and get jobs, with data.
- Ask about scholarships or no-cost programs from the state or community colleges.

California community colleges have low-cost programs and some student aid, including some that don't depend on immigration status.

> **Good to know:** in California, students who meet certain in-state schooling requirements can pay in-state tuition (AB 540) even without regular immigration status. Ask at the admissions office.

--- tema | fa-signal | Test it with less income
If the payment depends on steady overtime, that's a weakness in the plan.

Ask yourself: could I pay it in a month with 20% less income?

--- tema | fa-university | Arrive with an approved loan
Before going to the dealership, ask your credit union or bank for a preapproval. Many credit unions lend with an ITIN.

That way you know how much you can pay and you compare the dealer's offer with a real rate. If the dealer offers you something better, great; if not, you already have an option.

Read the whole contract before signing. Some dealerships add optional products to the monthly payment without explaining them. Ask what each charge is and remove what you don't want.

# M5 U06 | What will I live on when I work less or stop working?
objetivo: Identify your possible sources of retirement income and the questions you should check.
gancho: Rosa is 61. She worked 15 years in Guadalajara and has spent ten in California taking care of children. She wonders what she'll live on when she can't work anymore. In this lesson you'll learn your possible sources of retirement income and how to start, even with a little.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-hourglass-half | Start with your needs
Planning for retirement doesn't start with choosing an investment. It starts with understanding what expenses you'll have, what income you could receive and how much time you have.

It's never too late to start, and every step counts.

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | Possible sources of retirement income
* fa-university | U.S. Social Security | A benefit for years worked with a valid SSN. | Check your statement on my Social Security. | igual
* fa-building | Employer plan (401(k)) | You contribute from your pay; sometimes your employer does too. | Don't leave money on the table. | si
* fa-flag | CalSavers | California's program if your employer has no plan. | Ask if you can join. | si
* fa-money | Your own savings | Other accounts you manage. | Check their risk and access. | igual
* fa-globe | AFORE (Mexico) | Your retirement savings if you worked in Mexico with IMSS. | M5 U07. | igual

--- paso | fa-gift | The employer contribution
If your employer puts in 50 cents for each dollar you contribute, up to a limit, contributing 100 gets you 50 more.

It's money you don't want to leave on the table. Ask about the limit and how long you have to work for that contribution to be yours.

--- pasos | fa-list-ol | Three steps
1. **Write down** the expenses you'll probably still have.
2. **Identify** your possible sources and which ones still need to be confirmed.
3. **Choose one action** you can start today.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Andrés's employer puts in 50 cents for each dollar, up to 6% of his pay. Andrés was contributing only 2%.

He raised his contribution to 6%. Now his employer adds an extra 3% each month, without him spending more than he can already afford.

> **Key idea:** a small, steady contribution is different from a promised return. Nobody can guarantee how much it will grow.

--- paso | fa-calendar-check-o | It's never too late
If you start at 50, you have less time, but every year of contributions counts.

From age 50, some retirement plans and accounts allow extra contributions. Ask your plan how much you can put in.

You can also reduce expenses that will continue in retirement, like paying off debts before you stop working.

--- comprueba
1. Is a 20-year projection a promise? || No. It's a possible scenario, not a guarantee.
2. Should you cash out your 401(k) when you change jobs without looking at options? || No. It can cost taxes and penalties; first check whether you can transfer it.

--- recuerda
- Know your possible sources of retirement income.
- Take advantage of your employer's contribution.
- Start with a little, but start.

== profundiza

--- tema | fa-balance-scale | Traditional or Roth
The difference is when you pay taxes:

- **Traditional:** you don't pay taxes now, you do when you withdraw.
- **Roth:** you pay taxes now, not when you withdraw (if you follow the rules).

Neither is always better. It depends on your situation; ask a professional.

--- tabla | fa-line-chart | Scenarios, not forecasts
Contributing 100 a month:

| Scenario | 20 years | 30 years |
|---|---|---|
| No return | 24,000 | 36,000 |
| Hypothetical return of 5% a year | 41,103 | 83,226 |
| Same 5%, in today's dollars with 3% inflation | 22,757 | 34,288 |

The 5% is hypothetical. Inflation reduces what you'll be able to buy.

--- tema | fa-exchange | If you change jobs
Before taking money out of a plan when you change jobs, check the transfer options, the costs, the taxes and the penalties.

Cashing out before the allowed age can cost up to a third of the money in taxes and penalties.

> **Good to know:** CalSavers is required for many California employers that don't offer a retirement plan. If your employer enrolls you, you can choose how much to contribute or opt out.

--- tema | fa-home | Your housing and your retirement
Housing is one of the biggest expenses in retirement. Getting there without a mortgage, or with rent you can afford, changes a lot how much you need.

Some people plan to retire in Mexico, where the cost of living may be lower. If that's your case, check access to health care, the cost of sending money and how you'd receive your U.S. income.

Write down where you plan to live and what it would cost. That number makes your plan more real.
