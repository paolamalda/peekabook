# M1 U08 | How do I decide what to use my money for?
objetivo: Build a budget that works with your real resources and that you can adjust if your life changes.
gancho: Alex and Mar made a budget that added up perfectly. A month later, Mar got fewer shifts and the plan fell apart. It wasn't a bad budget: it was missing a plan B. In this lesson you'll learn to make a budget that can handle a hard month.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-map | A budget is a plan
A **{{budget|The plan for where your money will go before you spend it.}}** is the plan for where your money will go before you spend it.

It doesn't require money to be left over. If you're short, a good budget shows it so you can look for a solution in time.

--- ecuacion | fa-calculator | Add up where it goes
You have 100. You set aside 60 for food and transportation, 30 for a payment and 10 for another need.
= 60 | Food and transportation
+ 40 | Payment and another need
= 100 | All your money has a job
If you need 120 in total, you're 20 short. Knowing it today gives you time to decide.

--- pasos | fa-sort-amount-desc | The order of priorities
1. **The basics:** housing, food, health, child care and transportation to work.
2. **Obligations:** debts and taxes, and what happens if you don't pay them.
3. **Family support** you've agreed on, like remittances.
4. **Savings and goals.**

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | Three ways to organize it
* fa-list | By category | How much goes to each type of expense: rent, food, transportation. | The easiest way to start. | igual
* fa-bullseye | Zero-based | Every dollar has a job, including savings. | It doesn't mean spending it all. | igual
* fa-envelope-o | Envelopes | You split your money into envelopes, in cash or in apps. | It helps you not go over in each category. | igual

--- paso | fa-life-ring | Your plan B: the month with less income
Make two budgets: one with your usual income and one with 20% less.

For each adjustment, write what you'll do and for how long. For example: "I cut this category for one month" or "I ask to review this debt."

> **Key idea:** there is no percentage that works for every household. Use your real numbers.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Luis earns 2,600 a month. His rent is 750, food 400, transportation 130, phone 60, and he sends 300 to his mom. That adds up to 1,640 and leaves him 960.

It looks like a lot, but he hasn't counted work clothes, going out or expenses that aren't monthly, like his roommate's car registration that he helps pay.

With everything written down, he decides: 200 for savings, 150 for personal spending, 100 for non-monthly expenses and the rest for a study goal.

--- pasos | fa-check-square-o | Do it this week
1. Write down your net income for the month.
2. Write down your basic expenses, your obligations and your family support.
3. Subtract: do you have money left or are you short?
4. Do the same math with 20% less income.

--- comprueba
1. Does a budget require money to be left over? || No. If you're short, it should show it so you can look for a solution.
2. A 240 payment is due in 6 months and you haven't set anything aside. How much do you set aside each month? || 40, because 240 ÷ 6 = 40.

--- recuerda
- First the basics, then obligations, then family and goals.
- Have a budget for a month with 20% less income.
- A shortfall you've identified is already progress.

== profundiza

--- tema | fa-calendar | Expenses that aren't monthly
If a 240 yearly payment is due in 6 months and you haven't set anything aside, you need 40 a month until that date.

Dividing by 12 (20 a month) would leave you short. After the payment, 20 a month is enough for the next year.

Car registration, school supplies or renewing a document aren't emergencies: you can see them coming.

--- tema | fa-exclamation-triangle | If there's a deficit
A {{deficit|When what you need to pay is more than what you have.}} is when what you need to pay is more than what you have. Order your actions like this:

1. Protect basic needs.
2. Check for support programs and your rights.
3. Negotiate obligations.
4. Cut expenses that can be replaced.
5. Look for realistic extra income, minus its costs.

Don't fill the budget with made-up income, and don't call it "saving" when you put what was missing on a credit card.

--- tabla | fa-table | Alex and Mar's budget
| Item | Usual month | Month with 20% less |
|---|---|---|
| Income | 2,400 | 1,920 |
| Basic expenses | 1,800 | 1,800 |
| Debt | 200 | 200 |
| Remittance | 250 | 250 |
| Savings | 150 | 150 |
| **Result** | **0** | **−480** |

In a hard month they're 480 short of keeping everything the same.

--- tema | fa-folder-open | Keep an emergency fund
An {{emergency fund|Money you set aside only for surprises, like an illness or losing work hours.}} is money for surprises, like an illness or losing work hours.

Start with a small goal, for example one week of basic expenses. Keep it apart from your day-to-day money.

> **Good to know:** according to Federal Reserve surveys, many families in the U.S. couldn't cover a surprise 400-dollar expense with cash or its equivalent. Starting small already puts you ahead.

--- tema | fa-balance-scale | What about the 50/30/20 rule?
You've probably heard of the 50/30/20 rule: 50% for needs, 30% for wants and 20% for savings.

It can be a starting point, but in cities with high rent almost nobody can follow it. If rent is already 45% of your income, you didn't fail: your city is expensive.

Use it as a reference, not as a test. What matters is that your plan is realistic and that you can keep it up.

--- tema | fa-mobile | Tools for your budget
You can make your budget in a notebook, in a spreadsheet on your phone or with your bank's app, which often sorts your expenses on its own.

What matters isn't the tool, but reviewing it every month and adjusting it when your life changes.

--- casos
### Case 1. Alex and Mar's hard month
Their income drops from 2,400 to 1,920 and they're 480 short.
? What adjustments can they make? || Pause savings (150), agree with the family on a smaller remittance for two months (100) and ask for a payment plan on the debt (100). That adds up to 350.
? And the 130 still missing? || Look for support programs and review basic expenses. It's okay if a solution is still missing.

### Case 2. Is the subscription the problem?
Canceling a 15 subscription leaves a 465 shortfall.
? What does this show? || That the problem calls for reviewing big decisions and looking for support, not blaming the subscription.

### Case 3. Daniela's envelopes
Daniela gets income from her hourly job and from her cake orders. She used to mix it all together, and at the end of the month she didn't know what had happened.
? What changes? || She sets up three digital envelopes in her bank app: home, business and taxes. Each payment gets split up the same day.

--- errores
* Using an "ideal" percentage that isn't your reality | The plan fails from the first month | Start with your real numbers
* Dividing a yearly payment by 12 when only a few months are left | You come up short when it's due | Divide by the months that are left
* Calling it saving when you put what was missing on a card | The debt grows without you noticing | Record the shortfall as a deficit
* Having no plan for a hard month | You decide in a hurry and it costs more | Build a budget with 20% less

# M1 U09 | Does filing taxes mean I'll get money back?
objetivo: Tell apart filing, paying and getting a refund, and know what questions to ask.
gancho: Mar expected a refund like last year. This time, when she did her tax return, it turned out she owed 400 dollars. She didn't do anything wrong: the way she gets paid changed. In this lesson you'll learn why sometimes you get money back and sometimes you pay.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-file-text | Filing is not the same as getting money back
**Filing your {{tax return|The document you send the government each year with what you earned, to calculate how much tax you owe.}}** means reporting your information and calculating your tax for the year.

A **{{refund|The money you get back when you paid more tax during the year than you owed.}}** only exists if you paid too much during the year. They're different things.

--- ecuacion | fa-calculator | You paid too much or too little
The calculated tax is 100. During the year you already paid 120, because it was taken out of your wages.
= 120 | What you already paid
- 100 | What you owed
= 20 | Refund in your favor
If you had only paid 80, you would owe 20.

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | A deduction and a credit are not the same
* fa-minus-circle | {{Deduction|An amount subtracted from your income before the tax is calculated. It lowers what you pay a little.}} | It reduces the income your tax is calculated on. | It lowers part of your tax. | igual
* fa-gift | {{Tax credit|An amount subtracted directly from your tax. Some give you money even if you owe nothing.}} | It reduces your tax directly. | It lowers your tax dollar for dollar. | si

--- paso | fa-id-card-o | SSN and ITIN
The **SSN** is issued by Social Security. The **{{ITIN|A number the IRS gives people who can't get an SSN so they can pay taxes. It doesn't give permission to work.}}** is issued by the IRS to people who must file and can't get an SSN.

The ITIN **doesn't give you permission to work or change your immigration status**. Never use someone else's number or a made-up one.

> **Good to know:** if you don't use your ITIN on a federal return for three years in a row, the IRS deactivates it. Renewing it can take months and delay your refund and your state credits, like CalEITC and YCTC.

--- paso | fa-calendar | Filing and paying have dates
There's a date to file your return and another to pay what you owe. They usually both fall in mid-April.

An **extension to file** usually does **not** give you more time to pay.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Daniela is paid by the hour at her job, with withholding, and she also sells cakes, without withholding.

She does her tax return and finds she owes nothing for her job, but she does owe taxes on the cakes.

For next year, she sets aside part of each order in a digital "taxes" envelope. That way, when April comes, the money will already be there.

--- paso | fa-question-circle | Do I have to file?
It depends on how much you earned, your filing status and how you earned your money.

If you work for yourself, in many cases you must file even if you earned little. And even if you don't have to, filing can get you credits you're entitled to.

Ask at VITA or a preparer with credentials whether you have to file this year.

--- pasos | fa-check-square-o | Do it this week
1. Put your W-2 and 1099 forms in a folder.
2. Check whether your ITIN or SSN and your family's are active.
3. Write down this year's deadline.

--- comprueba
1. Does filing your return guarantee a refund? || No. There's only a refund if you paid too much during the year.
2. Does the ITIN allow you to work? || No. It's only for meeting your tax obligations.

--- recuerda
- Filing is reporting; a refund only exists if you paid too much.
- A credit lowers your tax more than a deduction of the same size.
- Use your ITIN every year so it doesn't get deactivated.

== profundiza

--- tema | fa-percent | Deduction versus credit with numbers
With a made-up rate of 10%:

- a 100 deduction lowers your tax by 10;
- a 100 credit lowers it by 100.

Some credits are **refundable**: they can give you money even if you owe no tax.

--- tema | fa-bar-chart | Marginal rate and effective rate
In a made-up scale, the first 1,000 pays 10% and the next 1,000 pays 20%.

With an income of 2,000, the tax is 100 + 200 = 300. The rate on the last bracket is 20%, but the {{effective rate|The percentage of tax you actually pay on all your income.}} is 300 ÷ 2,000 = 15%.

Moving into the next bracket does **not** make all your income pay the higher rate. Earning more always leaves you with more money.

--- tema | fa-university | Federal and state
There's a federal return, with the IRS, and in California a state return, with the Franchise Tax Board (FTB).

Your residency for tax purposes, your filing status and your dependents are decided by rules and facts, not by your nationality. Tax residency and immigration status are different things.

--- tema | fa-money | Withholding and estimated payments
What's taken out of your wages is an advance on your tax.

If you get paid through apps, in cash or on your own, nobody withholds anything. That's why you may owe at the end of the year. If that's your case, set money aside during the year (you'll see it in M1 U10).

> **Good to know:** the IRS usually contacts you first by mail. Be suspicious of calls, texts or emails demanding immediate payment.

--- tema | fa-folder-open | What you need to file
- Your ID and your SSN or ITIN, and your dependents'.
- Your W-2 and 1099 forms.
- Your record of income and expenses if you work for yourself.
- Last year's return, if you have it.
- Your bank account information to receive the refund.

Bring everything to your appointment. If something is missing, ask whether you can turn it in later.

--- tema | fa-shield | Watch out for tax scams
Some scams pretend to be the IRS: they call, text or email demanding immediate payment with gift cards or transfers.

The IRS doesn't ask you to pay with gift cards or threaten to arrest you over the phone. If you have doubts, go to IRS.gov yourself.

--- tema | fa-exclamation-circle | If you can't pay it all
If you find out you owe when you file and can't pay it all, file your return on time anyway. Not filing usually costs more than not paying.

The IRS and the FTB have installment payment plans. Request them on their official websites.

--- casos
### Case 1. The expected refund
Mar believes that "filing taxes is so they give you money back." This year she got part of her income through an app, with no withholding. When she does the math, she owes 400.
? What happened? || Nobody withheld taxes from what she got through the app.
? What can she do next year? || Set aside a little from each app payment or make payments during the year.

### Case 2. Dependents in Mexico
Alex sends money to Alex's mother in Michoacán.
? Can she be claimed as a dependent? || It isn't automatic. There are rules about relationship, residency and support that change. Alex should take the information to a preparer with credentials.

### Case 3. Luis's ITIN
Luis got his ITIN four years ago, but he didn't file for the last three.
? What can happen? || His ITIN may be deactivated. He should check it before filing so he can renew it in time.

--- errores
* Thinking that filing always brings money | A debt catches you by surprise | Check whether taxes were withheld during the year
* Believing that moving into a higher bracket takes money away | You turn down overtime or a raise | The higher rate only applies to what's above the bracket
* Not using your ITIN | It gets deactivated and your credits are delayed | File every year
* Paying someone who calls demanding an IRS payment | You lose money to a scam | Go to IRS.gov yourself to check

== practica

--- actividad
**What would you do? (H5P):** three situations from this lesson with Luis, Alex and Mar. Choose the best decision in each one; if you miss, you can try again. It earns experience points.

respuesta: In the first case you owe 300. In the second, you get a 200 refund. This isn't a calculation with real tax tables.

--- plan

== palabras
- *Tax return:* a document that reports your information and calculates your tax.
- *Refund:* money back when you paid too much.
- *Withholding:* an advance on taxes taken out of your wages.
- *Deduction:* it lowers the amount the tax is calculated on.
- *Tax credit:* it lowers the tax.
- *ITIN:* the IRS tax number for people who can't get an SSN.

# M1 U10 | Is everything I earn on my own for spending?
objetivo: Organize your records based on your type of income: wages, self-employment or both.
gancho: Daniela makes cakes to order on top of her hourly job. In April she realized she didn't know how much she had earned from the cakes or how much she had spent on ingredients. In this lesson you'll learn to keep your records so tax season isn't a headache.

== esencial

--- ecuacion | fa-calculator | Getting paid is not the same as earning
You charge 100 for a job and spent 30 on materials.
= 100 | What you charged
- 30 | What you spent to do the work
= 70 | Your profit before taxes
Keep the receipt for the 30. It helps you prove your expense.

--- tarjetas | fa-folder-open | Three folders depending on your work
* fa-building | Wages | Pay stubs, W-2 form and withholding. | Check that your information is correct. | igual
* fa-wrench | Self-employment | Payments in cash, through apps and by transfer; expenses with receipts; tax payments you made. | Keep records all year, not only in April. | no
* fa-random | Both | Both folders and one sheet that brings them together. | Count each income only once. | igual

--- paso | fa-book | The daily log
If you get paid in cash, through apps or get tips, write down at the end of each day how much you received and how.

Separately, write down what you spent on materials, gas or tools, and take a photo of the receipt that same day.

At the end of the month, add it up: what you received minus what you spent to work is your real profit.

--- paso | fa-mobile | App income counts too
The IRS says that what you earn through apps may have to be reported even if you don't get any form.

The **{{1099 form|A form some companies or apps send to tell the IRS how much they paid you.}}** reports certain payments, but it doesn't calculate your profit or decide whether you're an employee.

> **Key idea:** if nobody withholds taxes for you, set money aside from each payment from day one.

--- pasos | fa-check-square-o | Do it this week
1. Create two folders, on paper or on your phone: work income and work expenses.
2. Start your daily log of payments.
3. Take a photo of each receipt the same day.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Alex does weekend jobs with a pickup truck. In one year Alex charged 6,000 and spent 1,500 on materials and 900 on gas.

Because each trip was written down, Alex can prove how many miles were used for work. The profit before taxes is 3,600, not 6,000.

Without those records, Alex could pay taxes on money that was actually spent on the work.

--- paso | fa-money | Set money aside for taxes from day one
If you work for yourself, nobody takes taxes out for you. That's why it helps to open a separate envelope or account just for taxes.

Each time you get paid, move part of it to that envelope. That way, when it's time to pay, you won't have to take the money from rent or food.

--- comprueba
1. You move 500 from your business account to your personal account. Is it new income? || No. It only changed places.
2. An app didn't send you a form. Do you report that income? || It may have to be reported. Record everything you receive.

--- recuerda
- What you charge is not what you earn: subtract your costs.
- Write down your payments and expenses every day.
- Set money aside for taxes if nobody withholds them.

== profundiza

--- tema | fa-random | Reconcile so you don't count twice
If a platform reports 1,000 and your bank received two deposits of 500 for those same payments, your income is 1,000, not 2,000.

Moving money from the business account to your personal account isn't new income or a business expense.

A family remittance isn't a business expense, even if it comes out of the business account.

--- tema | fa-car | Using your car and phone for both
If you use your car or phone for work and for personal use, you need records that show how you separate each use.

For the car, write down each work trip: date, destination, purpose and miles. An estimate without data doesn't work as proof.

--- tema | fa-calendar-check-o | Estimated payments
If you work for yourself and nobody withholds taxes, you may need to make {{estimated payments|Tax payments you make several times a year when nobody takes them out of your pay.}} during the year.

There may be income tax and also self-employment tax, which covers your Social Security and Medicare.

Don't set aside "10%" just because someone else does. Calculate your case with professional guidance and update it when your income changes.

> **Good to know:** the IRS splits estimated payments into four dates a year. Paying late can bring penalties even if you pay it all in the end.

--- tema | fa-file-text-o | What each receipt should have
Date, seller, amount, what it was for and how you paid.

If one is missing, write down which one and how to get it back, for example by asking the seller for a copy. Never make up amounts or receipts.

--- tema | fa-id-card | If your business grows
If your business grows, you may need:

- a business license from your city;
- a seller's permit if you sell products, for example food;
- an employer identification number (EIN) if you hire someone.

You'll see it in detail in M5 U10. For now, keep good records: they're the basis for everything else.

--- tema | fa-university | Keep your accounts separate
Having an account just for your business makes everything easier: you see how much comes in, how much you spend and how much you earn, without mixing it with your personal life.

You don't need a business account with a fee. A second personal account with no fees can work at first.

Pay yourself a fixed amount, for example every week, by moving money from the business account to your personal account.

--- casos
### Case 1. Mar's tips and app
Mar gets her wages through payroll, tips in cash and some payments through an app. The app sends her a 1099 for 3,200. Her cash tips added up to 4,500 and don't show up on any form.
? What must she report? || All of it: wages, the app payments and the tips.
? How does she prove it? || With her daily tip log.

### Case 2. Alex's faded receipts
Alex does weekend jobs and kept the receipts for materials in a box. By the end of the year many had faded.
? What changes? || Now Alex takes a photo of each receipt the same day and saves it in a folder on the phone.

### Case 3. Daniela's cakes
Daniela gets paid for her orders in cash and through Zelle. She buys ingredients at three different stores.
? How does she get organized? || An order log with date, customer, amount and payment method, and a folder with photos of each ingredient receipt.

--- errores
* Counting the app deposit and the transfer to your account | You report too much | Reconcile and count each payment only once
* Not writing down cash | You can't prove your income | Keep a daily log
* Keeping receipts without checking them | They fade and become useless | Take a photo of them the same day
* Setting aside a percentage you "heard about" | You're short or have too much when you file | Calculate your case with a professional

== practica

--- actividad
**What would you do? (H5P):** three situations from this lesson with Daniela, Alex and Mar. Choose the best decision in each one; if you miss, you can try again. It earns experience points.

--- quiz
1. Can app income have to be reported even if you get no form? a) It can · b) Never · c) Only if it's over 600
2. Is moving 500 from the business account to your personal account new income? a) Yes · b) No · c) Yes, if it's more than 400
3. Should you set aside "10%" for taxes because a friend does? a) Yes · b) No, you need to calculate your case · c) Yes, and 5% if you're paid in cash
respuestas: 1-a: the IRS says it can be. 2-b: it only changed places. 3-b: every case is different.

--- ponlo
Payments received 1,000; work costs 250; transfer to your personal account 500. What is the result before taxes?
respuesta: 750. The 500 transfer isn't more income or another expense.

--- plan
Prepare folders for income, expenses, health, family and notices from the IRS or the FTB. If you have property or income in another country, write that question down for your preparer.

# M1 U11 | What tax benefits are there if I file with an ITIN?
objetivo: Learn how to get an ITIN, which credits may apply to your household and where to get no-cost help to file.
gancho: Mar thought that, because she files with an ITIN, she wasn't entitled to any benefit. At a no-cost tax help site she found out her family could get state credits. In this lesson you'll learn what benefits exist if you file with an ITIN in California.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-money | Filing with an ITIN can put money in your pocket
Filing with an ITIN can give you access to state credits that put money in your pocket.

It also creates a record that you meet your tax obligations every year.

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | California credits that accept an ITIN
* fa-briefcase | {{CalEITC|A California credit for people who work and have low incomes. You can claim it with an ITIN.}} | State credit for work income for low-income households. | You can claim it with an ITIN. | si
* fa-child | {{Young Child Tax Credit|An extra California credit if you qualify for CalEITC and have a child under 6.}} (YCTC) | Extra credit if you qualify for CalEITC and have a child under 6. | You can claim it with an ITIN. | si
* fa-flag | Federal credits (EITC and others) | Several federal credits require an SSN. | Check the requirements each year. | no

--- pasos | fa-list-ol | Three steps
1. **Find out if you need an ITIN:** if you must file and can't get an SSN.
2. **Check which credits exist** for your situation this year.
3. **Look at no cost, certified help** to prepare your return.

--- paso | fa-handshake-o | Free help to file
**{{VITA|An IRS program where certified volunteers help you prepare your tax return at no cost.}}** is an IRS program with certified volunteers. It helps people with incomes under a limit that is published every year, at no cost.

Many sites offer help in Spanish and help with the ITIN application.

== esencial

--- tarjetas | fa-id-badge | Credentials in California

# M1 U13 | What should I know before I apply for public benefits?

--- paso | fa-balance-scale | What public charge is
**{{Public charge|A review the government does in some immigration cases to see whether a person might depend mainly on government benefits.}}** is a review done in some immigration cases, mainly certain applications for permanent residency.

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | What the rule in effect since 2022 looks at

When you started the course you took, or can take, the CFPB financial well-being questionnaire.

Take it again every 90 days. It isn't about having more money, but about feeling more in control and less stressed.

> **Good to know:** writing down a goal with a date and sharing it with someone you trust makes you more likely to reach it.

--- tema | fa-trophy | Celebrate your progress
Changing the way you handle money takes time. Every action you complete counts.

Write down in your plan what you've already done, even if it seems small: "I made my first calendar," "I canceled a subscription," "I went to VITA."

Seeing your progress encourages you to keep going. And if something didn't work, write that down too: it's information to adjust, not a failure.

--- tema | fa-life-ring | If you need help
If your plan shows a shortfall you can't cover, don't try to solve it alone.

Look for county support programs, community organizations and no-cost financial counseling. The "Where to find help" chapter in the support materials has a list.

--- tema | fa-arrow-right | What's next
In Module 2 you'll learn to choose where to keep your money and to plan what you send to your family.

The calendar and budget from this module will help you decide how much you can send and when, without coming up short.

--- errores
* Writing goals without dates | They never start | Give each action a day
* Choosing too many priorities | You get overwhelmed and give up | Start with three
* Keeping an impossible savings amount | The plan breaks | Adjust to your real income
* Not reviewing the plan | You follow a plan that no longer works | Review it every 30 days
