# M4 U01 | Why is someone pushing me to act right now?
objetivo: Recognize how scams work and verify on your own before you act.
gancho: Rosa got a message: "Your account will be locked in 20 minutes. Confirm your code." Her heart started racing. She was about to answer when her granddaughter said: "Grandma, wait." In this lesson you'll learn to recognize rushing as the scammer's tool.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-hourglass-start | Rushing is the trap
A scam wants you to act before you think. That's why it uses urgency, fear, love or a gain that sounds too good.

You don't need to prove that a message is fake to give yourself time to check it. If someone won't let you think, that's already a sign.

--- tarjetas | fa-exclamation-triangle | Phrases that deserve a pause
* fa-clock-o | "Today only" or "you have 20 minutes" | They create urgency so you don't compare. | Stop. | no
* fa-user-secret | "Don't tell anyone" | They isolate you so nobody warns you. | Tell someone you trust. | no
* fa-key | "Give me the code you received" | With it they get into your account. | Never share it. | no
* fa-gift | "Pay with gift cards or cryptocurrency" | These payments are almost impossible to get back. | No serious company charges that way. | no

--- pasos | fa-list-ol | Four steps for any suspicious message
1. **Stop.** Don't pay, don't click and don't give information.
2. **Identify what they're asking for:** money, a code, your information or to install something.
3. **Verify on your own,** with a contact you already knew or by looking up the official website yourself.
4. **Decide with information.**

--- paso | fa-phone | What "verify on your own" means
It means using a number or a website that **you** looked up, not the one in the message.

For example: if the message says it's from your bank, call the number on the back of your card.

> **Key idea:** knowing your name or part of your account number doesn't prove they're legitimate.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Rosa hung up, waited and called the number on her card. The bank told her that her account was fine and that they never ask for codes by text.

Now she has a rule: "I never pay or give codes on the first call or message."

--- pasos | fa-check-square-o | Do it this week
1. Write your pause rule and stick it next to your phone.
2. Write down on paper the official numbers of your bank and your phone company.
3. Tell your family which scams are going around.

--- comprueba
1. A message says it's from your bank and includes a number to call. Which one do you call? || The one you look up, for example the one on your card. Never the one in the message.
2. Will your bank ask you for the code you got by text? || No. That code is only for you.

--- recuerda
- Rushing and secrecy are signs of a scam.
- Always verify through a channel you looked up.
- Never share codes or pay with gift cards.

== profundiza

--- tema | fa-lock | Things that prove nothing
- **The browser padlock** only says the connection is encrypted, not that the website is honest.
- **A nice-looking website**, a paid ad or testimonials don't prove anything either.
- **Knowing your name** or your last digits: that information leaks or is bought.

--- tabla | fa-list | Common scams
| Scam | How it works |
|---|---|
| Fake job | They hire you fast and ask you to pay for training or forward money |
| Rental that doesn't exist | They ask for a deposit on an apartment that isn't theirs |
| Fake immigration help | They promise guaranteed papers (M4 U02) |
| Fake tax notice | They say they're the IRS and demand immediate payment |
| Online romance | After weeks of trust, they ask for money |
| "Guaranteed" investment | They promise high gains with no risk |
| Family emergency | A voice or message asks for urgent money (M2 U11) |

--- tema | fa-money | The overpayment check
They send you a check for more than agreed and ask you to return the difference.

The money shows as available in your account, but the check bounces days later. You lose what you sent, and the bank can charge you for the check too.

--- tema | fa-shopping-cart | Buying on social media
Many scams happen on Marketplace, WhatsApp groups or ads.

If you buy from a stranger, prefer seeing the item in person, in a public place, or paying with a credit card, which has more protection.

> **Good to know:** according to the FTC, people in the U.S. reported fraud losses of more than 10 billion dollars in a single year. Reporting, even if you didn't lose money, helps stop scammers.

--- tema | fa-bullhorn | Where to report
Report at **ReportFraud.ftc.gov**. If you lost money, also tell your bank right away and, if it happened online, the FBI's IC3.

--- tema | fa-users | Protect your family
Older people and young people who just arrived are often targets of scams.

Talk with your family about the most common scams and agree on a simple rule: nobody pays or gives codes without first checking with another family member.

--- casos
### Case 1. The job with a check
An "employer" sends Alex a check to buy equipment and asks Alex to transfer part of it to a "supplier."
? What does Alex do? || Verifies the company independently and checks with the bank before moving any money.
? What does Alex find out? || The job didn't exist. It was the overpayment check scam.

### Case 2. The message from the bank
Rosa gets: "Your account will be closed in 20 minutes, send the code."
? What does she do? || She doesn't share the code and calls the number on her card.

### Case 3. Daniela's rental
Daniela found a cheap apartment on social media. The "owner" asked her for the deposit by Zelle before she saw it, because he "was out of town."
? What should she do? || Not pay without seeing the place and without verifying that the person is the owner or manager.

--- errores
* Acting in a hurry | You pay or give information to a scammer | Apply your pause rule
* Calling the number in the message | You talk to the scammer | Look up the official number yourself
* Believing the padlock proves it's real | You give information on a fake website | Check the website address
* Not reporting out of embarrassment | The scam keeps going with others | Report at ReportFraud.ftc.gov

# M4 U02 | Who can really help me with an immigration case?
objetivo: Recognize the frauds that hurt immigrants the most (notarios, fake lawyers, "express asylum" and government impostors) and know where to verify.
gancho: Mar's neighbor was offered a "work permit in three months" at an office with a "Notaria – Inmigración" sign. They asked for 5,000 dollars upfront. In this lesson you'll learn who can really help you and how to verify it.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-balance-scale | Only two can give you immigration advice
In the U.S., only these can give you advice about your immigration case:

1. **Licensed attorneys**, and
2. **{{accredited representatives|People authorized by the Department of Justice to give immigration advice within a recognized organization.}} of the Department of Justice (DOJ)**, who work at recognized organizations.

Nobody else: not notarios, not "helpers," not social media "advisors."

--- paso | fa-pencil-square-o | A "notario" in the U.S. is not a lawyer
In many Latin American countries, a notario is a legal professional. In the U.S., a **{{notary public|A person who only certifies signatures. In the U.S. they are not a lawyer and can't give immigration advice.}}** only certifies signatures.

In California it's illegal to advertise as a "notario" to offer immigration services.

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | Who can do what
* fa-gavel | Licensed attorney | Advises and represents you. | Check them with the State Bar. | si
* fa-certificate | Accredited representative (DOJ) | Advises and represents you from a recognized organization. | Check the DOJ list. | si
* fa-pencil | Immigration consultant | Only non-legal tasks: translating or filling out forms with your information. | Must be registered and give you a contract. | igual
* fa-ban | "Notario" or social media helper | No immigration advice at all. | A red flag. | no

--- paso | fa-exclamation-triangle | Watch out for "express asylum"
Be wary of anyone who promises a fast work permit by filing an asylum application, even if you don't qualify.

Filing a false application with USCIS is a serious crime and can put you into deportation proceedings that can't be undone.

> **Key idea:** always confirm your case with a licensed attorney or a DOJ-accredited representative.

--- pasos | fa-list-ol | Three steps before hiring help
1. **Ask:** "Are you an attorney or a DOJ-accredited representative?"
2. **Verify** their license or accreditation at the official source.
3. **Ask for a written contract** with the total cost. Never pay for guaranteed "arrangements."

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Rosa wanted to help her granddaughter with an application and found an "advisor" on social media who charged 800 dollars.

Before paying, she looked up the name with the State Bar and on the DOJ list. It wasn't on either. She called a recognized organization in Fresno and got a no-cost appointment.

--- pasos | fa-check-square-o | Do it this week
1. Find a recognized organization near you on the DOJ list.
2. Save its phone number and address.
3. Share this information with your family.

--- comprueba
1. Can a notary public give you immigration advice? || No. They only certify signatures.
2. Will ICE or USCIS ask you to pay by phone with gift cards? || No. No government agency charges that way.

--- recuerda
- Only attorneys and accredited representatives can advise you.
- Always verify the license or accreditation.
- Nobody can guarantee the outcome of a case.

== profundiza

--- tema | fa-search | How to verify
- **Attorney:** look up their name and license number with the State Bar of California, or of the state where they're licensed.
- **Accredited representative:** check the DOJ's (EOIR) official list of recognized organizations and accredited representatives.
- **Immigration consultant:** check their registration and bond with the California Secretary of State.

--- tema | fa-user-secret | Government impostors
People who call, write or visit saying they're from ICE, USCIS, the IRS or the police:

- demand immediate payment to "avoid a deportation," "release a relative" or "cancel a fine";
- ask you to pay with gift cards, cryptocurrency, wire transfers or apps;
- threaten to arrest you if you hang up.

Government agencies **don't** ask for payments like that. Hang up and check the official website.

--- tabla | fa-exclamation-circle | Red flags
| Sign | Why it's dangerous |
|---|---|
| They guarantee the outcome | Nobody can guarantee what the government decides |
| They charge for forms that cost nothing | USCIS forms can be downloaded at no cost |
| They ask you to sign blank forms or with false information | They can get you into a crime |
| They keep your originals | You lose important documents |
| They don't give you a contract or receipt | You have no way to complain |
| They sell "new programs" on social media | They're almost always fake |

> **Good to know:** USCIS forms cost nothing at uscis.gov. If someone charges you for the form, they're charging you for something that costs nothing.

--- tema | fa-bullhorn | Where to report
To the State Bar of California, the California Attorney General and the FTC (ReportFraud.ftc.gov).

Reporting helps other families and doesn't affect your situation. Many organizations will go with you to do it.

--- tema | fa-file-text-o | What an immigration services contract should have
- The name and license or accreditation number of the person helping you.
- Which services it includes and which it doesn't.
- The total cost and how it's paid.
- What happens if you cancel.

In California, immigration consultants must give you a written contract in your language and return your original documents.

--- casos
### Case 1. Mar's neighbor
A "Notaria – Inmigración" office promises Mar's neighbor "papers in 6 months" for 5,000 dollars.
? What does Mar do? || She goes with her neighbor to an organization with accredited representatives, which reviews her case at no cost.

### Case 2. The call from "ICE"
Alex gets a call: a cousin is detained and must pay 2,000 in gift cards to be released.
? What does Alex do? || Hangs up and calls the cousin and the family. The cousin was at work.

### Case 3. The fast permit
A social media "advisor" offers Daniela a work permit in three months "with an asylum application."
? What's the risk? || If she doesn't qualify, filing a false application is a crime and can lead to deportation proceedings.
? What does she do? || She checks with an accredited representative before doing any paperwork.

--- errores
* Trusting a "notario" or helper | You lose money and put your case at risk | Look for an attorney or accredited representative
* Paying for a guaranteed outcome | It's a scam | Nobody can guarantee it
* Signing applications with false information | A crime and deportation | Never sign something that isn't true
* Paying someone who says they're ICE on the phone | You lose money | Hang up and verify

# M4 U03 | What protects my accounts besides a password?
objetivo: Protect your access, your devices and the way you recover your accounts.
gancho: Alex used the same password for everything. An online store had a data breach and, with that password, someone got into Alex's email and from there tried to get into the bank. In this lesson you'll learn to protect your accounts with simple steps.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-envelope | Your email and your phone are the master key
Your email and your phone number are used to recover almost all your other accounts: bank, social media, apps.

That's why they're the first things to protect.

--- tarjetas | fa-shield | Five basic protections
* fa-key | Different passwords | One for your email and another for your bank. | A password manager helps. | si
* fa-mobile | Two-step verification | Besides the password, a code or an app. | Turn it on for email and bank. | si
* fa-refresh | Recovery information | Backup email and phone up to date. | Check them today. | si
* fa-lock | Phone lock | A PIN or fingerprint, and updates installed. | It keeps others from using your phone. | si
* fa-bell | Bank alerts | They let you know about every transaction. | You find out right away. | si

--- paso | fa-user-secret | Never share codes
The code you get by text is only for you. Nobody from your bank, your phone company or any other company will ask you for it.

If someone asks for it, it's a scam, even if they say they're from the bank.

--- paso | fa-desktop | Watch out for "tech support"
Don't install apps someone asks you to install during an unexpected call or pop-up. With them they can see and control your phone or computer.

> **Key idea:** protect your email and your phone number first.

--- pasos | fa-check-square-o | Do it this week
1. Change your email password to one you don't use anywhere else.
2. Turn on two-step verification for your email and your bank.
3. Check that your recovery email and phone are up to date.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Luis turned on two-step verification for his email and his bank. A month later he got a message: "Someone tried to sign in to your account from another country."

Since they didn't have the second code, they couldn't get in. Luis changed his password and carried on calmly.

--- comprueba
1. Which account do you protect first? || Your main email, because the others are recovered through it.
2. "The bank" calls and asks for the code you received. Do you share it? || No. You hang up and call the number on your card yourself.

--- recuerda
- Your email and your phone are the key to everything.
- A different password for each important account.
- Never share codes or install apps a stranger asks for.

== profundiza

--- tema | fa-mobile | Protect your phone number
There are scams where someone convinces your phone company to move your number to another SIM card. That way they get your codes.

Ask your phone company for a PIN or passcode for any change to your line. If your phone suddenly loses service for no reason, call your phone company.

--- tema | fa-mobile | App permissions
Before connecting an app, check what it can see, change and store, and how to remove its access.

A budgeting app doesn't need your contacts or your messages. Allowing it to read your transactions isn't allowing it to sell your information.

--- tema | fa-users | Shared devices
If you use a computer at a library or someone else's phone:

- sign out when you finish;
- don't save passwords;
- avoid banking on public Wi-Fi if you can.

--- tema | fa-exclamation-circle | If you lose your phone
1. Call your phone company to protect your line.
2. From another device, change your email password.
3. Tell your bank.
4. Use your paper contact list.

Always have a list of important contacts outside your phone.

> **Good to know:** turning on two-step verification blocks the vast majority of automated account takeover attempts, according to tech companies like Google and Microsoft.

--- tema | fa-heart | If there's control at home
If someone at home controls your phone or your accounts, changing all your passwords at once can increase the risk. First look for a safe path (M4 U05).

--- tema | fa-key | How to create a strong password
A long phrase is stronger and easier to remember than a short word with symbols.

For example, four words that have nothing to do with each other. Don't use your name, your date of birth or your children's names.

A password manager stores all your passwords and you only have to remember one.

--- casos
### Case 1. The same password
Alex used the same password for email and bank. The one from an online store leaked and someone got into Alex's email.
? Is changing the bank password enough? || No. The email was still compromised. Alex changed both and turned on two-step verification.

### Case 2. Tech support
A pop-up on Mar's computer says "virus detected, call this number." They ask her to install an app.
? What does she do? || She closes the browser and doesn't call.

### Case 3. Rosa's phone
Rosa shares her phone with her grandchildren.
? What changes? || She puts a PIN on her bank app and doesn't save the password on the phone.

--- errores
* Using the same password for everything | If one leaks, they get into everything | A different one for each important account
* Sharing codes over the phone | They get into your account | Never share them
* Installing "support" apps | They control your device | Close the window and hang up
* Keeping everything only on your phone | If you lose it, you lose access | A paper contact list

# M4 U04 | What do I do if I already shared information or see something strange?
objetivo: Respond quickly and in order to fraud or identity theft.
gancho: Mar gave a code over the phone. Ten minutes later she realized what she had done. She panicked, but she knew what to do. In this lesson you'll learn the exact steps to respond to fraud.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-bolt | Act quickly
Reporting deadlines matter. The sooner you report, the easier it is to stop the damage and get your money back.

Don't blame yourself. These scams are designed to fool anyone. What matters is acting.

--- tabla | fa-table | What happened and what you do first
| What happened | First action |
|---|---|
| You lost your card | Lock it from the app or by phone |
| Someone got into your account | Change passwords from a safe device and report it |
| You paid because you were tricked | Tell the bank or app right away |
| A new account in your name | Report it at IdentityTheft.gov and freeze your credit |
| A wrong charge | File a dispute with the card issuer |

--- pasos | fa-list-ol | General steps
1. **Stop talking** to the suspect.
2. **From a safe device,** contact the bank or company through its official channel.
3. **Describe** what happened: date, amount and what you did or didn't authorize.
4. **Ask** for protective measures and a confirmation number.
5. **Keep evidence:** messages, receipts and a timeline.

--- paso | fa-snowflake-o | Freeze your credit
**{{Freezing your credit|Blocking your report at no cost so nobody can open new accounts in your name.}}** costs nothing at all three agencies. It keeps new accounts from being opened in your name.

It doesn't protect your current accounts or erase debts, but it closes the door to new fraud. You can lift it when you need to apply for credit.

> **Key idea:** be wary of anyone who promises to "recover all your money" in exchange for an upfront payment. It's the second scam.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Daniela saw two charges in her account from an online store she didn't know.

She locked her card from the app, called the bank, disputed the charges and asked for a new card. She wrote down the confirmation number on her incident sheet. A week later she got her money back.

--- pasos | fa-check-square-o | Do it this week
1. Create your blank incident sheet, ready to use.
2. Write down on paper the phone numbers of your bank and the three credit agencies.
3. Consider freezing your credit if you don't plan to borrow soon.

--- comprueba
1. Does a credit freeze protect your existing accounts? || No. It prevents new accounts, but you have to keep watching the current ones.
2. Where do you create a recovery plan for identity theft? || At the FTC's IdentityTheft.gov.

--- recuerda
- Act quickly and through official channels.
- Ask for and keep a confirmation number.
- Freeze your credit if there's a risk of identity theft.

== profundiza

--- tema | fa-id-card | Identity theft
It's when someone uses your information to pretend to be you: opening accounts, taking out loans or even filing tax returns.

At **IdentityTheft.gov** you create a report and get a step-by-step recovery plan, with sample letters for banks and agencies.

--- tema | fa-snowflake-o | Freeze and alert
- **Freeze:** nobody can open new credit in your name. You request it at each of the three agencies.
- **Fraud alert:** companies must verify your identity before opening something in your name. You request it at one agency and it notifies the other two.

Both cost nothing.

--- tema | fa-file-text-o | Your incident sheet
Write down on a sheet:

- what happened and when;
- who you called, who you spoke with and what they told you;
- the confirmation numbers;
- the letters you sent and received.

This sheet saves you from repeating everything each time you call.

> **Good to know:** if someone filed a tax return with your SSN or ITIN, the IRS has a special process. You'll find it at IdentityTheft.gov, in the taxes section.

--- tema | fa-ambulance | If there's danger
If there's immediate physical danger, call 911. The course's questions aren't an emergency channel.

--- tema | fa-unlock | How to freeze and unfreeze
You request the freeze on each agency's website: Equifax, Experian and TransUnion. They give you a PIN or an account to manage it.

When you need to apply for credit or rent, you "thaw" it temporarily in minutes and then freeze it again. It costs nothing.

--- tema | fa-child | Your children's information
Children can also be victims of identity theft. You can freeze your minor children's credit at all three agencies, at no cost.

Don't share their Social Security numbers or photos of their documents.

--- casos
### Case 1. The unknown account
Alex's report shows a loan Alex never asked for.
? What does Alex do? || Documents it, contacts the institution and the agency, follows the IdentityTheft.gov guide and freezes the credit.
? What doesn't Alex do? || Pay the "cancellation fee" someone asks for over the phone.

### Case 2. The shared code
Mar gave a code over the phone. Ten minutes later she realizes it.
? What does she do? || She calls the bank, locks her account, changes her password and checks her transactions.

### Case 3. Rosa's lost card
Rosa lost her wallet at the market.
? What does she do first? || She locks her card from the app and calls the bank. Then she checks what other documents were in the wallet.

--- errores
* Waiting to see if something happens | The damage grows | Act as soon as you notice
* Negotiating with the scammer | You lose more money | Stop talking to them
* Paying someone who promises to recover your money | A second scam | Only with your bank and the authorities
* Not keeping confirmation numbers or evidence | You can't prove what happened | Keep your incident sheet

# M4 U05 | Is talking about money the same as controlling someone?
objetivo: Recognize financial abuse and know options for safe support.
gancho: This lesson uses a made-up case. A woman hands her whole paycheck to her partner every week and has to ask permission to buy anything. He keeps her passport "so it doesn't get lost." In this lesson you'll learn to tell a healthy agreement apart from a pattern of control.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-comments | Agreeing is not controlling
Agreeing on expenses as a couple or a family is healthy. Talking about money, making a budget together or asking for an opinion before a big purchase is too.

Taking someone's paycheck, keeping them from working or forcing them to sign a debt with threats is different.

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | Agreement or control
* fa-handshake-o | Agreement | You decide together, everyone gives an opinion and has some money of their own. | It's healthy. | si
* fa-lock | Control | One person decides everything, takes away money or documents, or threatens. | It may be financial abuse. | no

--- paso | fa-exclamation-triangle | Signs of control
- Taking away the paycheck or cards.
- Keeping someone from working or studying.
- Opening debts in another person's name.
- Keeping their ID documents.
- Threatening them for asking questions or spending.

Controlling another person's money, documents or decisions can be **{{financial abuse|When someone controls your money, your documents or your financial decisions to dominate you.}}**.

--- paso | fa-shield | Safety comes first
A visible change, like changing passwords, putting documents away or opening an account, can increase the risk in some relationships.

Look for confidential guidance from a safe device and at a safe time.

> **Key idea:** recognizing it doesn't mean you have to tell your private life. To pass this course you don't have to confront anyone or share your story.

--- paso | fa-phone | Where to get support
The **National Domestic Violence Hotline** answers 24 hours a day, at no cost, confidential and in Spanish: **1-800-799-7233**.

If there's immediate danger, call **911**.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Made-up case: a person tells a friend that their partner checks all their spending and gets angry if they buy something without permission.

The friend doesn't judge or tell them what to do. She shares the national hotline number and says: "I'm here whenever you need me."

--- pasos | fa-check-square-o | Do it this week
1. Save the national hotline number in a safe place.
2. Think of a trusted person you could turn to.
3. Check whether you have some money and documents that are yours alone.

--- comprueba
1. Is a disagreement about spending always abuse? || No. What's worrying is a pattern of control, not a disagreement.
2. To pass, do you have to confront the other person? || No. You can work with made-up cases.

--- recuerda
- Agreeing is healthy; controlling is not.
- Your safety comes before any change.
- There is confidential support in Spanish.

== profundiza

--- tema | fa-users | Older people and financial abuse
Older people or people who depend on care can also suffer financial abuse: relatives who use their money without permission, caregivers who ask to "borrow," or strangers who win their trust.

Helping manage someone's money requires their permission and limits. Having access doesn't give you the right to use it for yourself.

> **Good to know:** in California, each county's Adult Protective Services (APS) takes reports of financial abuse of older or dependent adults, 24 hours a day.

--- tema | fa-lightbulb-o | Safe steps if someone asks you for help
- Listen without judging and without pressuring.
- Don't tell them what to do; give them information.
- Share the national hotline number.
- Don't keep information where the other person can see it.

--- tema | fa-file-text | Documents and money of your own
Having a copy of your documents in a safe place, outside the home, and a little money of your own can give you options.

Do it only if it's safe for you and, preferably, with guidance from a specialized organization.

--- tema | fa-balance-scale | Your rights don't depend on your immigration status
Domestic violence laws protect everyone in the U.S. There are special protections for immigrant survivors.

An attorney or an accredited representative can explain your options confidentially.

--- tema | fa-mobile | Using your phone safely
If someone checks your phone, clear the history after looking for help or use a trusted person's phone.

Many support websites have a "quick exit" button that closes the page immediately.

--- casos
### Case 1. The loan she can't read
Made-up case: a person pressures their partner to sign a loan she can't read and asks for her passwords.
? What signs are there? || Lack of information, pressure and the risk of a debt.
? What response is safe? || Pause, look for independent support and not keep information where the other person can see it.

### Case 2. Alex and Mar's agreement
Alex and Mar go over their budget together every Sunday. Each one has 50 dollars a week for whatever they want, with no explanations.
? Is it control? || No. It's a healthy agreement: they decide together and each one has independence.

### Case 3. Rosa's pension
Made-up case: an older person gives her card to a nephew so he can do her shopping. He starts using it for his own expenses.
? What is it? || Financial abuse. Having access doesn't give the right to use the money.
? What can she do? || Change her PIN, ask someone she trusts for help and report it to Adult Protective Services.

* Making visible changes without a plan | The risk increases | Look for confidential guidance first
* Pressuring someone to leave | They pull away from the person helping | Listen and share information

# M4 U06 | What is setting aside a little money for?
objetivo: Build savings in stages according to their purpose.
gancho: Daniela's refrigerator broke down two days before rent. She had nothing set aside and had to use her card. She paid interest for months. In this lesson you'll learn to build a cushion little by little, even if you start with very little.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-umbrella | The unexpected doesn't wait
A repair, an illness or a week without work don't wait for your next paycheck.

An **{{emergency fund|Money set aside only for surprises, like an illness or losing work hours.}}** is money you can use quickly for the unexpected. It isn't money that already has a job, like rent.

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | Three different kinds of savings
* fa-exchange | Everyday cushion | For when a payment falls before your paycheck. | Small and used often. | igual
* fa-calendar | Planned expenses | Car registration, school supplies, a yearly insurance payment. | Set aside each month. | igual
* fa-medkit | Emergency | What can't be predicted: illness, losing your job. | Used only for that. | si

--- tabla | fa-calculator | How long does your savings cover you?
You have 600 set aside and your basic expenses are 1,500 a month, about 50 a day.

| Calculation | Result |
|---|---|
| 600 ÷ 1,500 | 0.4 months |
| 600 ÷ 50 a day | 12 days |

It isn't little: 12 days can be the difference between paying on time and going into debt.

--- pasos | fa-list-ol | Start in stages
1. **First goal:** a small surprise, for example 300.
2. **Second goal:** one week of basic expenses.
3. **Third goal:** one month of basic expenses.

If you have no margin today, the first step may be to stabilize your expenses and look for support.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Luis started setting aside 10 dollars every Friday in a separate account. He didn't feel it. In six months he had 260.

When his phone broke, he paid for it with his savings, with no card and no loan.

> **Key idea:** using your savings isn't failing. That's what it's for.

--- paso | fa-question-circle | Is it an emergency or not?
Before using your savings, ask yourself:

1. Is it necessary?
2. Is it urgent?
3. Was it unexpected?

If all three answers are yes, it's an emergency. If not, it's probably a planned expense or a want that can wait.

--- pasos | fa-check-square-o | Do it this week
1. Open a separate savings account or a digital envelope for your savings.
2. Set up an automatic transfer, even a small one.
3. Write down your first goal and the date you want to reach it.

--- comprueba
1. You have 600 in savings and spend 1,500 a month. How long does it cover you? || About 12 days, or 0.4 months.
2. Is your yearly car registration an emergency? || No. It's a planned expense: you set aside for it each month.

--- recuerda
- Keep emergencies apart from planned expenses.
- Start with a small goal.
- If you use it, write down how you'll refill it.

== profundiza

--- tema | fa-university | Where to keep it
Your savings should be **accessible, safe and not at risk of losing value**.

An insured savings account, separate from your everyday account, is a good option. An investment that can go down or a certificate with a penalty for early withdrawal isn't a good place for emergency money.

--- tema | fa-random | Don't count the same money twice
All three kinds of savings can be in the same account, but write down how much is for each one.

If you use the car registration money for an emergency, refill the registration money first.

--- tema | fa-signal | If part of your income keeps coming in
If you lose hours but still get 900 of your 1,500 in expenses, your savings only needs to cover the difference: 600 a month.

That way, the same savings lasts longer.

--- tema | fa-refresh | How to refill it
After using it, write down how much you used and what for. Decide how much you'll set aside each week to refill it and how long it will take.

> **Good to know:** in California, CalSavers is a state retirement savings program; some people also use it to save little by little, although its purpose is retirement. For emergencies, the best option is a savings account you can use with no penalty.

--- tema | fa-lightbulb-o | Ideas for setting money aside without feeling it
- An automatic transfer on payday.
- Saving your "change": rounding up each purchase and keeping the difference.
- Keeping part of your tax refund or the extra paycheck in three-paycheck months.

--- tema | fa-balance-scale | Savings or paying off debt
If you have high-interest debts, it may seem better to use everything to pay them off.

But without minimum savings, any surprise sends you back into debt. Many people start with small savings, for example 300 or 500, and then focus on the debts.

--- casos
### Case 1. Alex and Mar's savings
They have 900 and basic expenses of 1,800.
? How long does it cover them? || Half a month.
? And if their expenses go up to 2,000? || The same savings covers 0.45 months. It's worth reviewing when expenses go up.

### Case 2. The car registration
Mar thought the car registration, 600 a year, was an emergency.
? What was it? || A planned expense. With 50 a month for 12 months, she covers it.

### Case 3. Daniela's refrigerator
Daniela paid for the refrigerator with her card and still owes 400.
? What does she do now? || She pays down the card and, at the same time, sets aside 10 a week toward her first goal of 300.

--- errores
* Waiting to have a lot before starting | You never start | Start with 5 or 10 a week
* Keeping your savings in your everyday account | It gets spent without you noticing | Use a separate account
* Investing emergency money | It can drop when you need it | Keep it in insured savings
* Feeling guilty for using it | You go into debt to avoid touching it | Use it and refill it with a plan
