# M3 U06 | Which debt do I start with?
objetivo: Make a list of your debts and choose a strategy to pay them off.
gancho: Mar has a credit card, a loan from a relative, a medical bill and a "pay later" plan. Each month she pays whatever pushes hardest and feels she never gets ahead. In this lesson you'll learn to see all your debts together and decide where to start.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-list | First, see them all together
When debts are spread out, it's easy to forget one or to pay first the one that calls the most.

A **{{debt list|A list of all your debts: who you owe, how much, how much you pay each month and when.}}** is a list of all your debts. It helps you not forget payments and decide with information.

--- pasos | fa-list-ol | Three steps
1. **Write down every debt**, including medical, tax, family and remittance debts.
2. **Add up the minimum payments.**
3. **Compare** with what you can pay. Only if there's money left do you decide where to put extra.

--- ecuacion | fa-calculator | Is there enough for the minimums?
You have 120 a month for debts. Your minimum payments add up to 80.
= 120 | What you can pay
- 80 | Minimum payments
= 40 | Extra money to pay down
If the result were negative, no extra-payment strategy would be enough: you need to negotiate or look for help (M3 U07).

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | Two strategies for the extra money
* fa-fire | Avalanche | The extra money goes to the debt with the **highest rate**. | It saves the most interest. | si
* fa-dot-circle-o | Snowball | The extra money goes to the debt with the **smallest balance**. | You close debts faster and it keeps you motivated. | si

--- paso | fa-exclamation-circle | First, what puts the basics at risk
Sometimes the rate isn't what matters most. First take care of a debt that puts your housing, the car you use for work or a basic utility at risk, or that is already in court.

> **Key idea:** the best strategy is the one you can keep up. Both work if you pay all the minimums on time.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Luis owed 400 on his card at 28% and 150 to a friend, with no interest. He had 100 a month to pay.

He paid the 30 minimum on the card, 20 to his friend as agreed, and the extra 50 to the card. In seven months he paid off the card and kept going with his friend.

--- pasos | fa-check-square-o | Do it this week
1. Make your list of debts with balance, rate and minimum payment.
2. Add up the minimums and compare them with what you can pay.
3. Choose avalanche or snowball for the extra money.

--- comprueba
1. Which strategy saves the most interest? || The avalanche, because it goes after the highest rate first.
2. Should you always pay the highest rate first? || No. First check the debts that put your housing, your job or your utilities at risk.

--- recuerda
- Make a list of all your debts.
- Pay all the minimums on time.
- The extra money goes to one debt at a time.

== profundiza

| Item | Why it matters |

| Creditor | Who you pay |

| Strategy | Order | Months | Total interest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Avalanche | A, B, loan | 18 | 715 |

* fa-compress | Consolidate | Combine several debts into one. | Check the total cost. | igual

--- tema | fa-medkit | Medical debts
Medical debts have special rules. Many small or paid medical bills no longer show up on credit reports.

Before paying a large medical bill, check whether you qualify for the hospital's financial assistance. You'll see it in M4 U08.

> **Good to know:** many nonprofit hospitals must offer discounts or forgive bills for people with low incomes. In California, ask about the hospital's fair pricing program.

--- tema | fa-users | Debts with family and friends
Debts with people close to you have no interest and no reporting, but they matter: they affect the relationship.

Include them in your list with the amount and the date you agreed on. If you can't pay on time, let them know beforehand and suggest a new date.

--- casos
### Case 1. Mar's debts
Debt A: 300 at 30%, minimum 30. Debt B: 1,000 at 15%, minimum 50. She has 120 for debts.
? How much is left after the minimums? || 40, because 120 − 80 = 40.
? Which one does she pay down? || A: it has the highest rate and the smallest balance. Both strategies agree.

### Case 2. Andrés's consolidation
He's offered a lower payment, from 250 to 170, but from 12 to 24 payments.
? How much does each option cost? || 250 × 12 = 3,000 versus 170 × 24 = 4,080.
? Is it good for him? || It eases his monthly cash flow, but costs 1,080 more. Only if he really can't make the current payment.

### Case 3. Daniela's list
Daniela made her list and found a 380 medical bill she had forgotten.
? What does she do first? || She asks the hospital whether she qualifies for financial assistance before paying.

--- errores
* Paying first whatever pushes hardest | You forget important debts | Make your complete list
* Skipping minimums to pay down one debt | Late payments and fees | Pay all the minimums first
* Choosing a consolidation just for the payment | You pay more in total | Compare the total
* Forgetting family and medical debts | Your plan isn't real | Include them in the list

# M3 U07 | What do I do if I know I won't be able to pay?
objetivo: Prepare a documented response when you can't pay, and know what to do about debt collection or a lawsuit.
gancho: Andrés lost hours at work and knows his next card payment won't be covered. He's afraid to answer the bank's calls. In this lesson you'll learn to act before you fall behind and to defend yourself if you're being collected on.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-phone | Don't wait until you're behind
Ignoring the due date doesn't make the problem go away. On the contrary: fees, interest and calls arrive.

If you know you won't be able to pay, talk to the creditor **before** the due date. Many companies have temporary plans, but they only offer them if you ask.

--- pasos | fa-list-ol | Three steps
1. **Calculate** what amount and what date are actually possible for you.
2. **Contact** the creditor through a verified channel and ask about your options.
3. **Keep in writing** what you agreed on.

--- paso | fa-handshake-o | Negotiating isn't begging
Asking for a change is **negotiating**. It's normal and it's your right to ask.

But an agreement you asked for isn't accepted until they confirm it, preferably in writing.

--- tarjetas | fa-shield | If a debt collector calls you
* fa-search | Verify | Ask who they are, who they're collecting for and what debt it is. | Before giving information or paying. | si
* fa-file-text | Ask for validation | They must give you information about the debt in writing. | You usually have 30 days to dispute it. | si
* fa-ban | They can't | Harass you, threaten you, lie to you or say they'll report you to immigration. | Report it to the CFPB. | no

--- paso | fa-gavel | If you get court papers
A dispute with a credit agency does **not** stop a lawsuit.

If you get documents from a court, check the deadline to respond and get legal help right away. If you don't respond, a judgment can be entered against you and part of your wages can be taken.

> **Key idea:** court papers are never something to leave for later.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Andrés called his bank a week before the due date. He explained his hours had been cut and asked whether there was any option.

They offered him a three-month temporary plan with a lower payment. He asked them to send it by mail before accepting.

--- comprueba
1. Does a dispute with a credit agency stop a lawsuit? || No. If you're sued, you have to respond to the court by its deadline.
2. A collector says they'll report you to immigration if you don't pay today. Is that legal? || No. It's a prohibited threat; you can report it to the CFPB.

--- recuerda
- Talk to the creditor before you fall behind.
- Verify any collector before paying.
- If court papers arrive, get legal help now.

== profundiza

--- tema | fa-clipboard | Before you call, prepare
- Your current income.
- How much you can pay without giving up your needs, and for how long.
- Your documents: statements, letters, receipts.

Ask about the options, the fees, the effect on your report and how the agreement ends. Ask for the confirmation in writing.

--- tema | fa-clock-o | The time limit to sue you
In California, the time a creditor has to sue you over a credit card debt or a written contract is usually **4 years**. It's called the {{statute of limitations|The maximum time a creditor has to sue you in court over a debt.}}.

If a collector contacts you about a very old debt, don't make payments or promises to pay without first checking the date of the debt and getting guidance. The rules about what happens if you pay an old debt are complex. [POR CONFIRMAR]

> **Good to know:** in California, debt collectors must be licensed by the DFPI, and there are special rules for debts sold to other companies. If a collector isn't licensed, report it.

--- tabla | fa-life-ring | Relief services
| Service | What it does | Keep in mind |
|---|---|---|
| Nonprofit credit counseling | Reviews your budget and options | Ask about costs and credentials |
| Debt management plan | The agency negotiates rates; you make one monthly payment | It may close your cards |
| Debt settlement | They negotiate paying less | Risk to your history and taxes; beware of upfront fees |
| Bankruptcy | A legal process | Requires an attorney |

--- tema | fa-balance-scale | Where to get legal help
The State Bar of California has a list of free or low-cost legal aid services and certified lawyer referral services.

Many California courts have free self-help centers that explain how to respond to a lawsuit.

--- tema | fa-file-text | What the written agreement should say
- The amount of each payment and the dates.
- How long it lasts.
- Whether interest or fees are charged during the plan.
- What happens at the end.
- How it will be reported to the credit agencies.

Keep a copy and write down each payment you make.

--- tema | fa-shield | Your rights with debt collectors
Debt collectors can't call you before 8 in the morning or after 9 at night, or at work if you tell them you can't take calls there.

You can ask them in writing to stop contacting you. That doesn't erase the debt, but it stops the calls.

--- casos
### Case 1. Andrés's temporary agreement
Andrés can pay 80 a month for three months, instead of 150.
? What does he ask the bank? || "Is there a temporary option, how much will it cost, how will it affect my report and how will it be put in writing?"

### Case 2. The lawsuit
Mar is disputing a debt with a credit agency and gets a court notice.
? Does she wait for the dispute to finish? || No. She checks the court's deadline and gets legal help right away.

### Case 3. Luis's old debt
A collector calls Luis about a phone debt from five years ago, from when he lived in another state.
? What does he do? || He doesn't promise payments over the phone. He asks for the information in writing, checks the date and gets guidance before deciding.

--- errores
* Not answering the creditor out of fear | The debt grows with fees | Call before the due date
* Paying a collector without verifying | It may be a scam | Ask for validation in writing
* Ignoring court papers | Judgment and wage garnishment | Respond by the deadline with legal help
* Promising payments on a very old debt | You can make your situation more complicated | Check the date and get guidance

# M3 U08 | Can signing to help someone leave me with a debt?
objetivo: Weigh the risks of signing, lending or taking on debt for your family, and help without putting your stability at risk.
gancho: Mar's cousin asks her for a favor: "Just sign, I'll pay it." Mar loves him and trusts him. But that paper can turn her cousin's debt into her own. In this lesson you'll learn what to check before signing and other ways to help.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-pencil-square-o | Signing for someone else means committing to pay
When you sign as a **{{cosigner|A person who signs someone else's loan and promises to pay it if that person doesn't.}}**, you promise to pay if the other person doesn't.

For the {{lender|The bank, credit union, store or person lending the money.}}, that debt is yours too. They can collect from you, even if you don't use the car, the card or the money.

> **Key idea:** a family promise doesn't change the contract. If the contract says you're responsible, you're responsible.

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | Three ways of helping that can leave you with a debt
* fa-pencil | Cosigning a loan | You sign together with the other person. | You pay it all if they don't, and late payments damage your {{credit report|A history of how you pay your debts. Banks review it to decide whether to lend to you and at what cost.}}. | no
* fa-credit-card | Lending your credit card | The other person buys with your card. | Everything they spend is in your name. | no
* fa-key | Sharing your password | The other person gets into your bank or app. | They can move your money, and the bank may not reimburse you. | no

--- ecuacion | fa-calculator | Do the math before you sign
You earn 2,400 a month. After your expenses you have 150 free. Your cousin asks you to cosign a car with a 180 monthly payment.
= 150 | What you have free
- 180 | The payment if your cousin stops paying
= −30 | What you'd be short each month
And that's without counting late fees. In this case, signing puts your rent at risk.

--- pasos | fa-list-ol | Before you sign, follow three steps
1. **Read what responsibility you're taking on.** Ask for the contract and the {{cosigner notice|A form the lender must give you before you sign, explaining that you will have to pay if the other person doesn't.}}.
2. **Calculate what would happen if the other person doesn't pay.** Could you pay the full payment each month, on top of your expenses?
3. **Decide with that information.** If you couldn't pay it, don't sign, even if someone you love asks you.

--- paso | fa-question-circle | Five questions before you sign
1. What is the monthly payment and for how many months?
2. If the other person stops paying, can I pay it all myself?
3. Will they give me the cosigner notice and a copy of the contract?
4. How will I find out if there's a late payment?
5. Is there another way to help that doesn't leave me with a debt?

--- paso | fa-heart | Another way to help
You can offer an amount you can give without needing it back.

You can also help with information: go with your relative to compare options or look for a credit union.

Saying "no" or offering another kind of help is also caring for your family.

> **Before you act, check:** if someone threatens you or forces you to sign, put your safety first. You'll see it in M4 U05.

--- comprueba
1. Your brother promises he'll pay the loan you cosign. Does that protect you? || No. If the contract says you're responsible, the lender can collect from you.
2. You have 150 free a month and are asked to cosign a 180 payment. What does the math tell you? || That if the other person doesn't pay, you'd be 30 short each month. Signing puts your basic expenses at risk.

--- recuerda
- Signing, lending your card and sharing your password can leave you with a debt.
- Do the math before you sign, not after.
- Helping with an amount you could give away is safer than signing.

== profundiza

--- tema | fa-file-text-o | What being a cosigner means
The lender agrees to make the loan because you're responsible too. This means that:

- **They can collect from you** if the other person falls behind, sometimes without trying to collect from them first.
- **Late payments can show up on your credit report**, even if you don't use what was bought.
- **The debt counts as yours** when you apply for your own loan, and it can lower what you're lent.
- **Getting out of the contract isn't easy.** It usually ends only when the debt is paid off or {{refinanced|The loan is replaced with a new one, with other terms or in another person's name.}} without you.

Federal law requires many lenders to give you a cosigner notice before you sign. The FTC explains your risks in [Cosigning a Loan FAQs](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/cosigning-loan-faqs).

> **Good to know:** in California, the law requires the cosigner notice to be given in English and Spanish. If you didn't get it, ask for it before you sign. [POR CONFIRMAR]

--- tabla | fa-balance-scale | Ways to help: risks and alternatives
| Way to help | Who owes | Risk to you | Safer alternative |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cosigning a loan | You and the other person | Paying the whole balance and damage to your credit | Help with an amount you don't need back |
| Lending your credit card | You | Everything spent is in your name | Pay directly for one specific purchase yourself |
| Adding someone as an {{authorized user|A person who can use your credit card. You pay for what they spend.}} | You | Their spending adds to your debt | Set a spending limit or don't add them |
| Lending cash | The other person owes you | That they don't pay you back and you come up short on your expenses | Lend only what you could give away |

--- tema | fa-handshake-o | If you decide to lend money to a relative
Agree on these points in writing, even if it's just a message:

1. The exact **amount**.
2. **Schedule:** how much and when it's paid back.
3. **What happens if a payment is late:** is the date changed or paused?
4. **How each payment is recorded:** a shared note or receipts.

> **Key idea:** lend only what you could give away without falling behind on your rent, food and debts. That way, if you're not paid back, the relationship suffers, but not your stability.

--- tema | fa-paper-plane | A remittance you pay for later
Some services let you send today and pay later. If you send 200 today and have to pay 230 in a month, write two events on your calendar:

- today 200 goes out;
- in a month 230 goes out.

If your family promises to pay you back 100, don't count that money until there's a confirmed date and ability to pay.

Ask yourself: if I'm not paid back, which bill goes unpaid?

--- tema | fa-lock | Passwords and access to your accounts
Never share your {{login information|Your username and password to get into your bank or app.}}, not even with your family.

Anyone who logs in with your information can move your money, take out loans in your name and change your contact information.

Also, the bank may refuse to reimburse you because you "authorized" the access.

If someone needs to help you review a debt, look at it together on your screen.

--- tema | fa-shield | Pressure and control
Asking for help is normal. There's pressure or control when someone:

- threatens you if you don't sign;
- takes your documents;
- controls your income;
- forces you to take on debt.

In those cases, put your safety first (M4 U05). Saying no or negotiating a different amount is part of planning, not a lack of love.

--- tema | fa-child | Never use your children's information
Sometimes, out of need, some families open electric, phone or credit accounts using their children's name or number.

That can damage the child's credit history before they turn 18, and will cause them problems when they want to rent or study.

If you find out someone opened accounts with a minor's information, you can **freeze their credit for free** at all three agencies and report it at IdentityTheft.gov.

--- casos
### Case 1. Mar's cousin
Mar cosigns a car loan of 180 a month because "my cousin will pay it." Four months later, he loses his job.
? What happens now? || The lender collects from Mar, and the late payments show up on her credit report.
? What should she have done beforehand? || Include those 180 in her budget as a risk scenario. If they didn't fit, the answer was not to sign.
? What can she do today? || Talk to the lender before falling behind (M3 U07) and agree on a plan with her cousin in writing. If she has questions about her rights, she can check with the [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov).

### Case 2. The password
A relative asks Alex for the bank password to "manage" a debt.
? What does Alex suggest? || Reviewing the information together on Alex's own phone, without sharing the password.

### Case 3. The loan to her sister
Daniela lends her sister 500. They agree by message that her sister will pay 100 a month for 5 months. In the third month, her sister can only pay 50.
? Why doesn't Daniela fall behind on her rent? || Because she only lent what she could give away.
? What do they do? || They adjust the schedule in writing: 50 this month and the rest the next month.

--- errores
* Signing because "it's just a formality" | You're obligated to pay the whole debt | Read the cosigner notice and calculate whether you could pay
* Lending without dates or amounts | Misunderstandings and money that doesn't come back | Agree on everything in writing, even in a message
* Counting on money you were promised back | You come up short on your own bills | Count it only when you receive it
* Sharing passwords "out of trust" | You lose control of your account and the bank's protection | Review together, without sharing login information
* Opening services or credit in your children's name or number | You damage their history before they turn 18 | Never use their information; if it already happened, freeze their credit

# M3 U09 | Does a tanda help me build credit?
objetivo: Compare the traditional tanda with lending circles that report to the credit agencies, and use both with less risk.
gancho: Mar has been in tandas with her friends for years. She always keeps her word and has never missed a payment. When she applied for a card, she was told she had no credit history. All those years of on-time payments didn't show up anywhere. In this lesson you'll learn how to make your tanda count too.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-users | What a tanda is
In a **{{tanda|A group of people who each put in the same amount every week or month; taking turns, each one receives the total.}}**, each person puts in a fixed amount and, taking turns, each one receives the total.

It's a way of saving and lending among people who trust each other, very common in our communities.

--- ecuacion | fa-calculator | How it works with numbers
10 people put in 100 every week. Each week one of them receives the total.
= 100 | What each person puts in
+ 900 | What the other 9 put in
= 1,000 | What one person receives each week
Whoever receives first gets a loan with no interest. Whoever receives last is saving.

--- paso | fa-times-circle | A traditional tanda doesn't build credit
Even if you pay on time for years, a traditional tanda **doesn't show up on your credit report**. Nobody reports your payments to the agencies.

--- paso | fa-check-circle | A lending circle can
A **lending circle** works like a tanda, but it's organized by a nonprofit with a contract. Many **report your payments** to the credit agencies.

That way, what you already do in a tanda also helps you build a history, without paying interest.

--- pasos | fa-list-ol | Before joining a tanda
1. **Write down** how much you put in, how often and which turn you receive.
2. **Add up** how much you'll put in in total.
3. **Ask yourself** what happens if someone stops paying.

> **Key idea:** don't put money you need for the basics into a tanda.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Rosa organizes a tanda with her neighbors in Fresno: eight people, 50 a week. To avoid problems, she writes everything down in a notebook, and each person signs when they pay in and when they receive.

The tanda also has a rule: whoever has been in the group the longest receives first.

--- comprueba
1. Does a traditional tanda build credit history? || No. Nobody reports your payments to the agencies.
2. What does whoever receives first in a tanda get? || A loan with no interest: they receive it all and pay later.

--- recuerda
- A traditional tanda is saving and lending, but it doesn't build a history.
- An organized lending circle can report your payments.
- Write down turns and payments.

--- tema | fa-shield | To lower the risks of a tanda

- Write down the names, turns, dates and payments, and share it with everyone.
- Ask for an early turn if you know you'll need the money.
- Keep a small separate cushion in case someone fails.

> **Good to know:** Mission Asset Fund, a California organization, runs no-interest lending circles that report to the credit agencies. Their on-time payments have helped thousands of people build credit.

--- tema | fa-lightbulb-o | Using both together
You don't have to leave your tanda. You can keep your trusted group and, at the same time, join a lending circle that reports.

That way you keep the tradition that already works and also build a history.

--- tema | fa-calculator | The tanda and saving
If you get the last turn, the tanda works as saving with no interest. If you get the first, as a loan with no interest.

If you only want to save, an insured savings account pays you some interest and doesn't depend on others paying.

--- errores
* Believing the tanda builds credit | Years of payments that don't count | Look for a circle that reports
* Joining without writing anything down | Conflicts and losses | Write down turns and payments
* Putting in your rent money | You're left without the basics | Put in only what you can save
* Trusting an organizer you don't know | They may disappear with the money | Take part with people you trust

# M3 U10 | What's my next step with credit?
objetivo: Choose your credit path and build a 30- to 90-day plan.
gancho: Luis has no history. Andrés has an error in his report. Mar has a card with a high balance. Daniela has an account in collections. All four want to "improve their credit," but each one needs something different. In this lesson you'll choose your own path.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-map-signs | Each situation has its own path
Having no history, having an error and having late payments are different situations. Each one needs a different action.

Opening a card isn't the answer for everyone.

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | Choose your path
* fa-leaf | No history | A product that reports to all three agencies, without locking up your savings. | M3 U03 and U09. | si
* fa-wrench | Error in your report | A specific dispute with proof. | M3 U02. | igual
* fa-credit-card | High balances | Lower your utilization and organize your payments. | M3 U04 and U06. | igual
* fa-exclamation-triangle | Late payment or collections | Verify the debt and negotiate or get counseling. | M3 U07. | no
* fa-check | Stable situation | Pay on time and check your report every year. | Keep the routine. | si

--- pasos | fa-list-ol | Three steps
1. **Describe your situation** without judging yourself.
2. **Choose the action** that fits.
3. **Give it a date** and a way to prove you did it.

--- paso | fa-check-square | Measure actions, not points
"I'm going to raise my score 80 points" doesn't depend only on you. "I checked my report and filed a dispute with a confirmation number" does.

Checking your report is a completed action even if your score doesn't change right away.

> **Key idea:** asking for help in time is a skill, not a failure.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Luis has no history. In week 1 he asks two credit unions about secured cards with an ITIN. In week 3 he compares costs. On day 30 he chooses a card that reports to all three agencies and asks his landlord to report his rent.

At 90 days he checks his report: his first payments already show up.

--- pasos | fa-check-square-o | Do it this week
1. Write your credit situation in one sentence.
2. Choose your path in the table.
3. Write down your first action with a date and how you'll prove it.

--- comprueba
1. Which improvement can you prove: "I'll raise my score 80 points" or "I checked my report and filed a dispute with a confirmation number"? || The second: it's your own action with evidence.
2. Is opening a card always the first step? || No. It depends on your situation.

--- recuerda
- Identify your situation before acting.
- Measure your actions, not only your score.
- Review your plan at 30 and 90 days.

Once you have a history, take care of it:

- set up automatic minimum payments and pay the rest manually;
- check your free reports every few months;
- freeze your credit if you don't plan to borrow soon (M4 U04).

> **Good to know:** negative information doesn't stay on your report forever. Meanwhile, every on-time payment works in your favor.

* Measuring progress only by your score | You get discouraged | Measure completed actions
* Paying and still buying with the card | The debt barely goes down | Stop using it while you pay
* Not reviewing the plan | You lose your way | Review at 30 and 90 days
