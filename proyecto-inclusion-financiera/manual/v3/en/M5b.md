# M5 U07 | What happens to what I saved in Mexico?
objetivo: Check your AFORE from the U.S., know your options to keep contributing and verify before you decide.
gancho: Rosa worked 15 years in Guadalajara with IMSS. She's spent ten in Fresno and never checked her AFORE. She doesn't know how much she has or whether she can keep contributing from here. In this lesson you'll learn to find those savings and take care of them.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-search | Your AFORE is still yours
If you worked in Mexico with IMSS or ISSSTE, you have an **{{AFORE|Administradora de Fondos para el Retiro: the company that holds your Mexican retirement savings.}}** with your retirement savings. That money isn't lost because you live in another country.

To find it you need your **{{CURP|Clave Única de Registro de Población: your 18-character ID in Mexico.}}**.

--- pasos | fa-list-ol | Three ways to check your AFORE
1. **AforeMóvil app:** download it, sign up with your CURP and your email, and verify your identity with a photo. There you see your balance, your AFORE and your transactions.
2. **e-SAR:** on the SAR website you can find which AFORE you're in with your CURP.
3. **SARTEL:** call the CONSAR phone line, which also takes calls from the U.S.

--- paso | fa-plus-circle | You can keep contributing
From AforeMóvil you can make **{{voluntary contributions|Extra money you decide to put in your AFORE, on top of what your employer used to put in.}}**, even small amounts.

That money grows with your account and helps you reach your retirement in Mexico.

--- paso | fa-exclamation-triangle | Don't confuse one thing with another
Contributing voluntarily to your AFORE isn't the same as continuing to add IMSS contribution weeks.

Contribution weeks are what count toward the right to a pension. There are other options for that (see Profundiza).

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Rosa downloaded AforeMóvil, signed up with her CURP and found her account: she had more than she imagined and 780 contribution weeks.

She updated her beneficiaries and set aside a small voluntary contribution every month.

> **Key idea:** update your beneficiaries in your AFORE. If you don't, your family may have to go through a long process to receive that money.

--- tarjetas | fa-list | What you can do from AforeMóvil
* fa-eye | See your balance | How much you have and in which AFORE. | Check every year. | si
* fa-users | Update beneficiaries | Who receives your savings. | Do it today. | si
* fa-plus-circle | Contribute | Voluntary contributions by card or at stores. | Small amounts. | si
* fa-exchange | Switch AFORE | If another one suits you better. | Compare fees and returns. | igual

It's all free. Nobody should charge you to use the app.

> **Before you act, check:** that you're using CONSAR's official app and not a copy. Never pay to sign up.

--- comprueba
1. What do you need to find your AFORE? || Your CURP.
2. Does a voluntary contribution add IMSS contribution weeks? || No. It increases your savings, but doesn't add weeks.

--- recuerda
- Look up your AFORE with your CURP.
- Update your beneficiaries.
- You can contribute from the U.S. with the app.

== profundiza

--- tabla | fa-table | IMSS options for people living abroad [POR CONFIRMAR]
| Option | What it's for | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Modalidad 40 (voluntary continuation) | Keep adding weeks toward the pension | Requirements, prior weeks and cost with IMSS |

These options have requirements and costs that change. Confirm them directly with IMSS before deciding.

--- tema | fa-globe | U.S. and Mexican Social Security
The U.S. has social security agreements with several countries to add up periods worked in both. With Mexico, according to the SSA, **that agreement is not in effect**.

That's why your weeks in Mexico and your credits in the U.S. are reviewed separately.

--- tema | fa-shield | Watch out for middlemen
Some people offer to "recover your AFORE" or "advance your pension" in exchange for a percentage or your personal information.

Procedures with your AFORE, CONSAR and IMSS are free. Don't share your CURP, your NSS or your passwords with middlemen.

--- tema | fa-users | Unemployment withdrawals
Some accounts allow a partial withdrawal for unemployment. Doing it reduces your contribution weeks.

Before withdrawing, ask how many weeks you'd lose and whether it's worth it.

> **Good to know:** according to CONSAR, millions of AFORE accounts don't have updated information. If you don't know which one you're in, your account may be at the Banco de México as an "unassigned account," and it can be recovered.

--- tema | fa-bar-chart | Compare your AFORE
AFOREs charge different fees and have different returns. CONSAR publishes a comparison every year.

If your AFORE has low returns, you can switch, generally once a year. Switching is free and is done from AforeMóvil or at the new AFORE.

Don't switch because of a gift or an agent's promise. Compare with CONSAR's data.

--- casos
### Case 1. Rosa finds her AFORE
Rosa doesn't know which AFORE she's in.
? What does she do? || She uses e-SAR or AforeMóvil with her CURP.

### Case 2. Luis's middleman
A middleman offers Luis to "release his AFORE" for 30% of the balance.
? What does Luis do? || He doesn't accept. The procedures are free and he does them himself with his AFORE or CONSAR.

### Case 3. Weeks or savings
Rosa wants to increase her savings and also complete weeks for her pension.
? Are voluntary contributions enough? || No. Contributions increase her savings; to add weeks she must ask IMSS about Modalidad 40.

--- errores
* Not checking your AFORE | You don't know what you have | Look it up with your CURP
* Not updating beneficiaries | A long process for your family | Update them
* Paying a middleman | You lose money or your data | Do it yourself, it's free
* Believing contributing adds weeks | You plan your pension wrong | Ask IMSS

# M5 U08 | What happens to my family if something happens to me?
objetivo: Prepare the basics to protect your family: beneficiaries, documents and a plan for your children.
gancho: Luis has two children in Stockton. One night he wonders: if something happens to me, who takes care of them? Who knows where my money is? He has nothing in writing. In this lesson you'll prepare the basics, step by step, without fear.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-heart | Planning isn't thinking the worst
Preparing documents is an act of care. It spares your family confusion, expenses and long procedures at a hard time.

You don't need a lot of money to do it.

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | The basics
* fa-users | Beneficiaries | Who receives your account, your insurance or your AFORE. | Updated at each institution. | si
* fa-child | A plan for your children | Who takes care of them if you can't. | A caregiver form in California. | si
* fa-file-text | Power of attorney | Who you authorize to handle your affairs. | Only someone you fully trust. | igual
* fa-folder-open | Family folder | Where your documents and contacts are. | M1 U14. | si

--- paso | fa-child | A plan for your children
In California, the **{{Caregiver's Authorization Affidavit|A California form that lets a trusted adult enroll your children in school and authorize medical care.}}** lets a trusted adult enroll your children in school and authorize medical care.

It doesn't take away your custody. You can cancel it whenever you want.

--- paso | fa-list | Beneficiaries on every account
Every bank account, life insurance policy, retirement plan and AFORE has its own beneficiary form.

Your will doesn't change what that form says. Check them all.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Luis talked with his sister, who agrees to take care of his children. He filled out the caregiver form and gave her a copy.

He updated the beneficiaries on his account and his insurance, and told his sister where his family folder is.

> **Key idea:** if you're worried about your immigration situation, an attorney or a DOJ-accredited representative can guide you on a family preparedness plan.

--- tema | fa-comments | Talk with your family
Having documents isn't enough if nobody knows they exist. Sit down with the person who would take care of your children and explain where your papers are, what accounts you have and who to call.

If your children are older, explain the plan in simple words. Knowing there's a plan gives them peace of mind.

Put the phone numbers of your consulate, your children's school and their doctor in your folder.

> **Before you act, check:** that the form is the official one from the California courts and that the person who will care for your children agrees to do it.

--- comprueba
1. Does your will change the beneficiaries of your insurance? || No. They're updated on each institution's form.
2. Does the caregiver form take away your custody? || No. You can cancel it whenever you want.

--- recuerda
- Update beneficiaries on every account.
- Make a plan for your children.
- Tell someone you trust where your documents are.

You don't need to pay a lot to prepare basic documents. Look for:

- Free legal clinics from community organizations.
- California court self-help centers.
- Attorneys or DOJ-accredited representatives for immigration matters.

Be wary of "notarios" or "consultants" who offer immigration services. In the U.S., a notary public isn't a lawyer and can't give you legal advice.

# M5 U09 | How do I plan when my income changes every month?
objetivo: Make a base budget and a plan for good and bad months when your income varies.
gancho: Andrés works in construction. Some months have lots of work and rainy months have almost none. When there's money he overspends, and when there isn't he goes into debt. In this lesson you'll make a plan that works even when your income changes.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-signal | Plan with your low month
If your income changes, don't build your plan on a good month. Build it on a realistic low month.

Look at your last six months and take one of the lowest, not counting an unusual month.

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | Your base budget
* fa-home | Essential expenses | Rent, food, transportation, utilities. | They come first. | si
* fa-credit-card | Minimum payments | Debts and commitments. | Avoid fees. | si
* fa-shield | Reserve | A part for low months. | It gives you calm. | si
* fa-star | Everything else | Goals and treats. | When there's extra. | igual

--- ecuacion | fa-calculator | What to do in a good month
Your base budget is 2,200 and this month you earned 3,000.
= 3,000 | What came in
- 2,200 | Your base budget
= 800 | The extra: don't spend it all
For example, 500 to your reserve and 300 to a goal.

--- paso | fa-arrows-h | Smooth out your income
Some people "pay themselves a salary": everything that comes in goes to one account, and each week they move the same amount to their spending account.

That way the good months cover the bad ones.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Andrés looked at six months: his low month was 2,100. He built his budget on that.

In June he earned 3,400 and set aside 1,000. In December, with the rains, he used that reserve instead of his card.

> **Key idea:** in the good months, prepare for the bad months.

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | Three accounts for variable income
* fa-inbox | Income account | Everything you earn lands here. | You don't spend from here. | si
* fa-shopping-cart | Spending account | Each week you move your fixed "salary." | You pay your bills from here. | si
* fa-shield | Reserve account | The extra from good months. | For low months. | si

If you don't want three accounts, start with two: one for spending and one for the reserve. What matters is separating.

--- pasos | fa-list-ol | Your plan in four steps
1. **Review** your income for the last six months.
2. **Choose** a realistic low month.
3. **Make** your base budget with that amount.
4. **Decide** what percentage of the extra you'll set aside, for example half.

Review your plan at the end of each season and adjust the percentage if your reserve is already full.

--- comprueba
1. What month do you build your plan on if your income changes? || A realistic low month.
2. What do you do with the extra from a good month? || Set part of it aside for low months.

--- recuerda
- Plan with a realistic low month.
- Set aside the extra from good months.
- Pay yourself a fixed salary if you can.

== profundiza

--- tabla | fa-table | Andrés's six months
| Month | Income | Difference from his base (2,100) |
|---|---|---|
| January | 2,100 | 0 |
| February | 2,300 | +200 |

--- tema | fa-money | If you get cash or tips
Write down every day what you receive in cash and through apps. Without a record you can't know which is your low month.

Deposit your cash regularly. It helps you not spend it and gives you an income history (M1 U04).

--- tema | fa-file-text | Taxes if you work for yourself
If nobody withholds taxes for you, set aside part of what you earn to pay them. Many people set aside between 20% and 30%, depending on their situation.

Ask at a VITA center how much you should set aside and whether you should make estimated payments (M4 U04).

--- tema | fa-bell | Warning signs
- You use your card for essential expenses several months in a row.
- You have no reserve when the low season starts.
- Your good month gets spent completely.

If you see these signs, lower your base budget or look for extra income in the low season.

> **Good to know:** according to the Federal Reserve, many people with variable income say the swings make it hard to pay their bills. A plan based on the low month reduces that stress.

--- tema | fa-calendar | Know your season
Many jobs have seasons: construction slows with the rains, farm work changes with the harvests and made-to-order food picks up in December.

Mark your good and bad months from the last year on a calendar. That way you know when to set money aside and how much you'll need.

If your low season lasts three months and your essential expenses are 1,800, your reserve goal for that season is 5,400 minus what you do earn in those months.

--- tema | fa-life-ring | If a very bad month comes
If your reserve isn't enough, call before you fall behind: many utility companies, landlords and banks offer payment plans.

Prioritize housing, food, utilities and transportation to work. Avoid payday loans (M3 U09).

### Case 1. Andrés's rains
Andrés earns little in December and January.
? What does he do starting in June? || He sets aside part of the extra from each good month for those months.

### Case 2. Mar's tips
Mar gets most of her tips in cash and doesn't know how much she earns a month.
? What does she do first? || She writes down every day what she receives for one or two months.

### Case 3. Daniela's big order
Daniela got a big cake order and earned 1,500 extra in one week.
? Does she spend it all? || No. She sets money aside for taxes, her reserve and her next ingredient purchase.

--- errores
* Planning with the good month | You go into debt in the bad one | Use a realistic low month
* Spending all the extra | You have no reserve | Set part of it aside
* Not recording cash | You don't know your income | Write it down every day
* Not setting aside for taxes | Debt with the IRS | Set aside a percentage

# M5 U10 | How do I formalize my business?
objetivo: Separate business and personal money, know the first steps to formalize and compare a sole proprietorship with an LLC.
gancho: Daniela sells custom cakes in San Diego. Business is good, but she mixes order money with household money and doesn't know if she's really making a profit. A customer asked her for an invoice and she didn't know what to do. In this lesson you'll take the first steps to formalize your business.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-exchange | Separate the business money
The first step is to separate business money from household money. If you mix them, you don't know if you're making money.

Open an account just for the business. Many banks and credit unions open one with an ITIN.

--- ecuacion | fa-calculator | How much do you really make?
Daniela sold 1,200 in cakes in one month.
= 1,200 | Sales
- 450 | Ingredients and boxes
- 150 | Gas, fuel and other costs
= 600 | Real profit
If she charges without counting her costs, she thinks she makes twice as much.

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | First steps to formalize
* fa-id-card | ITIN or SSN | To report your income. | M4 U03. | si
* fa-file-text | Local license | Your city or county may require a business license. | Ask your city. | si
* fa-cutlery | Home food permit | If you sell food, a homemade food permit may apply. | Ask county health. | si
* fa-book | Record of sales and expenses | Keep receipts. | It helps with taxes. | si

--- paso | fa-balance-scale | Sole proprietorship or LLC
Many people start as a **{{sole proprietorship|A business that is you: there's no separate entity. It's the simplest to start.}}**: it's the simplest, can be done with an ITIN and you only need your local license and, if you use another name, a business name registration.

An **{{LLC|Limited liability company: a company separate from you that protects your personal property from certain business debts.}}** protects your personal property from certain business debts, but in California it costs at least **800 dollars a year** in minimum tax, even if you don't make money.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Daniela opened an account for her business, got her license and asked about the homemade food permit.

She started as a sole proprietorship. She'll move to an LLC when her profits comfortably cover the 800 a year.

> **Key idea:** start simple and grow when your business can pay for it.

--- paso | fa-tags | Set prices with your numbers
Many people set prices "like everyone else" and without realizing it make very little.

Add up the costs of one order: ingredients, packaging, gas and your time. If a cake costs you 25 in materials and 3 hours, and you want to earn at least 15 an hour, your price should be 70 or more.

Review your prices every time your ingredients go up.

--- comprueba
1. You sell 900, your ingredients cost 350 and other expenses 100. How much do you make? || 450.
2. Does an LLC in California pay tax even if it doesn't make money? || Yes. There's a minimum tax of 800 a year.

--- recuerda
- Separate the business money.
- Subtract your costs to know if you're making money.
- Start as a sole proprietorship if your business is small.

--- tema | fa-file-text | The business name (DBA)
If your business uses a name different from yours, like "Pasteles Dany," register that **{{DBA|"Doing Business As": the business name you operate under and register in your county.}}** in your county.

Some banks require it to open the business account under that name.

--- tema | fa-cutlery | Homemade food
In California there are permits to sell certain foods made at home, like cakes and cookies, and some counties allow small home kitchens with more types of food.

Ask your county health department which permit applies to what you sell.

--- tema | fa-calculator | Business taxes
If you earn 400 or more in the year working for yourself, you generally must file and pay self-employment tax.

Set aside part of each sale and ask for help at a VITA center or from a certified preparer (M4 U04).

> **Good to know:** Small Business Development Centers (SBDC) and many community organizations offer free advice in Spanish to start a business.

--- tema | fa-users | Business loans
Some community institutions (CDFIs) and microloan programs lend to small businesses with an ITIN.

Compare the total cost and avoid cash advances that take a percentage of your daily sales without showing the yearly rate.

--- tema | fa-mobile | Getting paid with apps and cards
If you get paid through payment apps, use a business account. Some apps send you and the IRS a Form 1099-K when you receive payments for goods or services above a certain amount. [POR CONFIRMAR]

That doesn't mean you pay more taxes, but it does mean the IRS knows about that income. That's why you keep your record of sales and expenses.

Keep personal payments, like money from family, separate from customer payments.

--- tema | fa-trophy | Celebrate your progress

--- tema | fa-users | Do it as a family
If you share expenses with your partner or your family, make the plan together. Talking about money without guilt or blame is part of the plan.

Agree on a fixed date, for example the first Sunday of each quarter, to check how you're doing.

You can use a simple rule: each person shares one achievement, one worry and one next step. That keeps the conversation short and useful.

If you have older children, include them in simple goals, like saving for something for the house. They learn by watching.
