# M4 U07 | Does having insurance mean I won't pay anything?
objetivo: Read a policy and compare insurance by what it really covers.
gancho: Alex chose the cheapest car insurance. After a crash during a weekend delivery job, the insurer didn't pay: the policy didn't cover using the car for work. In this lesson you'll learn to read what your insurance covers before you need it.

== esencial

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | Four insurance words
* fa-money | {{Premium|What you pay each month or each year to have the insurance.}} | What you pay to have the insurance. | Monthly or yearly. | igual
* fa-hand-paper-o | {{Deductible|What you pay first, when something happens, before the insurance starts paying.}} | What you pay before the insurance pays. | Lower = higher premium. | igual
* fa-percent | {{Coinsurance|The percentage of the bill you pay after the deductible.}} | The percentage you pay after the deductible. | For example, 20%. | igual
* fa-ban | {{Exclusion|What the insurance doesn't cover, even if you have a policy.}} | What the policy doesn't cover. | Read it before you sign. | no

--- paso | fa-question-circle | Having insurance isn't the same as not paying
You pay a premium to have protection. When something happens, you first pay the deductible. There are also limits and things it doesn't cover.

That's why a cheaper premium doesn't mean it will cost you less when something happens.

--- pasos | fa-list-ol | Three steps before you choose
1. **Identify what you want to protect:** your health, your car, your things, your family.
2. **Read what the policy pays** and what you would pay.
3. **Compare policies with the same coverage.**

--- paso | fa-car | Car insurance in California
Since 2025, the minimum required liability amounts are **30,000** per injured person, **60,000** per accident and **15,000** for property damage.

That insurance pays for what you damage of other people's, it does **not** fix your own car. If you use your car for work, for example for app deliveries, tell your insurer: you may need other coverage.

> **Key idea:** compare the cost over a year with and without an incident, not just the premium.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Andrés compared two car insurance policies with the same coverage. One cost 110 a month with a 500 deductible; the other, 85 a month with a 1,500 deductible.

Since he has 2,000 in savings, he chose the 85 one: he could pay the deductible if something happens, and he saves 300 a year.

--- pasos | fa-check-square-o | Do it this week
1. Find your car, health or renters insurance policy.
2. Write down the premium, the deductible and what it doesn't cover.
3. Ask your insurer about one question you have.

--- comprueba
1. Does liability insurance fix your own car? || No. It pays for damage you cause to others.
2. Does the building owner's insurance cover your things? || No. That's what renters insurance is for.

--- recuerda
- Read what your policy covers and what it excludes.
- Compare premium, deductible and coinsurance together.
- Tell your insurer if you use your car for work.

== profundiza

| | Monthly premium | Deductible | Yearly premium |
|---|---|---|---|
| Policy A | 70 | 1,500 | 840 |
| Policy B | 100 | 500 | 1,200 |

B costs 360 more per year, but its deductible is 1,000 lower. The better one depends on whether you could pay the deductible when something happens.

--- tema | fa-heartbeat | A health event with numbers
A 2,000 service; 500 left on the deductible; 20% coinsurance on the rest.

You pay 500 + 20% of 1,500 = 500 + 300 = **800**.

Check the **out-of-pocket maximum**: it's the most you would pay in a year, not counting the premium.

--- tema | fa-medkit | Health care in California
California has a state mandate to have health coverage, with exemptions. Covered California offers plans with help paying the premium based on your income.

Check the network of doctors, the covered services, authorizations and copays before choosing.

> **Good to know:** renters insurance in California usually costs between 15 and 30 dollars a month and covers your things if there's a theft or fire. Many people don't have it because they think the owner covers them.

--- tema | fa-check | Verify the insurer and the agent
Look up the insurer and the agent with the **California Department of Insurance (CDI)**. If they aren't licensed, don't sign up.

The CDI also takes complaints if an insurer doesn't pay what it should.

--- tema | fa-heart | Life insurance
Life insurance protects the people who depend on your income if you die.

**Term insurance** covers a number of years and is usually cheap. **Permanent** insurance costs more and combines insurance with savings. For many young families, term insurance is enough.

Be wary of anyone who pressures you to buy expensive life insurance as an "investment."

? Does the building's insurance cover it? || No. Now Mar is comparing renters insurance.

### Case 3. Rosa's health plan
Rosa compares two plans: one with a low premium and a 3,000 deductible; another with a higher premium and a 500 deductible.
? Which is better? || Since Rosa goes to the doctor often, the low-deductible plan may cost her less over the year.

--- errores
* Choosing by the premium only | You pay more when something happens | Add up premium, deductible and coinsurance
* Believing the building's insurance covers your things | You lose your belongings | Get renters insurance
* Not saying you use your car for work | Your accident isn't paid | Tell your insurer
* Signing up with an unlicensed agent | A fake policy or one with no backing | Check with the CDI

# M4 U08 | Is this medical document asking me to pay?
objetivo: Review medical bills, ask for financial assistance and complain if something is wrong.
gancho: Rosa went to the emergency room for a bad pain. Weeks later she got three different papers from the hospital and the insurance. She didn't know which one to pay or why the amounts were different. In this lesson you'll learn to read your medical documents and ask for help before paying.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-heartbeat | Never delay urgent care
First of all: **never delay urgent care out of fear of the cost**. Your health comes first. Payments get reviewed later, and there's help.

--- tarjetas | fa-file-text-o | Three different documents
* fa-book | Policy or plan information | What your insurance covers. | For reference. | igual
* fa-info-circle | {{Explanation of benefits|A paper from the insurance explaining how it paid for care. It isn't a bill.}} (EOB) | How your insurance processed the care. | **It isn't a bill.** | si
* fa-file-text | Bill | What you're asked to pay. | Check it before you pay. | no

--- pasos | fa-list-ol | Three steps before you pay
1. **Check** the name, date and service.
2. **Compare** the amounts and what the insurance already paid.
3. **Ask for an explanation** of any difference before you pay.

--- paso | fa-shield | Protection against surprise bills
If you go to an emergency room, federal law (the **No Surprises Act**) prohibits charging you excessive "out-of-network" rates for doctors you didn't choose.

And if you don't have insurance, you have the right to ask for a written **{{Good Faith Estimate|A written estimate of the total cost of scheduled medical care, which you should get if you don't have insurance.}}** of the total cost before scheduled care.

--- paso | fa-hand-o-right | Ask for financial assistance before you pay
California hospitals must have a **financial assistance** policy and offer no-cost or discounted care to people with low and middle incomes who qualify.

Ask **before** you pay or put the bill on a credit card.

> **Key idea:** if two papers describe the same service, they aren't two debts.

--- pasos | fa-check-square-o | Do it this week
1. Look up the financial assistance policy of your nearest hospital.
2. Keep your explanations of benefits together with your bills.
3. Write down your health plan's customer service phone number.

--- comprueba
1. Is the explanation of benefits (EOB) a bill? || No. It explains how your insurance paid; it doesn't ask you to pay.
2. You don't have insurance and a test is scheduled for you. What can you ask for? || A written Good Faith Estimate with the total cost.

--- recuerda
- Your health first; payments get reviewed later.
- Compare the bill with the insurance's explanation.
- Ask about financial assistance before you pay.

== profundiza

--- tema | fa-search | What to compare
Patient, date, service, amount charged, adjustments, what the plan paid and what you owe.

If there's a difference, ask for an **itemized bill** with each service and its code. Sometimes there are duplicate charges or services you didn't receive.

--- tema | fa-ambulance | Emergencies and Medi-Cal
**Emergency Medi-Cal** can cover emergency care for people who qualify by income, regardless of immigration status.

Ask at the hospital whether they can help you apply.

--- tema | fa-credit-card | Medical debts and your credit
In California, a law in effect since 2025 generally prohibits reporting medical debts to the credit agencies. Check whether there are exceptions or changes.

Even so, don't ignore a bill: ask for financial assistance or an no-interest payment plan.

> **Good to know:** if the Good Faith Estimate says 1,000 and you're charged 400 or more above it, you can start a federal dispute within 120 days of the bill.

--- tema | fa-bullhorn | Where to complain
The **DMHC** handles complaints about many health plans in California, including an independent medical review if you're denied treatment.

Other plans have a different regulator. Check your card or your policy to find out which one is yours.

--- tema | fa-calendar | Payment plans
If you owe a medical bill, ask for a payment plan. Many hospitals offer them with no interest.

Avoid putting a medical bill on a credit card: you lose the option of financial assistance and you pay interest.

--- tema | fa-comments | What to say to the hospital
"I received this bill and I want to know whether I qualify for financial assistance. Can you send me the application? While you review it, can you put the billing on hold?"

Always ask for the name of the person who helped you and the date of the call.

--- casos
### Case 1. The 300 bill
Mar's explanation of benefits says she owes 150, but the bill asks for 300 for the same service.
? What does she do? || She asks for an explanation before paying.

### Case 2. Alex's hospital stay
Alex gets a 4,000 bill.
? What does Alex do? || Asks about the hospital's financial assistance and turns in proof of income.
? What happens? || The bill goes down to 400 with a payment plan.

### Case 3. Rosa's three papers
Rosa received an EOB from the insurance, a bill from the hospital and another from the emergency room doctor.
? What does she find out? || The EOB isn't a charge. The emergency doctor's bill had an "out-of-network" rate that, under the surprise billing law, she shouldn't be charged. She calls and disputes it.

--- errores
* Delaying urgent care out of fear of the cost | Your health gets worse | Get care first
* Paying the EOB as if it were a bill | You pay too much | The EOB only informs
* Paying without asking for financial assistance | You miss discounts you're entitled to | Ask before paying
* Putting the bill on your card | You pay interest and lose assistance | Ask the hospital for a payment plan

# M4 U09 | How do I pay for the essentials if I can't work for a few days?
objetivo: Prepare your finances for a disaster, an accident or a temporary loss of income.
gancho: A fire near Fresno forced Rosa to leave her house with her grandchildren in twenty minutes. She took the medicine, but left all her papers. In this lesson you'll learn to prepare your finances for an emergency before it arrives.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-fire | An emergency takes away and adds at the same time
An accident, a disaster or an illness can take away income and, at the same time, add expenses: transportation, eating out, medicine.

A **{{contingency plan|Steps you prepare calmly so you know what to do if an emergency comes.}}** prepares you without having to guess what will happen.

--- pasos | fa-list-ol | Three quick calculations
1. **Write down which income** could be missing.
2. **Write down the new expenses** and the ones that stay the same.
3. **Check the money available** and the help that's already confirmed.

--- tarjetas | fa-suitcase | What to prepare
* fa-address-book | Contacts | A list on paper, outside your phone. | Keep it in your backpack. | si
* fa-file-text | Documents | Originals in a safe place and copies in another place. | Photos in the cloud too. | si
* fa-camera | Inventory | Photos of your valuable things. | Useful for the insurance. | si
* fa-credit-card | A way to pay | An extra card or some cash in small bills. | In case there's no power or internet. | si

--- paso | fa-heart | Safety first
In a fire, an evacuation or an accident, follow the emergency instructions. Then organize your resources.

> **Key idea:** don't count help that isn't confirmed. A payment from insurance or the government is only a resource once it's confirmed.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
After the fire, Rosa packed a backpack with copies of her documents in a sealed bag, a contact list on paper, medicine for three days and 100 dollars in small bills.

She keeps it by the door and told her daughter where it is.

--- pasos | fa-check-square-o | Do it this week

2. Write your contact list on paper.
3. Pack a backpack with the basics for three days.

--- comprueba
1. Does an insurance payment that isn't confirmed yet count as a resource? || No. It only counts when it's confirmed.
2. Do you keep the only copy of your documents at home? || No. Also keep a copy in another safe place.

--- recuerda
- Prepare your backpack and your paper contact list.
- Keep copies of your documents in two places.
- Don't count help that isn't confirmed.

== profundiza

--- tema | fa-umbrella | Insurance and disasters
Check the deductibles and exclusions of your insurance **before** something happens.

Earthquake and flood usually require separate policies. In California there's the California Earthquake Authority (CEA) for earthquake insurance.

--- tema | fa-user-secret | Scams after a disaster
After a disaster, fake donation campaigns appear, along with contractors who charge upfront and disappear.

Verify whoever asks for donations and whoever offers repairs. In California, contractors must be licensed by the CSLB.

--- tabla | fa-calculator | Two weeks without working
| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Missing income | 900 |
| New expense (temporary move) | 200 |
| Savings (transportation to work) | −50 |
| **Total effect** | **1,050** |
| Savings available | 400 |
| **Shortfall** | **650** |

With the shortfall clear, you can decide which payments to move, who to call and what help to ask for.

> **Good to know:** if you get hurt at work, in California you have the right to workers' compensation, regardless of immigration status. If you can't work because of an illness outside of work, ask about State Disability Insurance (SDI).

--- tema | fa-life-ring | Help after a disaster
When a disaster is declared, there may be help from the state, the county and community organizations. Some help doesn't depend on your immigration status.

Keep receipts for all your emergency expenses: they can help you claim assistance.

--- tema | fa-mobile | Emergency alerts
Turn on emergency alerts on your phone and sign up for your county's alert system.

In California, many alerts also come in Spanish. That way you find out about an evacuation in time.

--- casos
### Case 1. Two weeks without working
Alex gets hurt outside of work. Alex loses 900 in income; a temporary move costs 200, but saves 50 in transportation.
? How much is Alex short? || With 400 in savings, 650 short.
? What does Alex do? || Identifies which payments are due, asks about SDI and calls creditors before falling behind.

### Case 2. The phone lost in the evacuation

### Case 3. Rosa's contractor

--- errores
* Keeping everything only on your phone | You lose access to everything | A paper list and copies in another place
* Counting help that isn't confirmed | You commit to payments you can't cover | Count only what's confirmed
* Paying a contractor upfront | They disappear with your money | Check their license and pay as work progresses
* Donating without verifying | Your help doesn't arrive | Donate to organizations you know

# M4 U10 | What happens to my family and my money if I can't be there?
objetivo: Build a family preparedness plan: who takes care of your children, where your documents are and how bills get paid if you can't do it.
gancho: Daniela is a single mom. One day she thought: "If something happens to me, who picks up my son at school? Who pays the rent?" She had no answer. In this lesson you'll build a plan so your family can keep going if you can't be there.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-heart | Preparing isn't giving up
A detention, a hospital stay or an emergency trip can separate you from your family overnight.

A plan made calmly protects your family in a moment of crisis. Preparing isn't giving up: it's caring.

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | The five parts of the plan
* fa-child | Care of the children | Who takes care of them and a backup person. | Talk with them today. | si
* fa-folder-open | Documents | Birth certificates, passports, insurance, contracts. | In a place your family knows. | si
* fa-money | Money and accounts | How rent gets paid and who can access what. | With formal authorization. | si
* fa-address-book | Contacts | Family, school, doctor, attorney, consulate. | On paper. | si
* fa-refresh | Review | Every six months or if something changes. | Set a reminder. | si

--- pasos | fa-list-ol | Three steps to start
1. **Decide who would take care of your children** and talk with that person.
2. **Gather your important documents** in a place your family knows.
3. **Leave instructions** for paying the rent and the bills.

--- paso | fa-file-text | The caregiver affidavit
California has the **{{Caregiver's Authorization Affidavit|A California form that lets a trusted adult enroll your children in school and authorize some medical care. It doesn't change custody.}}**.

It lets a trusted adult enroll your children in school and authorize some medical care. It does **not** change custody. It costs nothing and doesn't need a judge.

--- paso | fa-key | Sharing your password isn't enough
Sharing your bank password does **not** give your family legal permission to act if you're in the hospital or detained.

A **{{durable financial power of attorney|A document that legally authorizes a person you trust to manage your money and pay your bills if you can't, even if you're incapacitated.}}** lets the person you choose pay the rent and manage your accounts legally while you're away.

> **Key idea:** this course doesn't give immigration or legal advice. For your options, go to an attorney or an accredited representative (M4 U02).

--- pasos | fa-check-square-o | Do it this week
1. Talk with the person who would take care of your children.
2. Download the Caregiver's Authorization Affidavit.
3. Put birth certificates and passports together in a folder.

--- comprueba
1. Does the Caregiver's Authorization Affidavit change custody? || No. It only allows handling school matters and some medical ones.
2. Does sharing your password give legal authorization to manage your money? || No. That requires a power of attorney or another formal authorization.

--- recuerda
- Choose a caregiver and a backup person, and talk with them.
- Gather your documents in a known place.
- Use formal authorizations, not passwords.

== profundiza

--- tema | fa-child | 1. Care of the children
- Choose a trusted person and a backup.
- Update the school's emergency card with their names.
- Fill out the Caregiver's Authorization Affidavit and give a copy to the caregiver.
- For broader decisions, like guardianship, talk to a family law attorney.

--- tema | fa-folder-open | 2. Documents
Gather originals and copies in a safe place:

- everyone's birth certificates and passports;
- if your children were born in the U.S., consider getting their U.S. passport and registering their dual nationality at the consulate;
- health insurance cards, prescriptions and health information;
- lease, vehicle titles, insurance policies;
- if you have an immigration case, the case number and the contact information of your attorney or accredited representative.

--- tema | fa-money | 3. Money and accounts
- A list of your accounts, automatic payments and dates, without passwords or full numbers.
- Who will be able to access your money and how. A joint account, an authorized user or a power of attorney give different powers (M5 U08).
- How the rent will be paid and who to notify.
- A small cushion the caregiver can use.

The durable financial power of attorney is prepared with an attorney or a certified legal service and signed before a notary. Ask at your legal aid organization.

> **Good to know:** California has a statutory form for a financial power of attorney. Even so, it's worth reviewing it with a legal service so it covers what you need.

--- tema | fa-address-book | 4. Contacts and 5. Review
Write down on paper: family, school, doctor, attorney or legal aid organization, consulate and employer.

Review the whole plan every six months or when something changes: a move, a new job, a new child.

--- tema | fa-university | If your children were born in the U.S.
Your children born in the U.S. are citizens and have the right to a U.S. passport. Having one can make travel and paperwork easier.

If you're Mexican, your children can also register their Mexican nationality at the consulate. Ask through MiConsulado which documents you need.

--- casos
### Case 1. Alex and Mar's plan
They choose Mar's sister as caregiver and a neighbor as backup.

### Case 2. The password isn't enough

* Thinking it won't happen to you | Your family is left without a plan | Prepare it calmly today
* Choosing a caregiver without talking to them | They don't know what to do | Talk and give them a copy of the plan
* Sharing passwords instead of authorizing | The bank won't let them act | Use a power of attorney
* Keeping documents where nobody knows | They can't be found when needed | Tell your caregiver where they are

# M4 U11 | How do I know if my protection plan works?
objetivo: Bring together prevention, protection and response, and test your plan with a drill.
gancho: Rosa had her plan written on a sheet: contacts, documents, savings. One day she lost her phone and realized she didn't remember her email password. The sheet looked complete, but it didn't work. In this lesson you'll test your plan before you need it.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-check-circle | A plan you can use
A plan works if you can use it, not if it looks complete on a sheet.

The best way to find out is to practice with a made-up situation: a **{{drill|Practice with a made-up emergency to see if your plan works.}}**.

--- tarjetas | fa-link | A plan connects four things
* fa-exclamation-triangle | Risk | What can happen. | Losing your phone. | no
* fa-bolt | Impact | What it affects. | You can't get into your bank. | no
* fa-shield | Protection | What you already have. | A recovery email and a paper list. | si
* fa-play | Action | What you do step by step. | Call, change passwords, notify. | si

--- pasos | fa-list-ol | Your drill in three steps
1. **Choose a situation**, for example: you lose your phone and rent is due tomorrow.
2. **Explain each step** in order, without skipping any.
3. **Mark** what you still couldn't do and how to solve it.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Rosa did her drill: she lost her phone. She found out she didn't know her email password and that her contact list was only on her phone.

She wrote her contacts on paper, asked her daughter to help her update her recovery email and kept the sheet in her backpack.

> **Key idea:** a plan that only works for one emergency needs adjustments.

--- pasos | fa-check-square-o | Do it this week
1. Choose a situation and do your drill in 15 minutes.
2. Write down what was missing.
3. Fix one thing this same week.

--- comprueba
1. Does a protection plan require buying every insurance? || No. It's about identifying gaps and deciding how to cover them.
2. What is a drill for? || To discover what still doesn't work, before a real emergency.

--- recuerda
- Test your plan with a made-up situation.
- Fix what didn't work.
- Review your plan when your life changes.

== profundiza

--- tema | fa-sort-amount-desc | Start with what affects you most
Prioritize the risks that can affect your housing, your income, your health or your safety.

Don't try to buy every insurance. Identify the gaps and decide what you can prevent, what you can cover with savings, what you can transfer to an insurance policy and what you need to handle with support.

--- tabla | fa-table | For each risk, define
| Part | Example: losing your phone |
|---|---|
| Warning sign | You can't find your phone or it's stolen |
| Safe immediate action | Call the phone company from another phone |
| Verified channel | Your bank's number on your paper list |
| Follow-up | Check transactions and change passwords |

--- tema | fa-gamepad | Three drills to practice
1. You lose your phone and rent is due tomorrow. How do you get access back? What information do you need and where is it?
2. An unexpected medical bill arrives. Which steps change?
3. A fake family emergency arrives when your income has dropped. Which parts of the plan do you use?

> **Good to know:** emergency plans that are practiced at least once a year work much better than ones that are only written. Many schools and workplaces hold drills for that reason.

--- tema | fa-refresh | When to review
Review your plan when your job, your home, your phone, your vehicle or your dependents change.

Set a reminder on your calendar to do a drill once a year.

--- tema | fa-users | Do the drill as a family
Practicing as a family helps everyone know what to do. Ask each person: where's the backpack? Who do you call? Where do we meet if there's no signal?

Children can also learn a relative's phone number by heart.

--- errores
* Writing the plan and not testing it | It fails when you need it | Do a drill
* Trying to cover everything with insurance | You spend too much | Prioritize your main risks
* Not updating the plan | It has old information | Review it when something changes
* Keeping the plan where nobody can find it | It's useless in an emergency | Tell your family where it is
