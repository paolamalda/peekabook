# M3 U01 | If they lend me money, does that mean I can pay it back?
objetivo: Tell apart being approved for a loan from your real ability to pay.
gancho: Andrés was approved for a loan on a new car in fifteen minutes. He left the dealership happy. In the first month he realized that, with insurance, the payment didn't fit in his budget. In this lesson you'll learn to know whether you can pay before you sign.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-check-circle | Approved is not the same as affordable
Being approved by a lender means **the lender** believes you'll pay them back. It doesn't mean the payment is good for you or comfortable.

Lenders look at your income and your history, but they don't know all your expenses, your remittances or your plans.

--- ecuacion | fa-calculator | Do the math yourself
You're offered a payment of 50 a month. After food, housing and commitments, you only have 30 left.
= 30 | What you have left over
- 50 | The payment offered
= −20 | You'd be short every month
Your **{{ability to pay|What you can pay each month without leaving your basic expenses uncovered, even in a hard month.}}** means being able to cover the payment with your resources, even in a hard month.

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | Three different things
* fa-file-text-o | {{Credit report|A history of how you pay your debts. Banks review it to decide whether to lend to you.}} | Information about your accounts and payments. | The lender reviews it. | igual
* fa-tachometer | {{Credit score|A number that sums up your history. The higher it is, the easier and cheaper it is to borrow.}} | A number that sums up part of your report. | It helps you get approved. | igual
* fa-balance-scale | Ability to pay | What's left after your expenses. | Only you really know it. | si

--- pasos | fa-list-ol | Four questions before you ask for a loan
1. **Will they approve me?** It depends on the lender.
2. **Can I pay it?** It depends on your calendar and your budget.
3. **Is it good for me?** It depends on the purpose and the alternatives.
4. **What do I risk?** If there's collateral, you can lose the item.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Mar was approved for a 1,000 loan and offered 500 more "just in case."

Mar did the math: the payment on 1,500 didn't fit in a month with fewer tips. She accepted only the 1,000 she needed.

Extra borrowed money is extra debt, even if it's easy to get.

> **Key idea:** a good score doesn't make the monthly payment for you.

--- pasos | fa-check-square-o | Do it this week
1. Calculate how much you have left each month after your basic expenses, debts and remittances.
2. Subtract the payment you're offered, plus insurance and other costs.
3. Do the math again with 20% less income.

--- comprueba
1. You were approved for a loan. Does that mean you can pay it? || No. You have to check whether the payment fits your budget, even in a hard month.
2. Is an available credit limit your money? || No. It's money they would lend you and you'd have to pay back.

--- recuerda
- Approved is not the same as affordable.
- Test the payment with a lower-income month.
- Ask only for what you need.

== profundiza

--- tarjetas | fa-credit-card | Types of credit
* fa-refresh | {{Revolving credit|Credit you can use again as you pay it down, like a credit card.}} | Cards and lines of credit. You can use it again. | The balance can grow if you don't keep it in check. | no
* fa-calendar | Installment | Personal or car loan, with fixed payments. | You know when you'll finish. | igual
* fa-lock | Secured by {{collateral|Something of value, like your car, that backs a debt. If you don't pay, it can be taken.}} | An item backs the debt, like the car. | If you don't pay, you can lose the item. | no

--- tema | fa-percent | Debt-to-income ratio
The {{debt-to-income ratio|What share of your income goes to paying debts each month.}} compares your debt payments with your income.

Debt payments of 450 ÷ gross income of 3,000 = 15%. With a net income of 2,400, it's 18.75%. Always say which one you used.

This ratio doesn't include your basic expenses, like rent or food. That's why it doesn't replace your budget.

--- tema | fa-bolt | Stress test
If you have 200 left after your obligations and the new payment is 150, your margin is 50.

If your income drops by 100, you'll have a deficit of 50. Ask yourself whether you could keep up that payment in a month with fewer hours or fewer tips.

--- tema | fa-car | The costs you don't see
Financing a car also means insurance, gas, maintenance, registration and plates.

A furniture loan may include credit insurance you don't need. Ask what the monthly payment includes.

> **Good to know:** in California, car insurance has minimum required liability amounts. If you finance a car, the lender will also require full coverage, which costs more.

--- tema | fa-thumbs-o-up | Credit as a tool
Credit lets you use resources today in exchange for a future obligation. It can help you reach a goal, like a car for work, and it can also take away flexibility.

Don't measure your progress by how many cards you have or by your available limit. A credit limit isn't your money.

--- tema | fa-comments | Questions for the lender
Before you sign, ask in writing:

- What is the APR and the total cost of the loan?
- Is there a fee to open it or to pay it off early?
- What happens if I'm late on a payment?
- Does the payment include insurance or products I didn't ask for?

If they won't give you the answers in writing, don't sign yet.

--- casos
### Case 1. Andrés's car
Andrés earns 2,500 net a month. His expenses and commitments add up to 2,250: he has 250 left. The car payment would be 220, plus 90 for insurance.
? Can he afford it? || No. He'd be 60 short every month, even though he was approved.
? What options does he have? || A cheaper car, a bigger down payment or waiting to save more.

### Case 2. Mar's extra 500
Mar is approved for 500 more than she asked for.
? Should she take it to "make the most of it"? || No. It's extra debt with its own cost. She should ask only for what she needs.

### Case 3. Luis's first card
Luis got a card offer with a 2,000 limit. He had never had so much "available money."
? How should he see it? || As a loan he'd have to pay back, not as his money.

--- errores
* Thinking that if you're approved you can pay | You fall behind from the first month | Do your own math
* Accepting more than you need | You pay interest on money you don't use | Ask only for what you need
* Forgetting the extra costs | The real payment is higher | Add insurance, gas and maintenance
* Seeing your credit limit as your money | You spend and the debt piles up | Use it only with a payment plan

--- quiz

3. If you have a 200 margin and the payment is 150, what happens if your income drops by 100? a) You're still fine · b) A deficit of 50 · c) You have 50 left
respuestas: 1-b: the score doesn't make the payment. 2-b: it's a loan available to you. 3-b: 200 − 100 − 150 = −50.

--- ponlo

respuesta: Not necessarily. Compare the need, the cost and the payment. Additional borrowed money is an additional obligation.

--- plan
Define the purpose, the amount you need, the payment you can cover and what you'd do if your income drops. If you have no margin, the first step may be to stabilize your finances.

# M3 U02 | How do I know what information is used about my debts?
objetivo: Read your credit report, spot errors and dispute them correctly.
gancho: Andrés was turned down for a loan because of "information in his report." He didn't know he had a report or what it said. When he checked it, he found a card he never opened. In this lesson you'll learn to check your report and fix errors.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-file-text-o | What your credit report is
Your **credit report** brings together information about your accounts and how you pay. Three agencies make it: **Equifax, Experian and TransUnion**.

Your **score** is a number calculated with part of that information.

If you ever had a card, a loan or a phone paid in installments, you probably have a report.

--- paso | fa-download | How to get it at no cost
You can see your reports from all three agencies **no-cost every week** on the official site **AnnualCreditReport.com**.

Checking your own report does **not** lower your score.

If you file with an ITIN, some agencies let you request your report by mail or phone instead of online. Ask about the process.

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | What to check in your report
* fa-user | Your information | Name, addresses, date of birth. | Is it yours? | igual
* fa-credit-card | Accounts | Creditor, balance, limit, status and payment history. | Do you recognize them? | igual
* fa-search | Inquiries | Who asked to see your report. | Did you authorize it? | igual
* fa-exclamation-triangle | Collections | Debts that went to a collector. | Are they correct? | no

--- pasos | fa-list-ol | Three steps
1. **Confirm** that the personal information is yours.
2. **Check** each account: balance, dates and payments.
3. **Mark** anything you don't recognize or that looks wrong.

--- paso | fa-balance-scale | An error is not the same as negative information
A late payment that really happened isn't an error, even if it lowers your score. You handle it by paying and getting organized.

Incorrect information is corrected with a well-documented **{{dispute|A formal request for the agency to investigate and correct wrong information in your report.}}**.

> **Key idea:** use only AnnualCreditReport.com. Other websites charge or ask for your card information.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Andrés requested his three no-cost reports. One showed an address in another city where he never lived and a card he didn't open.

He took screenshots, disputed both items online with the agency and froze his credit at all three agencies. He wrote down the confirmation numbers and set an alert to check again in 30 days.

--- pasos | fa-check-square-o | Do it this week
1. Go to AnnualCreditReport.com and request your three reports.
2. Check your personal information and each account.
3. Write down anything you don't recognize so you can look into it.

--- comprueba
1. Does checking your own report lower your score? || No. You can check it without affecting your score.
2. Your report shows a real late payment. Is it an error that can be erased? || No. Correct information isn't erased; you handle it by paying.

--- recuerda
- Check your three reports no-cost at AnnualCreditReport.com.
- Separate errors from correct negative information.
- Dispute only errors, with proof.

== profundiza

--- tema | fa-question-circle | Unknown information can be three things
1. **An error** by the agency or the creditor.
2. **A different business name** for your creditor: the store gave you the card, but the bank shows up.
3. **Possible fraud:** someone opened an account in your name.

Figure out which one it is before you act.

--- tabla | fa-list-ol | How to dispute an error
| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| 1 | Identify the exact item that's wrong |
| 2 | Explain why it's incorrect |
| 3 | Attach a copy of your proof, never the original |
| 4 | Send the dispute to the agency and, if you can, to the creditor |
| 5 | Keep a copy, the date and the confirmation number |

The agency usually has 30 days to investigate and answer you.

--- tema | fa-clock-o | How long information stays
Much correct negative information can stay up to seven years. Certain bankruptcies, up to ten.

Don't treat these as a "clean slate" date: it depends on the type of record. Meanwhile, paying on time from now on does improve your history.

> **Good to know:** you can freeze your credit at no cost at all three agencies. That way nobody can open new accounts in your name. You'll see it in M4 U04.

--- tema | fa-ban | Never lie in a dispute
Never claim identity theft to erase a real late payment. It's false and can have legal consequences.

Disputes are for correcting errors, not for erasing history.

--- tema | fa-envelope-o | How to write your dispute
A good dispute is short and specific:

"My name is [name]. My report shows the account ending in 1234 with a balance of 900. That account was closed at zero on March 10. I'm attaching a copy of the closing letter. I ask that you investigate and correct the balance."

Cover your sensitive information on the copies, like your full account number.

--- tema | fa-bell | If you're denied credit
If you're denied a loan or a rental because of your report, they must tell you which agency issued it.

You have the right to request that report no-cost within the next 60 days and to dispute any errors.

--- casos
### Case 1. Someone else's card
Andrés's report shows a card he never opened and a real late payment on a phone.
? What does he do about the card? || He disputes it as possible fraud and freezes his credit.
? And about the real late payment? || He handles it by paying and asking for an agreement.

### Case 2. The balance that wasn't updated
Mar paid off a card, but the report shows a balance of 900.
? Is it an error? || She checks the report date: it was generated before the payment. She waits for the next update before disputing.

### Case 3. Luis's first report
Luis requested his report by mail using his ITIN.
? What did he find? || His phone installment plan, paid on time. He already had a history without knowing it.

--- errores
* Using websites that charge for your report | You pay for something no-cost or your data gets stolen | Use AnnualCreditReport.com
* Disputing correct information | It isn't erased and you lose time | Dispute only errors
* Sending original documents | You lose them | Send copies
* Not keeping the dispute confirmation number | You can't follow up | Write down date, number and answer

# M3 U03 | How do I start if I have no credit history?
objetivo: Compare options for building a credit history without taking on too much debt.
gancho: Luis wants to rent his own apartment, but the landlord asks for a credit history. He has never had a card: he always pays in cash or with his app. He doesn't know where to start. In this lesson you'll learn the safest ways to build a history.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-leaf | Having no history isn't bad
Having no history doesn't mean you're irresponsible. Many people arrive in the U.S. with no history or prefer to use cash.

But without a history it's harder to rent, get a loan or get a good price on car insurance. That's why it's worth starting carefully.

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | Ways to start
* fa-credit-card | {{Secured card|A credit card that asks for a deposit as backing. You pay your purchases like with any card.}} | You leave a deposit that backs your limit. You pay your purchases each month. | Ask if it reports to all three agencies. | si
* fa-users | {{Lending circle|A group that lends with no interest, like a tanda, but organized, and that reports your payments to the agencies.}} | A group lends with no interest and reports your payments. | Look for a recognized organization. | si
* fa-home | Rent reporting | Your on-time rent payments can be reported to the agencies. | Ask your landlord. | si
* fa-money | Credit-builder loan | You make payments and at the end you get the money. | Check the total cost. | igual

--- paso | fa-exclamation-circle | Be careful about locking up your money
If you have 500 in savings and a product asks you to lock up 400 as a deposit, you only have 100 left for emergencies.

No product is worth it if it locks up your only rent money.

--- paso | fa-check | You don't need to pay interest
To build a history with a card, **use a little and pay the full balance every month**. That way you pay no interest and your history grows anyway.

> **Key idea:** a product that doesn't report to the credit agencies doesn't build your history, even if you pay on time.

--- pasos | fa-check-square-o | Do it this week
1. Ask your landlord whether they can report your rent to the agencies.
2. Ask at a credit union about secured cards or lending circles with an ITIN.
3. Write down how much money would be locked up with each option.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Andrés came to the U.S. with a good credit history in Mexico, but here he had nothing. He asked a company that uses credit history from other countries, and a card accepted him with his Mexican history.

Luis, with no history in any country, started with a 200 secured card and asked for his rent to be reported.

--- comprueba
1. Does a secured card's deposit pay for your purchases? || No. You pay your purchases each month; the deposit only backs the limit.
2. Do you need to pay interest to build a history? || No. If you pay the full balance every month, you pay no interest.

--- recuerda
- Choose products that report to all three agencies.
- Don't lock up your emergency money.
- Pay the full balance every month.

== profundiza

--- tabla | fa-table | Compare the options
| Option | How it works | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Secured card | A deposit backs the limit | Does it report? Annual fee? When do they return the deposit? |
| Credit-builder loan | You make payments and get the money at the end | Total cost, who it reports to |
| Savings-secured loan | Your savings back the loan | Money locked up, interest |
| Lending circle | A group lends with no interest and reports (M3 U09) | Who organizes it |
| Authorized user | Someone adds you to their card | How they manage it affects you too |
| Rent reporting | Your rent payments get reported | Whether the service charges a fee |

--- tema | fa-home | Your rent can build your credit
If you pay rent every month, you're already doing something that can count toward your history.

In California, a recent law (AB 2747) requires many landlords to offer tenants the option of reporting their on-time rent payments to the credit agencies. It applies to certain buildings, for example those with 15 or more units, and reports only positive payments if you ask for it. [POR CONFIRMAR]

Ask your landlord whether they offer this service and whether it has a cost.

> **Good to know:** there are also companies that report your rent for a fee. Before paying, confirm they report to all three agencies and that you can cancel.

--- tema | fa-id-card | With an ITIN or with history from another country
Several credit unions and some banks offer cards and loans with an ITIN. Ask directly.

Some companies let you use your credit history from another country, like Mexico, for certain applications in the U.S. Check which countries and institutions take part.

--- tema | fa-user-plus | Authorized user and cosigner are not the same
As an **authorized user** you use someone else's card, and their history can help you. In many cases you're not legally responsible for the debt.

As a **cosigner** you sign and you're responsible for the debt if the other person doesn't pay. You'll see it in M3 U08.

--- tema | fa-calendar | How long it takes to show up
Your first payments usually show up on your report one or two months after you open the account.

For a score to exist, many models need at least six months of history. Be patient: what matters is paying on time every month.

--- casos
### Case 1. Luis's secured card
Luis deposits 300 in a secured card and buys 40 in gas.
? Does the deposit pay those 40? || No. Luis has to pay the 40 according to his statement.
? How does he build a history without paying interest? || By paying the full balance every month.

### Case 2. Two offers for Mar
Offer A costs 36 a year and confirms in the contract that it reports to all three agencies. Offer B costs 120 and doesn't confirm that it reports.
? Which is better? || A: it costs less and does build a history.

### Case 3. Daniela's rent
Daniela pays 1,600 in rent every month, always on time, in a 40-unit building.
? What can she ask? || Whether her landlord offers to report her rent payments to the credit agencies.

--- errores
* Choosing a product that doesn't report | You pay and don't build a history | Confirm it in the contract
* Locking up your emergency savings | You're left without money for surprises | Use a smaller deposit or wait
* Paying only the card's minimum | You pay interest you don't need to | Pay the full balance
* Thinking you need many cards | More risk of debt | One, used well, is enough

# M3 U04 | Does improving my credit mean erasing everything?
objetivo: Choose valid actions to rebuild your history and recognize false promises.
gancho: Mar saw an ad: "We erase your debts from the credit bureau in 30 days, 499 dollars." She had some late payments and the offer sounded perfect. Before paying, she asked. In this lesson you'll learn what you can do and which promises are false.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-eraser | What really happened doesn't get erased
If something in your report is wrong, you can dispute it. If a late payment really happened, it doesn't get erased: the work is different.

Improving your credit means fixing errors and, above all, paying on time from now on.

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | Fixing and rebuilding are different
* fa-wrench | Fix | Make sure your report is accurate, with disputes and proof. | Only for errors. | igual
* fa-line-chart | Rebuild | Pay on time, lower balances and don't open more debt than you need. | It takes months, but it works. | si

--- paso | fa-percent | Your card utilization
**{{Credit utilization|How much of your card limit you're using. If you owe 300 on a 1,000 limit, you use 30%.}}** is how much of your limit you're using.

A 900 balance with a 1,500 limit = 60%. If you lower the balance to 450, it's 30%.

Using less of your limit helps your score. There's no magic percentage that guarantees a number.

--- paso | fa-exclamation-triangle | False promises
Be wary of anyone who promises to:

- erase all negative information;
- raise your score by a guaranteed amount;
- create a "new credit identity";
- charge you before doing the work.

Anything a legitimate credit repair company does, you can do yourself at no cost.

--- pasos | fa-check-square-o | Do it this week
1. Check your report and separate errors from correct information.
2. Dispute only errors, with proof.
3. Set alerts to pay everything on time.

> **Key idea:** nobody can guarantee you a number of points or erase correct information.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Daniela had two late payments last year. Instead of paying to "repair" her credit, she set up automatic minimum payments on her two cards and lowered her balance little by little.

A year later, her late payments are still on the report, but she has twelve months in a row of on-time payments. She's already been approved for a loan at a better rate.

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | What weighs on your score
* fa-calendar-check-o | On-time payments | What weighs the most. | Never fall behind. | si
* fa-percent | Utilization | How much of your limit you use. | Use a little. | si
* fa-hourglass-half | Length of history | How long you've had credit. | Think twice before closing your oldest card. | igual

--- comprueba
1. Can a real late payment be erased because it lowers your score? || No. Correct information stays; you improve by paying on time.
2. A company charges you before doing the "repair." Is that normal? || No. It's a red flag and the law prohibits it.

--- recuerda
- Fix errors and rebuild by paying on time.
- Lower your card balances.
- Nobody can erase what really happened.

== profundiza

--- tema | fa-search | Find the cause
First figure out why your history isn't good: an error, fraud, a late payment, high balances or recent changes.

Each cause has a different action. Fraud gets reported; an error gets disputed; a late payment is handled with a payment plan; a high balance is lowered little by little.

--- tema | fa-calendar-check-o | A routine that works
- Write down the due dates of all your debts.
- Make sure there's money before each automatic payment.
- If you see you're going to be late, contact the creditor before the due date.

One on-time payment doesn't change everything. Many on-time payments, one after another, do.

--- tema | fa-clock-o | The clocks are different
Your payment, the update to your report, how long an item stays and the change in your score happen at different times.

That's why you can pay today and see the change in your report one or two months later.

--- tema | fa-gavel | The law protects you
Federal law prohibits credit repair companies from charging you before doing the work. They also must give you a written contract, and you have 3 days to cancel it.

> **Good to know:** in California, credit services companies must register with the state. If one isn't registered, it's a red flag.

--- tema | fa-lightbulb-o | Ideas that do help
- Set a reminder or an automatic minimum payment on each card.
- Pay more than once a month if you can: it lowers your utilization.
- Don't open several new accounts in a short time.
- If you have an old card with no annual fee, keep it open and use it now and then.

--- tema | fa-user-secret | How to recognize a legitimate company
A legitimate credit counseling company doesn't promise you results, doesn't charge you upfront and gives you a written contract.

Many nonprofit agencies offer no-cost or low-cost counseling. Ask whether they're affiliated with a national credit counseling organization.

--- casos
### Case 1. The 30-day offer
Mar is offered to have her real late payments erased in 30 days for 499 dollars.
? Does she sign up? || No. She checks her information, disputes only errors and organizes her payments.

### Case 2. Andrés's utilization
Andrés has a 600 balance on a 1,000 limit: 60%. He pays 300 and drops to 30%.
? How many points will he gain? || He doesn't know, but he does know he lowered his debt and his utilization.

### Case 3. Luis's "new identity"
Luis is offered a "new number" to start a clean history.
? What is it? || A scam and a crime. Using a number that isn't yours can bring serious legal problems.

--- errores
* Paying to "erase" real late payments | You lose money and the item stays | Organize your payments
* Expecting a guaranteed number of points | You get frustrated | Measure actions: on-time payments, lower balance
* Opening many new cards | More inquiries and more risk | Use the ones you have well
* Accepting a "new number" | A crime and legal problems | Use only your own SSN or ITIN

# M3 U05 | Why can a small payment end up costing more?
objetivo: Compare loans and cards by their total cost, not by the monthly payment.
gancho: Daniela was offered a professional oven for her business "for only 70 dollars a month." It seemed cheap. When she multiplied it out, the oven cost her almost double its price. In this lesson you'll learn to see the total cost before you sign.

== esencial

--- tabla | fa-calculator | A low monthly payment can cost more
| Offer | Monthly payment | Months | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 10 | 10 | 100 |
| 2 | 8 | 15 | 120 |

Offer 2 has the lower payment, but it costs 20 more in total.

--- paso | fa-plus-circle | Add it all up
A small monthly payment can come from a longer term and a larger **{{total cost|Everything you pay for a loan: the payments, the interest and the fees, added up.}}**.

Add up all the payments and fees. Compare that total with what you receive.

--- pasos | fa-list-ol | Three steps to compare
1. **Find out how much you really receive**, after fees.
2. **Add up all the payments and fees.**
3. **Check whether the payments fit** your paydays.

--- paso | fa-credit-card | The card's minimum payment
The **{{minimum payment|The least you can pay on your card to avoid falling behind. If you only pay that, the debt takes years to go down.}}** keeps your card current, but it stretches the debt out a lot.

If you only pay the minimum, you can take years to finish and pay a lot in interest. Your statement shows how long it would take.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Luis bought a phone with "pay in 4" in an app, then some sneakers and then some headphones. Each plan was 35 dollars.

Separately they seemed small. Together they were 140 every two weeks, taken automatically from his debit card.

Now he writes down all his plans in one place before adding another.

> **Key idea:** compare by total cost, not by what you pay each month.

--- paso | fa-question-circle | Three questions before you accept
1. How much do I really receive?
2. How much do I pay in total, adding everything up?
3. What happens if I fall behind?

If you can't answer all three, you don't have enough information to sign yet.

--- comprueba
1. Does paying your card's minimum avoid interest? || No. It only avoids falling behind; interest keeps being charged.
2. Payments of 70 for 24 months on a 1,000 loan. How much do you pay in total? || 1,680, because 70 × 24 = 1,680.

--- recuerda
- Multiply the monthly payment by the number of payments.
- The minimum payment stretches the debt out.
- Add up all your "pay later" plans.

== profundiza

--- tema | fa-percent | APR
The **APR** is the yearly cost of credit calculated with official rules. It's used to compare loans with each other.

Check which fees it includes and which it doesn't. A loan with a lower APR usually costs less.

--- tabla | fa-table | How a payment is split
A 1,000 loan at 1% a month with payments of 100.

| Month | Starting balance | Interest | Toward principal | Ending balance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1,000.00 | 10.00 | 90.00 | 910.00 |
| 2 | 910.00 | 9.10 | 90.90 | 819.10 |
| 3 | 819.10 | 8.19 | 91.81 | 727.29 |

Interest is calculated on what's left to pay. That's why each month a little more goes to the {{principal|The amount you were lent, not counting interest.}}.

--- tarjetas | fa-credit-card | Words on your card
* fa-scissors | Statement closing date | The end of your statement period. | What you buy after goes on the next one. | igual
* fa-calendar-times-o | Payment due date | The last day to pay without being late. | Pay before. | no
* fa-gift | {{Grace period|Time when you aren't charged interest if you pay the full balance on time.}} | If you pay the full balance on time, there's no interest on purchases. | It doesn't apply to cash advances. | si
* fa-exclamation | Deferred interest | If you don't pay it off in time, you're charged all the accumulated interest. | It's not the same as 0%. | no

--- tema | fa-shopping-bag | Other products that commit your paychecks
"Buy now, pay later," rent-to-own and payday loans also commit your next paychecks.

Add up all your plans, not each one on its own.

--- tema | fa-scissors | Upfront fees
If you're lent 1,000 but 50 is kept as a fee, you receive 950. If you pay back 1,100, the cost is 150 on what you really received.

> **Good to know:** in California, rent-to-own has legal limits on the total price. Even so, it almost always costs much more than buying in cash or saving for a few months.

--- tema | fa-calendar | Buy now, pay later
"Pay in 4" apps usually don't charge interest if you pay on time, but they can charge late fees.

The risk is adding several plans at once and having them taken from your debit card when you don't have the balance.

Before using one, write down all the ones you already have and their dates.

--- tema | fa-ban | Payday loans
Payday loans are small and are paid back on your next paycheck. They usually have a very high yearly cost.

In California they have limits on the amount and the fees, but they're still expensive. First ask your credit union about a small, low-cost loan.

--- casos
### Case 1. Andrés's two loans
A: he receives 1,000, pays 50 upfront and 12 payments of 95. B: he receives 1,000, no fee, 12 payments of 98.
? How much does each one cost? || A: 50 + 1,140 = 1,190. B: 1,176.
? Which is better? || B, which costs 14 less.

### Case 2. Daniela's oven
The oven costs 900 in cash. The store offers payments of 70 a month for 24 months.
? How much would she pay in total? || 1,680: 780 more than in cash.
? What options does she have? || Save for a few months, look for a lower-cost loan from a credit union or buy a used one.

### Case 3. Luis's plans
Luis has four "pay in 4" plans of 35 every two weeks.
? How much does it add up to every two weeks? || 140, all together.

--- errores
* Comparing only the monthly payment | You pay much more in total | Add up all the payments
* Paying only the minimum | The debt lasts years | Pay more than the minimum or the full balance
* Believing deferred interest is 0% | You're charged all the interest at once | Pay it off before the date
* Adding "pay later" plans without control | They eat up your paychecks | Write them all down in one place
