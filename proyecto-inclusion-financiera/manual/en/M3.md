## M3 U01. Does getting approved mean I can afford it?

**What you will achieve:** tell a loan approval apart from your real ability to pay.

### The essentials

You are offered a payment of 50 a month. After food, housing and commitments, you only have 30 left.

1. Calculate what is left after your needs.
2. Compare it with the payment offered.
3. Test what happens if you earn less.

> **Key idea:** getting approved does not change your numbers. **Affordability** means being able to cover the payment with your resources, even in a hard month.

### Go deeper

Credit lets you use resources today in exchange for a future obligation. It can help you reach a goal and it can also take away flexibility.

**Three different things:**

- **Credit report:** information about your accounts and payments.
- **Score:** a number that summarizes part of that information using a model.
- **Affordability:** depends on your income, needs and commitments. A good score does not make the payment for you.

**Four questions before taking a loan:**

1. Will they approve me? (depends on the lender)
2. Can I pay it? (depends on your calendar)
3. Is it right for me? (depends on the purpose and alternatives)
4. What do I risk? (collateral, contract, consequences)

**Types of credit:**

- **Revolving** (cards, lines of credit): you can use it again.
- **Installment** (personal loan, car): fixed payments on a schedule.
- **Secured:** an item backs the debt and you can lose it if you do not pay.

**Debt-to-income ratio.** Debt payments of 450 ÷ gross income of 3,000 = 15%. With net income of 2,400 = 18.75%. Say which one you used. This ratio does not include your basic expenses, so it does not replace your budget.

**Stress test.** If after your obligations you have 200 left and the new payment is 150, your cushion is 50. If your income drops 100, you will have a 50 deficit.

**Extra costs.** Financing a car also means insurance, gas, maintenance and registration.

Do not measure your progress by the number of cards or the available limit. A credit limit is not your money.

### Cases

**Case 1. Alex's car.** Net income 2,500; expenses and commitments 2,250; 250 left. The car payment would be 220 plus 90 for insurance. Even if approved, he would be 60 short every month.

**Case 2. The extra 500.** Mar is approved for 500 more than she asked for. Accepting it adds debt even if it is easy to get. She asks only for what she needs.

### Interactive activity

**Can I afford it? (H5P simulator):** enter your monthly cushion and the payment; lower your income by 10%, 20% and 30% and see the result.

### Quiz

1. Does a good score guarantee you can pay? a) Yes · b) No
2. Is available credit your money? a) Yes · b) No
3. If your cushion is 200 and the payment is 150, what happens if your income drops 100? a) You are still fine · b) A 50 deficit

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-b · 3-b.

### Practice

The lender offers you 500 more than you asked for. Should you accept it to "take advantage"?

**Answer:** not necessarily. Compare need, cost and payment. Additional borrowed money is an additional obligation.

### For your plan

Define the purpose, the amount needed, the payment you can cover and what you would do if your income drops. If there is no cushion, the first step may be to stabilize your finances.

## M3 U02. How do I know what information they use about my debts?

**What you will achieve:** read your credit report, spot errors and dispute them correctly.

### The essentials

The **credit report** gathers information about your accounts and payments. The **score** is a number calculated from part of that information.

1. Check that the information is yours.
2. Review accounts, balances and dates.
3. Mark anything you do not recognize or that seems wrong.

> **Key idea:** unfavorable information is not necessarily an error. An error is corrected with a well-documented **dispute**.

### Go deeper

**How to get it.** You can check your reports from the three national agencies (Equifax, Experian and TransUnion) **free every week** at the official site AnnualCreditReport.com. Checking your own report does not lower your score.

**With an ITIN:** some agencies let you request your report with an ITIN, but you may need to do it by mail or phone instead of online. Ask about the procedure.

**Read it by section:**

1. Personal information and addresses.
2. Accounts: creditor, balance, limit, status, payment history, dates.
3. Inquiries.
4. Collection records.

**An unknown item can be:** an error, a different business name for your creditor, or possible fraud. Figure out which before acting.

**How to dispute an error:**

- Identify the exact item.
- Explain why it is wrong.
- Attach a copy (never the original) of your proof.
- Send the dispute to the agency and, if you can, to the creditor that reported it.
- Keep a copy, the date and the reference number.

**How long information stays.** Many accurate negative items can stay up to seven years and certain bankruptcies up to ten, with exceptions. Do not treat these as a personal "clean slate" date: it depends on the type of record.

**Never claim identity theft to erase a real late payment.** It is false and can have legal consequences.

### Cases

**Case 1. Someone else's account.** Alex's report shows a card he never opened and a real late payment on a phone bill. He disputes the card as possible fraud. He handles the real late payment by paying and asking for an arrangement.

**Case 2. The balance that wasn't updated.** Mar paid off a card, but the report shows a 900 balance. She checks the report date: it was generated before her payment. She waits for the next update before disputing.

### Interactive activity

**Sample report (H5P):** find four items that need action and choose the right route for each one.

### Quiz

1. Does checking your own report lower your score? a) Yes · b) No
2. Is a real late payment an error that can be erased? a) Yes · b) No
3. What do you send as proof in a dispute? a) The original · b) A copy

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-b · 3-b.

### Practice

Your report shows a 900 balance on an account that your closing statement shows at zero. Write the request.

**Model answer:** identify the account by its last digits, describe the difference, attach a copy of the statement with sensitive data covered and ask them to investigate and correct it.

> **Before you act, check:** that you are using the official site AnnualCreditReport.com and not a page that charges or asks for card information.

### For your plan

Record the date, finding, proof and next review. Do not give your full report to the course.

### Learn more

Free, official resources to go further (verified September 28, 2026):

- **Free credit reports** (AnnualCreditReport.com; language: EN): https://www.annualcreditreport.com
- **Credit reports and scores** (CFPB; language: ES/EN): https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/credit-reports-and-scores/

**Key words:** *Credit report:* record of your accounts and payments. *Score:* a number calculated with a model. *Dispute:* a request to correct incorrect information. *Reference number:* tracking number.

## M3 U03. How do I start if I have no credit history?

**What you will achieve:** compare options to build credit history without taking on too much debt.

### The essentials

A **secured card** requires a deposit that backs your limit. That deposit does not pay for your purchases: you must pay them.

1. Ask whether it reports to the credit agencies.
2. Calculate how much money will be locked up.
3. Check whether you can pay what you use without touching money for your needs.

> **Key idea:** having no history does not mean being irresponsible. Many people arrive in the U.S. without a history or use cash.

### Go deeper

**Options to get started:**

| Option | How it works | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Secured card | A deposit backs the limit; you pay your purchases | Does it report? Annual fee? When is the deposit returned? |
| Credit builder loan | You make payments and receive the money at the end | Total cost, when it is released, who it reports to |
| Savings-secured loan | Your savings back the loan | Locked money, interest |
| Lending circle | A group that lends without interest and reports to the bureaus (M3 U09) | Organization that runs it |
| Authorized user | Someone adds you to their card | Their behavior affects you; in many cases you are not legally responsible |
| Co-signer | You sign and answer for someone else's debt | It is your obligation if they do not pay |

**With an ITIN:** several credit unions and some banks offer cards and loans with an ITIN. Ask directly.

**History from another country.** Some companies let you use your credit history from another country for certain applications in the U.S. Check which countries and institutions take part.

**Watch your liquidity.** If you have a 500 reserve and a product locks up 400, you only have 100 left for emergencies. No product is right if it locks up your only rent money.

You do not need to pay interest to build history: with a card, pay the full balance every month.

### Cases

**Case 1. Mar's card.** Mar deposits 300 on a secured card and buys 40. She still has to pay that 40 according to her statement; the deposit is not deducted as if it were debit.

**Case 2. Two offers.** Offer A costs 36 a year and confirms in the contract that it reports to all three agencies. Offer B costs 120 and does not confirm that it reports. A has a lower cost and a useful, verifiable feature.

### Interactive activity

**Choose your first step (H5P decision tree):** answer questions about your documents, your reserve and your goal, and find out which options to review.

### Quiz

1. Does a secured card deposit pay for your purchases? a) Yes · b) No
2. Do you need to pay interest to build history? a) Yes · b) No
3. Does a product that does not report to the agencies build history? a) Yes · b) No

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-b · 3-b.

### Practice

You have a 500 reserve. A product requires locking up 400. There is a possible 250 emergency. What do you do?

**Answer:** the emergency would not be covered. Consider a product with a smaller deposit, waiting, or a route that does not lock up your reserve.

> **Before you act, check:** in the contract, which agencies it reports to, the total cost and when you get your deposit back.

### For your plan

Compare two products and the option of waiting to build up your reserve.

### Learn more

Free, official resources to go further (verified September 28, 2026):

- **Credit reports and scores** (CFPB; language: ES/EN): https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/credit-reports-and-scores/
- **Lending circles** (Mission Asset Fund; language: EN/ES): https://www.missionassetfund.org/lending-circles/
- **Newcomer's guides to managing money** (CFPB; language: ES/EN): https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/blog/the-newcomers-guides-to-managing-money/

**Key words:** *Credit history:* record of your credit experiences. *Secured card:* backed by a deposit. *Authorized user:* uses someone else's card. *Co-signer:* answers for someone else's debt.

## M3 U04. Does improving my credit mean erasing everything?

**What you will achieve:** choose valid actions to rebuild your history and recognize false promises.

### The essentials

No. If an item is wrong, you can dispute it. If a late payment really happened, the work is different: organize payments and look for arrangements.

1. Separate errors from accurate information.
2. Choose a valid action for each case.
3. Keep dates and responses.

> **Key idea:** no one can guarantee you a number of points or erase accurate information.

### Go deeper

**First identify the cause:** error, fraud, late payment, high balances or recent changes. Each one has a different action.

**Correcting and rebuilding are different:**

- **Correcting** seeks accuracy (disputes with proof).
- **Rebuilding** means paying on time from now on.

**A useful routine:** check due dates, make sure there are funds before automatic payments and contact the creditor before you fall behind.

**Utilization.** Balance ÷ limit on your cards. A 900 balance with a 1,500 limit = 60%. If you bring it down to 450, it is 30%. There is no magic percentage that guarantees a score.

**Different clocks:** your payment date, the report update, how long information stays and your score change all happen at different times.

**False promises.** Be wary of anyone who promises to:

- Erase all negative information.
- Raise your score by a guaranteed amount.
- Create a "new credit identity."
- Charge before doing the work.

The FTC warns that only scammers say they can remove all negative information. Everything a legitimate credit repair company does, you can do for free.

### Cases

**Case 1. The 30-day offer.** Alex is offered removal of his real late payments in 30 days for 499 dollars. He does not sign up. He reviews his information and organizes his payments.

**Case 2. Mar's utilization.** Mar has a 600 balance with a 1,000 limit (60%). She pays 300 and drops to 30%. She does not know how many points it will add, but she knows she reduced her debt.

### Interactive activity

**Legitimate or scam? (H5P cards):** read "credit repair" ads and decide which ones are warning signs.

### Quiz

1. Can a real late payment be erased because it lowers your score? a) Yes · b) No
2. Balance 240 and limit 800. Utilization? a) 30% · b) 24%
3. A company charges you before doing credit repair work. a) That is a warning sign · b) That is normal

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-a · 3-a.

### Practice

A service guarantees to erase your real late payments in 30 days. What do you do?

**Answer:** you do not sign up based on that promise. You verify your information, dispute only errors and organize your payments.

> **Before you act, check:** any credit repair company against FTC warnings and with the state regulator.

### For your plan

Choose three actions with dates: review, correct if needed and organize payments. Record results, without a guaranteed date.

### Learn more

Free, official resources to go further (verified September 28, 2026):

- **Credit reports and scores** (CFPB; language: ES/EN): https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/credit-reports-and-scores/
- **FTC consumer advice** (FTC; language: ES (EN: consumer.ftc.gov)): https://consumidor.ftc.gov

**Key words:** *Credit utilization:* balance divided by limit. *Delinquency:* not paying on time. *Collections:* efforts to collect a debt.

**Sources:** [S20] FTC, credit repair · [R21] FTC, 2022 case.

## M3 U05. Why can a small payment end up costing more?

**What you will achieve:** compare loans and cards by their total cost, not the monthly payment.

### The essentials

Offer 1: 10 a month for 10 months = 100. Offer 2: 8 a month for 15 months = 120.

1. Identify how much you receive.
2. Add up all payments and fees.
3. Check whether the payments fit your dates.

> **Key idea:** a small monthly payment can come from a longer term and a higher **total cost**.

### Go deeper

**Compare:** how much you receive, how much you pay in total, when you pay and what you risk.

**APR** is the yearly cost of credit calculated under official rules. Check which fees it includes.

**How a payment is split (amortization).** A 1,000 loan at 1% a month with payments of 100:

| Month | Opening balance | Interest | Principal paid | Closing balance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1,000.00 | 10.00 | 90.00 | 910.00 |
| 2 | 910.00 | 9.10 | 90.90 | 819.10 |

Interest is calculated on the remaining balance, not on the original 1,000.

**Credit cards:**

- **Statement closing date:** end of the statement period.
- **Payment due date:** last day to pay.
- **Minimum payment:** keeps the account current, but makes the debt last much longer.
- **Grace period:** if you pay the full balance on time, many cards do not charge interest on purchases. It usually does not apply to cash advances.
- **Deferred interest:** some promotions charge all the accumulated interest if you do not pay off in time. It is not the same as a true 0%.

**Other products:** buy now, pay later (BNPL), rent-to-own and payday loans also commit your next paychecks. Add up all your plans, not each one on its own.

**Upfront fees.** If you are lent 1,000 but 50 is withheld as a fee, you receive 950. If you repay 1,100, the cost is 150 on what you actually received.

### Cases

**Case 1. Alex's two loans.** A: receives 1,000, pays 50 upfront and 12 payments of 95 = 1,190. B: receives 1,000, no fee, 12 payments of 98 = 1,176. B costs 14 less.

**Case 2. Mar's plans.** Mar has four "pay in 4" purchases of 35 each per week. Separately they look small; together they are 140 a week.

### Interactive activity

**Amortization table (H5P):** change the amount, rate and term, and see how much interest you pay in total.

### Quiz

1. Loan A: 10 payments of 110 plus a 40 fee. B: 10 payments of 112. Which costs less? a) A (1,140) · b) B (1,120)
2. Does paying the minimum avoid interest? a) Yes · b) No
3. Is deferred interest the same as 0%? a) Yes · b) No

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-b · 3-b.

### Practice

Offer C has payments of 70 for 24 months, with no other fees, for the same 1,000. Is it cheaper?

**Answer:** 70 × 24 = 1,680. Lower payment, higher total cost.

> **Before you act, check:** the APR, the amount you actually receive, the sum of payments, late fees and whether there is a penalty for paying early.

### For your plan

Compare two offers for the same need: amount received, total, schedule, collateral and risks.

### Learn more

Free, official resources to go further (verified September 28, 2026):

- **CFPB in Spanish** (CFPB; language: ES): https://www.consumerfinance.gov/es/
- **Auto loans** (CFPB; language: ES/EN): https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/auto-loans/

**Key words:** *APR:* yearly cost of credit. *Amortization:* how the principal goes down with payments. *Minimum payment:* the least required. *Grace period:* time in which you can avoid interest.

**Sources:** [R28] CFPB, card interest · [R29] CFPB, grace period.

## M3 U06. Which debt do I start with?

**What you will achieve:** make an inventory of your debts and choose a strategy.

### The essentials

First you need to see them together.

1. Write down each debt.
2. Add up the minimum payments.
3. Compare with what you can pay. Only if money is left over do you decide where to put the extra.

> **Key idea:** a **debt inventory** keeps you from forgetting payments and lets you choose with information.

### Go deeper

**What to write down for each debt:** creditor, type, balance, rate, minimum payment, due date, late status, collateral and verified contact. Include medical, tax, family and remittance-related debts.

**In some cases the consequence matters more than the rate.** A debt that puts your housing, your work vehicle or a basic service at risk, or that is already in court, needs attention first.

**Two strategies** (after covering all minimums):

- **Avalanche:** extra money goes to the debt with the **highest rate**. Saves the most interest.
- **Snowball:** extra money goes to the debt with the **smallest balance**. You close debts faster and stay motivated.

If you cannot cover the minimums, no extra-payment strategy solves the problem: you need to negotiate or look for help (M3 U07).

**Consolidate, refinance, settle:**

- **Consolidate:** combine debts into one.
- **Refinance:** change the terms.
- **Settle for less:** can affect your history and create taxes.

A lower payment does not prove savings. Compare the total.

### Cases

**Case 1. Alex's debts.** Debt A: 300 at 30%, minimum 30. Debt B: 1,000 at 15%, minimum 50. He has 120 for debts. After the minimums (80), 40 is left. In this case both strategies choose A (highest rate and smallest balance).

**Case 2. The consolidation.** Mar is offered to lower her payment from 250 to 170, but from 12 to 24 payments. Total: 3,000 versus 4,080. The payment goes down, but it costs 1,080 more.

### Interactive activity

**Avalanche versus snowball (H5P simulator):** enter three debts and compare months and interest with each strategy.

### Quiz

1. Which strategy saves more interest? a) Avalanche · b) Snowball
2. Should you always pay the highest rate first? a) Yes · b) No, check consequences
3. Does a lower payment mean you pay less in total? a) Yes · b) Not necessarily

**Answers:** 1-a · 2-b · 3-b.

### Practice

A consolidation lowers your payment from 250 to 170, but extends it from 12 to 24 payments. What do you compare?

**Answer:** the totals: 3,000 versus 4,080, plus fees. It eases your cash flow, but costs 1,080 more.

### For your plan

Document two strategies and why you choose one. Do not plan new debt as a permanent solution.

### Learn more

Free, official resources to go further (verified September 28, 2026):

- **Money Smart for Adults** (FDIC; language: ES/EN): https://www.fdic.gov/consumer-resource-center/money-smart-adults
- **Financial education tools (includes Your Money, Your Goals)** (CFPB; language: EN, some in Spanish): https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/educator-tools/

**Key words:** *Debt inventory:* a list of all your debts. *Consolidation:* combining debts into one. *Refinancing:* replacing one debt with another.

## M3 U07. What do I do if I know I can't pay?

**What you will achieve:** prepare a documented response when you cannot keep up, and know what to do about collections or a lawsuit.

### The essentials

Ignoring the due date does not make the problem go away.

1. Calculate what amount and date are actually possible.
2. Contact the creditor through a verified channel and ask about options.
3. Keep in writing what was agreed.

> **Key idea:** asking for a change is **negotiating**; it is not accepted until they confirm it. If you receive court papers, you need legal help quickly.

### Go deeper

**Before calling, prepare:** current income, the amount you can pay without dropping your needs, for how long, and documents. Ask about options, fees, the effect on your report and how the arrangement ends. Ask for written confirmation.

**If a debt collector contacts you:**

- Verify who they are and what debt they are collecting before giving information or paying.
- Collectors covered by the law must give you debt validation information.
- You usually have **30 days** to dispute in writing; while they respond, they must stop certain collection activity.
- They cannot harass you, threaten you or lie to you.

**A dispute does not stop a lawsuit.** If you receive court papers, check the deadline to respond and look for legal help right away. If you do not respond, a judgment can be entered against you.

**Relief services are different from each other:**

| Service | What it does | Keep in mind |
|---|---|---|
| Nonprofit credit counseling | Reviews your budget and options | Ask about costs and credentials |
| Debt management plan | The agency negotiates rates and you make one monthly payment | It may close your cards |
| Debt settlement | They negotiate to pay less | Risk to your history and taxes; beware of upfront fees |
| Bankruptcy | Legal process | Requires an attorney |

### Cases

**Case 1. Mar's temporary arrangement.** Mar can pay 80 a month for three months. She asks: "Is there a temporary option, what will it cost, and how will it be put in writing?"

**Case 2. Alex's lawsuit.** Alex is disputing a debt with an agency and receives a court notice. He does not wait: he looks for a legal aid service and checks the deadline.

### Interactive activity

**Call with the collector (H5P scenario):** practice what to say, what to ask and what not to accept.

### Quiz

1. Does a dispute with an agency stop a lawsuit? a) Yes · b) No
2. Can a debt collector threaten you? a) Yes · b) No
3. Is an arrangement you asked for already accepted? a) Yes · b) No, not until they confirm

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-b · 3-b.

### Practice

You receive a court notice while you are disputing information with an agency. Do you wait for the dispute to finish?

**Answer:** no. Check the court deadline and look for legal help right away.

> **Before you act, check:** the collector's identity and the debt before paying. Look for legal help through services certified by the State Bar of California.

### For your plan

Create a file with facts, communications, options and dates. Find a support service in the directory.

### Learn more

Free, official resources to go further (verified September 28, 2026):

- **Debt collection: your rights** (CFPB; language: ES/EN): https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/debt-collection/
- **Find a lawyer and referral services** (State Bar of California; language: EN/ES): https://www.calbar.ca.gov

**Key words:** *Collections:* efforts to collect a debt. *Debt validation:* information the collector must give you. *Negotiate:* ask for a change in terms.

**Sources:** [R08] CFPB, debt collectors · [R23] State Bar of California.

## M3 U08. Can signing to help leave me with a debt?

**What you will achieve:** weigh the risks of signing, lending or borrowing for your family.

### The essentials

If you sign to answer for another person's loan, you can take on that debt (**co-signer or guarantor**).

1. Read what responsibility you take on.
2. Calculate what would happen if the other person does not pay.
3. Decide with that information.

> **Key idea:** a family promise does not change the contract with the lender.

### Go deeper

Before lending, co-signing or lending your card, ask who is responsible and what happens if the other person does not pay.

- **Co-signing** can make you responsible for the whole balance, even if you do not use the item.
- **Lending your card** leaves you responsible for what is spent.
- **Lending money to a relative:** agree on the amount, the schedule, what happens if payments are late and how it is documented. You can also decide to give an amount you can afford, or not to lend.

**A remittance with deferred payment.** If you send 200 today and must repay 230 in a month, write down both events. If your family promises to pay you back 100, do not count that 100 until you confirm the date and their capacity. Ask yourself: if they do not pay me back, which payment is left uncovered?

**Pressure and control.** If there are threats, withholding of documents or control of your income, put your safety first (M4 U05). Saying no or negotiating a different amount is part of planning, not a lack of love.

### Cases

**Case 1. Mar's cousin.** Mar co-signs a payment of 180 a month because "my cousin will pay it." Her risk scenario must include that 180 if the contract obligates her.

**Case 2. The password.** A relative asks Alex for his password to "manage" a debt. Alex suggests reviewing the information together without sharing credentials.

### Interactive activity

**Do I sign or not? (H5P scenario):** three relatives ask you for help; decide and see the consequences.

### Quiz

1. Can co-signing make you responsible for the whole debt? a) Yes · b) No
2. Does a family promise change the contract with the bank? a) Yes · b) No
3. Should you share your password to help? a) Yes · b) No

**Answers:** 1-a · 2-b · 3-b.

### For your plan

Write what support you can take on, what information is missing and what you will do if your capacity changes.

## M3 U09. Can a tanda help me build credit?

**What you will achieve:** compare the traditional tanda with lending circles that report to the credit bureaus, and use both with less risk.

### The essentials

In a **tanda**, each member contributes a fixed amount and, taking turns, each one receives the total.

1. Write down how much you contribute, how often and which turn you receive.
2. Add up how much you will contribute in total.
3. Ask yourself what happens if someone stops paying.

> **Key idea:** a traditional tanda is saving and lending among people you trust, but it **does not build credit history**. A **lending circle** run by an organization can report your payments to the agencies.

### Go deeper

**How a tanda works.** 10 people contribute 100 every week. Each week one person receives 1,000. Whoever receives first gets an interest-free loan; whoever receives last saves.

**Risks:**

- Someone who already received stops paying.
- The organizer disappears.
- You get a late turn and need the money earlier.
- There is no contract or protection if something fails.

**To reduce risks:** take part with people you trust, write down the turns and contributions, and do not contribute money you need for the basics.

**Lending circles that report to the bureaus.** Some nonprofits, such as Mission Asset Fund in California, organize interest-free lending circles that report payments to the credit agencies. That way, what you already do in a tanda also helps you build history.

**Compare:**

| | Traditional tanda | Organized lending circle |
|---|---|---|
| Interest | No | Usually no |
| Contract | Informal | Yes |
| Reports to bureaus | No | Yes, in many programs |
| If someone does not pay | The others lose | The organization has rules |

### Cases

**Case 1. Mar's tanda.** Mar is in a 10-person tanda, 100 a week, and her turn is number 8. In week 5 she needs money for an emergency. She cannot move her turn up. Next time, she will ask for an early turn or set aside a reserve.

**Case 2. Alex's circle.** Alex joins an organized lending circle. His on-time payments show up on his credit report within a few months.

### Interactive activity

## M3 U10. What is my next step with credit?

**What you will achieve:** choose your credit pathway and build a 30- to 90-day plan.

### The essentials

Having no history, having an error and having late payments are different situations.

1. Describe your situation without judging yourself.
2. Choose the action that fits.
3. Give it a date and a way to check it.

> **Key idea:** reviewing your report is a completed action even if your score does not change right away.

### Go deeper

**Choose your pathway:**

| Situation | First step |
|---|---|
| No history | An eligible product that reports, without locking up your reserve (U03, U09) |
| Error on the report | A specific dispute with proof (U02) |
| High balances | Affordability and utilization (U04, U06) |
| Late payment or collections | Verify the debt and negotiate or seek counseling (U07) |
| Stable situation | Keep paying on time and review every year |

Opening a card is not the universal answer.

**Connect report, contract and calendar.** Record the opening balance, interest, payments, new purchases and closing balance. A lower balance can come from a payment, an adjustment or a forgiveness; check before crediting it to your strategy.

**Review at 30 and 90 days:** action taken, response received, cost, obstacle and next step. Asking for help in time is a skill, not a failure.

### Cases

**Case 1. Alex's balance.** Opening balance 1,000; interest 20; new purchases 100; payment 150. Closing balance 970. He paid 150, but the debt only went down 30 because of other activity.

**Case 2. Mar's pathway.** Mar has no history. Week 1: she asks two credit unions about cards with an ITIN. Week 3: she compares costs. Day 30: she chooses one or decides to wait and build up her reserve.
