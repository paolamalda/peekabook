## M5 U01. How do I turn a wish into a goal?

**What you will achieve:** measure your net worth and set goals with an amount, a date and a priority, without counting the same money twice.

### The essentials

You want to pay for something that costs 120 in 6 months and have nothing set aside.

1. Write what it costs: 120.
2. Subtract what you already have set aside only for that goal: 0.
3. Divide by the months: 120 ÷ 6 = 20 a month. Check that it fits your budget.

> **Key idea:** a goal has an **amount, a date, a currency and a priority**. The same dollar cannot pay for two goals.

### Go deeper

**Net worth** = what you own − what you owe, on a given date. Not everything you own can be turned into cash quickly: a house, a car and a savings account have different **liquidity**.

| Assets | | Debts | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash | 800 | Car loan | 5,500 |
| Savings | 1,200 | Credit card | 1,000 |
| Car (estimated value) | 7,000 | | |
| **Total** | **9,000** | **Total** | **6,500** |

Net worth: 2,500. But the money you can use tomorrow is only 2,000.

Use realistic values and mark estimates. Sentimental value is not a selling price. Negative net worth describes a situation, not a person's worth.

**From wish to goal:** "I want a house" is a wish. "I want to save an estimated down payment and closing costs in five years" lets you research.

**Competing goals.** If you have 80 a month and your goals need 150, change amounts, dates or priorities. Do not assign the same 80 to every goal.

### Cases

**Case 1. Mar's course.** Mar wants a 600 course in 12 months without debt: 50 a month. She already has 180 set aside only for it: 420 to go, or 35 a month.

**Case 2. Alex's net worth.** Alex and Mar have a net worth of 2,500, but if a 2,300 bill arrives tomorrow, they cannot use the car to pay it.

### Interactive activity

**My goals (H5P organizer):** arrange three goals by priority and see whether they fit your monthly cushion.

### Quiz

1. Assets 12,000 and debts 8,500. Net worth? a) 3,500 · b) 20,500
2. Is net worth available cash? a) Yes · b) No
3. Can you assign the same 80 to three goals? a) Yes · b) No

**Answers:** 1-a · 2-b · 3-b.

### For your plan

Write three goals and separate the one that needs money soon from the long-term one.

### Learn more

- **Financial well-being questionnaire and tools** (CFPB; language: ES/EN): https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/financial-well-being/

**Key words:** *Net worth:* assets minus debts. *Liquidity:* how quickly you can use a resource. *Contribution:* money you add to a goal.

## M5 U02. If I have more money, did my investment earn it?

**What you will achieve:** choose between saving and investing based on time frame, risk and access, and calculate returns without fooling yourself.

### The essentials

You start with 100, add 20 of your own and end up with 125.

1. Write down the starting 100.
2. Separate the 20 you contributed.
3. Subtract: 125 − 100 − 20 = 5. That is what the investment earned.

> **Key idea:** what you contribute is not a gain. And having more dollars does not always mean you can buy more.

### Go deeper

**Saving and investing.** Saving means setting money aside. Investing means putting it into assets that can go up or down. The decision depends on when you need it, how much you could lose and how quickly you need to use it. Being able to handle a drop emotionally is not the same as being able to pay the rent if it happens.

**Where a gain comes from.** You buy an investment for 1,000. In one year it pays you 40 and at the end it is worth 1,030.

- Income return: 40 ÷ 1,000 = 4%.
- Price change: 30 ÷ 1,000 = 3%.
- Gross total return: 70, that is, 7%.
- If there were 15 in fees and 10 in taxes (exercise data): net gain 45, net return 4.5%.

**Real return.** With a 4.5% net return and 3% inflation: 1.045 ÷ 1.03 − 1 = 1.46%. That is the increase in your purchasing power.

**Receiving payments does not guarantee a gain.** If the investment ends up worth 900 and paid 40, you lost 60 (−6%).

**Averages that mislead.** One year +20% and another −20% average zero, but 1,000 → 1,200 → 960: you lost 4%. To recover from a 20% drop you need to gain 25%.

**Comparing periods.** 2% in three months is not 2% a year. From 1,000 to 1,210 in two years is 21% cumulative and 10% compounded annually.

**Diversifying** spreads risk, but does not eliminate losses. Having many investments in the same sector is not diversifying.

**Fees.** Small fees add up over the years and reduce what is left to grow.

**For a near-term need** (less than 1 or 2 years), access and stability matter more than chasing a high return.

### Cases

**Case 1. Mar's 1,000.** Mar needs 1,000 in three months. An investment could go up 15% or down 20%. If it goes down, she would have 800 and would miss her goal. She chooses a savings account.

**Case 2. The balance that "grew."** Alex's retirement account went from 2,000 to 2,300, but he contributed 250. The investment earned 50.

### Interactive activity

**An investment's path (H5P simulator):** choose a time frame and risk level, and watch possible paths with ups and downs.

### Quiz

1. 1,000 at 4% for two years, with no contributions. Balance? a) 1,080 · b) 1,081.60
2. An investment goes up 10% and then down 10%. Starting at 1,000, how much is left? a) 1,000 · b) 990
3. Net return 5% and inflation 4%. Approximate real return? a) 0.96% · b) 9%

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-b · 3-a.

### For your plan

For each goal write down the time frame, liquidity, the loss you could handle and costs. Do not choose only by past returns.

### Learn more

**Key words:** *Return:* gain or loss on an investment. *Real return:* adjusted for inflation. *Diversification:* spreading risk. *Volatility:* how much a value goes up and down.

## M5 U03. Does opening an account mean I already invested?

**What you will achieve:** tell apart the account (the container) from the investment (the contents) and verify whoever sells it to you.

### The essentials

An account can hold different products, like a bag and what you put in it.

1. Identify which account you use.
2. Ask what product you actually bought.
3. Review how it can gain or lose value and how to take your money out.

> **Key idea:** the name "retirement account" does not tell you what your money is invested in.

### Go deeper

**Container and contents.** An investment or retirement account (like an IRA) is a structure with rules. Inside there can be cash, stocks, bonds or funds. Opening the account does not mean the money is already invested.

**Basic products:**

| Product | What it is | Main risks |
|---|---|---|
| Deposit or certificate of deposit (CD) | Money at a bank for a set term | Early withdrawal penalty |
| Bond | A loan to a company or government | The issuer may not pay; the price changes |
| Stock | A share of a company | Value can go up or down a lot |
| Fund | A managed group of investments | Fees; can be concentrated |
| ETF | A fund bought and sold on the stock exchange | Same as a fund |
| Crypto assets | Speculative digital assets | Sharp drops, fraud, custody |

**Verify before investing:** the firm and the professional in FINRA BrokerCheck or the SEC adviser database; who holds your investments; all fees; and how you take your money out. Ask whether the money goes to the institution or to the seller's personal account.

**Being registered does not guarantee gains.** Custody protection (SIPC) does not protect against market drops.

**Warning signs:** "high, guaranteed returns," gains shown on social media, pressure to decide fast and payments only in cryptocurrency.

### Cases

**Case 1. The IRA in cash.** Alex opened an IRA a year ago and his money is still in cash because he never chose investments. The account did not grow as he expected.

**Case 2. The two funds.** Mar bought two "technology" funds that hold almost the same companies. She thought she was diversifying, but she doubled her exposure.

### Interactive activity

**Container or contents? (H5P sorting):** separate accounts from products, and match each product with its main risk.

### Quiz

1. Is an IRA the same as a stock? a) Yes · b) No
2. Is holding two similar funds diversifying? a) Yes · b) Not necessarily
3. Where do you check a broker? a) BrokerCheck · b) Their social media

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-b · 3-a.

### For your plan

Fill in the investment sheet: account, product, goal, risk, cost, access and verified firm. If you cannot explain it, write down what you need to learn before signing up.

### Learn more

Free, official resources to go further (verified September 28, 2026):

- **Check a broker or adviser** (FINRA BrokerCheck; language: EN): https://brokercheck.finra.org
- **Investor.gov in Spanish** (SEC; language: ES): https://www.investor.gov/informacion-en-espanol

**Key words:** *Retirement account:* a structure with tax and withdrawal rules. *ETF:* a fund traded on the stock exchange. *Credit risk:* the issuer may not pay. *Custody:* who holds your investments.

**Sources:** [S24] SEC, investment products · [R31] SEC, diversification · [R32] SEC, bonds.

## M5 U04. Rent or buy? What rights do I have as a tenant?

**What you will achieve:** know your basic rights as a tenant in California and the real costs of buying a home, including with an ITIN.

### The essentials

A 1,800 rent and a 1,800 mortgage payment do not cost the same. Buying has extra costs, and renting comes with rights you should know.

1. Write down all the costs of each option, not just the monthly payment.
2. Review your rights as a tenant.
3. If you are thinking of buying, talk with a certified housing counselor.

> **Key idea:** paying rent is not "throwing money away," and buying is not always better. It depends on the time frame, stability and costs.

### Go deeper

**Your rights as a tenant in California** (check whether your home is exempt and the current rules):

- **Rent increase cap:** many homes have a yearly cap (5% plus local inflation, up to a maximum of 10%). Some are exempt, such as many houses owned by an individual and newer buildings.
- **Just cause eviction:** in many homes, the landlord needs a valid reason to end the tenancy.
- **Security deposit:** in general, at most the equivalent of one month's rent (some small landlords can ask for two).
- **Your immigration status:** in California, a landlord cannot ask about or use your immigration status to rent to you or evict you.
- **Repairs:** the landlord must keep the home livable.

**If you receive eviction papers from a court,** the deadline to respond is only a few days. Look for legal help right away; do not ignore the papers.

**Keep:** your lease, rent receipts, photos of the home's condition when you moved in and all messages with the landlord.

**Buying: full costs.**

| Cost | What it is |
|---|---|
| Down payment | Percentage of the price you pay upfront |
| Closing costs | Paperwork, appraisal, insurance, fees |
| Mortgage | Principal and interest |
| Property tax | Yearly, based on value |
| Homeowners insurance | Required with a mortgage |
| Maintenance | Repairs (worth setting aside each month) |
| HOA dues | In some developments |

**Buying with an ITIN.** Some lenders offer ITIN mortgages. They usually require a larger down payment and have higher rates. Compare at least two options and check the total cost.

**Housing counselor.** A HUD-certified counselor can guide you for free or at low cost to prepare for a purchase or avoid foreclosure.

### Cases

**Case 1. The rent increase.** Mar's landlord gives notice of a 15% increase. Mar checks whether her building has a rent cap. Since it does, the allowed increase is lower and she tells the landlord in writing.

**Case 2. Alex's dream.** Alex wants to buy a house with an ITIN. A housing counselor helps him calculate the down payment, closing costs, taxes and insurance: he needs 18 more months of saving and to work on his credit.

### Interactive activity

**Rent or buy? (H5P comparer):** enter rent, purchase price, down payment, rate and taxes, and compare the real monthly cost and the five-year cost.

### Quiz

1. Can a landlord in California ask about your immigration status? a) Yes · b) No
2. Does a 1,800 mortgage cost the same as 1,800 rent? a) Yes · b) No, there are more costs
3. If you receive eviction papers from the court, what do you do? a) Wait · b) Look for legal help right away

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-b · 3-b.

### For your plan

Keep your lease and receipts. If you want to buy, schedule an appointment with a certified housing counselor.

### Learn more

Free, official resources to go further (verified September 28, 2026):

- **2026 guide to tenants' and landlords' rights** (Departamento de Bienes Raíces de California; language: EN): https://www.dre.ca.gov/publications/ResourceGuidebook/2026_Landlord_Tenant_Guide.pdf
- **Find a housing counselor** (CFPB / HUD; language: EN): https://www.consumerfinance.gov/find-a-housing-counselor/
- **Buying a house** (CFPB; language: ES/EN): https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/

**Key words:** *Down payment:* the upfront payment when buying. *Closing costs:* expenses of the purchase. *Mortgage:* a loan to buy a home. *Rent cap:* the maximum allowed increase.

**Sources:** [R44] California, tenant rights guide · [R22] CFPB, housing counselors.

## M5 U05. Is the advertised price all it costs?

**What you will achieve:** compare a car or a course by its total cost and flexibility.

### The essentials

A car may ask for a 200 monthly payment and also require insurance, gas and maintenance.

1. Write down the advertised payment.
2. Add the other costs.
3. Compare alternatives with the same list.

> **Key idea:** the **total cost of ownership** includes everything you pay to have and use something.

### Go deeper

**Car: total monthly cost.** Price, financing, insurance, gas or charging, maintenance, repairs, parking, registration and loss of value. A longer loan lowers the payment, but can leave you owing more than the car is worth.

**Leasing** has mileage limits, return conditions and fees. Do not compare only the monthly payment.

**Education.** Add tuition, materials, transportation, childcare and income you stop earning. Verify the provider and its recognition. "Guaranteed job" advertising is not guaranteed income. The benefits of studying are not only financial.

**Test with less income.** If the payment depends on steady overtime, that is a weakness in the plan.

### Cases

**Case 1. Alex's two cars.**

| | Car A | Car B |
|---|---|---|
| Payment | 280 | 340 |
| Insurance | 160 | 120 |
| Gas | 140 | 80 |
| Maintenance | 70 | 60 |
| **Total** | **650** | **600** |

The payment alone is misleading. Still, the price, term, down payment, condition of the car and loss of value are missing.

**Case 2. Mar's course.** Tuition 1,200, materials 300 and lost income 500: total cost 2,000.

### Interactive activity

**Total cost calculator (H5P):** compare two cars or two courses with all their costs.

### Quiz

1. Car A: payment 250 and other costs 320. Car B: payment 310 and other costs 230. Which costs less per month? a) A (570) · b) B (540)
2. Is a longer loan always better because the payment is lower? a) Yes · b) No
3. Is "guaranteed job" in an ad secure income? a) Yes · b) No

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-b · 3-b.

### For your plan

Compare two alternatives and the option of waiting. Write down at least five costs and one contingency.

**Key words:** *Total cost of ownership:* everything it costs to have and use something. *Depreciation:* loss of value over time.
