# Module 2. Understand the financial system and plan your remittances

**Module result:** a map of institutions, a comparison of accounts based on how you use money, three comparable remittance quotes, a sending calendar and a family agreement.

**Lessons:** 13 · **Estimated time:** 12 to 16 hours with practice.

---

## M2 U01. Who holds my money and who provides the service?

**What you will achieve:** tell apart what each institution does (bank, credit union, insurer, remittance company, app) and who is responsible if there is a problem.

### The essentials

A store sells you a card, an app shows your balance and a bank holds the money. They can be three different companies.

1. Identify what you need to do (save, pay, send, borrow, insure).
2. Look for the legal name of whoever is responsible for that service.
3. Keep its official contact information.

> **Key idea:** the logo you see is not always the responsible company. Look for the **responsible entity** in the contract.

### Go deeper

The financial system connects payments, savings, loans, investments and protection.

| Institution | What it does | Example question |
|---|---|---|
| Central bank (Federal Reserve) | Public functions: monetary policy, stability. Does not open accounts for people. | It is not where you dispute a charge. |
| Bank | Takes deposits, processes payments, lends. | Who owes me my balance? |
| Credit union | Like a bank, but owned by its members. Requires membership. | Can I become a member? |
| Insurer | Takes on risks defined in a policy. | What does it cover and what doesn't it? |
| Remittance company (money transmitter) | Sends money to other people or countries. | Who is responsible if it doesn't arrive? |
| Investment intermediary | Buys and sells investments for you. | Who holds what I bought? |
| Fintech (app) | Uses technology to provide services; sometimes through a partner bank. | Which bank holds my money? |

**How they make money.** Fees, interest, exchange-rate margins, advertising or referral payments. A recommendation can be useful and have a commercial interest at the same time. Knowing this helps you evaluate.

**A card payment step by step.** You authorize; the merchant accepts; a network processes; an institution charges your account or lends to you. The logo on the card does not by itself identify everyone responsible.

**Oversight.** Each institution is supervised according to what it does. Being registered helps confirm it exists and is authorized, but it does not make every offer good for you. Look for the exact legal name: a fake company can copy the name of a real one.

**Banks and money.** When a bank lends, it creates a deposit in the customer's account. Still, it cannot lend without limits: capital, liquidity, risk and regulation restrict it.

### Cases

**Case 1. The store card.** Mar bought a prepaid card at a pharmacy. A charge appears that she does not recognize. The pharmacy says it cannot help her. The back of the card shows the name of the issuing bank and the right phone number.

**Case 2. Four needs.** Alex needs to hold the rent money, receive payments from clients, send money to Mexico and insure his truck. These are four needs, not necessarily four institutions. For each one he writes down the service, who is responsible, cost, access and how to complain.

### Interactive activity

**Who is responsible? (H5P matching):** match each problem with the institution that should handle it.

### Quiz

1. Can you open an account at the central bank? a) Yes · b) No
2. Who should explain a policy deductible? a) The insurer · b) The central bank · c) The remittance company
3. A company is registered with a regulator. Does that guarantee its offer is right for you? a) Yes · b) No

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-a · 3-b.

### Practice

For each financial service you use, write down: brand, responsible entity, function and how to verify it.

> **Before you act, check:** the legal name in the contract and the contact on the official website, not in an ad or a message.

### For your plan

Build your services map. A logo is not enough.

**Key words:** *Responsible entity:* the company responsible for the service under the contract. *Central bank:* a public institution that does not handle personal accounts. *Fintech:* a company that uses technology for financial services. *Exchange-rate markup:* the difference between a reference rate and the rate you are offered.

**Sources:** [S06] Federal Reserve · [R01] Bank of England, money creation.

## M2 U02. Is a money app always a bank?

**What you will achieve:** compare banks, credit unions and apps by who holds your money, what protection it has and how it works in your life.

### The essentials

Not necessarily. A company can offer services using technology without being a bank (fintech). Another can be owned by its members (credit union).

1. Look for the legal name.
2. Check who holds the money and what protection it has.
3. Review how you deposit, withdraw and get help.

> **Key idea:** "digital" describes how you access it, not what license the company has.

### Go deeper

**Deposit insurance.**

- At banks insured by the **FDIC**, the general rule protects up to **250,000 dollars per depositor, per bank, per ownership category**. It is not 250,000 for each app or each account with the same ownership.
- At credit unions, the equivalent protection comes from the **NCUA**, with similar rules.
- Investments (stocks, funds, cryptocurrency) are **not** insured deposits, even if a bank sells them.
- Deposit insurance protects you if the **insured institution fails**. It does not protect against scams or the failure of an app that is not a bank.

**If you use an app:** ask which bank your money is deposited in, who keeps the record of your balance and under what conditions protection applies. Check the app's website and verify the bank in FDIC BankFind.

**Compare by function and access, not by the label:**

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| How am I paid? (cash, payroll, app) | Determines how you will deposit. |
| Is there somewhere nearby to deposit cash and what does it cost? | Many apps charge to deposit cash. |
| Do they help in Spanish? | Solving a problem in your language is part of the service. |
| What happens if I lose my phone? | A single way in can leave you without money. |
| Do they require membership or a member share? | At credit unions, the share is not always available to spend. |

**Continuity test.** Imagine you lost your phone and must pay tomorrow. Is there a physical card, a branch, a help line or secure recovery?

**Numbers comparison.** Account A charges 5 a month and includes cash deposits. Account B has no monthly fee, but each cash deposit costs 3 and you make 4 a month: 12. Per year: 60 versus 144. For you, the "free" one costs more.

### Cases

**Case 1. "We work with banks."** An app tells Mar her money is protected because it "works with banks." Mar looks in the terms for the bank's name and checks it in BankFind.

**Case 2. The 280,000.** A person has two individual accounts at the same bank: 180,000 and 100,000. That is 280,000. They should not assume it is all covered under the 250,000 limit.

### Interactive activity

**Bank, credit union or app? (H5P sorting game):** read descriptions and decide what type of institution it is and what you would verify.

### Quiz

1. Does "digital" mean it is a bank? a) Yes · b) No
2. Is an investment bought at a bank insured by the FDIC? a) Yes · b) No
3. Does deposit insurance protect against a scam? a) Yes · b) No

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-b · 3-b.

### Practice

An app says "we work with banks." What four facts do you need to know whether your money is protected?

**Answer:** the bank's name, that the bank is insured, how your ownership is recorded and the terms of the agreement between the app and the bank.

> **Before you act, check:** the bank in FDIC BankFind or the credit union in the NCUA locator, with the date you checked.

### For your plan

Keep the official lookup and its date. Mark what you could not verify.

**Key words:** *Deposit insurance:* protects eligible deposits if the institution fails. *Credit union:* a member-owned institution. *Neobank:* a marketing name for mainly digital services; check the bank behind it.

**Sources:** [S07] NCUA · [S08] FDIC, third-party apps · [S09] FDIC BankFind · [R02] FDIC, deposit insurance · [R03] NCUA, coverage.

## M2 U03. What ID can I use for financial services?

**What you will achieve:** learn which documents banks, credit unions and remittance companies usually accept, and how to get the ones you are missing safely.

### The essentials

To open an account or send money you will be asked to identify yourself. Not every institution accepts the same documents.

1. Make a list of the documents you have.
2. Ask what combination the product and channel (branch or online) accept.
3. If you are missing one, find out how to get it through the official route.

> **Key idea:** asking for your ID is the institution's legal duty to prevent fraud. What matters is finding the combination of documents it does accept.

### Go deeper

**Why they ask for ID.** Financial institutions must verify their customers' identity. For people who are not citizens, the law allows them to accept, according to their policy, a tax number (SSN or ITIN) or the number of a passport or other official ID.

**Common documents:**

| Document | What it is for | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| Passport | ID accepted almost everywhere | Expiration date |
| Consular ID (matrícula consular) | ID issued by the Mexican consulate; many institutions accept it | Not all; sometimes they ask for another ID too |
| California AB 60 driver's license | License for people who cannot prove lawful presence | Not valid for federal purposes (not REAL ID) |
| ITIN | Tax number; some institutions accept it instead of an SSN | Not a photo ID or a work permit |
| Proof of address | Utility bills, lease | Must be in your name or come with another document |

**How to get documents safely:**

- **Consular ID and Mexican passport:** only at the consulate, with an official appointment (MiConsulado). Be wary of anyone selling appointments.
- **AB 60 license:** at the California DMV, with an appointment on its official website.
- **ITIN:** with IRS Form W-7 (see M1 U11).

**Ask the bank before you go:** "What combination of documents do you accept to open this account at a branch if I don't have an SSN?" Ask for the answer in writing or write down the name of the person who helped you.

**If you are turned down,** ask for the reason. If it is due to a banking history report, you have the right to know which company issued it and to request a free copy.

**Protect your documents.** Do not hand originals to anyone who is not the official institution. Do not send photos of your ID by message to strangers.

### Cases

**Case 1. Mar and the branch account.** Mar has a passport and a consular ID, but no SSN or ITIN. She calls two banks and a credit union. One accepts her combination of documents at a branch; another requires an SSN; the credit union accepts it if she becomes a member.

**Case 2. The appointment for sale.** Someone offers Alex a "fast" consulate appointment for 80 dollars. Alex knows appointments are free in the official system and does not pay.

### Interactive activity

**My document kit (H5P checklist):** check the documents you have and the system tells you what to ask each type of institution.

### Quiz

1. Is the AB 60 license a REAL ID? a) Yes · b) No
2. Is an ITIN a photo ID? a) Yes · b) No
3. Where do you get a consular ID? a) At any store · b) At the consulate, with an official appointment

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-b · 3-b.

### Practice

Write the exact question you would ask a bank by phone before going to open an account without an SSN.

**Suggested answer:** "I'd like to open a checking account at a branch. I don't have an SSN; I have a passport and a consular ID. Which documents do you accept and how much do I need to open it?"

> **Before you act, check:** the requirements for the specific product and branch; they vary by institution and channel.

### For your plan

Build your document kit: what you have, what is about to expire and what you still need to get.

**Key words:** *Consular ID:* identification issued by the Mexican consulate. *AB 60:* California driver's license for people who cannot prove lawful presence. *REAL ID:* identification valid for federal purposes.

**Sources:** [R14] Wells Fargo, identification · [R42] California DMV, AB 60.

## M2 U04. What do I need to open an account and what does it cost to keep it?

**What you will achieve:** compare accounts by your need, your documents and how you use money.

### The essentials

An account requires 25 to open and charges 5 a month unless you meet a condition.

1. Ask which documents it accepts.
2. Write down the opening deposit and all fees.
3. Ask what condition avoids the monthly fee and whether you can meet it.

> **Key idea:** the opening deposit and the **maintenance fee** are different things. Meeting one does not remove the other.

### Go deeper

**Start with how you will use it:** receiving pay, depositing cash, paying bills, keeping a reserve or saving.

**Two different balances:**

- **Opening deposit:** the minimum to open it.
- **Balance to avoid the fee:** a monthly condition, sometimes a daily average.

If opening costs 25 but avoiding the 5 fee requires keeping 500 every day, having only 25 does not avoid the fee.

**"Direct deposit" usually means payroll** or an eligible electronic deposit, not any transfer. If you are paid in cash, you may not meet that condition.

**Bank On accounts.** These are accounts certified under a national low-cost standard: no overdraft fees, low fees and basic features. Ask whether the institution has a Bank On certified account.

**Ownership:**

- **Individual:** only you.
- **Joint:** two or more owners; any of them can withdraw and all are responsible.
- **Authorized access:** someone can operate the account without owning it.

Sharing your password is not the same as giving formal authorization. Before adding someone, understand who can withdraw, close the account and receive notices.

**Keeping personal and business separate.** A second account helps you stay organized, but it does not replace bookkeeping or create a company. Using a personal account for business depends on the contract.

### Cases

**Case 1. Alex's "free" account.** The account is free with payroll direct deposit. Alex is paid by check and deposits it at the counter. For him, the account costs 12 a month.

**Case 2. The joint account.** Mar's cousin asks her to add him to her account "to help him." Mar knows her cousin could withdraw the whole balance. She prefers another solution.

### Interactive activity

**My account calculator (H5P):** enter how you are paid and how often you deposit or withdraw, and compare the yearly cost of three sample accounts.

### Quiz

1. Are the opening deposit and the minimum balance the same? a) Yes · b) No
2. An account is free with direct deposit and you are paid in cash. Can you assume it is free? a) Yes · b) No
3. Does a joint account let the other person withdraw money? a) Yes · b) No

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-b · 3-a.

### Practice

You use cash every week. What should you compare besides the monthly fee?

**Answer:** where and how much it costs to deposit cash, free ATMs, when the money is available, travel, limits and help in your language.

> **Before you act, check:** in the program's comparison matrix, each account's verification date, and the full terms on the official website.

### For your plan

Fill in the comparison sheet for two accounts. You do not need to open one to pass.

**Key words:** *Maintenance fee:* monthly charge for having the account. *Checking account:* for frequent use. *Savings account:* for saving. *Bank On:* national standard for low-cost accounts.

**Sources:** [R13] Wells Fargo Clear Access · [R15] Bank of America SafeBalance · [R16] Self-Help · [R46] Bank On, national standards.

## M2 U05. Is all the balance I see free to spend?

**What you will achieve:** use your payment methods knowing when money is available and how to dispute a charge.

### The essentials

Your screen shows 100, but you scheduled a 40 payment for tomorrow.

1. Look at the **available balance**.
2. Check the payments not yet taken out.
3. Count each commitment only once: if the 40 has not been taken out, you have 60 free.

> **Key idea:** the money you see and the money that is not already committed can be different.

### Go deeper

**Three different dates.** A transaction can be authorized today, posted tomorrow and the money available another day. A check you already see in your balance can still be returned. A scheduled transfer is not done until it runs.

**Payment methods:**

| Method | What it uses | Keep in mind |
|---|---|---|
| Debit card | Your money in the account | If money is stolen, report it quickly. |
| Credit card | A loan from the issuer | Creates debt and interest. |
| Prepaid card | Money you loaded | Check reload and withdrawal fees. |
| ACH transfer | Bank network | Can take days. |
| Wire transfer | Direct transfer | Fast and hard to reverse. |
| Person-to-person apps | Instant transfer | Very hard to recover if you were tricked. |

**An unauthorized payment and a payment you were tricked into are not the same.** If someone uses your card without permission, there are protections. If you authorized a payment because you were deceived, getting it back can be much harder. Always verify who you are paying.

**Reconciling** means comparing your record with the bank's. Mark known charges, pending ones, duplicates and unknown ones.

**Automatic payments:** set an alert a few days before. To cancel a service, cancel it with the provider, keep the confirmation and review the payment authorization.

**Switching accounts:** make a list of deposits and automatic charges, wait for pending items to post and confirm the closing and final balance.

**The overpayment check scam.** Someone deposits a check and asks you to send part back. The money appears available, but days later the check bounces and you lose what you sent.

### Cases

**Case 1. The duplicate payment.** Alex does not see a payment go through and repeats it. The next day both appear. Before repeating, he should check the payment status and the receipt.

**Case 2. Mar's reconciliation.** Opening balance 100, deposit 500, payments 320 and 60: she expected 220. The statement shows 210. Mar looks into the 10 difference: a fee for using another bank's ATM.

### Interactive activity

**What balance do I have? (H5P simulator):** move forward day by day and watch the current, pending and available balances.

### Quiz

1. Is a check you already see in your balance guaranteed? a) Yes · b) No
2. Which is harder to recover? a) An unauthorized charge · b) A payment you authorized because you were tricked
3. Is cancelling the card enough to cancel a subscription? a) Yes · b) No

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-b · 3-b.

### Practice

Your own record: opening 300, deposit 500, purchases of 120 and 80, scheduled payment of 200. The bank shows 600. How much is free?

**Answer:** 400. The 200 scheduled payment already has a purpose, even though the bank has not taken it out yet.

> **Before you act, check:** if someone asks you to send back part of a deposit, call the bank using a number you look up yourself.

### For your plan

Set up useful alerts and a weekly review. Never share passwords or codes.

**Key words:** *Available balance:* what the bank lets you use today. *Reconcile:* compare your record with the bank's. *Dispute:* a request to review an incorrect charge. *ACH:* electronic transfer network between banks.

## M2 U06. How do I support my family without deciding at the last minute?

**What you will achieve:** identify what the person receiving needs and what you can sustain, so you can plan your remittances.

### The essentials

Sending money to your family in another country is a **remittance**. To plan it you need two facts: what the person receiving needs and what you can sustain.

1. Ask for the amount, the currency and the date of the need.
2. Review your upcoming payments.
3. Agree on what you can confirm and what you cannot yet.

> **Key idea:** planning a remittance does not mean loving your family less. It means making sure the support can continue.

### Go deeper

A remittance can cover food, health, school or housing, and it is a way of showing care.

**Sort each request:**

| Type | Example | How to plan |
|---|---|---|
| Regular | Monthly food | Fixed amount and date |
| Foreseeable, not monthly | Start of the school year | Set aside in advance |
| Emergency | Hospital stay | Verify and review options |
| Project | Building a room | Goal with a deadline (M2 U12) |

**Define the need in the currency it is spent in.** If the need is a bill for 3,400 pesos, the dollars needed change with the exchange rate and fees. Thinking in pesos helps you compare better.

**Consider the person receiving:** documents, account, phone, distance to the pickup point, hours, safety and travel cost. An app that is easy for you can be hard for an older person.

**Ask without intruding:** how much really arrives and what difficulties there are, without asking for passwords or full control of their money.

**Limits and autonomy.** A sustainable agreement accepts job changes and emergencies for the person sending. If the conversation brings threats or pressure, put your safety first.

### Cases

**Case 1. Alex's mother.** She asks for 200 dollars every month for food, 120 at the start of school for her granddaughter and, this month, 400 for a hospital stay. Alex sorts them: food is regular, school is foreseeable and the hospital is a possible emergency. Before deciding, he verifies the hospital amount and the alternatives.

**Case 2. Small transfers or one big one.** Sending 75 every week is not the same as sending 300 once: the number of fees, when the family has the money and your risk of running short between transfers all change.

### Interactive activity

**Sort the requests (H5P):** sort messages from relatives into regular, foreseeable, emergency or project.

### Quiz

1. Does planning a remittance mean loving your family less? a) Yes · b) No
2. In what currency should you define a need that is paid in Mexico? a) Dollars · b) Pesos
3. Should you ask for your relative's bank password to help them? a) Yes · b) No

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-b · 3-b.

### Practice

Your family asks for 200 a month for food, 120 at the start of school and 400 for a hospital stay. Sort them and say what information is missing.

**Answer:** food, regular; school, foreseeable; hospital, possible emergency. You still need to verify the amount, urgency and alternatives, and review your capacity.

### For your plan

Make a map: person, purpose, frequency and range. You can use made-up names.

**Key words:** *Remittance:* money sent to another person or country. *Exchange rate:* the price of one currency in another. *Priorities:* what you take care of first.
