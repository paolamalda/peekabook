## M4 U07. Does having insurance mean I won't pay anything?

**What you will achieve:** read a policy and compare insurance by what it actually covers.

### The essentials

Not necessarily. You pay a **premium** to have the protection. When something happens, you pay a part first (the **deductible**). There are also **limits** and situations that are not covered (**exclusions**).

1. Identify the event you want to protect against.
2. Read what the policy pays and what you would pay.
3. Compare policies with the same coverage.

> **Key idea:** a cheaper premium does not mean it will cost you less when something happens.

### Go deeper

**Read the policy with questions:** what event does it cover? who? for how long? up to how much? what does it exclude? how much is the deductible? how do I file a claim?

**Types of insurance:**

| Insurance | What it protects | Keep in mind |
|---|---|---|
| Auto (liability) | Damage you cause to others | Required. It does not repair your own car. |
| Auto (own damage) | Your vehicle | Optional unless your loan requires it. |
| Renters | Your belongings and some liability | The landlord's insurance does not cover your things. |
| Homeowners | The house | Earthquake and flood are usually separate policies. |
| Health | Medical care | Check the network, copays and out-of-pocket maximum. |
| Life | Your dependents' needs if you die | Term or permanent, with different costs. |
| Disability | Part of your income if you cannot work | Conditions and waiting periods. |

**Auto in California.** Since 2025 the minimum liability limits are 30,000 per injured person, 60,000 per accident and 15,000 for property damage. Disclose correctly if you use your car for work (for example, app deliveries): it may require other coverage.

**Health in California.** There is a state requirement to have coverage, with exemptions. Check the doctor network, covered services, authorizations, copays, coinsurance and the **out-of-pocket maximum**.

**Calculating a health event (example):** allowed service 2,000; remaining deductible 500; coinsurance 20% of the rest. You pay 500 + 20% of 1,500 = 500 + 300 = 800.

**Comparing premiums and deductibles:**

| | Monthly premium | Deductible | Yearly premium |
|---|---|---|---|
| Policy A | 70 | 1,500 | 840 |
| Policy B | 100 | 500 | 1,200 |

B costs 360 more a year but lowers the deductible by 1,000. Which is better depends on whether you could pay the deductible and how likely the event is.

**Verify** the insurer and the agent with the California Department of Insurance (CDI).

### Cases

**Case 1. Alex's cheap policy.** A policy costs 20 less a month, but excludes using the car for work. Alex makes deliveries. For him, that policy does not work.

**Case 2. The apartment break-in.** Mar's TV is stolen. The building owner's insurance does not cover her belongings. Now she compares renters insurance.

### Interactive activity

**Event calculator (H5P):** change the premium, deductible and coinsurance and see how much you pay in a year with and without an event.

### Quiz

1. Does liability insurance repair your car? a) Yes · b) No
2. Does the building owner's insurance cover your belongings? a) Yes · b) No
3. Service 1,500, deductible 300, coinsurance 25%. How much do you pay? a) 600 · b) 375

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-b · 3-a (300 + 25% of 1,200).

### For your plan

Write down your risks, what you already have covered, what is missing and a question for a licensed agent.

### Learn more

Free, official resources to go further (verified September 28, 2026):

- **Insurance guides and complaints** (Departamento de Seguros de California; language: EN/ES): https://www.insurance.ca.gov
- **Covered California health plans** (Covered California; language: EN/ES): https://www.coveredca.com

**Key words:** *Premium:* what you pay for insurance. *Deductible:* what you pay before the insurance pays. *Coinsurance:* the percentage you pay after the deductible. *Exclusion:* what is not covered.

**Sources:** [S21] CDI · [R10] DMV, insurance requirements · [R11] FTB, health coverage mandate.

## M4 U08. Is this medical document asking me to pay?

**What you will achieve:** review medical bills, ask for financial assistance and file a complaint if something is wrong.

### The essentials

One document can explain how your insurance processed a visit (**explanation of benefits**). Another asks you to pay (**bill**).

1. Check the name, date and service.
2. Compare amounts and what has already been paid.
3. Ask for an explanation of any differences before paying.

> **Key idea:** if they describe the same service, they are not two debts. And **never delay urgent care** because of a money worry.

### Go deeper

**Three different documents:**

- **Policy or plan information:** what it covers.
- **Explanation of benefits (EOB):** how it was processed; it is not a bill.
- **Bill:** what you are asked to pay.

**Compare:** patient, date, service, amount charged, adjustments, what the plan paid and what you owe.

**If there is a difference:** ask for an itemized bill, check the coverage and ask about payment options.

**Financial assistance at California hospitals.** Hospitals must have a financial assistance policy and offer free or discounted care to low- and middle-income people who qualify. Ask about it **before** paying or putting the bill on a credit card.

**Emergencies.** Emergency Medi-Cal can cover emergency care for people who qualify by income, regardless of immigration status.

**Medical debt and your credit.** In California, a law in effect since 2025 generally prohibits reporting medical debt to the credit agencies. Check for exceptions and changes.

**Complaints.** The DMHC handles complaints about certain health plans in California, including independent medical review. Other plans have another regulator: find out which is yours.

### Cases

**Case 1. The 300 bill.** Mar's explanation of benefits says she owes 150, but the bill asks for 300 for the same service. Mar asks for clarification before paying.

**Case 2. Alex's hospital stay.** Alex receives a 4,000 bill. He asks about the hospital's financial assistance policy, provides proof of income and his bill is reduced to 400 with a payment plan.

### Interactive activity

**Bill or explanation? (H5P comparison):** compare two documents for the same service and find the difference.

### Quiz

1. Is the explanation of benefits a bill? a) Yes · b) No
2. Must California hospitals have a financial assistance policy? a) Yes · b) No
3. Do you delay urgent care to finish the exercise? a) Yes · b) Never

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-a · 3-b.

### For your plan

Keep the contacts for your plan, your doctors and the regulator. Ask for the financial assistance policy of the nearest hospital.

### Learn more

Free, official resources to go further (verified September 28, 2026):

- **Hospital Fair Billing Program** (HCAI de California; language: EN): https://hcai.ca.gov/affordability/hospital-fair-billing-program/
- **File a complaint about your health plan** (DMHC; language: EN/ES): https://www.dmhc.ca.gov/FileaComplaint.aspx
- **Medi-Cal** (DHCS de California; language: EN/ES): https://www.dhcs.ca.gov

**Key words:** *Explanation of benefits:* summary of how a visit was processed. *Financial assistance:* a discount or free care based on income. *DMHC:* regulator of certain health plans in California.

**Sources:** [R12] DMHC · [R52] California HCAI, hospital fair billing.

## M4 U09. How do I pay for the essentials if I can't work for a few days?

**What you will achieve:** prepare your finances for a disaster, an accident or a temporary loss of income.

### The essentials

A disruption can take away income and add expenses at the same time.

1. Write down what income could be missing.
2. Write down new expenses and the ones that continue.
3. Review available money and confirmed help.

> **Key idea:** a **contingency plan** prepares you without having to guess what will happen.

### Go deeper

**Physical safety first.** In a fire, evacuation or accident, follow emergency instructions and then organize your resources.

**What to prepare:**

- Important contacts and a copy outside your phone.
- Essential documents in a safe place and a copy somewhere else.
- An inventory of your belongings with photos.
- An alternative way to pay.
- Your dependents' needs (medicine, school, care).

**Insurance and disasters.** Review deductibles and exclusions before something happens. Earthquake and flood usually require separate policies.

**After a disaster,** fake fundraisers and opportunistic contractors appear. Verify anyone asking for donations and anyone offering repairs.

**Do not count unconfirmed help.** An insurance payout or government aid is only a resource once it is confirmed.

### Cases

**Case 1. Two weeks without work.** Alex gets hurt. He loses 900 in income; temporary relocation costs 200, but he saves 50 on transportation: net effect 1,050. His reserve is 400: 650 short. He identifies payments coming due, confirmed help and who to call.

**Case 2. The phone lost in the evacuation.** All the family's information was on Mar's phone. A secure copy and an alternate contact would have helped recover accounts and documents.

### Interactive activity

**Pack the bag (H5P game):** choose which documents, contacts and resources you would take in an evacuation with limited time.

### Quiz

1. Does an unconfirmed payout count as a resource? a) Yes · b) No
2. Does homeowners insurance usually cover earthquakes? a) Yes · b) It is usually a separate policy
3. Do you keep the only copy of your documents at home? a) Yes · b) No, also somewhere else safe

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-b · 3-b.

### For your plan

List six parts of your continuity folder and who knows where it is.

### Learn more

Free, official resources to go further (verified September 28, 2026):

- **Prepare for emergencies** (Ready.gov; language: ES (EN: ready.gov)): https://www.ready.gov/es
- **Insurance guides and complaints** (Departamento de Seguros de California; language: EN/ES): https://www.insurance.ca.gov

**Key words:** *Contingency plan:* steps prepared to respond to a problem. *Inventory of belongings:* a list with photos of your things.

## M4 U10. What happens to my family and my money if I can't be there?

**What you will achieve:** build a family preparedness plan: who takes care of your children, where your documents are and how bills get paid if you cannot do it.

### The essentials

A detention, a hospital stay or an emergency trip can separate you from your family overnight.

1. Decide who would take care of your children and talk with that person.
2. Gather important documents in a place your family knows.
3. Leave instructions for paying rent and bills.

> **Key idea:** a plan made calmly protects your family in a moment of crisis. **Preparing is not giving up**; it is taking care.

### Go deeper

**1. Children's care.**

- Choose a trusted person and a backup. Talk with them.
- Update your children's school emergency card with those people.
- California has the **Caregiver's Authorization Affidavit**. It lets an adult enroll your children in school and authorize certain medical care. It does **not** change custody.
- For broader decisions (guardianship), consult a family law attorney.

**2. Documents.** Gather originals and copies in a safe place:

- Birth certificates and passports for everyone.
- If your children were born in the U.S., consider getting their U.S. passports and registering their dual nationality at the consulate.
- Health insurance cards, prescriptions and health information.
- Leases, vehicle titles, policies.
- If you have an immigration case in process: case number and your attorney's or accredited representative's contact.

**3. Money and accounts.**

- List of accounts, automatic payments and dates (no passwords or full numbers).
- Who will be able to access your money and how. A joint account, an authorized user or a power of attorney give different powers (M5 U08). Sharing your password does not give legal authorization.
- How rent will be paid and who to notify.
- A small reserve the caregiver can use.

**4. Contacts.** Family, school, doctor, attorney or legal aid organization, consulate and employer.

**5. Review.** Every six months or when something changes.

**Important:** this course does not give immigration advice. To learn your rights and options, go to an attorney or an organization with accredited representatives (M4 U02).

### Cases

**Case 1. Alex and Mar's plan.** They choose Mar's sister as caregiver and a neighbor as backup. They fill out the affidavit, keep a folder with the documents and a sheet with the month's payments. Mar's sister knows where it is.

**Case 2. A password is not enough.** A family thought sharing the bank password was enough. When the account holder was hospitalized, the bank did not allow anyone else to make transactions. They needed formal authorization.

### Interactive activity

**My family plan (H5P guided checklist):** go through the five sections and mark what you already have, what is missing and the date to complete it.

### Quiz

1. Does the Caregiver's Authorization Affidavit change custody? a) Yes · b) No
2. Does sharing your password give legal authorization? a) Yes · b) No
3. How often should you review the plan? a) Never · b) Every six months or if something changes

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-b · 3-b.

### For your plan

Complete the five sections of the plan (you can use made-up data for the course). Keep real documents only in your private folder.

### Learn more

Free, official resources to go further (verified September 28, 2026):

- **Caregiver's Authorization Affidavit** (Tribunales de California; language: EN): https://courts.ca.gov/documents/caregiver.pdf
- **Recognized organizations and accredited representatives roster** (DOJ EOIR; language: EN): https://www.justice.gov/eoir/recognized-organizations-and-accredited-representatives-roster-state-and-city
- **Prepare for emergencies** (Ready.gov; language: ES (EN: ready.gov)): https://www.ready.gov/es

**Key words:** *Family preparedness plan:* instructions so your family can carry on if you cannot be there. *Caregiver's Authorization Affidavit:* a California document so an adult can handle a minor's school and certain medical matters. *Power of attorney:* a document that authorizes another person to act for you.

**Sources:** [R39] California Courts, caregiver authorization · [R38] DOJ EOIR, accredited representatives.

## M4 U11. How do I know if my protection plan works?

**What you will achieve:** bring together prevention, protection and response, and test your plan with a drill.

### The essentials

Imagine you lose your phone and rent is due tomorrow.

1. Choose a situation.
2. Explain each step in order.
3. Mark what you still could not do and how to fix it.

> **Key idea:** a plan works if you can use it, not if it looks complete on paper.

### Go deeper

**A protection plan connects:** risk → impact → control → action.

Start with what could affect your housing, income, health or safety. Do not try to buy every insurance: identify gaps and decide what you can prevent, set aside for, transfer (with insurance) or handle with support.

**For each risk define:** the warning sign, the safe immediate action, the verified channel and the follow-up.

**Tabletop drill:**

1. You lose your phone and rent is due tomorrow. How do you recover access? What information do you need and where is it?
2. An unexpected medical bill arrives. Which steps change?
3. A fake family emergency arrives when your income is down. Which parts of the plan do you use?

A plan that only works for one emergency needs adjusting.

**Review it** when your job, home, phone, vehicle or dependents change.

### Cases

**Case 1. Alex's risk.** Risk: losing hours due to an accident. Controls: some work coverage and a 300 reserve. Gap: basic expenses of 1,800 a month. Action: verify the coverage and support, and build a scenario; do not assume 300 solves the risk.

### Interactive activity

**Drill (H5P branching scenario):** live through three emergencies in a row and use your plan to respond.

### Quiz

1. Does a protection plan require buying every insurance? a) Yes · b) No
2. A fake emergency arrives when your income is down. Which parts do you use? a) Only the reserve · b) Verification, calendar, reserve and remittance agreement

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-b.

### For your plan: submit portfolio E4

Submit: five priority risks, a comparison of two coverages, an incident protocol, the index of your continuity folder and your family preparedness plan, without sensitive data. Your reasoning is assessed, not whether you bought products. Also solve **integrative case E4** at the end of the manual.

### Learn more

Free, official resources to go further (verified September 28, 2026):

- **Identity theft: report and recovery plan** (FTC; language: ES/EN (robodeidentidad.gov)): https://www.identitytheft.gov
- **Prepare for emergencies** (Ready.gov; language: ES (EN: ready.gov)): https://www.ready.gov/es

**Key words:** *Gap:* the difference between your risk and your protection. *Drill:* practicing a made-up situation.
