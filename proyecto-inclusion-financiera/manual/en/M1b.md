## M1 U08. How do I decide what my money is for?

**What you will achieve:** build a budget that works with your real resources and can be adjusted if your life changes.

### The essentials

You have 100. You set aside 60 for food and transportation, 30 for a payment and 10 for another need.

1. Write down what you actually have.
2. Write down what you need to cover.
3. Add up the uses: 60 + 30 + 10 = 100. If you need 120, you are 20 short.

> **Key idea:** a **budget** is the plan for where your money will go. It does not require money left over: if you are short, it must show it so you can look for a solution.

### Go deeper

A useful budget recognizes your limits. If your income does not cover the basics, writing "mandatory savings" does not create the money.

**Order of priorities:**

1. Housing, food, health, childcare and transportation to work.
2. Obligations (debts, taxes) and what happens if you do not pay them.
3. Remittances and agreed family support.
4. Reserves and goals.

No percentage works for every household. Use your real numbers and, if you like, describe them afterwards as percentages.

**Structures you can use:**

- **By category:** how much goes to each type of expense.
- **Zero-based:** you give every dollar a job (including savings). It does not mean spending everything.
- **Envelopes:** physical or digital, to keep limits separate.

**Expenses that are not monthly.** If a 240 annual payment is due in 6 months and you have set nothing aside, you need 40 a month until that date. Dividing by 12 (20 a month) would leave you short. After you pay, 20 a month is enough for the next year.

Tell a known expense apart from an emergency: car registration, school supplies or renewing a document can be planned for.

**Two budgets:** one with your usual income and one with less income (for example, 20% less). For each adjustment write what you will do and for how long: "reduce this category for one month," "ask about this support," "ask to review this debt."

**If there is a deficit,** order the actions:

1. Protect basic needs.
2. Review support and rights.
3. Negotiate obligations.
4. Cut expenses that can be replaced.
5. Look for realistic extra income, subtracting its costs.

Do not fill the budget with made-up income or call paying the gap with a credit card "savings." If the deficit continues, documenting it helps you ask for specific help. Pinpointing the gap is already progress.

### Cases

**Case 1. Alex and Mar's budget.** Income 2,400; basic expenses 1,800; debt 200; remittance 250; reserves 150. Total: 2,400. It comes out to zero. If income drops 20% (to 1,920), they are 480 short to keep everything.

**Case 2. Is the subscription the problem?** Cancelling a 15 subscription leaves a 465 gap. The problem requires reviewing bigger decisions and seeking support, not blaming the subscription.

### Interactive activity

**Digital envelopes (H5P):** split a sample income into envelopes. Then the simulator lowers the income 20% and you decide what to adjust.

### Quiz

1. Does a budget require money left over? a) Yes · b) No
2. A 240 payment is due in 6 months and you have set nothing aside. How much per month? a) 20 · b) 40
3. Does zero-based budgeting mean spending everything? a) Yes · b) No, it means giving every dollar a job

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-b · 3-b.

### Practice

Using Case 1 with the reduced income, write three adjustments and how much each one contributes. Are they enough?

**Suggested answer:** for example, pausing the reserve (150), agreeing with the family on a smaller remittance for two months (100) and asking for a debt payment plan (100) add up to 350. There is still a 130 gap: it is time to look for support and review basic expenses. A valid answer can show that a solution is still missing.

> **Before you act, check:** the requirements of any support before counting it as income.

### For your plan

Build your usual budget and one with 20% less income. Write one immediate change, one conversation and one source of help.

**Key words:** *Budget:* a plan for where your money goes. *Deficit:* when what you need is more than what you have. *Emergency fund:* money set aside for the unexpected. *Sinking fund:* money set aside for a known payment.

**Sources:** [S04] CFPB, Your Money, Your Goals.

## M1 U09. Does filing taxes mean I will get money back?

**What you will achieve:** tell apart filing, paying and getting a refund, and know which questions to ask.

### The essentials

In the example, the calculated tax is 100. During the year you already paid 120 (it was withheld from your pay).

1. Calculated tax: 100.
2. Already paid: 120.
3. Difference: 20 in your favor. If you had only paid 80, you would owe 20.

> **Key idea:** **filing a tax return** means reporting your information and calculating your tax. A **refund** only exists if you paid too much. They are different things.

### Go deeper

The tax return reports your income and other information to calculate the tax for the year. Withholding from your pay and estimated payments are **prepayments**. In the end there may be a balance due or a refund.

**A deduction and a credit are not the same:**

- A **deduction** reduces the income your tax is calculated on.
- A **credit** directly reduces your tax. Some credits are **refundable**: they can give you money even if you owe no tax.

Made-up example with a flat 10% rate: a 100 deduction lowers your tax by 10; a 100 credit lowers it by 100.

**Marginal and effective rates.** In a made-up schedule, the first 1,000 is taxed at 10% and the next 1,000 at 20%. With 2,000 of income, the tax is 100 + 200 = 300. The rate on the last bracket (marginal) is 20%; the effective rate is 300 ÷ 2,000 = 15%. Moving into the second bracket does **not** make all your income taxed at 20%.

**Federal and state.** There is a federal return (IRS) and, in California, a state return (Franchise Tax Board, FTB). Your tax residency, filing status and dependents are decided by rules and facts, not by your nationality.

**SSN and ITIN.**

- The **SSN** is issued by the Social Security Administration.
- The **ITIN** is issued by the IRS to people who need to file taxes and cannot get an SSN. **It does not authorize work or change your immigration status.**
- Never use someone else's number or a made-up one. If you have neither, get guidance on how to apply for an ITIN (you will see this in M1 U11).

**Dates.** Tell the filing deadline apart from the payment deadline. An **extension to file** usually does **not** give you more time to pay.

**Tax residency and immigration status are different concepts.** The course cannot determine your tax situation from a short story. Prepare dates and facts for a confidential consultation.

### Cases

**Case 1. The expected refund.** Mar thinks "filing taxes is so they give you money back." This year she had app income with no withholding. When she calculates, she owes 400. Planning during the year would have avoided the surprise.

**Case 2. Dependents in Mexico.** Alex sends money to his mother. Can he claim her as a dependent? It is not automatic: there are rules about relationship, residence and support, and recent changes to the requirements. He should bring the information to a credentialed preparer.

### Interactive activity

**Deduction or credit? (H5P drag and sort):** sort examples and see how the tax changes in each case.

### Quiz

1. Does filing a return guarantee a refund? a) Yes · b) No
2. Does an ITIN authorize work? a) Yes · b) No
3. Does an extension to file extend the time to pay? a) Usually yes · b) Usually no

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-b · 3-b.

### Practice

Calculated tax 1,400; prepayments 1,100. What is the result? And if prepayments were 1,600?

**Answer:** in the first case you owe 300; in the second, you get a 200 refund. This does not use real tax tables.

> **Before you act, check:** this year's dates, limits and credits at IRS.gov and FTB.ca.gov. They change every year.

### For your plan

Write down the tax year, your documents, the dates you verified and your unanswered questions.

**Key words:** *Tax return:* a document that reports your information and calculates your tax. *Refund:* money returned when you paid too much. *Withholding:* tax prepayment taken from your pay. *Deduction:* lowers the income your tax is based on. *Tax credit:* lowers the tax. *ITIN:* IRS tax number for people who cannot get an SSN.

**Sources:** [S12] IRS, ITIN · [R06] IRS, deadlines and extensions.

## M1 U10. Is everything I collect working for myself mine to spend?

**What you will achieve:** organize your records by type of income (wages, self-employment or mixed).

### The essentials

You collect 100 for a job and spent 30 on materials.

1. Write down the 100 you collected.
2. Write down the 30 in materials.
3. Subtract: 70 remains before taxes. Keep the receipts.

> **Key idea:** what you collect and what is left after costs are not the same. And an expense existing does not mean you can deduct all of it on your taxes.

### Go deeper

**Three folders depending on your work:**

| Type of income | What to gather |
|---|---|
| Wages | Pay stubs, W-2 form, withholding |
| Self-employment | Payments received through every channel (cash, apps, transfers), expenses with receipts, business use of your car or phone, estimated payments made |
| Mixed | Both folders plus a sheet that reconciles them |

**Check your W-2:** name, identification number and amounts.

**Income from apps and platforms.** The IRS says this income can be reportable even if you do not receive an information form. A Form 1099 reports certain payments, but it does not calculate your profit or decide your worker classification.

**Reconcile so you do not count twice.** If a platform reports 1,000 and your bank received two 500 deposits for those same payments, the income is 1,000, not 2,000. Moving money from your business account to your personal account is not new income or a business expense.

**Mixed use.** If you use your phone or car for work and personal use, you need records showing how you split each use. An estimate without data does not count as proof.

**Estimated payments.** If you work for yourself and no one withholds taxes, you may need to make payments during the year. There can be income tax and also self-employment tax. Do not set aside "10%" just because someone else does: calculate with professional guidance and update when your income changes. State taxes are reviewed separately.

**Each receipt should show:** date, vendor, amount, what it was for and how you paid. If one is missing, write down which one and how to get it back. Never make up numbers or receipts.

A family remittance is not a business expense even if it comes out of the business account.

### Cases

**Case 1. Mar and the two apps.** Mar cleans houses for direct clients (cash) and through an app. The app sends her a 1099 for 3,200. Her cash clients paid her 4,500 that does not appear on any form. Both are income and she must record them.

**Case 2. Alex's materials.** Alex does weekend jobs. He keeps material receipts in a box. At the end of the year he finds many are faded. Now he takes a photo of them the same day.

### Interactive activity

**Build the folder (H5P):** drag sample documents (W-2, 1099, receipts, bank statements) into the right folder by type of income.

### Quiz

1. Is app income reportable even if you do not get a form? a) It can be · b) Never
2. Is moving 500 from the business account to your personal account new income? a) Yes · b) No
3. Should you set aside "10%" for taxes because a friend does? a) Yes · b) No, calculate your own case

**Answers:** 1-a · 2-b · 3-b.

### Practice

Receipts 1,000; case costs 250; transfer to your personal account 500. What is the result before taxes?

**Answer:** 750. The 500 transfer is not another income or another deductible expense.

> **Before you act, check:** with a credentialed preparer, which expenses are deductible in your case and whether you must make estimated payments.

### For your plan

Prepare folders for income, expenses, health, family and notices from the IRS or FTB. If you have property or income in another country, write that question down for your preparer.

**Key words:** *Business receipts:* what you collect before costs. *Business cost:* what you spend to work or sell. *Estimated payments:* tax prepayments during the year. *Form 1099:* reports certain payments received. *W-2:* annual summary of wages and withholding.

**Sources:** [S13] IRS, gig economy · [R05] IRS, Publication 505 · [R27] IRS, self-employed individuals.

## M1 U11. What tax benefits exist if I file with an ITIN?

**What you will achieve:** learn how to get an ITIN, which credits may apply to your household and where to get free help filing.

### The essentials

Filing with an ITIN can give you access to state credits that put money in your pocket, and it builds a record of compliance.

1. Find out whether you need an ITIN (if you must file and cannot get an SSN).
2. Review which credits exist for your situation.
3. Look for free, certified help to prepare your return.

> **Key idea:** some federal credits require an SSN, but California has state credits available to people who file with an ITIN.

### Go deeper

**How to get an ITIN.** You apply with Form W-7, usually together with your tax return, and documents that prove identity and foreign status (for example, a passport). To avoid mailing your original passport, you can go to a **Certifying Acceptance Agent** (CAA), who verifies your documents. An ITIN expires if you do not use it on a federal return for three years in a row; check whether yours needs renewal.

**Credits that may apply in California** (check each year's requirements):

- **CalEITC:** state earned income credit for lower-income households. It can be claimed with an ITIN.
- **Young Child Tax Credit (YCTC):** an additional state credit if you qualify for CalEITC and have at least one child under 6.

**Federal credits with SSN requirements.** The federal Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC) requires a valid SSN. The federal Child Tax Credit has SSN requirements that changed recently. There is a smaller credit for "other dependents" with its own rules. Always check the current rules.

**Free help to file:**

- **VITA and TCE:** IRS programs with certified volunteers, for people with income below a limit published each year. Many sites help in Spanish and with ITINs.
- **CalFile:** a free FTB tool for simple state returns, if you qualify.

**Why file even if it does not "pay off" this year.** It may be required depending on your income, it builds a record of compliance and it lets you claim credits you are entitled to.

### Cases

**Case 1. Mar and CalEITC.** Mar files with an ITIN. With her income and two young children, her VITA preparer checks whether she qualifies for CalEITC and YCTC. The result changes her year: instead of owing, she receives a state credit.

**Case 2. The passport in the mail.** A neighbor tells Alex that for an ITIN he must mail his original passport to the IRS. Alex finds out that a Certifying Acceptance Agent can verify his documents without mailing them.

### Interactive activity

**The ITIN path (H5P timeline):** put the steps to apply for an ITIN in order and find out where free help is available.

### Quiz

1. Does an ITIN allow you to work? a) Yes · b) No
2. Can CalEITC be claimed with an ITIN? a) Yes · b) No
3. What happens if you do not use your ITIN on a federal return for three years in a row? a) It expires · b) Nothing

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-a · 3-a.

### Practice

Write three questions to bring to your VITA appointment if you file with an ITIN and have children.

**Suggested answer:** Do I qualify for CalEITC and YCTC this year? Are my ITIN and my children's current? What documents do I need to claim my dependents?

> **Before you act, check:** this year's requirements and income limits at FTB.ca.gov (CalEITC) and IRS.gov (VITA, ITIN). Never pay someone who "sells" ITINs or promises credits without reviewing your documents.

### For your plan

Find the nearest VITA site and write down its hours, language and the documents it asks for.

**Key words:** *ITIN:* IRS tax number for people who cannot get an SSN. *CalEITC:* California's earned income tax credit. *Refundable credit:* can give you money even if you owe no tax. *VITA:* free IRS tax help from certified volunteers. *Certifying Acceptance Agent:* a person authorized by the IRS to verify ITIN documents.

**Sources:** [S12] IRS, ITIN · [S14] FTB, ITIN · [S15] FTB, free tax help · [R19] IRS, VITA · [R48] FTB, CalFile.

## M1 U12. How do I choose someone to help with my taxes?

**What you will achieve:** compare tax preparation services and recognize dangerous practices.

### The essentials

One person advertises their service for 50 and another for 80. You still do not know which costs less: the first may charge extra for what you need.

1. Explain what you need.
2. Ask what is included, what is not and what the whole thing costs.
3. Verify who will do the work and their credential.

> **Key idea:** compare the **scope** of the service, not just the advertised price. Never sign something you have not read.

### Go deeper

**What to ask:**

- Does it include the federal and state returns?
- Does it include self-employment, ITIN, dependents, late returns or corrections?
- Will they help me if the IRS sends a notice later?
- Who signs the return and what credential do they have?

**Credentials in California.** A paid preparer must have an IRS PTIN. In addition, in California, anyone who prepares returns for pay and is not a certified public accountant (CPA), enrolled agent (EA) or attorney must be registered with **CTEC** (California Tax Education Council). You can check on its website. EAs and CPAs can be checked in the IRS directory.

**Compare the whole bill:**

| | Advertised price | Self-employment | State | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Service A | 90 | 100 | 60 | 250 |
| Service B | 230 (all included) | included | included | 230 |

The comparison only works if both include the same work.

**Loans against your refund.** Getting money in advance "against your refund" is a separate product with its own terms. The speed of the loan is not the speed of the IRS.

**Warning signs:**

- They promise "the maximum refund, guaranteed" before seeing your documents.
- They ask you to sign blank pages.
- They want the refund deposited into their account, not yours.
- They suggest making up expenses or dependents.
- They do not sign the return as the preparer.

**Before signing,** review your information, income, expenses, dependents and the refund account. **Afterwards,** keep a full copy and the filing confirmation.

**If a notice arrives,** write down the date you received it and the deadline. Verify it is real by going to IRS.gov or FTB.ca.gov yourself; do not use the phone number in the message. The IRS usually contacts people first by mail.

### Cases

**Case 1. The guaranteed refund.** A social media ad promises Alex a "3,000 refund, guaranteed." They ask him to sign before reviewing anything. Alex does not sign and looks for a VITA site.

**Case 2. The two quotes.** Mar compares two preparers. The cheap one charges extra for the state return and self-employment. The expensive one includes everything and gives her a copy. The second turns out cheaper for her case.

### Interactive activity

**Would you hire this preparer? (H5P scenario):** review three ads and conversations and spot the warning signs.

### Quiz

1. What must every paid preparer have? a) A PTIN · b) Nothing
2. In California, a paid preparer who is not a CPA, EA or attorney must register with: a) CTEC · b) DMV
3. Is it safe to sign a blank return? a) Yes · b) No

**Answers:** 1-a · 2-a · 3-b.

### Practice

A preparer promises "the maximum refund, guaranteed" before seeing your documents and asks you to sign blank pages. What do you do?

**Answer:** you do not sign, you protect your documents and you look for a verifiable option (VITA or a registered preparer). A refund cannot be promised without reviewing your information.

> **Before you act, check:** the preparer's credential with CTEC or in the IRS directory, and ask for the total price in writing.

### For your plan

Compare two services and one free option. Write down what you confirmed and what is still missing.

**Key words:** *Scope of service:* the work included in the price. *Credentials:* records that prove training or authorization. *PTIN:* IRS preparer number. *CTEC:* California's tax preparer registry.

**Sources:** [S15] FTB, free help · [S16] FTB, Publication 982 · [R36] CTEC, preparer lookup.

## M1 U13. What should I know before applying for public benefits?

**What you will achieve:** understand what "public charge" is, what support exists and where to confirm your case before deciding.

### The essentials

Many families do not apply for support they are entitled to out of fear it will affect an immigration process. Others apply without knowing whether it applies.

1. Identify what support you need (food, health, housing, cash).
2. Find out who in your household might qualify.
3. Confirm with a reliable source how the public charge rule applies to your case.

> **Key idea:** the **public charge** rule only applies to certain immigration processes and only considers certain benefits. Get informed before deciding; do not decide based on rumors.

### Go deeper

**What public charge is.** It is an assessment the government makes in some immigration processes, mainly certain applications for permanent residence, to see whether a person is likely to depend mainly on government support.

**What the rule in effect since 2022 says** (check whether it has changed):

- It mainly considers **cash assistance** for income maintenance (for example, SSI, CalWORKs cash aid or general assistance) and **long-term institutional care** paid by the government.
- It does **not** consider, among others: CalFresh (food assistance), Medi-Cal (except long-term care), WIC, school meals, housing, disaster aid, tax credits such as CalEITC, or unemployment insurance.
- Benefits received by **other family members** (for example, U.S.-citizen children) are not counted as yours.
- It **does not apply** to many processes and categories: for example, permanent residents applying for citizenship, or refugees and asylees.

**The rules can change.** The federal government has proposed changes. A proposal is not a rule until it is published as final and takes effect. Check up-to-date sources.

**Health coverage in California.** Medi-Cal recently expanded coverage to people without regular immigration status, but the 2025 state budget approved changes for adults (a pause on new enrollment starting in 2026 and other adjustments). Confirm the current rules before deciding.

**Where to confirm your case:**

- An immigration attorney or a Department of Justice (DOJ) accredited representative at a recognized organization.
- Organizations such as *Protecting Immigrant Families* (up-to-date materials on public charge).
- Your county social services office, for each program's requirements.

**Beware of rumors and "paperwork helpers."** No one who is not an attorney or accredited representative can give you immigration advice (you will see this in M4 U02).

### Cases

**Case 1. Mar's children.** Mar's two children were born in California. Mar was afraid to apply for food assistance for them. At a community organization they explain that her children's benefits are not counted as hers and that CalFresh is not considered under the current rule. Mar confirms her case with an accredited representative.

**Case 2. The neighbor's rumor.** A neighbor tells Alex that "any benefit takes away your green card." Alex looks for a reliable source before deciding.

### Interactive activity

**Myth or fact? (H5P flip cards):** flip ten statements about public charge and find out which are correct under the current rule.

### Quiz

1. Does the public charge rule apply to all immigration processes? a) Yes · b) No
2. Under the current rule, is CalFresh considered for public charge? a) Yes · b) No
3. Who can give you immigration advice? a) A notary · b) An attorney or a DOJ-accredited representative

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-b · 3-b.

### Practice

Write three questions to bring to a legal aid organization before applying for support for your family.

**Suggested answer:** Does the public charge rule apply to my process? Is this benefit considered? Do my children's benefits affect my case?

> **Before you act, check:** the current status of the rule and of each program with an attorney or accredited representative. This course does not give immigration advice.

### For your plan

Write down what support your household needs, who might qualify and where you will confirm your case.

**Key words:** *Public charge:* an assessment in certain immigration processes about dependence on government support. *Accredited representative:* a person authorized by the DOJ to give immigration advice at a recognized organization. *CalFresh:* food assistance in California.

**Sources:** [R33] USCIS, public charge · [R34] Protecting Immigrant Families · [R35] California DHCS, Medi-Cal.

## M1 U14. How do I turn what I learned into something I can actually do?

**What you will achieve:** build a 90-day plan with concrete actions, dates and an alternative if something changes.

### The essentials

"I'm going to get organized" does not say how to start. "On Saturday I will write down the income and payments for the next two weeks" does.

1. Choose a specific problem.
2. Write a small action and a date.
3. Keep a sample of what you did.

> **Key idea:** a goal you can check has an **action, a date and evidence**.

### Go deeper

Your plan should say what you will do, when, with what information and how you will know you made progress. Choose a few priorities that respond to your diagnosis; an impossible list does not get done.

**Connect the pieces:** calendar (U07), budget (U08) and tax folder (U10). If the remittance is already in the calendar, do not add it again as an extra expense. If you set aside a reserve, say where you keep it and what it is for.

**Three steps:**

| Time frame | Action | Possible obstacle | Alternative |
|---|---|---|---|
| This week | ... | ... | ... |
| 30 days | ... | ... | ... |
| 90 days (review) | ... | ... | ... |

"I need to find out" is a valid action if it has a date.

If you lose work hours, the right action may be asking for support or renegotiating a payment. Do not keep a fictional contribution so the plan "looks good."

**Consistency check:**

- Did you add the gross and net of the same paycheck?
- Did you count transfers between your accounts as income?
- Do taxes and remittances have dates?
- Did you assign the same reserve to two things?
- Does the plan depend on one-time income?

### Cases

**Case 1. Alex and Mar's plan.** Problem: money runs short before rent. Week 1: record dates and payments. Week 2: ask to change the car insurance due date and confirm whether it is accepted. Week 3: recalculate with the confirmed date or use another option. Evidence: the original calendar and the revised calendar.

**Case 2. "I want to save more."** A household with a deficit cannot start by saving. First it documents the gap for 4 weeks, reviews two support options and resolves one obligation. The first goal can be to stabilize.

### Interactive activity

**Turn wishes into goals (H5P):** turn five vague sentences into goals with an action, a date and evidence.

### Quiz

1. What does a checkable goal have? a) Only a wish · b) Action, date and evidence
2. Can "I need to find out" be an action? a) Yes, if it has a date · b) No
3. If your income drops, must you keep the same savings contribution? a) Yes · b) Not necessarily; adjust the plan

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-a · 3-b.

### Practice

Turn "I want to save more" into a doable action for a household that still has a deficit.

**Suggested answer:** "For 4 weeks I will record income and expenses, on the 15th I will review two support options and on the 30th I will ask for a payment plan on one debt."

### For your plan: submit portfolio E1

Submit: diagnosis, 8-week calendar, usual budget and lower-income budget, tax checklist and three 90-day actions. What is assessed is consistency and the ability to adjust, not how much money you have. Also solve **integrative case E1** at the end of the manual.

**Key words:** *Checkable goal:* an action with a date and evidence. *Evidence:* a record that shows what you did. *Periodic expense:* a payment that repeats but is not monthly.
