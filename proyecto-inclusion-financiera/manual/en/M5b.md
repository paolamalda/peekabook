## M5 U06. What will I live on when I work less or stop working?

**What you will achieve:** identify your possible sources of retirement income and the questions you need to verify.

### The essentials

Planning for retirement does not start with choosing an investment, but with understanding your needs, your possible income and the time you have.

1. Write down the expenses that will probably continue.
2. Identify your possible sources and which ones still need confirming.
3. Choose an action you can start today.

> **Key idea:** a small, steady contribution is different from a promised return. No one can guarantee how much an investment will grow.

### Go deeper

**Possible sources:**

| Source | What it is | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Social Security (U.S.) | A benefit based on years worked with an SSN | Your statement in my Social Security; contributions are only credited with a valid SSN |
| Employer plan (401(k), 403(b)) | You contribute from your pay; sometimes the employer does too | Employer contribution and rules for it to become yours |
| CalSavers | California retirement program for workers whose employer does not offer a plan | Whether you can participate with your ID; how to withdraw |
| Traditional or Roth IRA | Personal retirement account with tax rules | You need earned income; yearly limits |
| Your own savings | Other accounts | Liquidity and risk |
| AFORE (Mexico) | Retirement savings in Mexico | M5 U07 |

**Employer match.** If your employer puts in 50 cents for every dollar you contribute, up to a limit, contributing 100 gets you 50 more. Check the limit and how long you must work for that contribution to become yours (**vesting**).

**Traditional or Roth:** they differ in when you pay taxes. Neither is always better; it depends on your situation.

**Scenarios, not forecasts.** Contributing 50 a month for 20 years adds up to 12,000 with no return. With a hypothetical rate of 0.25% a month, the simulation gives about 16,415 before fees and taxes. Repeat with zero return and adjust for inflation: 16,415 dollars in 20 years will not buy what it buys today.

**Before taking money out of a plan when you change jobs:** review transfer options, costs, taxes and penalties. Withdrawing without knowing can cost a lot.

### Cases

**Case 1. Alex changes jobs.** Alex leaves his job and is offered to cash out his 401(k). Before doing so, he asks the plan administrator about rolling it over to an IRA and what taxes and penalties would apply.

**Case 2. Mar's CalSavers.** Mar's employer has no retirement plan and enrolls her in CalSavers. Mar reviews how much is deducted, how to change it and how to withdraw if she needs to.

### Interactive activity

**My retirement in scenarios (H5P simulator):** choose a contribution, time frame and hypothetical rate, and compare with zero return and with inflation.

### Quiz

1. Does an IRA require earned income to contribute? a) Yes · b) No
2. Is a 20-year projection a promise? a) Yes · b) No
3. Should you cash out your 401(k) when you change jobs without reviewing options? a) Yes · b) No

**Answers:** 1-a · 2-b · 3-b.

### For your plan

Make your inventory of sources, your questions and a first step. You do not have to contribute to pass.

**Key words:** *Employer match:* what your employer puts in. *Vesting:* conditions for that contribution to become yours. *IRA:* personal retirement account. *CalSavers:* California's retirement program.

**Sources:** [S27] CalSavers · [R53] Social Security, my Social Security.

## M5 U07. Do my accounts and rights work the same in two countries?

**What you will achieve:** map what you own and owe in each country, and prepare questions for the right institutions.

### The essentials

Not necessarily. An account, a benefit or a document can have different rules in each country.

1. Write down what you own or owe in each country.
2. Record the currency, the owner and how you access it.
3. Prepare questions for the right institutions or professionals.

> **Key idea:** do not assume a benefit or process transfers automatically from one country to another.

### Go deeper

**AFORE, IMSS and U.S. Social Security have different rules.** They do not add up automatically.

**The U.S.–Mexico social security agreement** was signed, but according to the Social Security Administration it is **not yet in force**. Until it takes effect, it does not allow combining weeks or years worked in both countries.

**Your AFORE.** If you worked in Mexico under IMSS, you have an AFORE account. You can locate it and check your balance through CONSAR's official channels. Some AFOREs allow Mexicans abroad to make voluntary contributions; check how.

**Map by country:** for each account, debt, property or benefit, record the owner, currency, access from abroad, official contact and what you need to ask.

**Taxes.** Having income or accounts in another country can raise tax or reporting questions in the U.S. Ask a preparer; do not assume it is always taxable or always exempt.

**Exchange rate.** A future expense of 10,000 pesos equals 500 dollars at 20 pesos per dollar, or 625 at 16. Steady income in dollars does not guarantee covering the same expense in pesos.

**Beware of anyone promising to recover your AFORE** for an upfront payment. The procedures are done through official channels.

### Cases

**Case 1. Alex's weeks.** Someone tells Alex that "all his weeks add up automatically." Alex checks the official source: the agreement is not in force.

**Case 2. The forgotten AFORE.** Mar worked five years in Guadalajara. She locates her AFORE with her CURP and finds a balance she did not know about.

### Interactive activity

**Cross-border map (H5P):** place your accounts, property and benefits in the right country and mark what you need to ask about.

### Quiz

1. Is the U.S.–Mexico social security agreement in force? a) Yes · b) No, according to the SSA
2. Are an AFORE and a 401(k) equivalent? a) Yes · b) No
3. Do you pay someone who promises to recover your AFORE? a) Yes · b) I verify and use official channels

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-b · 3-b.

### For your plan

Make your map by country with what is confirmed, what is missing and who you will ask.

**Key words:** *Cross-border planning:* organizing money and obligations in more than one country. *AFORE:* retirement fund administrator in Mexico.

**Sources:** [S25] SSA, U.S.–Mexico agreement · [R54] CONSAR.

## M5 U08. Who can receive or handle things if I can't?

**What you will achieve:** organize beneficiaries, documents and authorizations so your resources can be found.

### The essentials

The person designated to receive a benefit is a **beneficiary**. Someone who can act for you needs **legal authorization**.

1. Review who appears on your documents.
2. Ask what each person can do.
3. Update when your life changes.

> **Key idea:** sharing a password does not make someone a beneficiary or give them legal power.

### Go deeper

**Different roles:**

| Role | What they can do |
|---|---|
| Account owner | Owns the account |
| Joint owner | Also an owner; can withdraw |
| Beneficiary | Receives the account or insurance if the owner dies |
| Agent (power of attorney) | Acts for you according to the document |
| Emergency contact | Only receives notices |

**Different documents:** a will, a financial power of attorney, health care instructions. Each serves a different purpose and depends on the laws of the place. Consult an attorney.

**Private index:** accounts, insurance, debts and documents, with the institution and where to find the information. Do not include full numbers or passwords.

**Review beneficiaries** after a marriage, separation, birth or death. Updating your phone number does not change your beneficiary.

**If you have property in Mexico,** you may need advice in both California and Mexico.

### Cases

**Case 1. "My sister knows my password."** That does not give her legal authority to act if Alex becomes incapacitated, and it does not make her a beneficiary.

**Case 2. The old life insurance.** Mar finds that her work life insurance still names her ex-partner as beneficiary. She updates it with the insurer's form.

### Interactive activity

**Who can do what? (H5P matching):** match each role with what it allows.

### Quiz

1. Does sharing your password create a power of attorney? a) Yes · b) No
2. Does updating your phone number change your beneficiary? a) Yes · b) No
3. Can an emergency contact withdraw money from your account? a) Yes · b) No

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-b · 3-b.

### For your plan

Choose five actions: locate a document, check a beneficiary, ask about a power of attorney, agree on a contact or update your index.

**Key words:** *Beneficiary:* the person who receives a benefit. *Power of attorney:* authorizes someone to act for you. *Account owner:* the person who owns an account.

## M5 U09. What do I do if my job or my family changes?

**What you will achieve:** recalculate your plan when your income or responsibilities change.

### The essentials

A plan built on an income of 500 does not work the same if you now receive 400. You did not fail: a fact changed.

1. Write down what changed and since when.
2. Review the income and expenses affected.
3. Decide what you keep, what you change and what help you need.

> **Key idea:** adjusting the plan means recalculating with your new situation.

### Go deeper

**A change affects several pieces at once:** budget, taxes, insurance and beneficiaries. Birth, separation, death, unemployment, caring for a relative or moving.

**Order for responding:**

1. **First week:** safety, needs and urgent dates.
2. **First month:** support, agreements and coverage.
3. **90 days:** goals and how long the adjustment will last.

Avoid irreversible decisions under pressure. Some processes have deadlines: putting things off indefinitely also has consequences.

**Moving back to Mexico.** Review access to your accounts from abroad, the currency of your expenses, health coverage, retirement accounts and tax obligations with professionals.

**Comparing scenarios:**

- Before: income 2,600, expenses 2,400, cushion 200.
- After: income −300, care +180, transportation −80.
- New cushion: 200 − 300 − 180 + 80 = −200.

Cancelling a 15 subscription helps, but leaves a 185 deficit. Small cuts are not always enough for a big change.

### Cases

**Case 1. Mar's hours.** Mar loses hours: 400 less a month and 100 more in transportation to care for her mother. The effect is 500, not 400.

**Case 2. The separation.** A couple separates. Splitting expenses in two is not enough: housing, childcare, contracts, debts, taxes and beneficiaries need review.

### Quiz

1. Does adjusting the plan mean you failed? a) Yes · b) No
2. Income −400 and transportation +100. Effect on your cash flow? a) 400 · b) 500
3. After separating, is it enough to split expenses in two? a) Yes · b) No

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-b · 3-b.

### For your plan

Fill in a change sheet with four affected pieces, verified dates and a support contact.

**Key words:** *Scenario:* a possible version to test your plan. *Cash flow:* money in and out over time.

## M5 U10. How do I start a business if I have an ITIN?

**What you will achieve:** learn the first steps to formalize a small business and separate your personal finances from the business's.

### The essentials

Mar sells tamales on weekends. She collects 600 a month and spends 250 on ingredients.

1. Separate the business money from the household money.
2. Calculate the real profit (including the value of your time).
3. Find out what permits your activity needs in your city.

> **Key idea:** with an ITIN you can file taxes and, in many cases, formalize a business. Starting organized avoids problems later.

### Go deeper

**Price and profit.** Price − direct costs − indirect costs = profit. Include the value of your hours: if you work 20 hours to earn 350, you earn 17.50 an hour before taxes.

**Steps to formalize** (they vary by city and activity):

| Step | What it is | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Employer Identification Number (EIN) | The business's tax number | IRS; you can apply with an ITIN |
| Business license | Permission to operate | Your city or county |
| Seller's permit | If you sell products subject to sales tax | California CDTFA |
| Food permits | If you prepare food at home or sell on the street | County health department |
| Legal structure | Sole proprietor, LLC or other | Check yearly costs (for example, California's minimum franchise tax for an LLC) |

**Street vending and home-made food.** California allows sidewalk vending with local permits and, in some counties, operating home kitchens with a permit. Ask your city and county.

**Business finances:**

- A separate account for the business.
- Record every sale and every expense with a receipt.
- Set money aside for taxes (M1 U10).
- Pay yourself a fixed "salary" from the business to your personal account.

**Free help.** Small Business Development Centers (SBDC) and community organizations offer advice in Spanish, sometimes for free.

**Watch out for** business loans with hidden costs, "programs" that promise fast licenses for a payment and franchise contracts you have not reviewed.

### Cases

**Case 1. Mar's tamales.** Mar collects 600 and spends 250 on ingredients. She works 20 hours. Her profit is 350: 17.50 an hour. She decides to raise her price 10% and write down every sale.

**Case 2. Alex's jobs.** Alex does remodeling on weekends. Some clients ask him for an invoice. He applies for an EIN with his ITIN and checks his city's license requirements.

### Interactive activity

**Price calculator (H5P):** enter costs and hours and find the minimum price to cover your costs and your time.

### Quiz

1. Can you apply for an EIN with an ITIN? a) Yes · b) No
2. Is it a good idea to mix business and household money? a) Yes · b) No
3. Is your time part of the cost? a) Yes · b) No

**Answers:** 1-a · 2-b · 3-a.

### For your plan

Calculate the real profit of your activity and write down which permit you need to look into in your city.

**Key words:** *EIN:* a business's tax number. *Seller's permit:* registration to collect sales tax. *LLC:* a type of limited liability company.

**Sources:** [R45] IRS, EIN · [R55] CDTFA · [R56] SBA, Small Business Development Centers.

## M5 U11. How do I know all the parts of my plan fit together?

**What you will achieve:** bring the five modules together into one plan with review dates and alternatives.

### The essentials

If you have 50 free, you cannot put those same 50 toward a debt and also a goal.

1. Bring your plans together.
2. Check that each dollar has only one job.
3. Test what happens if you earn less or an expense appears.

> **Key idea:** counting a resource more than once is **double counting**, the most common mistake in a plan.

### Go deeper

**Step-by-step cross-check:**

1. Add up your available resources only once.
2. Subtract obligations and reserves by date.
3. Check that debt payments match your inventory.
4. Check that remittances match your family agreement.
5. Update insurance and periodic expenses.
6. Split only the remaining cushion among your goals.

**Three scenarios:** usual, with less income and with a family emergency. Write down what changes, what stays and who you would ask. A good answer does not always end positive: it can show a real gap and a reasonable order for asking for help.

**Reviews at 30, 90 and 180 days.** Separate what you did from what does not depend on you: reviewing your report can be checked; getting approved for credit does not depend only on you.

### Cases

**Case 1. The forgotten insurance.** Alex assigned 100 a month to a goal, but when he brings his plan together he finds a 60 insurance payment he had forgotten. Only 40 is left. He adjusts the goal instead of keeping numbers that do not add up.

**Case 2. The triple reserve.** Mar assigned the same 1,000 reserve to emergencies, a home down payment and retirement. That is triple counting. She splits the money or recognizes which goals are not funded yet.

### Quiz

1. Can you count the same emergency fund as a down payment? a) Yes · b) No
2. Does a good plan always end positive? a) Yes · b) No, it can show a gap and how to handle it

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-b.

### Practice

Explain two decisions with this structure: need, options, information, choice, risk and review.

**Suggested answer:** "I chose it because everyone uses it" is not enough. "I compared cost, documents and access, and I'll review in three months" shows reasoning.

### For your plan: final submission

Submit portfolios E1 to E5, sources, assumptions and your follow-up plan. You can use made-up data. You do not have to buy, invest, reduce remittances or post a review to receive your diploma. Also solve **integrative case E5** at the end of the manual.

**Key words:** *Double counting:* using the same money for two things. *Cross-check:* confirming that all parts of the plan add up.
