## M2 U07. How much can I send, and when?

**What you will achieve:** calculate your sending capacity by date, without neglecting your obligations.

### The essentials

You have 100. You need 60 for payments and food before your next paycheck. Sending costs 5.

1. Subtract what is committed: 100 − 60 = 40.
2. Subtract the transfer cost: 40 − 5 = 35.
3. Decide whether you need to keep an extra cushion.

> **Key idea:** your **sending capacity** is what you can send without failing to cover your commitments, including the cost of sending.

### Go deeper

Calculate capacity **by payday**, not with what is left after shopping.

**Formula:** money available today − needs until next payday − payments due − agreed reserves − cushion = capacity.

Do not subtract a reserve twice if you already took it out of the balance.

**Example with two dates:**

- Today you have 700. Tomorrow you pay 450. Until your next paycheck you need 160.
- 90 remains. The transfer costs 5 and you want a 20 cushion.
- You can send 65.
- Promising 100 would require 35 more.
- When you get paid on Friday, recalculate. Do not add money that arrives later to today.

**If the result is negative,** do not turn it into zero: show a shortfall. Options: a partial transfer, another date, a contribution from another relative, a verifiable direct payment of the bill or local help.

**Variable income:** agree on a **range** instead of a fixed amount, and on what information will trigger a review. Do not make a permanent commitment based on a one-time bonus.

**Communicate before, not after.** "My usual support is between 150 and 180. If my hours are cut, I'll let you know before the date." This keeps you from going into debt to keep your word.

### Cases

**Case 1. Rent and the transfer.** Alex gets paid three days after paying rent. The month closes positive, but on rent day he is short if he sends first. He decides to send after he gets paid.

**Case 2. The 120 available.** Mar has 900. Needs until next paycheck 650, tax reserve 80 and agreed reserve 50. She has 120 left. If she sends 200, she creates an 80 shortfall.

### Interactive activity

**Capacity calculator (H5P):** enter your balance, payments and dates; the calculator tells you how much you can send today and how much on Friday.

### Quiz

1. Is the cost of sending subtracted from capacity? a) Yes · b) No
2. If capacity comes out negative, what do you do? a) Write zero · b) Show the shortfall and look for options
3. Should you promise a fixed amount if your income varies? a) Yes · b) A range is better

**Answers:** 1-a · 2-b · 3-b.

### Practice

If your next paycheck arrives three days after rent is due, is a positive monthly total enough?

**Answer:** no. There must be money on rent day. Set it aside earlier or review the dates.

### For your plan

Write your usual range, your sending dates and what will make you review the agreement.

## M2 U08. Does "no fee" always mean more money arrives?

**What you will achieve:** compare transfers by what leaves your pocket and what reaches your family.

### The essentials

Two services can turn your dollars into different amounts of pesos.

1. Use the same budget to compare.
2. Subtract fees and any taxes that apply.
3. Compare how much arrives and when.

> **Key idea:** a "no fee" service can deliver fewer pesos if its **exchange rate** is worse.

### Go deeper

**What to record for each quote:** fee, exchange rate, payment-method charges (for example, paying with a credit card), pickup charges, any applicable tax, delivery time and travel cost for the person receiving.

**Two ways to compare:**

- **Fixed budget:** you have 300 dollars in total. How many pesos arrive with each service?
- **Fixed amount to receive:** your family needs 4,000 pesos. How much does it cost you with each service?

Do not compare an offer that sends 300 with another that charges 300 in total: they are different bases.

**Fixed budget (sample data, no tax):**

| | Fee | Dollars converted | Exchange rate | Arrives |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 5 | 295 | 17.00 | 5,015 pesos |
| B | 0 | 300 | 16.60 | 4,980 pesos |

A delivers 35 more pesos even though it charges a fee.

**Fixed amount to receive:** 4,000 pesos needed. Exchange rate 16 and fee 6: 4,000 ÷ 16 = 250 dollars, plus 6 = 256. With exchange rate 16.5 and fee 10: 4,000 ÷ 16.5 = 242.42, plus 10 = 252.42. If your family is charged 50 pesos at pickup, calculate with 4,050.

**The exchange-rate markup.** Compare the offered rate with a reference from the same moment. If the reference is 17 and you are offered 16.5, you lose 0.5 pesos per dollar: on 200 dollars that is 100 pesos. The reference is for comparing; you will not always be able to exchange at that rate.

**Tax on remittances paid in cash.** Since January 2026 there is a **1%** federal tax on certain remittances paid with **cash, a money order, a cashier's check or a similar physical instrument**. It is not a tax on all remittances and it does not depend on your nationality. Paying from a bank account or with a debit card usually is not subject to this tax; confirm with your provider how it applies to your transfer.

**Promotions.** The price of the first transfer does not describe the usual cost. Compare with the price of later transfers.

### Cases

**Case 1. The app promotion.** Mar is offered "first transfer free and a better exchange rate." The second month, the same app delivers fewer pesos than her previous option. Mar compares quotes from the same day.

**Case 2. Alex's cash.** Alex always pays his transfers in cash at the store. After learning about the 1% tax, he compares paying from his debit account. On 300 dollars, he saves 3.

### Interactive activity

**Remittance comparer (H5P):** enter three sample quotes and find out which delivers the most pesos with the same budget and which costs least for a fixed amount.

### Quiz

1. Does "no fee" guarantee more money arrives? a) Yes · b) No
2. Does the 1% tax apply to all remittances? a) Yes · b) No, to certain remittances paid with cash or physical instruments
3. To compare, what must be the same? a) The base: same budget or same amount to receive · b) Nothing

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-b · 3-a.

### Practice

A transfer subject to the 1% tax is 300 dollars and the fee is 5, charged separately. How much leaves your pocket?

**Answer:** 300 + 3 + 5 = 308. If your total budget were 300, you would need to recalculate the amount to send.

> **Before you act, check:** the full quote (how much arrives, how long it takes and where it is picked up) at the moment you send, and how the tax applies to your payment method.

### For your plan

Compare three quotes from the same day with the same base and the same delivery time.

### Learn more

Free, official resources to go further (verified September 28, 2026):

- **Compare remittances (Remesamex)** (CONDUSEF; language: ES): https://www.condusef.gob.mx/?p=remesas
- **Remittance Prices Worldwide** (Banco Mundial; language: EN): https://remittanceprices.worldbank.org
- **What is a remittance transfer and what are my rights?** (CFPB; language: EN; Spanish on CFPB en español): https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-remittance-transfer-and-what-are-my-rights-en-1161/

**Key words:** *Exchange rate:* pesos you receive for each dollar. *Exchange-rate markup:* the difference between the reference rate and the offered rate. *Quote:* terms offered for a given moment and amount.

## M2 U09. What should I check before sending, and what if something goes wrong?

**What you will achieve:** verify the remittance company, know your rights and know what to do if there is a problem.

### The essentials

The **remittance receipt** shows the transaction you made. It has the amount, date, provider and a tracking number (**reference number**).

1. Before paying, check the recipient, the amount and the delivery date.
2. Keep the receipt.
3. If something fails, contact the provider through a verified channel.

> **Key idea:** cancelling, correcting an error and reporting fraud are different requests.

### Go deeper

**Check that the provider is licensed.** In California, companies that send money must be licensed by the **Department of Financial Protection and Innovation (DFPI)**. You can look them up in the DFPI license search or in NMLS Consumer Access. An unlicensed company is a warning sign.

**Before paying, confirm:** the recipient's exact name, country, currency, amount they will receive, total cost, availability date, where and how it is picked up, what ID the recipient needs and what happens if the name does not match.

**Your rights on transfers covered by federal rules** (according to the CFPB):

- The provider must give you information before you pay and a receipt.
- In general you can **cancel at no cost within 30 minutes** of paying, if the money has not been picked up or deposited.
- You have **180 days** from the stated delivery date to report certain errors.

There are exceptions (for example, scheduled transfers). Act quickly: do not wait for the deadline.

**Incident record:** date, provider, reference number, amount, promised availability, what happened and what solution you are asking for. Contact the official website or phone and keep the response. If it is not resolved, file a complaint with the CFPB or the DFPI.

**Watch out for the second scam.** If someone promises to "recover your transfer" in exchange for another urgent payment, verify before paying.

### Cases

**Case 1. The money that doesn't arrive.** Alex's receipt says the money would be available on Monday. On Tuesday it is not there. Alex checks the status with his reference number, confirms the recipient's details and calls the provider. He does not send a second remittance without understanding what happened to the first.

**Case 2. The market remittance shop.** A shop offers an excellent exchange rate. Mar searches for the name in the DFPI lookup and it does not appear. She decides not to use it.

### Interactive activity

**Receipt detective (H5P):** review three sample receipts and find the information that is missing to be able to file a claim.

### Quiz

1. Where do you check a remittance company's license in California? a) DFPI or NMLS · b) In its ad
2. In general, how long do you have to cancel at no cost? a) 30 minutes · b) 30 days
3. Does "you have 180 days" mean you should wait? a) Yes · b) No

**Answers:** 1-a · 2-a · 3-b.

### Practice

Write your incident record for a transfer that did not arrive on the promised date.

**Suggested answer:** date and time of the transfer; provider; reference number; amount sent and amount to receive; promised date; what happened; what you are asking for (delivery or refund); who you contacted and the response.

> **Before you act, check:** the provider's license with the DFPI or NMLS and save a screenshot with the date.

### For your plan

Create your remittance follow-up sheet: provider, license verified, reference number, date, problem, request and response.

### Learn more

Free, official resources to go further (verified September 28, 2026):

- **Directory of licensed money transmitters in California** (DFPI; language: EN): https://dfpi.ca.gov/regulated-industries/money-transmitters/directory-of-money-transmitters/
- **Verify financial company licenses** (NMLS Consumer Access; language: EN): https://www.nmlsconsumeraccess.org
- **What is a remittance transfer and what are my rights?** (CFPB; language: EN; Spanish on CFPB en español): https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-remittance-transfer-and-what-are-my-rights-en-1161/

**Key words:** *Remittance receipt:* proof with the transfer details. *Reference number:* tracking number. *DFPI:* California's financial regulator. *NMLS:* national registry of financial company licenses.

## M2 U10. What happens when I borrow to send money?

**What you will achieve:** calculate the effect of going into debt to send money.

### The essentials

You borrow 100 to help your family and must repay 120.

1. Record the 100 as borrowed money, not earned money.
2. Record the 120 repayment and its date.
3. Check where that 120 will come from.

> **Key idea:** today's support becomes a future payment. The 20 difference is the cost of the loan; the transfer can have other fees.

### Go deeper

Financing a remittance joins two decisions: how to send and how to repay. A low transfer fee can hide an expensive loan.

**Ask before accepting:** payment schedule, upfront fees, how much you will actually receive and how much you will repay in total. If you use a credit card, ask whether the transfer is treated as a purchase or as a **cash advance** (it usually costs more and charges interest from the first day).

**The risk of rolling over.** You must repay 240 plus a 30 fee in two weeks, but you will only have 100 free. You are 170 short. If you are offered to "renew" by paying only the 30, you still owe 240 and already spent 30. Extend the calendar to see when you can get out of the debt.

**Alternatives before borrowing:** send part now and part later, ask your family to review the due date of their expense, help from another relative or a local program. No alternative is made up: verify it.

A pay advance from an app, a loan from someone you know or a card cash advance are also debt, even if they are not called that.

### Cases

**Case 1. Alex's mother's emergency.** Alex only has 180 dollars free and needs to send 300. A loan would cover the 120 difference with a flat 18 fee, due in 14 days: 138. If on that date he will only have 100 free, he is 38 short. Before accepting, he looks at a partial transfer or help from his brother.

**Case 2. The app advance.** Mar uses an app that advances her pay for a "voluntary tip" of 5 dollars each time. She does it every week. That is 20 a month to get her own money early.

### Interactive activity

**Remittance debt simulator (H5P):** choose the loan amount, cost and term, and watch how your calendar for the next 4 weeks changes.

### Quiz

1. Is borrowed money earned income? a) Yes · b) No
2. If you "renew" by paying only the fee, do you reduce what you owe? a) Yes · b) No
3. Is an app pay advance debt? a) Yes · b) No

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-b · 3-a.

### Practice

You borrow 300 and must repay 345 in 30 days; you also pay 5 for the transfer. After your basic expenses you will only have 250 on the due date. What problem appears?

**Answer:** the loan costs 45 and the transfer 5. You are 95 short to repay the debt even if you send nothing else. Review the deal before accepting it or look for help if you already have it.

> **Before you act, check:** the total cost in dollars, the due date and what happens if you do not pay on time.

### For your plan

Write what you would do in an emergency before borrowing (three options in order).

### Learn more

Free, official resources to go further (verified September 28, 2026):

- **What is a remittance transfer and what are my rights?** (CFPB; language: EN; Spanish on CFPB en español): https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-remittance-transfer-and-what-are-my-rights-en-1161/
- **CFPB in Spanish** (CFPB; language: ES): https://www.consumerfinance.gov/es/

**Key words:** *Cash advance:* taking out money with a credit card, usually more expensive. *Refinancing or rolling over:* replacing a debt with another under new terms. *APR:* yearly cost of credit.

## M2 U11. How do I say how much I can help without overpromising?

**What you will achieve:** communicate your capacity respectfully and agree on how to verify emergencies.

### The essentials

You can say: "I can send 50 on Friday. I can't confirm another 30 yet."

1. Confirm the amount and date you can do.
2. Explain what is still unconfirmed.
3. Agree on when you will talk again.

> **Key idea:** separating what is certain from what is uncertain is the basis of a **family agreement**.

### Go deeper

**A good agreement has six parts:**

1. **Purpose:** what the support is for.
2. **Range:** minimum and maximum.
3. **Date:** when it is sent.
4. **Emergencies:** what counts as an emergency and what to do.
5. **Verification:** how to confirm an urgent request.
6. **Review:** when you will talk again (for example, if work hours change or every three months).

Listen to the recipient's expenses and explain your obligations. Do not promise uncertain money to end an uncomfortable conversation.

**Script:** "I want to keep helping. This month I can send between 150 and 180 after rent. If something extraordinary happens, let's first verify the amount and the date and look at options."

**Fake emergencies.** Today there are fake voices and videos created with artificial intelligence. Before a crisis, agree on:

- A known contact to verify with (another relative or a saved number).
- A second check (a question only the family would know, without relying only on a code word).

When a new request arrives, call a number you already knew, not the one in the message. Pressure and secrecy are warning signs.

**Safety.** If there is control or violence, an agreement should not put you at risk. You are not obligated to confront anyone.

### Cases

**Case 1. The fake accident.** A message arrives from a new number: "It's your nephew, I had an accident, don't call anyone, send 500." Mar calls her sister at the usual number: her nephew is fine.

**Case 2. Alex's agreement.** Alex and his mother agree: food, between 150 and 180 dollars, on the 15th; emergencies verified by calling his aunt; review every three months or if Alex's hours change.

### Interactive activity

**Real or fake? (H5P scenario):** you receive messages and calls from "relatives." Decide what to verify before sending.

### Quiz

1. What number do you call to verify an emergency? a) The one in the message · b) One you already knew
2. Does a voice that sounds exactly like your relative prove it is real? a) Yes · b) No
3. How many parts does a good family agreement have? a) 2 · b) 6

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-b · 3-b.

### Practice

Write an agreement with purpose, range, date, emergency, verification and review.

**Suggested answer:** food; 150 to 180; the 15th; verified emergency; a call to a known contact; review if hours change or every three months.

### For your plan

Keep your agreement private and prepare a version for lower-income months.

### Learn more

Free, official resources to go further (verified September 28, 2026):

- **FTC consumer advice** (FTC; language: ES (EN: consumer.ftc.gov)): https://consumidor.ftc.gov
- **Report fraud** (FTC; language: ES (EN: reportfraud.ftc.gov)): https://reportefraude.ftc.gov

**Key words:** *Family agreement:* what both sides understand and accept about the support. *Independent verification:* confirming through a channel you already knew.

## M2 U12. How do I save for something paid in pesos?

**What you will achieve:** plan goals in another country taking into account the exchange rate, ownership and documents.

### The essentials

If what you will pay for costs 1,000 pesos, start with that amount.

1. Write down the price and the currency.
2. Calculate the dollars needed with today's quote.
3. Repeat with a less favorable exchange rate.

> **Key idea:** define the goal in the currency it is paid in. The dollars needed change with the exchange rate.

### Go deeper

**A complete goal has:** amount, date, currency, who owns it and who benefits.

**Exchange-rate scenarios.** A 30,000-peso repair:

- At 15 pesos per dollar: 2,000 dollars.
- At 17 pesos per dollar: 1,764.71 dollars.

No rate is guaranteed. If the price also goes up 10%, the goal becomes 33,000 pesos.

**Ownership and documents.** "Helping build a house" does not say who will legally own it. Sending money does not make you an owner. If land is bought in someone else's name, your transfer receipt proves a transfer, not ownership. Before a major commitment, review deeds and contracts and consult a notary or attorney in Mexico.

**Taxes.** A gift, support, a loan or an investment can be treated differently for taxes in each country. Describe the real purpose and ask; do not use a label just because it seems to save taxes.

**Tracking:** contributions, fees, currency, balance set aside and updated cost of the goal. The same contribution does not count toward the goal and the emergency fund at the same time.

### Cases

**Case 1. The room for Alex's mother.** The room costs 34,000 pesos: 2,000 dollars at 17 pesos, or 2,125 at 16. Alex saves with a cushion for the less favorable scenario.

**Case 2. Land in the brother's name.** Mar sent money for three years for a piece of land. It is in her brother's name. Now she wants her share. Without a document that recognizes it, it is hard to prove.

### Interactive activity

**Goal in pesos (H5P simulator):** set a goal in pesos and move the exchange rate to see how many dollars you need each month.

### Quiz

1. In what currency do you define a goal paid in Mexico? a) Pesos · b) Dollars
2. Does your transfer receipt prove you own a piece of land? a) Yes · b) No
3. Can one contribution count toward two goals at once? a) Yes · b) No

**Answers:** 1-a · 2-b · 3-b.

### Practice

You send money for land in someone else's name. What needs to be clarified?

**Answer:** who owns it, what rights you have, what agreement exists, what documents prove it, how the money is used and what the tax consequences are.

> **Before you act, check:** the deed and property registry with a notary or attorney in Mexico before sending large amounts.

### For your plan

Choose a cross-border goal and record what is confirmed, what is pending and the professional you need.

### Learn more

Free, official resources to go further (verified September 28, 2026):

- **Compare remittances (Remesamex)** (CONDUSEF; language: ES): https://www.condusef.gob.mx/?p=remesas
- **Newcomer's guides to managing money** (CFPB; language: ES/EN): https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/blog/the-newcomers-guides-to-managing-money/

## M2 U13. How do I bring my account and remittances into one plan?

**What you will achieve:** bring together your account, payments, remittances and family agreement in a plan you can review.

### The essentials

Choosing an account and choosing how to send are connected decisions.

1. Draw where the money comes from.
2. Write each step until it reaches your family.
3. Note the cost, date and who is responsible for each step.

> **Key idea:** compare the money's **full journey**, not each piece separately.

### Go deeper

**Check for consistency:**

- An account that does not accept your documents is not a ready option.
- A transfer that arrives late does not solve an urgent need.
- A promotion is not the usual cost.
- Paying the remittance in cash can trigger the 1% tax; paying from the account usually does not.

**Monthly record:** how much left, how much arrived, date and reason. Compare what you planned with what happened and explain the differences. Sending less is not always better: it can reflect less capacity; sending more can fund an important goal or hide a debt.

**Stress test:** what happens if your income drops 20% and an extraordinary request arrives? And if the account asks for a document you do not have, the exchange rate gets worse and the transfer takes two extra days? Mark which part of the plan depends on each condition and have an alternative.

**Maintenance:** keep the version of your sheet and the next review date. Update it when fees, how you are paid or needs change.

### Cases

**Case 1. Alex's journey.** Payroll check → deposit at the counter → account with a deposit fee → remittance paid in cash at a store. After reviewing, he switches to an account with free counter deposits and pays the remittance from his account: he saves the deposit fee and the 1% tax.

**Case 2. The promotion that ended.** Mar's provider was the best with the introductory promotion, but now it delivers less than another with the same budget. Mar switches and verifies the recipient's details again.

### Interactive activity

**Map the journey (H5P diagram):** connect pay, account, payment and transfer, and see the total cost of the journey.

### Quiz

1. What better shows that you compared? a) Downloading an app · b) A sheet with two offers and a date
2. Is sending less always better? a) Yes · b) No
3. Does a promotion represent the usual cost? a) Yes · b) No

**Answers:** 1-b · 2-b · 3-b.
