# Integrative cases

The integrative cases use made-up data so you can practice without sharing personal information. Amounts without a currency are in dollars. Solve each case before reading the key.

## E1. Alex and Mar's calendar (Module 1)

**Data.** Alex is paid 600 at the start of each week for eight weeks. Mar is paid 200 at the start of weeks 2, 4, 6 and 8. The household's opening balance is 200.

- Every week: 220 for food and transportation.
- Weeks 1 and 5: rent of 1,000.
- Weeks 2 and 6: utilities of 100.
- Weeks 3 and 7: card payment of 80.
- Weeks 4 and 8: insurance of 120 and remittance of 150.

Income arrives before the same week's payments. These are net wages, with no taxes pending.

**Tasks.**

1. Calculate each week and explain the shortfall.
2. Design an alternative without making up income.
3. Assume Alex earns 480 in weeks 5 to 8 and recalculate.
4. Sort the expenses into essential, foreseeable and family support.
5. Write three 90-day actions.

**Key.**

| Week | Opening balance | Income | Outflows | Closing balance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 200 | 600 | 1,220 | −420 |
| 2 | −420 | 800 | 320 | 60 |
| 3 | 60 | 600 | 300 | 360 |
| 4 | 360 | 800 | 490 | 670 |
| 5 | 670 | 600 | 1,220 | 50 |
| 6 | 50 | 800 | 320 | 530 |
| 7 | 530 | 600 | 300 | 830 |
| 8 | 830 | 800 | 490 | 1,140 |

Totals: income 5,600; outflows 4,660; net change 940; plus opening balance 200 = 1,140.

Week 1 shows 420 of rent with no funds. The positive closing balance does not erase that problem.

**Possible alternative:** ask the landlord to split the first rent into 580 (week 1) and 420 (week 2) at no charge, and confirm the agreement in writing. Then the balances for weeks 1 and 2 would be 0 and 60, and the rest would not change. Not paying without an agreement is not a solution.

**With less income for Alex** (without the alternative), weeks 5 to 8 close at −70, 290, 470 and 660. A new shortfall appears in week 5.

**Additional tax practice.** A self-employed worker receives 2,000 through a platform and 300 in cash. The exercise's costs are 400. The platform deposits 2,000 into her bank and she moves 1,000 to another of her own accounts. Result before taxes: 2,300 − 400 = 1,900. The deposits and the transfer to her own account are not counted again.

## E2. An emergency and three remittances (Module 2)

**Data.** The family asks for 5,000 pesos. Your maximum is 310 dollars in total.

| Provider | Exchange rate | Fee | Delivery | Other costs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 17.00 | 5 | Next day | — |
| B | 16.70 | 0 | Same day | — |
| C | 17.10 | 8 | Same day | The recipient spends 80 pesos on transportation |

You pay from your debit account (no tax on cash-funded remittances). Made-up quotes from the same moment.

**Tasks.** Calculate how much you pay so that 5,000 pesos arrive net. Decide whether timing and access change your choice. Then verify a request that came from a new number and write a support agreement.

**Key.**

- A: 5,000 ÷ 17 + 5 = 299.12.
- B: 5,000 ÷ 16.70 = 299.40.
- C: (5,000 + 80) ÷ 17.10 + 8 = 305.08.

All fit within 310. A is the cheapest, but arrives the next day. If the need is for the same day, B covers it at a lower cost than C. Before sending, verify the urgency and the provider.

**Debt variant.** You only have 180 free. A loan would cover B's difference (119.40) with a flat 18 fee, due in 14 days: 137.40. If on that day you will only have 100 free, you are 37.40 short. Do not accept automatically: consider a partial transfer or other real options.

**Cash variant.** If you paid B in cash at a store and the transfer were subject to the 1% tax, you would add about 3 dollars.

## E3. Report and debt plan (Module 3)

**Data.** The sample report shows:

- Account A: limit 1,000, balance 600, recognized.
- Account B: balance 900, does not belong to the character.
- Account C: balance 250, although there is a payoff statement.
- Account D: a real two-month late payment.

**Key.** A's utilization: 60%. Investigate B as possible fraud and dispute C with the payoff statement. Do not report D as identity theft: it is real. Write two requests (B and C) with the account partially identified, the facts, the proof and what you are asking for. For D, build a plan based on your capacity.

**Loan comparison.** X provides 1,000 with 12 payments of 100 plus 30 upfront: total 1,230. Y provides 1,000 with 18 payments of 75: total 1,350. Y lowers the payment but costs 120 more. If your monthly cushion is 85, X does not fit and Y leaves only 10. Looking for another alternative is also a valid conclusion.

## E4. Protection and response (Module 4)

**Data.** Policy A: 80 a month, 1,000 deductible and 20% coinsurance. Policy B: 110 a month, 500 deductible and 10% coinsurance. Allowed service: 3,000. Reserve: 400. A message asks for an access code "to process the payment," and the bill includes an unknown 200 service.

**Key.**

- Event with A: 1,000 + 20% of 2,000 = 1,400. With B: 500 + 10% of 2,500 = 750.
- Yearly premiums: A 960; B 1,320.
- Event plus premiums: A 2,360; B 2,070.
- With no event, B costs 360 more a year.
- The 400 reserve does not cover the event with either policy.
- Do not share the code. Verify the contact and ask for an itemization of the unknown charge.
- B is not better for everyone: it depends on the risk and the ability to pay.

**Additional task.** Build the index of a family preparedness plan for Alex and Mar with the five sections from M4 U10, using made-up data.

## E5. Future and consistency (Module 5)

**Data.** Liquid assets 2,000; car estimated at 6,000; car loan 4,000; credit card 500. Basic expenses 1,800 a month. Cushion for goals: 120 a month, but a 40 insurance premium was forgotten. Goals: reserve, a 600 course in 12 months and long-term retirement.

**Key.**

- Net worth: 8,000 − 4,500 = 3,500. Liquidity: 2,000.
- Coverage if all liquidity were free: 2,000 ÷ 1,800 = 1.11 months.
- Corrected cushion: 120 − 40 = 80.
- The course requires 50 a month; 30 is left for other goals.
- Do not count the same 2,000 for every goal.

**Adverse scenario.** Income drops 200 a month: the 80 cushion becomes −120. The contributions can no longer be sustained. Review needs, support and dates, and document the gap.

**Final result.** Explain two decisions with: need, options, information, choice, risk and review date.

# Forms for your plan

Fill them in with case data or with your private data.

**F1 Calendar:** period; date; opening balance; confirmed income; estimated income; essential expense; debt; tax; remittance; reserve; closing balance; alert; action.

**F2 Compared decision:** need; option A; option B; amount; total cost; date; access; requirements; risk; source and date; what still needs confirming; decision; review.

**F3 Family agreement:** purpose; range; date; emergency; verification; review; who takes part; open items.

**F4 Debts:** creditor; type; balance; rate; fees; minimum payment; due date; collateral; late status; consequence; contact; strategy; next review.

**F5 Protection:** risk; impact; coverage or control; exclusion; reserve; gap; action; person responsible; date; contact.

**F6 Goal:** purpose; amount or range; currency; time frame; resources; possible contribution; assumption; adverse scenario; document; consultation; review.

**F7 Professional consultation:** minimum facts; decision; documents available; specific question; deadline; specialty; confirmed cost; secure channel; answer; next step.

**F8 Family preparedness plan:** caregiver and backup; document location; accounts and payments (no full numbers); authorizations; contacts; review date.
