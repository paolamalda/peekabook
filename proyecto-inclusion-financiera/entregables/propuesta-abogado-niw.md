# Proposed Endeavor Brief for Counsel

**Prepared by:** Paola Malda Arozarena

**Purpose:** Discussion document for an EB-2 National Interest Waiver (NIW) consultation

**Date:** September 28, 2026

**Status:** Draft prepared by the prospective petitioner for counsel's review. It is not a legal filing, and every factual claim and statistic must be verified before use in a petition.

## 1. Summary

I propose to build, operate and scale a free, bilingual (Spanish–English) financial capability program for immigrant and underserved households in the United States. The program helps households enter and safely use the regulated U.S. financial system. It covers:

- cash-flow management;
- tax compliance;
- safe use of regulated remittance providers;
- credit building and debt management;
- fraud and identity-theft prevention;
- long-term financial security.

The program combines four elements:
- a structured online curriculum;
- a continuously verified comparison of financial products and providers;
- a free companion planning app;
- a referral network to verified professional help.

It launches in California and expands to Texas, Illinois, New York and Florida. It has a federal core curriculum and state-specific modules. Outcomes are measured with nationally benchmarked instruments.

I bring twenty years of experience in financial services, public financial-inclusion policy and financial education. That experience includes national-level work on Mexico's National Financial Inclusion Policy and leadership of financial inclusion at a national development bank serving underserved households and remittance recipients.

## 2. The proposed endeavor

### 2.1 Components

| Component | Description |
|---|---|
| Curriculum | Five modules, about 59 short lessons, in separate Spanish and English editions: money and cash flow; the financial system and remittances; credit and debt; protection (fraud, identity, insurance, emergencies); and wealth and retirement. Each lesson has a three-minute core, deeper study, cases, interactive exercises and a quiz. |
| Living comparison matrix | Accounts, remittance providers, credit-building products and support services, each record carrying source, verification date and requirements by documentation type (SSN, ITIN, consular ID, none). Editorially independent: no provider can pay for placement. |
| Companion app | Free, privacy-first planning tools: dated cash-flow calendar, true-cost remittance calculator, goals in dollars or pesos, family remittance agreement, scam check, credit pathway and continuity folder. The app never holds or moves money and never collects SSNs, immigration status or banking credentials. |
| Verified help network | Directory of free tax preparation sites (VITA), certified legal referral services, housing counselors, consulate financial-advice windows, credit unions and community organizations. |
| Certificates | Program-issued, verifiable digital badges (Open Badges) for each module and for the full program. They are educational completion records, not licenses or accreditation. |

### 2.2 Rollout

| Phase | Scope | Indicative timing |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Pilot | California: curriculum, matrix, app MVP, partner onboarding and impact baseline | Months 0–12 |
| 2. Texas | State module and Texas partners | Months 12–18 |
| 3. Illinois | State module and Illinois partners | Months 18–24 |
| 4. New York | State module and New York City partners | Months 24–30 |
| 5. Florida | State module and Florida partners | Months 30–36 |

About 70% of the content is a shared federal core: banking, IRS, federal remittance rules, credit reporting and fraud. About 30% is state-specific: regulators, state tax credits, tenant rules, health coverage and identification.

### 2.3 Delivery and sustainability

- **Delivery:** Moodle-based learning platform (mobile-first, offline-capable), WhatsApp micro-learning, and in-person sessions through partner organizations.
- **Distribution partners (targeted):** consulate financial-advice windows (Ventanillas de Asesoría Financiera / IME), CDFI credit unions, Bank On coalitions, community and worker organizations, community colleges and libraries.
- **Funding model:** free to participants. Funded through foundation grants, corporate philanthropy and white-label licensing to partner institutions, with strict editorial independence.
- **Legal entity:** to be determined with counsel (see section 7).

## 3. Substantial merit and national importance (Prong 1)

### 3.1 National priorities the endeavor advances

| National priority | How the endeavor advances it | Federal reference points |
|---|---|---|
| Financial inclusion | Moves unbanked and underbanked households into safe, low-cost accounts | FDIC National Survey of Unbanked and Underbanked Households; Bank On National Account Standards |
| Consumer protection | Teaches rights under federal remittance, credit-reporting and debt-collection rules; reduces use of predatory products | CFPB (Remittance Rule, FCRA and FDCPA resources) |
| Fraud and identity-theft prevention | Scam-recognition training and response protocols tailored to schemes targeting immigrants, such as notario fraud and government impersonation | FTC Consumer Sentinel; IdentityTheft.gov |
| Tax compliance | Helps ITIN and SSN filers organize records, use free VITA help and avoid fraudulent preparers | IRS (ITIN, gig economy, VITA/TCE) |
| Formal-channel remittances | Encourages regulated, licensed providers and documented transactions over informal channels, supporting anti-money-laundering objectives | FinCEN / Bank Secrecy Act framework; CFPB Remittance Rule |
| Financial literacy | Implements the goals of the national strategy at scale for an underserved population | U.S. National Strategy for Financial Literacy (2020), Financial Literacy and Education Commission |
| Retirement and economic security | Introduces workplace and state retirement programs, beneficiary designations and continuity planning | DOL; SEC Investor.gov; state auto-IRA programs |

### 3.2 Indicators of scale (to verify and cite with sources and years)

- Share of U.S. households that are unbanked, and the higher rate among Hispanic households (FDIC 2023 survey).
- Annual remittances from the United States to Mexico and Latin America (Banco de México; World Bank).
- Reported consumer fraud losses nationally (FTC Consumer Sentinel Data Book 2024).
- Taxes paid by ITIN filers and immigrant households (IRS data; independent research such as ITEP).
- Immigrant population in the five rollout states (Census Bureau, American Community Survey).

### 3.3 Why the impact is national, not local

- Multi-state design with a replicable federal core and a documented state-module template.
- Distribution through national networks (credit-union associations, Bank On coalitions, national Latino organizations, consular networks across the country).
- Outcomes measured with national instruments (CFPB Financial Well-Being Scale and questions comparable with the FDIC and FINRA National Financial Capability Study). Results can therefore be compared with national benchmarks and reused by other programs.
- Open publication of the curriculum framework and annual impact reports.

## 5. Balance of factors (Prong 3)

- The endeavor serves the public interest and is free to participants. It does not depend on a single employer, and a labor certification process would not fit a founder-led public-benefit initiative.
- The need is urgent and ongoing: fraud targeting immigrants, recent federal tax changes affecting remittances, and changes to benefits and credits.
- The petitioner's combination of bilingual expertise, public-policy experience, remittance and cooperative-finance experience, and instructional design is uncommon.

## 6. Measurement plan (summary)

| Level | Indicator | Instrument | Timing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reach | Unique participants started, assessed, completed | Platform records | Continuous |
| Learning | Knowledge gain (paired pre/post) | Program item bank | Baseline and module close |
| Application | Documented comparison before a financial decision; remittance plan applied; credit action taken | Voluntary follow-up with evidence levels | Days 30 and 90 |
| Financial situation | Reserve coverage; debt and arrears change; share of financed remittances | Voluntary, paired, comparable periods | Days 90 and 180 |
| Well-being | CFPB Financial Well-Being Scale | Official instrument and scoring | Baseline and day 180 |

An academic partner will be invited to conduct an independent evaluation. Missing responses are reported as missing. Before-and-after results are reported as associations, not causal effects.

## 7. Questions for counsel

1. Is the endeavor framed specifically enough, and is national importance best argued through financial inclusion, consumer protection, fraud prevention, tax compliance and formal remittance channels?
2. How should the immigrant focus be presented given current adjudication trends? Should the endeavor be described as serving "immigrant and underserved households"?
3. What entity structure best supports the petition and the funding model: 501(c)(3), fiscal sponsorship, public benefit corporation, cooperative, or a U.S. affiliate of Desarrolla Talento?
4. How much operating evidence (pilot results, partner letters, funding) should exist before filing?
5. Which recommendation letters carry the most weight: U.S.-based experts, Mexican government officials, academic evaluators or partner organizations?
6. How should talks without certificates be documented: programs, videos, archived pages, organizer letters?
7. Does the MBA require a credential evaluation for the advanced-degree requirement, and is exceptional ability worth arguing as an alternative?
8. How do recent USCIS policy-manual updates on NIW affect the framing or the evidence?
9. Timing and process: consular processing or adjustment of status, and whether the petitioner can work on the pilot remotely before approval.
10. Are there activities that must be avoided to stay clear of unauthorized practice of law or immigration consulting? The curriculum does not provide immigration advice.
