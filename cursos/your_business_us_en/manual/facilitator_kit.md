# Facilitator kit · Your Business, Your Money, Your Future · U.S.

One 60-minute session per module, in person or online, for groups of 8 to 20 people. Everything comes from the module's lessons: key ideas, cases and practice. Use it after the group has worked through the module or as a review.

## Rules for every session

- No one shares personal data, account numbers or real amounts: we use made-up examples.
- We don't recommend institutions or products: we teach how to verify and compare.
- We don't give personalized tax, legal or immigration advice: we point to where to go.
- Talking about money is hard: thank every contribution and don't judge.

## Stress signs and where to refer

Watch for someone who misses sessions often, withdraws, mentions skipping food or medicine, borrowing from lenders or apps for basic expenses, threats over a debt, or not sleeping because of money. Talk privately, without pressure, and share these options:

- 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text, English and Spanish)
- CFPB: consumerfinance.gov (complaints about financial companies)
- 211 (local help with food, rent and bills)
- National Domestic Violence Hotline: 1-800-799-7233
- 911 (emergencies)

## Module 1. Your business and your home: separate money

**Outcome:** Your business money separate from your home, a tax account, a fixed salary and up-to-date records.

**Materials:** phone or projector with the module book, printed pages of the support book, case cards, pens.

| Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | Welcome and opening question: “What step did you take with your money this week?” |
| 5–15 | Key ideas of the module (below). Ask someone to explain one in their own words. |
| 15–35 | Cases in groups of 3 or 4 (below). Each group decides and explains why. |
| 35–50 | Practice: solve the exercises (below) and compare. |
| 50–57 | My commitment: each person writes one step with a date and tells their group partner. |
| 57–60 | Close: remind them of the module self-assessment and the next meeting. |

**Key ideas**

- M1 U01 · Is your business making money or just selling?: don't measure your business by what you sell, but by what you keep after paying everything.
- M1 U02 · Keep business money separate from home money: separate the money and pay yourself a salary; you'll know whether your business makes money and your expenses will be ready for taxes.
- M1 U03 · Pay yourself a salary: taxes first, then reserve, then your fixed salary; that way neither your home nor the IRS catches you off guard.
- M1 U04 · Records in 10 minutes a day: write things down every day, keep receipts and check every week; without records there are no decisions and no correct tax return.
- M1 U05 · Online betting: business money isn't for gambling: business money and tax money are never for betting; chasing losses or betting on credit means it's already a problem.
- M1 U06 · Your business, your decisions: your money in your name: your skills are your business's capital; keep your account, your payments and your decisions in your name, and if someone controls your money, get help.

**Cases**

- **M1 U01 · Lupita's month.** Lupita sold more than ever in December thanks to tamales, but she paid more for ingredients and help. *What does she do to know whether she made money?* — Expected answer: She subtracts all the month's costs from her sales before celebrating.
- **M1 U02 · Lupita's account.** Lupita receives business payments and her paycheck in the same account. *What does she do?* — Expected answer: She opens an account just for the business and links her payments there.
- **M1 U03 · Lupita's great week.** Lupita sold three times as much in Christmas week. *What does she do?* — Expected answer: She pays herself her usual salary and sets the extra aside for taxes and the reserve.

**Practice**

- **M1 U01.** You sell $2,500 a month. You spend $1,100 on materials, $300 on gas and $100 on fees. How much do you earn? — Answer: $2,500 minus $1,500: $1,000.
- **M1 U02.** In one week $900 came into the business account and you paid $520 for materials. How much is left in the business? — Answer: $380.

**Key ideas**

- M2 U01 · Fixed and variable costs: add to each product what you spend on it plus its share of your fixed costs.
- M2 U02 · How much should you charge?: your cost is the floor; your margin lets you grow and pay taxes; competition and value tell you how high you can go.
- M2 U03 · Your break-even point: know your minimum daily sales; it's your target so you don't lose.
- M2 U04 · Inventory and waste: buy what moves, measure what you throw out and don't leave your money sitting still.

**Cases**

- **M2 U01 · Daniela's earrings.** Daniela thinks she makes $19 a pair because materials cost $6 and she sells them for $25. *What is she missing?* — Expected answer: Packaging, shipping, fees and her share of fixed costs.
- **M2 U02 · Javier's price.** Javier charges the same as other painters, even though his costs are higher. *What does he do?* — Expected answer: He figures out his cost per hour and sets a price with margin, explaining his insurance and guarantee.
- **M2 U03 · Don Ramón's spot.** Don Ramón's parking spot goes up $400 a month. *What does he do?* — Expected answer: He recalculates his break-even point and daily target.

**Practice**

- **M2 U01.** A cake costs you $18 in ingredients and $2 for the box. Your fixed costs are $400 a month and you sell 50 cakes. What is the cost per cake? — Answer: $18 plus $2 plus $8 ($400 divided by 50): $28.
- **M2 U02.** A service costs you $120. You want a 25% margin on price. How much do you charge? — Answer: $120 divided by 0.75: $160.

**Key ideas**

- M3 U01 · Profit isn't cash: look at your next four weeks: cash problems get solved ahead of time, not on the day money runs out.
- M3 U02 · Customer credit, invoices and collections: selling on credit is lending; ask for a deposit, put everything in writing and invoice the same day.
- M3 U03 · Your reserve and slow seasons: in good months, set money aside for slow ones; a reserve keeps you away from expensive debt.
- M3 U04 · Inflation, interest rates and compound interest in your business: inflation raises your costs; review costs and prices every quarter and protect your margin.

**Cases**

- **M3 U01 · Javier's late payment.** The contractor pays Javier in 30 days and he needs materials now. *What does he do?* — Expected answer: He asks for a deposit and negotiates terms with his supplier.
- **M3 U02 · Lupita's orders.** A customer orders 20 dozen for a wedding and promises to pay later. *What does she do?* — Expected answer: She asks for a 50% deposit and puts the price and payment date in writing.
- **M3 U03 · Don Ramón's winter.** Don Ramón knows January and February are slow. *What does he do in summer?* — Expected answer: He sets aside a percentage of each sale in a savings account.

**Practice**

- **M3 U01.** Starting balance $800. Week 1: you collect $1,200 and pay $1,500. Week 2: you collect $600 and pay $1,200. How do you end each week? — Answer: Week 1: $500. Week 2: $500 plus $600 minus $1,200: $100 short.
- **M3 U02.** A $480 order with a 50% deposit. How much do you collect up front and how much at delivery? — Answer: $240 and $240.

**Key ideas**

- M4 U02 · Safe app and transfer payments: deliver only when the money is available in your account, not when you get a screenshot or an email.
- M4 U03 · Scams when you sell: never send back money from an overpayment; rush, extra money and changed accounts are signs of a scam.

**Cases**

- **M4 U01 · Don Ramón's reader.** Don Ramón is offered a "at no cost" reader with a two-year contract. *What does he do?* — Expected answer: He calculates the total cost with his sales and reads the contract before signing.

- **M4 U03 · Javier's check.** A customer sends Javier a $2,500 check for a $1,500 job and asks for $1,000 back. *What does he do?* — Expected answer: He doesn't deposit it or send anything back; he asks for payment of the exact amount.

**Practice**

- **M4 U01.** With option B (2.3% plus $30 a month), above what monthly sales is it cheaper than A (2.6% with no fee)? — Answer: When 0.3% of sales is more than $30: over $10,000 a month.
- **M4 U02.** You get 10 app orders a day. If checking each one takes 30 seconds, how much time a day does confirming take? — Answer: 5 minutes a day.

**Key ideas**

- M5 U01 · Sole proprietor, LLC or something else?: an LLC protects, but it costs; compare it with your risk and your insurance, and do the paperwork on official sites.
- M5 U02 · Numbers and permits: EIN, ITIN and licenses: the EIN and the seller's permit cost nothing on official sites; check with your city and county which licenses your trade needs.
- M5 U03 · Your federal taxes as an owner: as an owner you pay 15.3% for Social Security and Medicare, plus income tax; set aside 25% to 30% of your profit and deduct your expenses with receipts.
- M5 U04 · Quarterly payments and 1099 forms: pay your taxes every quarter and report all your income; the IRS never demands gift cards or threatens arrest by phone.
- M5 U05 · Sales tax: sales tax belongs to the state, not you; find out which sales are taxable, charge it separately and set it aside.

**Cases**

- **M5 U01 · Javier's LLC.** A website charges Javier $299 to open a "required" LLC. *What does he do?* — Expected answer: He compares costs and risks, and if he opens one, he uses the state's official site.
- **M5 U02 · Lupita's EIN.** A website charges Lupita $150 to get her EIN. *What does she do?* — Expected answer: She gets it no-cost at irs.gov with her ITIN.
- **M5 U03 · Daniela's surprise.** Daniela owes the IRS more than $2,000 and didn't set anything aside. *What does she do from today on?* — Expected answer: She sets aside 25% of each profit and keeps receipts to deduct.

**Practice**

- **M5 U01.** You keep an LLC in California for 3 years. What is the minimum you pay in annual tax? — Answer: $800 times 3: $2,400.
- **M5 U02.** A website charges you $150 for the EIN and $100 for the seller's permit. How much do you save by doing it yourself? — Answer: $250.

## Module 6. Credit for your business

**Outcome:** Knowing whether you need credit, what it really costs and how not to mix it with personal debt.

**Materials:** phone or projector with the module book, printed pages of the support book, case cards, pens.

| 15–35 | Cases in groups of 3 or 4 (below). Each group decides and explains why. |
| 35–50 | Practice: solve the exercises (below) and compare. |
| 50–57 | My commitment: each person writes one step with a date and tells their group partner. |
| 57–60 | Close: remind them of the module self-assessment and the next meeting. |

**Key ideas**

- M6 U01 · Do you need credit?: borrow for something that brings in sales and run the numbers with a cautious scenario.
- M6 U02 · Credit options and how to check them: compare banks, credit unions and CDFIs; anyone who asks for money up front to give you a loan is a scammer.
- M6 U03 · The total cost: APR, fees and guarantees: compare APR and total repayment, not the daily payment or how fast you get the money.
- M6 U04 · Don't mix debts, and be careful about cosigning: record business debts separately, lend with rules and only cosign if you could pay it yourself.
- M6 U05 · Cosigner, guarantor, authorized user and reference: what are you signing?: cosigners and guarantors pay if the other person or the business doesn't; a reference only gives their details. Read what you're signing and sign only if you could pay that debt.

**Cases**

- **M6 U01 · Don Ramón's equipment.** Don Ramón figures the loan pays for itself, but with optimistic sales. *What does he do?* — Expected answer: He checks a cautious scenario before signing.
- **M6 U02 · The message to Lupita.** A message offers Lupita $5,000 if she deposits a $200 opening fee. *What does she do?* — Expected answer: She doesn't deposit and looks for verified options; it's a scam.
- **M6 U03 · Don Ramón's two offers.** Don Ramón compares a loan at 14% APR with an advance at a 1.35 factor. *What does he do?* — Expected answer: He asks for each one's total repayment in writing and compares them.

**Practice**

- **M6 U01.** Equipment would bring $600 a month in extra profit. In a cautious scenario, half that. The payment is $250. Is it worth it? — Answer: Cautious: $300 minus $250: you keep $50. It's worth it, with a small margin.
- **M6 U02.** An advance gives you $10,000 and charges you $13,500 in 6 months. How much extra do you pay? — Answer: $3,500 in just 6 months.

**Key ideas**

- M7 U01 · Your health when you work for yourself: if you work for yourself, check the marketplace, Medi-Cal and community clinics; an emergency without insurance can cost you your business.
- M7 U02 · Insurance for your business: insure what you couldn't replace and what your clients ask for; your personal insurance almost never covers the business.
- M7 U03 · Scams against businesses: no authority collects by phone or demands immediate payment with gift cards; check the official site before paying any letter.
- M7 U04 · Your brand and your name: search the USPTO before investing in a name and register it once you start selling beyond your area.
- M7 U05 · Identity theft and unwanted calls: freeze your credit, check your reports and register your number; if someone uses your data, report it at IdentityTheft.gov.
- M7 U06 · AI scams: fake voices, videos and messages: a familiar voice or face is no longer proof; confirm any request for money through a channel you choose.
- M7 U07 · If disaster strikes: your business ready: backups, photos, the right coverage and a reserve in an account; if a disaster is declared, federal help costs nothing on official sites.

**Cases**

- **M7 U01 · Javier's back.** Javier has no health insurance and works for himself. *What does he do?* — Expected answer: He checks the marketplace and Medi-Cal with his estimated income.
- **M7 U02 · The food truck fire.** Don Ramón's personal insurance didn't cover his food truck fire. *What does he do?* — Expected answer: He buys commercial insurance with auto, liability and equipment.
- **M7 U03 · Daniela's letter.** An "official" letter charges Daniela $243 for an LLC certificate. *What does she do?* — Expected answer: She checks the sender and verifies on the Secretary of State's site before paying.

**Practice**

- **M7 U01.** Your premium is $180 a month and your deductible is $1,500. How much could you pay in a year if you use the whole deductible? — Answer: $2,160 in premiums plus $1,500: $3,660.
- **M7 U02.** A theft causes $6,000 in damage and the deductible is $1,000. How much does the insurer pay? — Answer: $5,000.

**Key ideas**

- M8 U02 · Selling online and on platforms: set your price by channel, with all fees and shipping, and report everything you sell.
- M8 U03 · Your monthly numbers: five numbers a month tell you what to improve: sales, margin, profit, cash and average ticket.
- M8 U04 · Stress, business and family: your business needs you to be well; set hours, one day off and a weekly money talk lower the stress.
- M8 U05 · Your one-page business plan and how to pitch it: your plan fits on one page and your pitch in two minutes; with numbers from your records and no-cost advice, seek financing from verified sources, and if you hear no, ask what to improve.

- **M8 U01 · Lupita's neighbor.** Lupita wants to pay her neighbor in cash "as a contractor" to work in her kitchen on her schedule. *What does she do?* — Expected answer: She recognizes the neighbor would be an employee and registers as an employer.
- **M8 U02 · The platform price.** Daniela charges the same on the platform as on her own site. *What does she check?* — Expected answer: That the platform price covers fees, shipping and ads.
- **M8 U03 · Don Ramón's margin.** Don Ramón's margin dropped five points. *What does he do?* — Expected answer: He checks which costs went up and adjusts suppliers or prices.

**Practice**

- **M8 U01.** Someone works 15 hours a week at $16.90. What are their wages over 4 weeks? — Answer: 60 hours times $16.90: $1,014.
- **M8 U02.** You sell 60 pieces at $30 in a month; the platform charges 12% in total and shipping is $4 a piece. How much do you keep before materials? — Answer: $1,800 minus $216 minus $240: $1,344.

**Key ideas**

- M9 U01 · Your retirement when you work for yourself: keep your retirement separate from your business; check your Social Security and put something into a retirement account every month.
- M9 U02 · If the business changes or you're not there: put in one folder what someone would need to keep your business running, or to close it, without you.
- M9 U03 · Your one-page plan: your plan fits on one page; review it every three months, improve one thing at a time and keep your commitment with a name, a witness and a reminder.
- M9 U04 · If you're not there: beneficiary accounts, your home, digital access and taxes: with beneficiaries on your accounts, a transfer on death deed for your home and a will or living trust, your family avoids court; inheriting pays no federal tax except for huge estates, but a large foreign inheritance is reported on Form 3520.

**Cases**

- **M9 U01 · Don Ramón's retirement.** Don Ramón doesn't know how much he'd get from Social Security. *What does he do?* — Expected answer: He checks his record and estimate at ssa.gov.
- **M9 U02 · Lupita's illness.** Lupita's daughter didn't know how to keep orders going. *What does Lupita prepare?* — Expected answer: A folder with suppliers, accounts, payments and contacts.
- **M9 U03 · Daniela's page.** Daniela is missing her retirement account and her trademark. *What does she do?* — Expected answer: She makes them goals for next quarter.

**Practice**

- **M9 U01.** You put $200 a month in an IRA for 25 years, not counting returns. How much did you contribute? — Answer: $200 times 300 months: $60,000.
- **M9 U02.** You forget to dissolve your California LLC for 3 years with no activity. How much minimum tax could you owe, not counting penalties? — Answer: $2,400.
