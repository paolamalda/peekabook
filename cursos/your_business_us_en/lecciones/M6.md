# M6 U01 | Do you need credit?
objetivo: Tell apart credit that helps you grow from credit that only plugs holes, and decide with numbers whether it's worth it.
gancho: Don Ramón is offered $15,000 to buy a second set of kitchen equipment and open longer hours. He's excited, but doesn't know whether the food truck can pay it back. In this lesson you'll see how to decide.

== esencial

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | What you borrow for
* fa-line-chart | To grow | Equipment or merchandise that brings sales. | It can pay for itself. | si
* fa-refresh | For working capital | Seasons or big orders. | If you already have the customer. | igual
* fa-medkit | To cover losses | The business isn't making money. | It makes the problem bigger. | no
* fa-home | For household expenses | With business credit. | Mixing. | no

--- paso | fa-calculator | The key question
How much more will you earn with what you buy on credit? If the extra monthly profit is **greater** than the monthly payment, the credit can pay for itself. If not, you'll pay it from your salary or reserve.

--- ecuacion | fa-calculator | Don Ramón's equipment
= $700 | Expected extra monthly profit
- $480 | Monthly loan payment
= $220 | What he keeps
If extra sales were half as much, he'd be $130 short each month.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Don Ramón ran the numbers with a cautious scenario: only half the expected sales. He'd be $130 short a month. He decided to borrow $8,000 for smaller equipment, with a $260 payment.

> **Key idea:** borrow for something that brings in sales and run the numbers with a cautious scenario.

--- comprueba
1. When can a loan pay for itself? || When the extra profit it brings is greater than the monthly payment.
2. Why not borrow to cover losses? || Because the business isn't making money and the debt makes the problem bigger.

--- recuerda
- Credit to grow, not to plug holes.
- Extra profit greater than the payment.
- Cautious scenario.

Customer deposits, supplier terms, selling stuck inventory or using part of your reserve can solve it without credit. If the problem is price or costs (M2), credit won't fix it.

# M6 U02 | Credit options and how to check them
objetivo: Learn the credit options for small businesses, including ones that accept an ITIN, and spot expensive or fake loans.
gancho: Lupita got a message: "Business loan with no credit check. Deposit a $200 opening fee and get $5,000 today." In this lesson you'll see the real options and how to spot scams.

== esencial

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | Options you can look at
* fa-university | Banks and credit unions | Loans and lines of credit. | They ask for history. | si
* fa-hand-o-right | CDFIs and nonprofit microlenders | Small loans; some accept an ITIN. | With coaching. | si
* fa-building | SBA microloans | Through intermediaries; all owners must be citizens. | Up to $50,000. | si
* fa-exclamation-triangle | Merchant cash advances (MCA) | Daily payments; very expensive. | Avoid them. | no

> **Current fact:** the SBA microloan program offers loans of up to $50,000 through nonprofit organizations, which often also provide coaching. Since April 1, 2026, all business owners must be U.S. citizens or nationals. People with an ITIN, green card or visa can look for CDFIs that lend their own funds. Accessed September 30, 2026 through the SBA (policy notice 5000-877232).

--- pasos | fa-search | Check before you apply
1. Look up the lender's exact name and official website.
2. In California, check its license with the {{DFPI|California Department of Financial Protection and Innovation, which oversees lenders and debt collectors.}}; in other states, with the state financial regulator.
3. Ask for the total cost in writing (M6 U03).
4. Never pay up front to "release" a loan.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Lupita deleted the message: asking for an up-front fee is a sign of a scam. She found a CDFI in her area that works with ITINs; it offered her a $3,000 microloan with no-cost business classes.

> **Key idea:** compare banks, credit unions and CDFIs; anyone who asks for money up front to give you a loan is a scammer.

--- comprueba
1. What are CDFIs? || Community development financial institutions that lend to small businesses, sometimes with an ITIN.
2. What do you do if you're asked for a fee to release a loan? || Don't pay: it's a scam.

--- recuerda
- Banks, credit unions, CDFIs, SBA.
- Check the license.
- Never pay up front.

== profundiza

--- tema | fa-exclamation-triangle | Merchant cash advances and payday loans
A merchant cash advance gives you money in exchange for a percentage of your daily sales. It isn't presented as a loan or with an {{APR|Annual percentage rate: the yearly cost of credit, including interest and some fees.}}, and its cost can be extremely high. Payday loans are also very expensive. Avoid them or compare very carefully.

--- tema | fa-id-card-o | Credit with an ITIN
Some banks, credit unions and CDFIs offer accounts, secured cards and loans with an ITIN. Filing your taxes and keeping an orderly business account help you prove your income.

--- tema | fa-file-text-o | What they'll ask for
ID, tax returns, business account statements, licenses and sometimes a business plan. That's why separating your money (M1) and filing (M5) open better options.

> **Before you act, check:** the lender's license with your state regulator (in California, the DFPI) and compare the total cost in writing.

--- casos
### Case 1. The message to Lupita
A message offers Lupita $5,000 if she deposits a $200 opening fee.
? What does she do? || She doesn't deposit and looks for verified options; it's a scam.

### Case 2. Don Ramón's advance
A company offers Don Ramón $10,000 in exchange for 15% of his daily sales.
? What does he do? || He figures out the total cost and compares it with a bank, credit union or CDFI loan.

### Case 3. Daniela's loan
Daniela has an ITIN and thinks nobody will lend to her.
? What does she do? || She looks for credit unions and CDFIs that work with ITINs.

--- errores
* Paying to "release" | Scam | Never
* Not checking the license | Illegal lender | State regulator
* Taking merchant advances | Extremely high cost | Compare
* Thinking an ITIN means no options | You miss healthy credit | Look for CDFIs

# M6 U03 | The total cost: APR, fees and guarantees
objetivo: Compare credit by its total cost, understand APR and "factor rate," and review the guarantees.
gancho: Don Ramón is offered two options for $10,000. One is a 12-month credit union loan at 14% APR. The other is an advance with a "factor of 1.35." They sound similar, but they're not. In this lesson you'll see how to compare them.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-percent | APR
The {{APR|annual percentage rate: the yearly cost of credit, with interest and certain fees.}} sums up the yearly cost of credit with interest and certain fees. It's for comparing credit: **lower APR, cheaper.** If someone won't give you the APR, ask for the total you'll repay.

--- ecuacion | fa-calculator | What you actually pay
= $10,000 | Advance received
× 1.35 | Factor rate
= $13,500 | Total to repay in 6 months
The credit union loan, at 14% APR over 12 months, costs about $775 in total interest.

--- pasos | fa-list-ol | Compare with these questions
1. What is the APR?
2. How much will I pay in total?
3. What fees are there: origination, late payment, prepayment?
4. What guarantee do they ask for: personal guarantee, equipment, home?
5. Can I pay early without a penalty?

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Don Ramón asked for everything in writing. The advance would cost him $3,500 in six months; the credit union, about $775 in a year. He chose the credit union even though approval took two weeks longer.

> **Key idea:** compare APR and total repayment, not the daily payment or how fast you get the money.

--- comprueba
1. What is APR? || The yearly cost of credit with interest and certain fees.
2. What do you do if they won't give you the APR? || Ask for the total repayment in writing and compare.

--- recuerda
- Lower APR, cheaper.
- Total repayment.
- Ask about fees and guarantees.

--- tema | fa-lock | Guarantees
Many business loans require your personal guarantee: if the business doesn't pay, you do, even if you have an LLC. Some ask for property as collateral. Don't put your home up as collateral for a business that hasn't proven it makes money.

--- tema | fa-file-text-o | California disclosure
California requires many commercial lenders to disclose the estimated cost in writing, including an APR, on loans and advances of up to $500,000. Ask for it.

--- casos

# M6 U04 | Don't mix debts, and be careful about cosigning
objetivo: Avoid using personal debt for the business, lend to family with rules and understand the risk of cosigning.
gancho: Daniela pays for her materials with her personal credit card and already owes $4,500. And Don Ramón's nephew asks him to cosign a car loan. In this lesson you'll see the risks.

== esencial

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | Dangerous mixes
* fa-credit-card | Personal card for the business | High interest if you don't pay in full. | Hidden debt. | no
* fa-users | Loans to family | Without rules, they don't come back. | Business money. | no
* fa-handshake-o | Cosigning | If they don't pay, you do. | Hurts your credit. | no
* fa-columns | Separate, recorded debts | You know what the business owes. | Control. | si

--- paso | fa-credit-card | The personal card
Using your card for the business can work if you pay in full each month (no interest) and keep receipts. If you only pay the minimum, the debt grows at more than 20% interest a year and mixes with your personal spending.

--- paso | fa-handshake-o | Cosigning
A {{cosigner|a person who signs a loan and agrees to pay it if the borrower doesn't.}} is responsible for the debt if the borrower doesn't pay. It shows up on your credit report. **Only sign for a debt you could pay yourself without putting your business at risk.**

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Don Ramón figured out the nephew's car payment: $520 a month. He couldn't pay it if the nephew fell behind. He kindly said no and helped him find a cheaper car he could pay on his own.

> **Key idea:** record business debts separately, lend with rules and only cosign if you could pay it yourself.

--- comprueba
1. When does a personal card work for the business? || Only if you pay in full each month and keep receipts.
2. What do you risk by cosigning? || Paying the debt yourself and hurting your credit.

--- recuerda
- Card: pay in full every month.
- Family loans with rules.
- Cosign only if you could pay.

== profundiza

--- tema | fa-list | If you already mixed
List the debts you used for the business. Pay them first with business money, starting with the highest interest. Stop buying materials with the card while you pay it down.

--- tema | fa-line-chart | Business credit
A card in the business's name, used carefully and paid in full, helps separate expenses. Check your personal credit report at no cost every year at AnnualCreditReport.com.

--- tema | fa-comments | How to say no
"I want to help you, but I can't risk my business. Let's find another way." Saying no protects your family and your business.

--- casos
### Case 1. Daniela's card
Daniela owes $4,500 on her personal card for materials.
? What does she do? || She stops using it for the business and pays it off with business money.

### Case 2. The cosigner
Don Ramón's nephew asks him to cosign a $520-a-month car loan.
? What does he do? || He doesn't accept because he couldn't pay it, and helps him find another option.

### Case 3. The loan to a friend
Lupita's close friend asks her for $400 of business money.
? What does she do? || She lends only from her salary or savings, in writing and with dates.

--- errores
* Paying the minimum | Debt grows | Pay in full
* Signing without doing the math | You pay | Only if you could
* Lending from the business | No cash | From your salary, in writing
* Not checking your credit | Uncorrected errors | No-cost report

# M6 U05 | Cosigner, guarantor, authorized user and reference: what are you signing?
objetivo: Tell apart cosigner, guarantor, authorized user and reference, know what you can be charged in each case and decide before signing for another person or for your business.
gancho: Don Ramón's son asks him to cosign a car loan. The bank asks Daniela for a personal guarantee on her LLC's loan. And a collector called Javier about a coworker's debt. In this lesson you'll see what each role means.

== esencial

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | Each role, a responsibility
* fa-pencil | Cosigner | You sign the loan: if they don't pay, you owe all of it. | It shows on your credit report. | no
* fa-shield | Guarantor (personal guarantee) | You pay with your money if the business or person doesn't. | Common on LLC loans. | no
* fa-credit-card | Authorized user | You use someone else's card. | Generally not responsible for the debt. | igual
* fa-address-card | Reference | You only give your contact details. | You can't be charged. | si

--- paso | fa-briefcase | Your business's personal guarantee
Even with an LLC, many lenders ask for a {{personal guarantee|a promise to pay with your own money and property if the business doesn't repay the loan.}}. If you sign it, the LLC no longer separates that debt from your personal assets. Read it before signing and negotiate a limit if you can.

--- paso | fa-address-card | Being a reference isn't being a cosigner
When someone applies for a card, a loan or a rental, they're asked for references. Being a {{reference|a person who only gives their details to confirm they know the applicant; they don't sign or promise to pay.}} doesn't obligate you to pay anything. If you didn't sign the loan, the debt isn't yours.

> **Current fact:** the federal Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA) only lets a collector contact other people to find out how to locate you. It forbids telling them you owe a debt. A cosigner may have to pay the full amount, plus late fees or collection costs. Accessed September 30, 2026 through the CFPB and the FTC.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Don Ramón read that, as a cosigner, he'd owe the whole car if his son stopped paying. It would also show on his report. He chose to help his son save a bigger down payment instead. Javier learned that, as a reference, he can't be charged anything.

> **Key idea:** cosigners and guarantors pay if the other person or the business doesn't; a reference only gives their details. Read what you're signing and sign only if you could pay that debt.

--- comprueba
1. Can you be charged if you were only a reference? || No. You only gave your details; you didn't sign the loan.
2. What happens if you sign a personal guarantee for your LLC? || You pay with your own money if the business doesn't.

--- recuerda
- Cosigner and guarantor: pay if the other doesn't.
- Reference: details only; you're not charged.
- Sign only if you could pay.

== profundiza

|---|---|---|---|

--- tema | fa-comments | How to say no
Asking you to cosign is asking you to take on a debt. You can say: "I want to help, but I can't sign a debt I couldn't pay." Offer other help: saving a down payment, comparing loans or building credit with a secured card.

--- tema | fa-shield | If a collector calls you as a reference
If a collector pressures you to pay a debt for which you were only a reference, don't pay or share extra details. Write down the name, date and time and submit it to the CFPB.

> **Before you act, check:** what the document you're asked to sign says and what role you have in it.

* Thinking the LLC always protects you | You pay with your money | Check the guarantee
* Thinking a reference pays | You pay what you don't owe | It doesn't obligate you
* Cosigning out of pressure | You end up paying | Only if you could pay
