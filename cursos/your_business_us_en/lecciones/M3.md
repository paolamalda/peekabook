# M3 U01 | Profit isn't cash
objetivo: Understand cash flow and build a simple calendar of money in and out so you don't run short.
gancho: Javier finished painting a house for a contractor for $4,500, but he'll be paid in 30 days. Meanwhile he has to pay for paint, gas and his quarterly tax payment. In this lesson you'll see why making a profit isn't the same as having money.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-exchange | What cash flow is
{{Cash flow|the money that actually comes into and goes out of the business, and when.}} is the money that really comes in and goes out, and **when**. You can have a profit on paper and not have cash to pay this week, because you get paid late or because you bought a lot at once.

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | What affects your cash flow
* fa-clock-o | Late payments | Net-30 invoices or customers who fall behind. | Money arrives later. | no
* fa-shopping-cart | Big purchases | Materials or equipment. | A lot goes out at once. | igual
* fa-calendar | Fixed payments | Insurance, permits, quarterly taxes. | They always come. | igual
* fa-handshake-o | Deposits | You get paid before working. | Improves your cash flow. | si

--- pasos | fa-calendar | Your 4-week calendar
1. Write down how much money the business has today.
2. Write down, by week, what you expect to collect.
3. Write down, by week, what you have to pay (include quarterly taxes).
4. Add and subtract: if any week goes negative, act ahead of time.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Javier's calendar showed he would be $700 short in week 3 because of his tax payment. He asked for a 30% deposit on the next job and arranged 30-day terms with the paint store.

> **Key idea:** look at your next four weeks: cash problems get solved ahead of time, not on the day money runs out.

--- comprueba
1. Why can you make a profit and have no cash? || Because you get paid later or pay before you collect.
2. What is the calendar for? || To see ahead of time the weeks when money will be short.

--- recuerda
- Cash flow = when it comes in and goes out.
- 4-week calendar.
- Act before it runs out.

--- tema | fa-handshake-o | Deposits and terms
Ask for a deposit on big jobs, in writing. If a customer pays in 30 days, ask your supplier for similar terms. Check your state's rules on deposits: in California, for example, home improvement contracts limit the down payment.

--- tema | fa-exclamation-triangle | Expensive credit to plug holes
If your calendar shows a gap, don't plug it with credit card cash advances, payday loans or advances on future sales (merchant cash advances): their cost eats your profit (M6).

--- casos
### Case 1. Javier's late payment
The contractor pays Javier in 30 days and he needs materials now.
? What does he do? || He asks for a deposit and negotiates terms with his supplier.

### Case 2. Don Ramón's purchase
Don Ramón wants to buy all the supplies for a festival at once, which would leave him without cash for two weeks.
? What does he do? || He checks his calendar and buys in parts or on terms.

### Case 3. Daniela's advance
Daniela is $300 short to pay her supplier and an app offers her an advance on future sales.
? What does she do first? || She checks her reserve and asks the supplier for more time before using expensive credit.

--- errores
* Looking only at profit | You run out of cash | Look at cash flow
* Not asking for deposits | You finance the customer | Written deposit
* Forgetting quarterly taxes | Gap | Put them on the calendar
* Plugging with expensive credit | You lose profit | Reserve and terms

# M3 U02 | Customer credit, invoices and collections
objetivo: Set rules for selling on credit, send clear invoices and collect respectfully and within the law.
gancho: Lupita has $650 owed by customers who ordered food for parties and will "pay later." Javier has a $1,200 invoice that is 45 days late. In this lesson you'll see how to set rules and collect.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-handshake-o | Selling on credit is lending
When you deliver before you get paid, you lend your money no-interest. It can help you sell more, but if it grows without control, it leaves you without cash.

--- pasos | fa-list-ol | Clear rules
1. For big orders, ask for a deposit (for example, 50%).
2. Put the price, delivery date and payment date in writing.
3. Send an {{invoice|a document with what you sold, the amount and the payment due date.}} the same day you deliver.
4. Keep a list of who owes you, how much and since when.
5. If someone doesn't pay, no more credit until they pay in full.

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | How to collect respectfully
* fa-calendar | Remind them of the date | A friendly message before the day. | No pressure. | si
* fa-file-text-o | Resend the invoice | With the amount and date. | Clarity. | si
* fa-money | Accept partial payments | Something is better than nothing. | Log each payment. | si
* fa-bullhorn | Shaming or threatening | Never: it hurts your business and may be illegal. | No. | no

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Lupita now asks for a 50% deposit on party orders and the rest at delivery. She no longer has balances older than a week.

> **Key idea:** selling on credit is lending; ask for a deposit, put everything in writing and invoice the same day.

--- comprueba
1. What do you put in writing for a big order? || Price, delivery date, deposit and payment date.
2. When do you send the invoice? || The same day you deliver.

--- recuerda
- Deposit on big orders.
- Everything in writing.
- Invoice the same day.

== profundiza

--- tabla | fa-table | Who owes you
| Customer | Invoice date | Amount | Payments | Owes | Days late |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | |

--- tema | fa-balance-scale | If they don't pay
First, remind them in writing. Then send a formal letter with a deadline. For small amounts, your county's small claims court is an option without a lawyer; in California the limit for individuals is $12,500 (check with the court). If you work in construction in California, look into a mechanics lien early: it has strict deadlines.

--- tema | fa-ban | What not to do
Don't post debtors on social media, don't threaten them and don't contact their family or workplace. Besides hurting your business, it can get you into legal trouble.

--- casos
### Case 1. Lupita's orders
A customer orders 20 dozen for a wedding and promises to pay later.
? What does she do? || She asks for a 50% deposit and puts the price and payment date in writing.

### Case 2. Javier's invoice
A $1,200 invoice from Javier is 45 days late.
? What does he do? || He reminds in writing, sends a letter with a deadline and considers small claims court.

### Case 3. Don Ramón's customer
An office wants food from Don Ramón every Friday and to pay at the end of the month.
? What does he do? || He agrees with a written arrangement, invoices each delivery and sets a limit.

--- errores
* No deposit | You lose if they cancel | Ask for a deposit
* Verbal agreements | Arguments | In writing
* Invoicing late | You get paid late | Same day
* Shaming debtors | Legal trouble | Collect respectfully

# M3 U03 | Your reserve and slow seasons
objetivo: Build a business reserve and prepare for slow months, taxes and yearly expenses.
gancho: Don Ramón's food truck sells a lot in summer and at fairs, but almost nothing in January and February. Last year he used his credit card to pay his insurance. In this lesson you'll see how to prepare.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-shield | What the reserve is
The {{business reserve|money set aside to cover the business's fixed costs in slow months or emergencies.}} is the business's cushion: money to pay fixed costs and your salary when you sell less or something unexpected happens. It's different from your personal emergency fund and your tax account.

--- ecuacion | fa-calculator | Don Ramón's reserve
= $6,000 | Monthly fixed costs (with his salary)
× 2 | Months of cushion
= $12,000 | Reserve goal
Set aside a percentage of each sale in the good months until you reach the goal.

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | Where to keep it
* fa-university | Separate savings account | Insured by the FDIC or NCUA. | Available. | si
* fa-line-chart | High-yield savings account | Insured and earns more interest. | Check fees. | si
* fa-home | Cash at home or in the truck | Risk of theft or spending. | Avoid it. | no
* fa-credit-card | Your credit card | Not a reserve: it's debt. | Avoid it. | no

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Don Ramón sets aside 10% of each sale from May to October in a savings account. In January he pays his insurance and salary from the reserve, without using the card.

> **Key idea:** in good months, set money aside for slow ones; a reserve keeps you away from expensive debt.

--- comprueba
1. What is the reserve for? || To pay fixed costs and your salary in slow months or emergencies.
2. Is it the same as your tax account? || No: taxes are set aside in another account.

--- recuerda
- Reserve = the business's cushion.
- Set aside a % of each sale.
- Separate from taxes.

# M3 U04 | Inflation, interest rates and compound interest in your business
objetivo: Understand how inflation raises your costs and erodes your cash, when and how to adjust prices, and how compound interest grows your reserve or your debt.
gancho: Don Ramón has sold his burrito at the same price for a year, but meat, tortillas and gas went up. He sells the same amount and keeps less. In this lesson you'll see how to protect your margin.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-line-chart | Inflation raises your costs
With {{inflation|a general rise in prices over time.}}, your supplies, rent and gas go up. If your prices stay the same, your margin shrinks even if you sell the same amount.

> **Current fact:** the Federal Reserve's long-run inflation goal is 2% a year; the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) publishes the Consumer Price Index (CPI) every month. Accessed September 30, 2026 through the Federal Reserve and the BLS.

--- ecuacion | fa-calculator | Don Ramón's margin
Here's how the margin on one burrito changed.
= 12 | Sale price
- 8 | Cost after the increases (was 6.50)
= 4 | Margin per item (was 5.50)
He sells the same, but earns almost 30% less per item.

--- paso | fa-refresh | Review costs and prices every quarter
Every three months, compare your costs with last quarter. If they went up, recalculate your price (M2 U02) and your break-even point (M2 U03). Raise prices a little and tell your customers, instead of one big jump.

--- paso | fa-user | A case in one minute
Don Ramón reviewed his five best-selling dishes: four had higher costs. He raised prices a dollar, updated his menu board and recovered his margin without losing customers.

> **Key idea:** inflation raises your costs; review costs and prices every quarter and protect your margin.

--- comprueba
1. What happens to your margin if your costs rise and your prices don't? || It shrinks.
2. How often do you review costs and prices? || Every three months.

--- recuerda
- Inflation raises your costs.
- Review every quarter.
- Small, announced changes.

== profundiza

--- tema | fa-university | Your reserve loses value too
A business reserve kept in cash loses buying power with inflation. Keep it in an insured savings account that pays interest and is still available (M3 U03).

--- tema | fa-percent | Rates and your loans
When the Federal Reserve raises its rate, card and loan rates usually go up. If your loan has a variable rate, your payment can change. Prefer a fixed rate or calculate the payment with a higher rate.

--- tabla | fa-table | $10,000 at 10% a year, compounded
| Year | In your reserve | In a debt |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $11,000 | you owe $11,000 |
| 3 | $13,310 | you owe $13,310 |
| 5 | $16,105 | you owe $16,105 |

> **Before you act, check:** last year's CPI at the BLS and whether your loan rate is fixed or variable.

--- casos
### Case 1. Don Ramón's burrito
The burrito's cost went from $6.50 to $8 and Don Ramón still sells it for $12.
? What does he do? || He reviews his costs and raises the price a little, telling his customers.

### Case 2. Lupita's reserve
Lupita has kept her business reserve in cash for two years.
? What does she do? || She moves it to an insured savings account that pays interest and stays available.

### Case 3. Javier's credit line
Javier is offered a variable-rate credit line for a van.
? What does he do? || He calculates the payment with a higher rate or looks for a fixed rate.

--- errores
* Frozen prices | Margin slips away | Review every quarter
* Reserve in cash | Loses value | Insured account
* Variable rate without math | Payment rises | Calculate higher
* Raising all at once | You lose customers | Small and announced
