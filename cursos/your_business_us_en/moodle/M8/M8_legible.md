# Module 8. Grow in an orderly way

## M8 U01. Hiring help

**What you will be able to do:** Figure out the real cost of hiring, tell the difference between an employee and an independent contractor, and know your basic obligations.

**To start:** Lupita can't keep up with weekend orders anymore. She wants her neighbor to help and to pay her $15 an hour in cash "as a contractor." In this lesson you'll see why that can get her in trouble.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### Employee or contractor?

An independent contractor decides how to do the work and has their own business. If you set the schedule, say how to do it and the work is a core part of your business, it's generally an **employee**. California uses a strict test (the ABC test): most people who help on a regular basis are employees.

> **Current fact:** California's minimum wage is $16.90 an hour in 2026 (the federal minimum is $7.25, and many cities have a higher one). Accessed September 29, 2026 through the California Department of Industrial Relations and the U.S. Department of Labor.



#### What an employee costs beyond wages

| Type | Description | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Payroll taxes | Social Security, Medicare, unemployment. | You withhold and pay. |
| Workers' compensation | Required in California. | Insurance. |
| Paid sick days | California law. | They accrue. |
| "Contractor" for convenience | High fines for misclassification. | Classify correctly. |

#### Lupita's helper (approximate)

- Monthly wages (20 hours a week at $16.90): **$1,352**
- Payroll taxes and insurance (approx.): **$250**
- Real monthly cost: **$1,602**
Lupita recalculates her break-even point (M2 U03) before hiring.


#### A case in one minute

Lupita saw her neighbor would be an employee: working in her kitchen, on her schedule, with her recipes. She used a payroll service to register with the EDD, pay minimum wage and get workers' comp. She raised her weekend prices to cover it.

> **Key idea:** classify your help correctly, pay at least minimum wage and figure the real cost before hiring.



#### Check your understanding

1. What defines an independent contractor?
*Answer:* Having their own business and deciding how to do the work.

2. What is California's minimum wage in 2026?
*Answer:* $16.90 an hour.


#### Remember

- Employee or contractor: it isn't chosen for convenience.
- Minimum wage.
- Real cost with taxes and insurance.



### Go deeper (5 more minutes)

#### If you have employees

You need an EIN, to register with your state's employment agency (in California, the EDD), withhold and pay payroll taxes, carry workers' comp and verify each person's employment eligibility with Form I-9. A payroll service or accountant can do it for you.



#### Real contractors

If you hire another business for a one-time job (a design, a repair), ask for their W-9 and, if you pay them $2,000 or more in the year, send them a 1099-NEC.



#### Family who helps

There are special rules for minor children of a sole proprietor and for spouses. If family helps regularly, review the rules with a tax preparer.

> **Before you act, check:** classification and your employer obligations with the EDD, DIR and IRS; rules change by state and city.



#### Cases


**Case 1. Lupita's neighbor**

Lupita wants to pay her neighbor in cash "as a contractor" to work in her kitchen on her schedule.
- *What does she do?* She recognizes the neighbor would be an employee and registers as an employer.


**Case 2. Javier's helper**

Javier wants a helper on a $3,000 painting job.
- *What does he check?* That with helpers he needs a CSLB license and workers' comp.


**Case 3. Daniela's designer**

Daniela hires an independent designer for her logo for $400.
- *What does she do?* She asks for a W-9 and keeps the invoice; no 1099 is needed under $2,000 for the year.


#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| "Contractor" for convenience | High fines | Classify correctly |
| Less than minimum wage | Illegal | Minimum wage |
| No workers' comp | Lawsuits | Required |
| Looking only at wages | You come up short | Real cost |

### Practice

#### Interactive activity

**What would you do? (H5P):** three situations from this lesson with Lupita, Javier and Daniela. Pick the best decision in each one; if you miss, you can try again. It earns experience points.



#### Quiz

1. Someone works at your place, on your schedule and following your instructions. What are they usually? a) An employee · b) An independent contractor · c) A partner
2. Which insurance is required in California if you have employees? a) Life insurance for each employee · b) Workers' compensation · c) Auto insurance
3. You pay an independent designer $2,500 in the year. What do you send them? a) A W-2 · b) Nothing · c) A 1099-NEC
**Answers:** 1-a: you control the work. 2-b: required. 3-c: from $2,000 in 2026.



#### Put it into practice

Someone works 15 hours a week at $16.90. What are their wages over 4 weeks?
**Answer:** 60 hours times $16.90: $1,014.



#### Your plan

If you're thinking of hiring, decide whether it would be an employee or a contractor and figure the real cost.



### Learn more

- **Employee or contractor** (DIR · English): https://www.dir.ca.gov | What to look for: "ABC test".
- **Employers** (EDD · English): https://edd.ca.gov | What to look for: "employer registration".
- **Minimum wage** (DIR · English): https://www.dir.ca.gov | What to look for: "minimum wage".

### Key words

- *Independent contractor:* a person who has their own business, decides how to do the work and works for several clients.
- *Payroll taxes:* taxes the employer withholds and pays for its employees.
- *ABC test:* California's test to determine whether someone is an employee or a contractor.

### Sources

DIR · EDD · IRS · U.S. Department of Labor, accessed September 29, 2026.

## M8 U02. Selling online and on platforms

**What you will be able to do:** Figure out the real cost of selling online and on platforms, and understand the 1099-K and sales tax on platforms.

**To start:** Daniela sells a necklace for $40 in an online store. After selling, payment and ad fees, plus shipping, she keeps much less. In this lesson you'll see what you really keep.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### What gets taken out

| Type | Description | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Selling fee | A percentage per sale. | Varies by platform. |
| Payment fee | For processing the payment. | Percentage plus a flat fee. |
| Ads | If you pay to show up more. | Measure whether it pays. |
| Shipping | You or your customer pays it. | Build it into the price. |

#### Daniela's necklace

- Price: **$40**
- Selling and payment fees (approx.): **$5**
- Shipping: **$5**
- Materials and packaging: **$10**
- Before her fixed costs and taxes: **$20**


#### A case in one minute

Daniela figured out what she keeps per channel. On one platform she kept $20; on her own site, $26, but she sells less. She raised her platform price to $44 and offers free shipping on her site for two or more pieces.

> **Key idea:** set your price by channel, with all fees and shipping, and report everything you sell.



#### Check your understanding

1. What fees do platforms charge?
*Answer:* Selling, payment and, if you use them, ad fees.

2. Do you report your sales even if you don't get a 1099-K?
*Answer:* Yes, all income is reported.


#### Remember

- Fees + shipping + ads.
- Price by channel.
- Everything is reported.



### Go deeper (5 more minutes)

#### The 1099-K

If your payments on a platform exceed $20,000 and 200 transactions in the year, you'll get a 1099-K. It shows gross payments: subtract refunds, fees and costs on your Schedule C. Even if you don't get one, report everything.



#### Sales tax on platforms

In most states, large platforms collect and send the sales tax on sales made through them. On your own site or social media sales, it's up to you (M5 U05).



#### Reviews and returns

Ratings sell. Describe well, ship on time and respond quickly. Figure out what returns cost you and build it into the price.

> **Before you act, check:** each platform's current fees; they change often.



#### Cases


**Case 1. The platform price**

Daniela charges the same on the platform as on her own site.
- *What does she check?* That the platform price covers fees, shipping and ads.


**Case 2. Daniela's 1099-K**

Daniela got a 1099-K for $24,000 gross.
- *What does she do?* She reports the payments and subtracts refunds, fees and costs using her records.


**Case 3. Lupita's ads**

Lupita pays $100 a month for ads and doesn't know if they bring sales.
- *What does she do?* She measures how many sales come from the ads and how much they leave her.


#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Same price everywhere | You lose on the platform | Price by channel |
| Reporting the 1099-K without subtracting costs | You overpay | Use your records |
| Not reporting without a 1099-K | IRS trouble | Report everything |
| Unmeasured ads | Spending with no return | Measure |

### Practice

#### Interactive activity

**What would you do? (H5P):** three situations from this lesson with Daniela and Lupita. Pick the best decision in each one; if you miss, you can try again. It earns experience points.



#### Quiz

1. When does a platform send a 1099-K? a) Always, from the first sale · b) With over $20,000 and over 200 transactions · c) Only if you ask for it
2. Your 1099-K shows $24,000 gross. How much do you report as profit? a) The full $24,000 · b) Nothing, the platform already reported it · c) Payments minus refunds, fees and costs
3. Who usually collects sales tax on large platform sales? a) The platform · b) Nobody · c) Your customer, directly to the state
**Answers:** 1-b: current threshold. 2-c: costs are subtracted. 3-a: marketplace facilitator.



#### Put it into practice

You sell 60 pieces at $30 in a month; the platform charges 12% in total and shipping is $4 a piece. How much do you keep before materials?
**Answer:** $1,800 minus $216 minus $240: $1,344.



#### Your plan

Figure out how much you keep per product on each channel where you sell.



### Learn more

- **Form 1099-K** (IRS · English and Spanish): https://www.irs.gov | What to look for: "Form 1099-K".

### Key words

- *Platform:* a site or app that connects sellers and buyers and charges a fee.
- *Gross payments:* the total collected before subtracting fees, refunds and costs.

### Sources

IRS · CDTFA, accessed September 29, 2026.

## M8 U03. Your monthly numbers

**What you will be able to do:** Review five numbers about your business each month and make decisions with them.

**To start:** Don Ramón feels the food truck is doing "so-so." His daughter suggests checking five numbers every month. In this lesson you'll see what they are and what they tell you.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### Your five numbers

| Type | Description | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Sales | How much you sold. | Compare with last month. |
| Margin | The % you keep after what you sell. | Going up or down? |
| Profit | Sales minus all costs. | The one that matters. |
| Cash at month-end | What you have at the end of the month. | Is it enough? |

#### The fifth: average ticket

The average ticket is what each customer spends on average. If it goes up, you sell more without needing more customers.



#### Don Ramón's ticket

- Monthly sales: **$12,000**
- Number of sales: **1,000**
- Average ticket: **$12**
If each customer spent $1.50 more, he'd sell $1,500 more a month.


#### A case in one minute

Don Ramón saw his margin drop from 60% to 55%: meat went up and he didn't adjust. He changed suppliers and offered a combo with a drink. The next month his ticket rose to $13.40.

> **Key idea:** five numbers a month tell you what to improve: sales, margin, profit, cash and average ticket.



#### Check your understanding

1. What is the average ticket?
*Answer:* What each customer spends on average: sales divided by number of sales.

2. What do you do if your margin drops?
*Answer:* Check prices and costs: suppliers, waste and price.


#### Remember

- Five numbers a month.
- Compare with last month.
- One decision per number.



### Go deeper (5 more minutes)

#### Your monthly dashboard

| Number | Last month | This month | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sales | | | |
| Margin | | | |
| Profit | | | |
| Cash at month-end | | | |
| Average ticket | | | |



#### Compare with the same month last year

If your business is seasonal, compare July with July, not with June.



#### One decision at a time

Choose one improvement (price, supplier, hours), apply it and measure the next month.



#### Cases


**Case 1. Don Ramón's margin**

Don Ramón's margin dropped five points.
- *What does he do?* He checks which costs went up and adjusts suppliers or prices.


**Case 2. Daniela's sales**

Daniela's sales fell in January compared with December.
- *What does she compare with?* With January of last year, because of the season.


**Case 3. Lupita's ticket**

Lupita has many customers, but each one buys little.
- *What does she try?* A combo or a drink to raise the average ticket.


#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Looking only at sales | Hidden profit | Five numbers |
| Comparing wrong | False conclusions | Same month last year |
| Changing everything at once | You don't know what worked | One at a time |
| Not writing down decisions | They get forgotten | Dashboard |

### Practice

#### Interactive activity

#### Quiz

1. Sales of $6,000 from 400 sales. Average ticket? a) $2,400 · b) $150 · c) $15
2. Your margin dropped. What do you check first? a) Prices and costs of what you sell · b) The color of your logo · c) Your card limit
3. Your business is seasonal. What do you compare July with? a) June · b) July last year · c) January
**Answers:** 1-c: $6,000 divided by 400. 2-a: margin depends on them. 3-b: same season.



#### Put it into practice

You have 800 sales a month. If your average ticket goes from $10 to $11, how much more do you sell a month?
**Answer:** $800.



#### Your plan

Fill in your dashboard with last month's numbers and choose one decision for this month.

## M8 U04. Stress, business and family

**What you will be able to do:** Recognize the stress of running a business, separate work time from rest and talk about money at home without fighting.

**To start:** Lupita cooks Monday to Sunday, answers orders until midnight and argues with her husband about money. She feels that if she rests, she'll lose customers. In this lesson you'll see how to take care of your energy and your business.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### Warning signs

| Type | Description | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| You sleep badly | You think about bills and orders all night. | Your health suffers. |
| Fights at home | Every money talk becomes an argument. | Nobody decides. |
| Avoiding your numbers | You don't want to see sales or taxes. | Deciding blind. |
| Hours and rest | Fixed days and hours. | Better decisions. |

#### Four habits

1. Posted order hours; outside them, an automatic reply.
2. One day off a week.
3. A 20-minute money talk at home every week, with numbers.
4. Your fixed salary (M1 U03): your home doesn't depend on the business's day-to-day.



#### A case in one minute

Lupita set order hours from 8 to 6 and an automatic reply. She takes Mondays off. Every Sunday she and her husband review the household budget and her fixed salary. She sleeps better and makes fewer mistakes on orders.

> **Key idea:** your business needs you to be well; set hours, one day off and a weekly money talk lower the stress.



#### Check your understanding

1. What helps you stop answering orders at midnight?
*Answer:* Posted hours and an automatic reply.

2. Why does a fixed salary help at home?
*Answer:* Because your family doesn't depend on each day's sales.


#### Remember

- Posted hours.
- One day off.
- Weekly money talk.



### Go deeper (5 more minutes)

#### If the stress doesn't go down

If you haven't slept for weeks, feel there's no way out or think about hurting yourself, call or text 988: the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline is free, 24 hours a day, in English and Spanish.



#### Family in the business

If your family works with you, keep business talks separate from home talks and agree on clear pay (M8 U01). That way a bad sales day doesn't become a family fight.



#### Cases


**Case 1. Lupita's orders**

Lupita answers orders until midnight and never rests.
- *What does she do?* She posts order hours and turns on an automatic reply.


**Case 2. Daniela's sales**

Daniela doesn't want to look at her numbers because sales were low.
- *What does she do?* She reviews her numbers calmly and decides on one action.


**Case 3. Don Ramón's family**

Don Ramón and his daughter argue about the food truck at dinner.
- *What do they do?* They set one time for the business and another for home.


#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Working with no rest | Mistakes and illness | Day off |
| Avoiding the numbers | Deciding blind | Review |
| Mixing business and dinner | Fights | Separate times |
| Keeping stress quiet | It gets worse | Ask for help |

### Practice

#### Interactive activity

**What would you do? (H5P):** three situations from this lesson with Lupita, Daniela and Don Ramón. Pick the best decision in each one; if you miss, you can try again. It earns experience points.



#### Quiz

1. Customers message you at midnight. What do you do? a) Always answer so you don't lose them · b) Post your hours and an automatic reply · c) Turn off your phone for a week
2. Sales were low this month. What do you do with your numbers? a) Review them calmly and decide on one action · b) Don't look so you don't worry · c) Delete them
3. You haven't slept for weeks and feel there's no way out. Who do you call? a) Nobody, you tough it out · b) Someone who'll lend you money · c) 988
**Answers:** 1-b: clear limits. 2-a: decide with data. 3-c: free help 24 hours.



#### Put it into practice

You work 7 days of 11 hours. If you take one day off, how many hours do you get back each week?
**Answer:** 11 hours.



#### Your plan

Post your order hours and choose your day off.



### Learn more

- **988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline** (English and Spanish): call or text 988.

### Key words

- *Financial stress:* constant worry about money that affects your sleep, health or relationships.
