Case studies

Four business cases that bring together what you learned. Solve them before opening the key.

The cases use made-up data. Amounts are in dollars. Solve each case before opening the key.

E1. Lupita's tamales (Modules 1 and 2)

Data. Lupita sells $4,200 a month. She spends $1,800 on ingredients and packaging (variable) and has $700 in fixed costs. She sells about 140 dozen a month at $30. She sets nothing aside for taxes.

Tasks.

  1. How much does she earn before taxes?
  2. If she sets aside 25% for taxes, how much is that?
  3. How much does each dozen cost her in ingredients?
  4. How much does each dozen leave after ingredients, and how many does she need to sell a month to cover fixed costs?
See the key

Key.

  • $4,200 minus $1,800 minus $700: $1,700.
  • 25% of $1,700: $425.
  • $1,800 divided by 140: about $12.86.
  • $30 minus $12.86: about $17.14; $700 divided by $17.14: about 41 dozen.

E2. Javier's quarter (Modules 3 and 4)

Data. Javier has $900 in the business account. He finished a $4,500 job that will be paid in 30 days. He needs $1,200 of paint now and his $1,100 estimated payment is due in three weeks. A new customer sends a $2,500 check for a $1,500 job and asks for $1,000 back.

Tasks.

  1. Can he cover the paint and the estimated payment without collecting?
  2. Which two actions improve his cash flow?
  3. What does he do with the check?
  4. When is his next estimated payment due if it's August 2026?
See the key

Key.

  • $900 against $2,300: he's $1,400 short.
  • Ask for a deposit on the next job and terms at the paint store.
  • He doesn't deposit it or send anything back; he asks for the exact payment. It's the overpayment scam.
  • September 15, 2026.

E3. Daniela's taxes (Modules 5 and 8)

Data. Daniela sold $30,000 in the year: $22,000 on a platform (over 200 transactions) and $8,000 on her own site. Her costs and fees totaled $14,000. Her net profit was $16,000.

Tasks.

  1. Does she get a 1099-K from the platform?
  2. Does she report her own site's sales?
  3. About how much is her self-employment tax?
  4. Who collects sales tax on her platform sales, and who on her site's sales?
See the key

Key.

  • Yes: over $20,000 and over 200 transactions.
  • Yes: all income is reported.
  • $16,000 times 92.35% times 15.3%: about $2,261.
  • In general, the platform collects on its sales; she collects on her site.

E4. Don Ramón's food truck (Modules 6, 7 and 9)

Data. Don Ramón gets two offers for $10,000: a 12-month credit union loan with $898 payments and a 6-month advance with a 1.35 factor. He's 60, has 52 Social Security credits and no retirement savings. His personal auto insurance doesn't cover the food truck.

Tasks.

  1. How much does he repay with each offer?
  2. Which is better?
  3. What insurance does he need?
  4. Which two retirement actions can he start this month?
See the key

Key.

  • Loan: $898 times 12: $10,776. Advance: $13,500.
  • The credit union loan: $2,724 less.
  • Commercial insurance with auto, liability and equipment.
  • Check his estimate at ssa.gov and open an IRA with a fixed contribution.
