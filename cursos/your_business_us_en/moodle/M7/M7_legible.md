# Module 7. Protect your business

## M7 U01. Your health when you work for yourself

**What you will be able to do:** Learn the health insurance options for self-employed people and how to protect your income if you get sick.

**To start:** Javier hurt his back carrying a ladder and couldn't work for two weeks. He had no health insurance and paid for the urgent care visit out of pocket. In this lesson you'll see your options.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### Health insurance options

| Type | Description | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Health insurance marketplace (in California, Covered California) | Plans for people without job-based insurance. | There may be help paying the premium, based on income. |
| Medi-Cal (California's Medicaid) | For low incomes. | Eligibility depends on income and situation. |
| Community clinics | Sliding-scale fees. | Basic care. |
| No insurance | An emergency can cost thousands. | High risk. |

#### When to enroll

The marketplace has an open enrollment period every year (in fall and winter). Outside that period, you can only enroll if you have a qualifying life event, such as losing other coverage or moving. In California, Covered California and Medi-Cal offer help in Spanish and English.



#### A case in one minute

Javier checked Covered California with his estimated business income. He qualified for help with the premium and chose a plan with low-cost visits. He also keeps a small reserve for his deductible.

> **Key idea:** if you work for yourself, check the marketplace, Medi-Cal and community clinics; an emergency without insurance can cost you your business.



#### Check your understanding

1. When do you enroll in the marketplace?
*Answer:* During each year's open enrollment or after a qualifying life event.

2. What is Medi-Cal?
*Answer:* California's health program for people with low incomes.


#### Remember

- Marketplace every year.
- Medi-Cal by income.
- Community clinics.



### Go deeper (5 more minutes)

#### Health insurance and your taxes

If you're self-employed and pay for your own health insurance, in many cases you can deduct the premiums on your return. If you get help with the premium, it's adjusted at tax time based on your actual income: report changes on time.



#### If you can't work

As an owner you don't get employer disability pay. Your reserve (M3 U03) and, if you can afford it, disability or accident insurance protect your income. In California, sole proprietors can voluntarily join state disability insurance (EDD Elective Coverage); check costs and requirements.



#### Information and immigration status

Eligibility rules for health programs depend on your situation. Talk with a certified Covered California enroller or a community clinic; the information you give for health coverage has privacy protections.

> **Before you act, check:** eligibility and costs with Covered California (or your state's marketplace) and Medi-Cal; they change every year.



#### Cases


**Case 1. Javier's back**

Javier has no health insurance and works for himself.
- *What does he do?* He checks the marketplace and Medi-Cal with his estimated income.


**Case 2. Lupita's change**

Lupita left her part-time job and lost her employer coverage in June.
- *What does she do?* She checks whether that change lets her enroll outside open enrollment.


**Case 3. Daniela's income**

Daniela gets help with her premium and her sales went up a lot.
- *What does she do?* She updates her estimated income to avoid owing at tax time.


#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| No insurance | Medical debt | Check options |
| Waiting until you're sick | Outside enrollment | Enroll on time |
| Not reporting income changes | Debt at tax time | Update it |
| No reserve for the deductible | Surprise | Set aside |

#### Quiz

2. You lost your job-based coverage mid-year. What can you check? a) Nothing, you wait for November · b) Whether that change lets you enroll now · c) A loan for doctor visits

Check your health insurance options and write down the date of the next open enrollment.

## M7 U02. Insurance for your business

**What you will be able to do:** Identify your business's risks and learn which insurance covers them and what to ask before buying.

**To start:** Don Ramón's food truck had a small fryer fire. His personal auto insurance didn't cover it because it was a business vehicle. In this lesson you'll see what insurance exists for small businesses.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### Risks and insurance

| Type | Description | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| General liability | If a customer gets hurt or you damage someone's property. | Many clients require it. |
| Commercial auto | Vehicles you use for the business. | Personal auto may not cover it. |
| Property and equipment | Fire, theft, damage. | Space, equipment, inventory. |
| Workers' compensation | Required in California if you have employees. | On-the-job injuries. |

#### Before you buy, ask

1. What does it cover and not cover (exclusions)?
2. What is the deductible?
3. How much does it pay at most (limit)?
4. Does it cover business use of my vehicle?
5. How do I file a claim and how long until they pay?



#### A case in one minute

Don Ramón bought a food truck package with commercial auto, liability and equipment. It costs $210 a month. Fair organizers now accept him because he shows them his certificate of insurance.

> **Key idea:** insure what you couldn't replace and what your clients ask for; your personal insurance almost never covers the business.



#### Check your understanding

1. What does general liability cover?
*Answer:* Damage you cause to other people or their property.

2. Does your personal auto insurance cover business use?
*Answer:* Often not; check or buy commercial auto.


#### Remember

- General liability.
- Commercial auto.
- Workers' comp if you have employees.



### Go deeper (5 more minutes)

#### Your risks

| Risk | Could you pay it yourself? | Insurance? |
|---|---|---|
| A customer gets hurt | | |
| Crash with the business vehicle | | |
| Equipment theft or fire | | |
| An employee gets hurt | | |



#### The certificate of insurance

Many contractors, markets and events ask for a certificate of insurance (COI) before letting you work. Having one opens doors to clients and can justify a better price.



#### Check the insurer

Check the agent and the insurer with your state's Department of Insurance (in California, the CDI). Ask for the full policy and keep receipts for your equipment.



#### Cases


**Case 1. The food truck fire**

Don Ramón's personal insurance didn't cover his food truck fire.
- *What does he do?* He buys commercial insurance with auto, liability and equipment.


**Case 2. Javier's contractor**

A contractor asks Javier for his certificate of insurance before giving him work.
- *What does he do?* He buys general liability and requests his certificate.


**Case 3. Lupita's helper**

Lupita will hire a helper as an employee.
- *What does she check?* That in California she needs workers' compensation insurance.


#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Thinking personal insurance covers it | No payout | Commercial insurance |
| No certificate | You lose clients | Have it ready |
| Employees without workers' comp | Fines and lawsuits | Required |
| Not checking the agent | Fraud | Department of Insurance |

## M7 U03. Scams against businesses

**What you will be able to do:** Recognize the scams small businesses face and respond with a plan.

**To start:** Daniela got an "official" letter charging her $243 for an "LLC certificate," threatening a penalty. Lupita got a call from someone "from the health department" to collect a fine over the phone. In this lesson you'll see how to recognize these tricks.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### Common scams against businesses

| Type | Description | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Letters that imitate the government | They charge for certificates or reports you don't need or that cost less. | Check the sender. |
| Fake authority by phone | "Health dept.," "IRS" or "police" demand immediate payment. | Hang up and check. |
| Fake supplier email | Changed bank account. | Call the number you already had. |
| Fake trademark notices | They charge for private "registries." | Only the USPTO. |

#### Your plan

1. Don't pay in a hurry: real authorities give you time and notify in writing.
2. Check the sender: fine print that says "we are not a government agency."
3. Verify by going to the official site yourself or calling the official number.
4. Never pay with gift cards, crypto or transfers to individuals.
5. Report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov and warn other businesses.



#### A case in one minute

Daniela read the fine print: "This is not a government document." On the Secretary of State's site she saw that her Statement of Information costs $20 and she had already filed it. She threw the letter away and warned the community.

> **Key idea:** no authority collects by phone or demands immediate payment with gift cards; check the official site before paying any letter.



#### Check your understanding

1. How do you recognize a letter imitating the government?
*Answer:* By fine print saying it's not from the government, a high charge and urgency.

2. What do you do if someone calls to collect a fine?
*Answer:* Hang up and check with the official number.


#### Remember

- No rush.
- Check the sender.
- You verify.



### Go deeper (5 more minutes)

#### Your email and business accounts

Many scams start when someone gets into your email. Use different passwords, two-step verification and check your email's forwarding rules. If a supplier "changes banks," confirm by phone.



#### Extortion

If someone threatens you to collect "protection" or calls saying they have a relative, hang up, reach your relative another way and call 911 or local police. Don't share your hours or photos of your cash register on social media.



#### Business identity theft

Check your record with the Secretary of State from time to time: if someone changes your LLC's details, they could get credit in your name. Report identity theft at IdentityTheft.gov.



#### Cases


**Case 1. Daniela's letter**

An "official" letter charges Daniela $243 for an LLC certificate.
- *What does she do?* She checks the sender and verifies on the Secretary of State's site before paying.


**Case 2. The call to Lupita**

Someone "from the health department" demands Lupita pay a fine by phone today.
- *What does she do?* She hangs up and calls the health department's official number.


**Case 3. The supplier's email**

An email from Javier's supplier says they changed accounts.
- *What does he do?* He calls the supplier's number he already had before paying.


#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Paying letters without checking | You lose money | Read the fine print |
| Paying by phone | Scam | You verify |
| Gift cards | Sure scam | Never |
| No two-step on email | Accounts stolen | Turn it on |

### Practice

#### Interactive activity

**What would you do? (H5P):** three situations from this lesson with Daniela, Lupita and Javier. Pick the best decision in each one; if you miss, you can try again. It earns experience points.



#### Quiz

1. A letter charges you for an LLC "certificate" and threatens a penalty. What do you do first? a) Pay to avoid the penalty · b) Call the number on the letter · c) Check the sender and verify on the official site
2. What does a real authority never accept as payment? a) Gift cards · b) Checks · c) Payment on its official site
3. Where do you report a scam against your business? a) In the company's reviews · b) At ReportFraud.ftc.gov · c) Nowhere
**Answers:** 1-c: verify before paying. 2-a: sign of a scam. 3-b: official report.



#### Put it into practice

Three businesses on your street got the same $243 letter. If all of them paid, how much would they lose?
**Answer:** $729.



#### Your plan

Turn on two-step verification on your email and write your anti-scam plan.



### Learn more

- **Report fraud** (FTC · English and Spanish): https://reportfraud.ftc.gov.
- **Identity theft** (FTC · English and Spanish): https://www.identitytheft.gov.
- **California businesses** (Secretary of State · English): https://bizfileonline.sos.ca.gov | What to look for: your business.

### Key words

- *Government-imitation letter:* mail from a private company that looks official in order to charge you for services.
- *Two-step verification:* signing in with a password plus a code or approval on your phone.

## M7 U04. Your brand and your name

**What you will be able to do:** Know when it pays to register your trademark, how to search whether it already exists and how to do it without abusive middlemen.

**To start:** Daniela has sold jewelry as "Luna Morena" for two years. A shop in another state wrote to say the trademark is theirs. In this lesson you'll see how to protect your name.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### What a registered trademark is

Registering your trademark with the USPTO gives you nationwide rights for certain products or services (classes). Using a name gives you some local rights, but federal registration protects much more.



#### Steps

1. Search the USPTO trademark search to see whether the name already exists.
2. Identify the class of your products or services.
3. File the application online and pay the fee.
4. Respond on time to USPTO requests.

> **Current fact:** the USPTO base trademark application fee is $350 per class; extra charges may apply if the description isn't from the ID Manual or information is missing. Accessed September 29, 2026 through the USPTO (fees in effect since January 18, 2025).



#### When it pays

| Type | Description | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Your name already sells | Customers look for you by it. | Protect it. |
| You sell online to other states | More visible, more risk. | Federal registration. |
| You only sell in your city | A state registration may be enough. | Cheaper. |
| Notices charging to "register" | Not from the USPTO. | Be wary. |

#### A case in one minute

Daniela searched the USPTO: "Luna Morena" had been registered for jewelry before her. She switched to an available name and registered it in one class. Now she can grow online without worry.

> **Key idea:** search the USPTO before investing in a name and register it once you start selling beyond your area.



#### Check your understanding

1. Where do you search whether a trademark exists?
*Answer:* In the USPTO trademark search.

2. How much is the base application per class?
*Answer:* $350.


#### Remember

- Search the USPTO.
- Register by class.
- Only the USPTO registers federal trademarks.



### Go deeper (5 more minutes)

#### Fake notices and emails

After you apply for your trademark, your information is public. You'll get letters and emails that look like they're from the USPTO charging to "publish" or "monitor" your trademark. Only trust communications from uspto.gov domains.



#### Usernames and domains

Your social media account and your domain are not registered trademarks. They help you sell, but they don't protect you if someone registers the name.



#### If someone claims your name

If someone says your name is their trademark, check the USPTO before replying. If their registration is real and covers your products, changing your name is usually the wisest move. Some universities have no-cost intellectual property law clinics.



#### Cases


**Case 1. Daniela's brand**

A shop tells Daniela that "Luna Morena" is their trademark.
- *What does she do?* She checks the USPTO and, if the registration is real, changes her name.


**Case 2. The food truck name**

Don Ramón wants to paint a new name on his food truck.
- *What does he do first?* He searches the USPTO and his state to make sure the name is available.


**Case 3. The email to Javier**

Javier gets an email charging $1,200 to "protect his brand internationally."
- *What does he do?* He checks that it isn't from uspto.gov and doesn't pay.


#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Not searching first | You lose the name | USPTO |
| Thinking social media protects you | It gets taken | Register |
| Paying fake notices | Scam | Only uspto.gov |
| Free-form descriptions unchecked | Extra fees | Use the ID Manual |

### Practice

#### Interactive activity

**What would you do? (H5P):** three situations from this lesson with Daniela, Don Ramón and Javier. Pick the best decision in each one; if you miss, you can try again. It earns experience points.



#### Quiz

1. Where do you search whether a name is already a federal trademark? a) The USPTO · b) Social media · c) An internet search engine
2. Does your social media account give you exclusive nationwide rights to the name? a) Yes, if it has many followers · b) Yes, if you opened it first · c) No
3. Which domain do real USPTO notices come from? a) Any email with "trademark" in the subject · b) uspto.gov · c) A law firm that writes to you first
**Answers:** 1-a: official search. 2-c: only registration does. 3-b: official domain.



#### Put it into practice

You register your trademark in two classes at the base fee. How much do you pay?
**Answer:** $700.



#### Your plan

Search your business name at the USPTO and in your state's registry.

### Key words

- *Trademark:* a name, logo or combination that identifies your product or service.

## M7 U05. Identity theft and unwanted calls

**What you will be able to do:** Protect your identity with a credit freeze, fraud alerts and your no-cost reports, know what to do if someone uses your data and register your number so you don't get sales calls.

**To start:** Don Ramón was turned down for credit because his report showed a card he never opened. He also gets ten calls a day offering "loans for his food truck." In this lesson you'll see how to stop both.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### What identity theft is

Identity theft happens when someone uses your SSN or ITIN, your name or your documents to open cards, take out loans or file a tax return in your name. As a business owner, your personal information is on many forms: protect it.



#### Your locks

| Type | Description | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Credit freeze | Nobody opens new credit in your name. | At no cost at all 3 bureaus. |
| Fraud alert | Lenders must verify it's you. | Lasts one year. |
| Your credit reports | At no cost every week at AnnualCreditReport.com. | Check what you don't recognize. |
| IRS IP PIN | Nobody files with your number (M5 U04). | At no cost. |

> **Current fact:** freezing and unfreezing your credit costs nothing at Equifax, Experian and TransUnion (you must ask all three); an initial fraud alert costs nothing, lasts one year and you only need to ask one bureau; and you can see your reports no-cost every week at AnnualCreditReport.com. Accessed September 29, 2026 through the FTC.

#### Stop the calls

Register your number on the National Do Not Call Registry at donotcall.gov. It costs nothing and never expires. After you register, sales calls that keep coming are usually illegal or scams.

> **Current fact:** the National Do Not Call Registry costs nothing, your number shows up the next day, companies have up to 31 days to stop calling and your registration never expires. Accessed September 29, 2026 through the FTC (donotcall.gov).



#### A case in one minute

Don Ramón pulled his reports, found the fake card, reported it at IdentityTheft.gov and froze his credit at all three bureaus. He registered his cell phone at donotcall.gov. When he needs a loan, he'll lift the freeze for a few days, at no cost.

> **Key idea:** freeze your credit, check your reports and register your number; if someone uses your data, report it at IdentityTheft.gov.



#### Check your understanding

1. What does a credit freeze do?
*Answer:* It stops anyone from opening new credit in your name; it costs nothing and you can lift it when you need to.

2. Where do you report identity theft?
*Answer:* At IdentityTheft.gov, which gives you a recovery plan.


#### Remember

- Freeze at all 3 bureaus.
- No-cost weekly reports.
- donotcall.gov costs nothing.



### Go deeper (5 more minutes)

#### If someone already used your data

1. Report it at IdentityTheft.gov: you get a step-by-step plan and letters for companies.
2. Call the fraud department of the company where the account was opened and ask them to close it.
3. Place a fraud alert or freeze your credit.
4. Check your reports and dispute what you don't recognize.
5. If your number was used for taxes, follow the IRS instructions and get your IP PIN.



#### If you have an ITIN

If you have a credit history under an ITIN, ask each bureau how to request a freeze or alert with your ITIN; some requests need extra documents. The IRS IP PIN is also available to people who file with an ITIN.



#### What the registry doesn't cover

The Do Not Call Registry doesn't stop debt collection calls, surveys, charities, political calls or companies you already do business with. It doesn't stop scammers either. Your phone carrier offers no-cost tools to block suspicious calls; use them.

> **Before you act, check:** that you're on donotcall.gov, AnnualCreditReport.com or IdentityTheft.gov; some sites imitate these names and charge for what's at no cost.



#### Cases


**Case 1. Don Ramón's card**

Don Ramón's report shows a card he never opened.
- *What does he do?* He reports it at IdentityTheft.gov and freezes his credit at all three bureaus.


**Case 2. Lupita's calls**

Lupita gets sales calls every day.
- *What does she do?* She registers her number at donotcall.gov and turns on her carrier's call blocking.


**Case 3. Daniela's site**

A website charges Daniela $30 for "her official credit report."
- *What does she do?* She doesn't pay and goes to AnnualCreditReport.com, where it costs nothing.


#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Freezing at only one bureau | Credit still gets opened | All three |
| Paying for reports | Unnecessary cost | AnnualCreditReport.com |
| Not reporting the theft | You're billed for others' debts | IdentityTheft.gov |
| Thinking the registry stops scams | You fall for them | Hang up and check |

### Practice

#### Interactive activity

#### Quiz

2. Where do you report identity theft? a) At IdentityTheft.gov · b) At the bank where you have your account · c) On social media

## M7 U06. AI scams: fake voices, videos and messages

**What you will be able to do:** Recognize scams that use voices, videos or messages made with artificial intelligence against your business and family, and protect yourself with simple rules.

**To start:** Don Ramón got a call from "his meat supplier," in the supplier's own voice, asking him to pay the invoice to a new account by Zelle. It was a cloned voice. In this lesson you'll see how to spot it.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### What they can imitate

With a few seconds of audio or some photos from your social media, artificial intelligence can imitate the voice of a relative, supplier or customer; create fake videos of celebrities "recommending" investments; or write emails identical to your bank's or the IRS's.



#### Your rules

| Type | Description | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Account changes | Confirm by calling the number you already had. | Never through the same message. |
| Code word | With your family and main suppliers. | If they don't say it, it's not them. |
| Urgency | "Pay today by Zelle or lose the order." | Sign of a scam. |
| Celebrity investing | Fake video. | Not real. |

#### A case in one minute

Don Ramón hung up and called the supplier's number he had saved. They hadn't changed accounts. Now he confirms any change in payment details by phone, and his family has a code word for emergencies.

> **Key idea:** a familiar voice or face is no longer proof; confirm any request for money through a channel you choose.



#### Check your understanding

1. What do you do if a supplier asks you to pay a new account?
*Answer:* Confirm by calling the number you already had, never through the same message.

2. Does a familiar voice prove it's that person?
*Answer:* No: it may be cloned.


#### Remember

- The voice may be fake.
- Confirm another way.
- Code word.



### Go deeper (5 more minutes)

#### Less material to copy

Avoid posting long audio clips of your voice, make your personal accounts private and don't post your hours or photos of your cash register. The less data, the less believable the scam.



#### Your customers are targets too

If someone poses as your business to ask for payments, post on your social media and website your only payment method. Report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

> **Before you act, check:** any request for money, account change or "recommended" investment through a channel you choose.



#### Cases


**Case 1. Don Ramón's supplier**

A voice identical to his supplier's asks Don Ramón to pay a new account by Zelle.
- *What does he do?* He hangs up and confirms by calling the number he already had.


**Case 2. Javier's video**

Javier sees a video of a famous businessman promising to double his money with crypto.
- *What does he do?* He doesn't invest: it's a fake video and a scam.


**Case 3. Daniela's customers**

Someone uses Daniela's photo and voice to ask her customers for deposits.
- *What does she do?* She posts her only payment method on her social media and reports it to the FTC.


#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Trusting the voice | You pay a scammer | Confirm |
| Changing accounts by message | You lose the payment | You call |

## M7 U07. If disaster strikes: your business ready

**What you will be able to do:** Prepare your business for a fire, earthquake or flood with backed-up records, insurance, a reserve and a plan to get back to work, and know the federal help available.

**To start:** A wildfire forced an evacuation where Don Ramón parks his food truck. He lost a week of sales and inventory, and didn't know if his insurance covered it. In this lesson you'll see how to prepare.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### Your disaster plan

| Type | Description | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Digital backup | Records, receipts and contacts in the cloud. | For taxes and insurance. |
| Photos of equipment and inventory | With dates. | To file claims. |
| Insurance with the right coverage | Flood and earthquake are usually separate. | Ask. |
| Reserve in an account | Not all in cash. | Available. |

#### Federal help

In declared disaster areas, FEMA offers assistance to individuals and the SBA offers low-interest loans to households and businesses. The IRS usually gives extra time to file and pay taxes.

> **Current fact:** SBA disaster loans go up to $500,000 to repair a primary residence, $100,000 for personal property and $2 million for businesses (physical damage or working capital); the rate is no more than 4% if you can't get credit elsewhere. Accessed September 29, 2026 through the SBA.



#### A case in one minute

Don Ramón backs up his receipts and equipment photos in the cloud every week. He reviewed his policy and added business interruption coverage. His reserve is in an account. He knows that if a disaster is declared, he can apply for help at DisasterAssistance.gov and the SBA.

> **Key idea:** backups, photos, the right coverage and a reserve in an account; if a disaster is declared, federal help costs nothing on official sites.



#### Check your understanding

1. What does basic home or business insurance usually not cover?
*Answer:* Flood and earthquake, which are usually separate policies or coverages.

2. What does the SBA offer after a disaster?
*Answer:* Low-interest loans for households and businesses.


#### Remember

- Backups and photos.

### Go deeper (5 more minutes)

#### The IRS in a disaster

When the government declares a disaster area, the IRS usually postpones filing and payment deadlines, including estimated payments. Check the IRS disaster relief page before paying penalties.



#### Scams after a disaster

Fake FEMA inspectors, contractors who want payment up front and fake charities show up. FEMA doesn't charge to help you or ask for payment to register. In California, check a contractor's license with the CSLB.

> **Before you act, check:** what your policy covers (flood, earthquake, business interruption) and apply for help only at DisasterAssistance.gov, sba.gov and irs.gov.
