# Module 4. Get paid and sell without losing

## M4 U01. Cash, card, apps or payment link

**What you will be able to do:** Compare ways to get paid by cost, speed, protection and security, and choose the ones that suit your business.

**To start:** Don Ramón's customers want to pay by card and with apps. He's offered a "free" card reader, but he doesn't know what he'll be charged or when he'll get the deposits. In this lesson you'll see how to compare.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### Ways to get paid

| Type | Description | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Cash | No fee. | Theft risk; also reportable income. |
| Payment apps (Zelle, business profiles in apps) | Fast; some charge for business accounts. | Little protection if there's fraud. |
| Card reader | You take cards. | Fee per payment. |
| Payment link or online invoice | You get paid remotely. | Fee; check chargebacks. |

#### What taking cards costs

- Card payments per month: **$8,000**
× 2.9% | Approximate fee
- Monthly cost: **$232**
Add it to your variable costs; it's also a deductible business expense.


#### Before you sign up, ask

1. Fee per payment in person, remote and keyed in.
2. Monthly fee, device cost and minimum contract.
3. How many days until deposit and whether instant deposit costs extra.
4. What happens with chargebacks and how much each one costs.



#### A case in one minute

Don Ramón compared two options: one with no monthly fee at 2.6% plus 15 cents; another with $30 a month at 2.3%. With $8,000 a month and 800 payments, the first costs him about $328 and the second $214. He chose the second.

> **Key idea:** compare the total monthly cost with your real sales, not just the percentage.



#### Check your understanding

1. What do you ask before signing up for a card reader?
*Answer:* Fees, monthly charge, contract, deposit days and chargebacks.

2. Why are payment apps risky?
*Answer:* Because if you're paid from a stolen account or tricked, there's almost no protection.


#### Remember

- Compare total cost.
- Ask about deposits and chargebacks.
- Fees are a business cost.



### Go deeper (5 more minutes)

#### Compare your options

| Item | Option A | Option B | Option C |
|---|---|---|---|
| In-person fee | | | |
| Flat fee per payment | | | |
| Monthly charge | | | |
| Deposit days | | | |
| Total cost with your sales | | | |



#### Card surcharges

Some states limit or ban charging a fee for paying by card, and card networks have their own rules. The simplest thing is to include the cost in your price. If you want to offer a cash discount, check your state's rules.



#### Personal apps for business payments

Many payment apps prohibit using personal accounts for business payments and may close them. Use the business option or an account linked to your business account. Remember: everything you collect is income, however it arrives.



#### Cases


**Case 1. Don Ramón's reader**

Don Ramón is offered a "free" reader with a two-year contract.
- *What does he do?* He calculates the total cost with his sales and reads the contract before signing.


**Case 2. Lupita's payments**

Lupita collects her orders in her personal payment app.
- *What does she do?* She uses the business option or an account linked to her business account.


**Case 3. Daniela's link**

Daniela wants to charge customers she doesn't know with a payment link.
- *What does she check?* Fee, deposit days and who pays if there's a chargeback.


#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Looking only at the percentage | Hidden cost | Total cost |
| Long contracts unread | Penalty | Read first |
| Personal apps for business | Account closed | Business option |
| Thinking apps aren't reportable | IRS problems | It's all income |

**Answers:** 1-a: 3% of $5,000. 2-b: all income is reported. 3-a: $104 versus $92 plus $30.

With option B (2.3% plus $30 a month), above what monthly sales is it cheaper than A (2.6% with no fee)?

## M4 U02. Safe app and transfer payments

**What you will be able to do:** Get paid through apps and transfers by confirming each payment and protecting your account.

**To start:** Daniela ships orders when the customer sends a screenshot of the payment. Once the screenshot was fake and she lost an $80 necklace. In this lesson you'll see how to confirm every payment.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### A screenshot isn't proof

Payment apps and transfers arrive fast, but **the customer's screenshot is not proof of payment**: it can be faked or show a "pending" payment that never arrives.



#### Before you deliver, confirm

1. Open your app or bank and check that the money is **already available**.
2. Check amount, name and date.
3. Don't accept "I sent it, check your email": fake emails imitate the apps.
4. Only then deliver.



#### Protect your payment account

| Type | Description | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Alerts | Notice for each payment received. | You confirm instantly. |
| Two-step verification | Password plus code. | Less account theft. |
| Share only your payment username or email | Never codes or passwords. | That's all you need to get paid. |
| Screenshot as proof | Easy to fake. | It isn't proof. |

#### A case in one minute

Daniela turned on alerts in her app and her bank. Now she ships only when she sees the payment available. Two customers with fake screenshots disappeared when she said she'd wait.

> **Key idea:** deliver only when the money is available in your account, not when you get a screenshot or an email.



#### Check your understanding

1. Why isn't a screenshot proof of payment?
*Answer:* Because it can be faked or show a payment that didn't go through.

2. What do you share to get paid?
*Answer:* Only your payment username, email or phone; never codes or passwords.


#### Remember

- Check your app before delivering.
- Alerts and two-step verification.
- Never share codes.



### Go deeper (5 more minutes)

#### The verification code

If someone asks for the code you got by text "to verify you're real" or "to send you the payment," it's a scam: with that code they get into your account or open one in your name.



#### Fake "pending payment" emails

Some emails imitate payment apps and say the payment is "on hold" until you pay for a "business account upgrade." Real apps don't work that way: always check inside the app.



#### If you send money by mistake

Person-to-person app payments are usually like cash: once sent, they're hard to get back. Double-check who you're paying, especially suppliers.



#### Cases


**Case 1. Daniela's screenshot**

A customer sends Daniela a screenshot and asks her to ship now.
- *What does she do?* She checks her app and ships only when the payment is available.


**Case 2. Lupita's email**

Lupita gets an email from "her payment app": a customer's payment is on hold until she pays $50.
- *What does she do?* She doesn't pay and checks inside the app; it's a scam.


**Case 3. Javier's code**

A supposed customer asks Javier for the code he received "to confirm his identity."
- *What does he do?* He doesn't share it: to pay him, they only need his payment username.


#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Accepting screenshots | You lose merchandise | Check your app |
| Sharing codes | Your account gets stolen | Never |
| Believing emails | You pay a scam | Check in the app |
| No alerts | You don't see payments | Turn them on |

### Practice

#### Interactive activity

**What would you do? (H5P):** three situations from this lesson with Daniela, Lupita and Javier. Pick the best decision in each one; if you miss, you can try again. It earns experience points.



#### Quiz

1. When do you deliver an order paid through an app? a) When they send you the screenshot · b) When the customer swears on the phone they already paid · c) When the money is available in your account
2. What do you share to get paid? a) Your payment username or email · b) Your password · c) The code you got by text
3. An email says your payment is on hold until you pay for an "upgrade." What is it? a) A normal app procedure · b) A scam: check inside the app · c) A bank fee
**Answers:** 1-c: a screenshot isn't proof. 2-a: that's enough to get paid. 3-b: apps don't charge that way.



#### Put it into practice

You get 10 app orders a day. If checking each one takes 30 seconds, how much time a day does confirming take?
**Answer:** 5 minutes a day.



#### Your plan

Turn on payment-received alerts and two-step verification in your app and your bank.



### Learn more

### Key words

- *Two-step verification:* signing in with a password plus a code or approval on your phone.
- *Pending payment:* a payment that isn't available yet and may never arrive.

## M4 U03. Scams when you sell

**What you will be able to do:** Recognize the most common scams against sellers and have a plan so you don't fall for them.

**To start:** A customer sent Javier a $2,500 check for a $1,500 job and asked for $1,000 back by Zelle. Daniela got a big order with rush shipping to another state. In this lesson you'll see these and other scams.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### Common scams against sellers

| Type | Description | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Overpayment check | They overpay and ask for the difference back. | The check bounces days later. |
| Fake screenshot | A payment that doesn't exist. | Check your app. |
| Stolen card | Big rush purchase to another address. | Chargeback. |
| Fake supplier | "We changed accounts, pay here." | Confirm another way. |

#### Your plan

1. Deliver only when the money is available in your account.
2. Never send back "extra" from a payment; if someone overpaid, cancel and have them pay the correct amount.
3. Wait until a check truly clears: "available" doesn't always mean it has been paid.
4. If a supplier changes accounts, call the number you already had.
5. Be wary of rush, big orders from strangers and shipping to another address.



#### A case in one minute

Javier didn't deposit the $2,500 check. He asked the customer for payment of the exact amount and the customer disappeared. His bank confirmed it was a known scam.

> **Key idea:** never send back money from an overpayment; rush, extra money and changed accounts are signs of a scam.



#### Check your understanding

1. What do you do if someone overpays and asks for the difference?
*Answer:* Send nothing back; ask for payment of the correct amount.

2. Does "available" in your account mean the check is good?
*Answer:* Not always: the bank can charge you back if it bounces days later.


#### Remember

- Never send back overpayments.
- Wait for the check to clear.
- Changed accounts: you call.



### Go deeper (5 more minutes)

#### Chargebacks

If you take a card and the cardholder doesn't recognize the purchase, their bank can refund them and your processor deducts it from you. Keep proof of delivery and avoid big shipments to strangers with remote payments.



#### If it already happened

Save screenshots, messages and numbers. Tell your bank or payment processor. Report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov and, if you lost money online, at the FBI's IC3 (ic3.gov). Warn other businesses in the community, without personal data.

> **Before you act, check:** any change to a supplier's payment details by calling the number you already had, never the number or link in the same message.



#### Cases


**Case 1. Javier's check**

A customer sends Javier a $2,500 check for a $1,500 job and asks for $1,000 back.
- *What does he do?* He doesn't deposit it or send anything back; he asks for payment of the exact amount.


**Case 2. Daniela's order**

A stranger orders 40 necklaces from Daniela with rush shipping to another state and pays by link.
- *What does she do?* She is wary, verifies and doesn't ship without confirming.


**Case 3. Don Ramón's supplier**

An email "from his meat supplier" asks him to pay the invoice to a new account.
- *What does he do?* He calls the supplier's number he already had before paying.


#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Sending back overpayments | You lose money | Never |
| Trusting "available" | Check bounces | Wait |
| Rush shipping to strangers | Chargeback | Verify |
| Paying new accounts | Fake supplier | You call |

#### Interactive activity

#### Quiz

1. You're overpaid by check and asked for the difference. What do you do? a) Send the difference as soon as the check shows as available · b) Send nothing back and ask for the exact amount · c) Send back half
2. Your supplier writes that they changed accounts. What do you do? a) Pay the new account · b) Reply to the same email to confirm · c) Call the number you already had

**Answer:** $1,230.



#### Your plan

Write your anti-scam plan on a card and put it where you take payments.

### Key words

- *Overpayment scam:* a scam in which you're overpaid with a fake check and asked to send back the difference.
- *Supplier impersonation:* a scam in which someone poses as your supplier so you pay another account.
