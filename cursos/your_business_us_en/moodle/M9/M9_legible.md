# Module 9. Your future

## M9 U01. Your retirement when you work for yourself

**What you will be able to do:** Learn how to save for retirement when you're self-employed: Social Security, IRAs, SEP IRAs and Solo 401(k)s.

**To start:** Don Ramón is 60. He thinks "the food truck is his retirement," but he doesn't know how much he'd get from Social Security or whether it's enough. In this lesson you'll see your options.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### Your business isn't your retirement

Your business depends on you working. If you get sick or tired, it may stop paying. That's why you need retirement savings **separate** from the business.



#### Social Security

The self-employment tax you pay (M5 U03) counts toward your Social Security. To qualify for a retirement benefit, you generally need 40 credits (about 10 years of work). Check your record and estimate in my Social Security (ssa.gov).

> **Current fact:** in 2026 you earn one Social Security credit for every $1,890 in earnings, up to 4 credits a year ($7,560). Accessed September 29, 2026 through the Social Security Administration (SSA).



#### Retirement accounts for the self-employed

| Type | Description | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Traditional or Roth IRA | For anyone with earnings. | Up to $7,500 in 2026 ($8,600 at 50 or older). |
| SEP IRA | Easy for owners to open. | Up to 25% of adjusted profit, maximum $72,000. |
| Solo 401(k) | For owners with no employees. | $24,500 as the employee plus the business's contribution, maximum $72,000. |
| Only the business | Depends on your health. | Risky. |

#### A case in one minute

Don Ramón checked his account at ssa.gov: he has 52 credits and an estimate of $1,300 a month at 67. He opened an IRA and contributes $300 a month. He knows that each year he waits to claim, his monthly benefit goes up.

> **Key idea:** keep your retirement separate from your business; check your Social Security and put something into a retirement account every month.



#### Check your understanding

1. How many credits do you generally need for a Social Security retirement benefit?
*Answer:* 40 credits.

2. Which retirement account can anyone with earnings open?
*Answer:* A traditional or Roth IRA.


#### Remember

- Retirement separate from the business.
- Check ssa.gov.
- A fixed contribution every month.



### Go deeper (5 more minutes)

#### Tax advantages

Contributions to a traditional IRA, SEP or Solo 401(k) can lower your taxes today; with a Roth you pay taxes today and withdraw tax-free in retirement, if you follow the rules. Withdrawing before age 59½ usually carries a penalty. Ask your preparer which one fits you.



#### If you file with an ITIN

Earnings are recorded with Social Security under a Social Security number. If you currently file with an ITIN, ask the SSA or a professional how your earnings might be recognized in the future. Some institutions open IRAs with an ITIN.



#### CalSavers

In California, CalSavers is the state retirement savings program. Employers with employees and no other plan must offer it, and it's also open to self-employed people.



#### Cases


**Case 1. Don Ramón's retirement**

Don Ramón doesn't know how much he'd get from Social Security.
- *What does he do?* He checks his record and estimate at ssa.gov.


**Case 2. Javier's SEP**

Javier did well this year and wants to pay less tax and save.
- *What does he look at?* Opening a SEP IRA or Solo 401(k) with his tax preparer.


**Case 3. Daniela's savings**

Daniela is 27 and thinks it's too early for retirement.
- *What does she do?* She starts with a small, fixed IRA contribution.


#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| The business as a pension | No income if it fails | Separate savings |
| Not checking ssa.gov | Surprises | Check your record |
| Withdrawing early | Penalties | Let it grow |
| Starting late | Less savings | Start small |

### Practice

#### Interactive activity

**What would you do? (H5P):** three situations from this lesson with Don Ramón, Javier and Daniela. Pick the best decision in each one; if you miss, you can try again. It earns experience points.



#### Quiz

1. Where do you check your Social Security record? a) At your bank · b) At ssa.gov · c) At the IRS
2. How much can you put in an IRA in 2026 if you're under 50? a) $72,000 · b) $24,500 · c) $7,500
3. Why isn't the business enough for retirement? a) It depends on you being able to work · b) Because small businesses don't pay taxes · c) Because it's small
**Answers:** 1-b: official site. 2-c: 2026 limit. 3-a: your health changes.



#### Put it into practice

You put $200 a month in an IRA for 25 years, not counting returns. How much did you contribute?
**Answer:** $200 times 300 months: $60,000.



#### Your plan

Create your ssa.gov account, check your estimate and set a monthly retirement contribution.



### Learn more

- **my Social Security** (SSA · English and Spanish): https://www.ssa.gov | What to look for: "my Social Security".
- **Retirement plans for self-employed people** (IRS · English): https://www.irs.gov | What to look for: "retirement plans for self-employed".
- **CalSavers** (English and Spanish): https://www.calsavers.com | What to look for: "self-employed".

### Key words

- *Social Security credit:* a unit you earn from your earnings; you need 40 for a retirement benefit.
- *IRA:* an individual retirement account with tax benefits.
- *SEP IRA:* a retirement account for business owners and their employees.

### Sources

SSA · IRS · CalSavers, accessed September 29, 2026.

## M9 U02. If the business changes or you're not there

**What you will be able to do:** Prepare to close, pause or hand over your business, and leave your information in order in case you're not there.

**To start:** Lupita was sick for three weeks and her daughter didn't know which supplier to pay, where the accounts were or how to collect on orders. In this lesson you'll see how to prepare your business for changes.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### Three situations

| Type | Description | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Pause | Season, illness, move. | Give notice and get organized. |
| Close | The business no longer pays. | Formal closing. |
| Hand over | To a relative or someone else. | Written agreement. |
| If you're not there | Nobody knows what to do. | Leave your information in order. |

#### Your business folder

1. List of suppliers, customers and contacts.
2. Business accounts and who can operate them (no written passwords).
3. Debts, loans and pending payments.
4. Tax numbers, permits, tax preparer and dates.
5. Insurance and beneficiaries.



#### A case in one minute

Lupita put together a folder with suppliers, accounts, payment dates and her preparer's contact. She added her daughter as an authorized user on the business account. The next time she was out, orders kept going.

> **Key idea:** put in one folder what someone would need to keep your business running, or to close it, without you.



#### Check your understanding

1. What goes in your business folder?
*Answer:* Suppliers, accounts, debts, tax information, permits, insurance and contacts.

2. Why not write down passwords?
*Answer:* Because if someone finds the folder, they can steal your accounts.


#### Remember

- Pause, close or hand over in an orderly way.
- Business folder.
- Never written passwords.



### Go deeper (5 more minutes)

#### Closing properly

File your last return and mark it as final if it applies, cancel your seller's permit and licenses and, if you have an LLC, formally dissolve it: in California, if you don't, the $800 minimum tax keeps running every year.



#### Handing over the business

If a relative will continue the business, agree in writing on when, under what conditions and what happens with equipment, inventory and debts. Permits and licenses are almost always in your name and must be applied for again.



#### Will and beneficiaries

The business's equipment, inventory and money are part of your estate. A will or living trust and up-to-date beneficiaries on accounts, IRAs and insurance spare your family long proceedings. There are free or low-cost legal clinics.

> **Before you act, check:** the steps to close with the IRS, your state and your city, and talk to an attorney about a will or transfer.



#### Cases


**Case 1. Lupita's illness**

Lupita's daughter didn't know how to keep orders going.
- *What does Lupita prepare?* A folder with suppliers, accounts, payments and contacts.


**Case 2. Daniela's LLC**

Daniela will stop selling and won't use her LLC anymore.
- *What does she do?* She formally dissolves it so she doesn't keep paying $800 a year.


**Case 3. Don Ramón's food truck**

Don Ramón wants his daughter to take over the food truck in a few years.
- *What does he do?* He makes a written agreement and reviews his will or trust.


#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Everything in your head | Nobody can continue | Folder |
| Written passwords | Account theft | Authorizations |
| Leaving the LLC open | $800 a year | Dissolve it |
| Handing over by word of mouth | Family conflict | In writing |

### Practice

#### Interactive activity

**What would you do? (H5P):** three situations from this lesson with Lupita, Daniela and Don Ramón. Pick the best decision in each one; if you miss, you can try again. It earns experience points.



#### Quiz

1. You close your California LLC business. What do you do? a) Nothing: you just stop selling until you come back · b) Close the bank account and that's it · c) Formally dissolve it so you stop owing the minimum
2. What should not be written in your business folder? a) Your passwords · b) The list of suppliers · c) The payment dates
3. How do you hand the business over to a relative? a) By word of mouth · b) With a written agreement · c) Without telling anyone
**Answers:** 1-c: the minimum keeps running. 2-a: theft risk. 3-b: avoids conflict.



#### Put it into practice

You forget to dissolve your California LLC for 3 years with no activity. How much minimum tax could you owe, not counting penalties?
**Answer:** $2,400.



#### Your plan

Put together your business folder and tell someone you trust where it is.



### Learn more

- **Closing a business** (IRS · English): https://www.irs.gov | What to look for: "closing a business".
- **Dissolving an LLC** (California Secretary of State · English): https://bizfileonline.sos.ca.gov | What to look for: "dissolution".

### Key words

- *Living trust:* a legal document to pass on your assets without long court proceedings.
- *Dissolution:* the formal process to close a company.

## M9 U03. Your one-page plan

**What you will be able to do:** Bring together on one page the decisions from the course: money, price, cash flow, formality, protection and future.

**To start:** Daniela learned a lot, but it's in scattered notes. She wants one page she can pin next to her worktable. In this lesson you'll put together your one-page plan.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### Why one page

One page forces you to choose what matters. You pin it where you can see it and review it every three months.



#### Your one-page plan

| Topic | My decision |
|---|---|
| Separate money | Business account: ___ · Tax account: ___ · My salary: ___ |
| Price | Cost of my main product: ___ · Price: ___ · Margin: ___ |
| Break-even point | ___ a month · ___ a day |
| Cash flow | Reserve goal: ___ · % I set aside: ___ · Credit rules: ___ |
| Getting paid | Payment methods: ___ · Anti-scam plan: yes / no |
| Formality | Structure: ___ · EIN and permits: ___ · Estimated payment dates: ___ |
| Credit | Do I need it? ___ · APR and total repayment: ___ |
| Protection | Health insurance: ___ · Business insurance: ___ · Trademark: ___ |
| Monthly numbers | Review on day ___ |
| Future | Social Security checked: yes / no · Retirement account: ___ · Folder: yes / no |



#### Review every three months

Set a fixed date, for example alongside your estimated payments. Update the numbers, check whether you followed through and change one decision if needed.



#### A case in one minute

Daniela filled in her page in one afternoon. Two things were missing: her retirement account and checking her trademark. She made them goals for next quarter.

> **Key idea:** your plan fits on one page; review it every three months and improve one thing at a time.



#### Check your understanding

1. How often do you review your plan?
*Answer:* Every three months.

2. What do you do if something is missing?
*Answer:* Make it a goal for next quarter.


#### Remember

- One page.
- Where you can see it.
- Quarterly review.



### Go deeper (5 more minutes)

#### Prioritize

If a lot is missing, start with: 1) separating money and setting aside taxes, 2) a price with margin, 3) a reserve, 4) health and business insurance. The rest can wait a few months.



#### Share it

Share your plan with someone you trust or in the community (with no personal data or real amounts). A free adviser from an SBDC or SCORE can also review it with you.



#### Cases


**Case 1. Daniela's page**

Daniela is missing her retirement account and her trademark.
- *What does she do?* She makes them goals for next quarter.


**Case 2. Javier's priorities**

Javier has many pending tasks and doesn't know where to start.
- *What does he do?* He starts by separating money, setting aside taxes and his price.


**Case 3. Don Ramón's review**

Don Ramón made his plan three months ago and hasn't reviewed it.
- *What does he do?* He sets a fixed date and updates his numbers.


#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Scattered notes | They get forgotten | One page |
| Wanting everything at once | You get overwhelmed | Prioritize |
| Not reviewing | It gets outdated | Every three months |
| Keeping it in a drawer | You don't see it | Pin it up |

### Practice

#### Interactive activity

**What would you do? (H5P):** three situations from this lesson with Daniela, Javier and Don Ramón. Pick the best decision in each one; if you miss, you can try again. It earns experience points.



#### Quiz

1. How often do you review your one-page plan? a) Every three months · b) Once in a lifetime · c) Every day
2. Where do you start if a lot is missing? a) Registering your trademark · b) Separating money and setting aside taxes · c) Taking out a loan to get everything organized at once
3. What do you share in the community? a) Your real amounts, accounts and monthly sales · b) Your Social Security number · c) Your plan with no personal data
**Answers:** 1-a: quarterly review. 2-b: the foundation. 3-c: no data.



#### Put it into practice

You review your plan every three months. How many reviews do you do in two years?
**Answer:** 8.



#### Your plan

Fill in your one-page plan and set the date of your first review.

### Key words

- *One-page plan:* a summary of your business decisions on one page.
- *Quarterly review:* reviewing your plan every three months.
