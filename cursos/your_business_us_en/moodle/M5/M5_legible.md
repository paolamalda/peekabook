# Module 5. Formalize and handle taxes without fear

## M5 U01. Sole proprietor, LLC or something else?

**What you will be able to do:** Learn the most common ways to set up a small business, what they protect and what they cost, so you can decide with information.

**To start:** Javier was told he "has to open an LLC" to work with contractors. A website charges $299 to do it. He doesn't know if he needs one. In this lesson you'll see the options.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### The most common forms

| Type | Description | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Sole proprietor | You start when you sell; no company paperwork. | You are personally responsible. |
| LLC | A company registered in your state. | Separates your personal assets from business debts, if you run it properly. |
| Partnership | Two or more owners. | Written agreement. |
| Corporation | More paperwork and rules. | For larger businesses. |

#### The key question

An LLC can protect your home and savings if the business has debts or lawsuits, but it costs money every year and requires keeping everything separate. For many new businesses, being a sole proprietor with good insurance is enough at first.

> **Current fact:** in California, an LLC pays an $800 minimum annual tax to the Franchise Tax Board, even with no income, until it is formally dissolved; it also files a Statement of Information every two years with the Secretary of State. Accessed September 29, 2026 through the California Franchise Tax Board and Secretary of State.



#### A case in one minute

Javier did the math: an LLC would cost him at least $800 a year in California. He decided to start as a sole proprietor with liability insurance (M7 U02) and revisit the LLC when he grows. If he opens one, he'll do it on the state's official site, without paying $299 to a middleman.

> **Key idea:** an LLC protects, but it costs; compare it with your risk and your insurance, and do the paperwork on official sites.



#### Check your understanding

1. What does an LLC protect?
*Answer:* Your personal assets against business debts or lawsuits, if you run it properly.

2. What is the minimum yearly cost of an LLC in California?
*Answer:* The $800 minimum annual tax.


#### Remember

- Sole proprietor: simple, no separation.
- LLC: protects, but costs.
- Paperwork on official sites.



### Go deeper (5 more minutes)

#### Business name (DBA)

If as a sole proprietor you use a name different from your own ("Tamales Lupita"), in California you register a Fictitious Business Name (DBA) with your county. It isn't a company: it only registers the name.



#### For the LLC to protect you

Use a bank account only for the LLC, sign contracts in the LLC's name and don't mix money. If you mix, a judge may disregard the separation.



#### Watch out for middlemen

Many websites charge to form an LLC or get an EIN, things you can do yourself on official sites. Letters that imitate the government also arrive charging for "certificates" or "annual reports." Always check the sender.

> **Before you act, check:** your state's rules and costs on the Secretary of State's site; if you have questions, talk to an accountant or a no-cost SBA center (SBDC).



#### Cases


**Case 1. Javier's LLC**

A website charges Javier $299 to open a "required" LLC.
- *What does he do?* He compares costs and risks, and if he opens one, he uses the state's official site.


**Case 2. Lupita's name**

Lupita sells as "Tamales Lupita" and is a sole proprietor.
- *What does she do?* She registers her fictitious business name with her county.


**Case 3. Daniela's LLC account**

Daniela has an LLC, but takes payments in her personal account.
- *What does she do?* She opens an account just for the LLC and stops mixing.


#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Opening an LLC without doing the math | $800 every year | Compare |
| Paying middlemen | Unnecessary cost | Official site |
| Mixing money in the LLC | You lose protection | Separate account |
| Skipping the DBA | Trouble with banks | Register your name |

### Practice

#### Interactive activity

**What would you do? (H5P):** three situations from this lesson with Javier, Lupita and Daniela. Pick the best decision in each one; if you miss, you can try again. It earns experience points.



#### Quiz

1. Which business form starts without company paperwork? a) Sole proprietor · b) LLC · c) Corporation
2. Your California LLC had no sales this year. Does it pay the minimum tax? a) No, if it didn't sell · b) Only if it earned over $10,000 · c) Yes, $800 while it exists
3. What makes an LLC lose its protection? a) Having insurance · b) Mixing LLC money with yours · c) Having its own LLC account
**Answers:** 1-a: you start when you sell. 2-c: even with no sales. 3-b: the separation breaks.



#### Put it into practice

You keep an LLC in California for 3 years. What is the minimum you pay in annual tax?
**Answer:** $800 times 3: $2,400.



#### Your plan

Write down your business's current form and the risk that worries you; check your state's rules.



### Learn more

- **Choose a business structure** (SBA · English): https://www.sba.gov | What to look for: "business structure".
- **LLCs in California** (Secretary of State · English): https://bizfileonline.sos.ca.gov | What to look for: "LLC".

### Key words

- *LLC:* limited liability company: a company registered in a state that separates your personal assets from the business's debts.
- *Sole proprietor:* a person who owns a business in their own name, without creating a separate company.
- *DBA:* the fictitious business name you operate under.

## M5 U02. Numbers and permits: EIN, ITIN and licenses

**What you will be able to do:** Know which tax number to use, how to get a no-cost EIN and which licenses and permits your business may need.

**To start:** Lupita wants to sell tamales at a market and they ask for her "license, health permit and seller's permit." She doesn't know where to start or whether she can do it with her ITIN. In this lesson you'll see the most common numbers and permits.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### Your tax numbers

| Type | Description | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| SSN or ITIN | Your personal tax number. | The ITIN lets you file if you don't have an SSN. |
| EIN | Your business's number with the IRS. | At no cost on irs.gov. |
| Seller's permit (California) | To sell taxable products. | From the CDTFA, at no cost. |
| Sites that charge for an EIN | The process costs nothing. | Don't pay. |

#### The EIN

An EIN identifies your business. You need it if you have employees or certain kinds of LLC, and it's useful for opening accounts and giving to customers instead of your SSN. You get it no-cost on the IRS website; the person applying needs an SSN or ITIN.



#### Permits you may need

1. **Business license** from your city or county.
2. **Seller's permit** from the CDTFA if you sell products (M5 U05).
3. **Health permit** from the county if you sell food (for example, a Cottage Food Operation or a food truck permit).
4. **Professional license** if your trade requires one (for example, the CSLB for construction).

> **Current fact:** in California, construction or repair jobs over $1,000 in total (labor and materials), or that require a permit, need a Contractors State License Board (CSLB) license. Accessed September 29, 2026 through the CSLB (AB 2622, effective January 1, 2025).



#### A case in one minute

Lupita got her EIN no-cost at irs.gov with her ITIN, requested her seller's permit from the CDTFA at no cost and registered as a Cottage Food Operation with her county health department. Now she can sell at the market.

> **Key idea:** the EIN and the seller's permit cost nothing on official sites; check with your city and county which licenses your trade needs.



#### Check your understanding

1. How much does an EIN cost?
*Answer:* Nothing: it costs nothing at irs.gov.

2. What permit do you need in California to sell taxable products?
*Answer:* A seller's permit from the CDTFA.


#### Remember

- No-cost EIN at irs.gov.
- Seller's permit at no cost.
- Licenses by city and trade.



### Go deeper (5 more minutes)

#### The ITIN

The ITIN is an IRS number for people who must file taxes and can't get an SSN. With it you can report your business income and apply for an EIN. You apply with Form W-7, directly or through a Certifying Acceptance Agent (CAA). Filing your income builds a history that can help you with credit and other processes.



#### Your immigration status

This course does not give immigration advice. If you have questions about how your status affects your business, talk to an immigration attorney or an organization accredited by the Department of Justice. Beware of "notarios" who offer immigration services.



#### Find what your city requires

CalGold (calgold.ca.gov) shows the permits usually required in California by city and type of business. In other states, look for your state's small business office or your local SBDC.



#### Cases


**Case 1. Lupita's EIN**

A website charges Lupita $150 to get her EIN.
- *What does she do?* She gets it no-cost at irs.gov with her ITIN.


**Case 2. Javier's big job**

Javier is offered a $4,000 bathroom remodel.
- *What does he check?* That in California he needs a CSLB license for jobs over $1,000.


**Case 3. Don Ramón's notario**

A "notario" offers to fix Don Ramón's immigration papers and permits for $2,000.
- *What does he do?* He doesn't pay and looks for an attorney or accredited organization.


#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Paying for an EIN | Unnecessary cost | irs.gov |
| Selling without permits | Fines or shutdown | Check with your city |
| Big jobs without a license | Fines and no pay | Check the CSLB |
| Trusting "notarios" | Fraud | Attorney or accredited organization |

### Practice

#### Interactive activity

**What would you do? (H5P):** three situations from this lesson with Lupita, Javier and Don Ramón. Pick the best decision in each one; if you miss, you can try again. It earns experience points.



#### Quiz

1. Where do you get your EIN? a) From a private site that charges a fee · b) At no cost on irs.gov · c) At your bank
2. What is the ITIN for? a) Filing taxes if you can't get an SSN · b) Working as an employee · c) Voting
3. In California, when do you need a CSLB license? a) Never for small painting or repair jobs · b) Only if you have full-time employees · c) For jobs over $1,000 or that require a permit
**Answers:** 1-b: it costs nothing. 2-a: IRS tax number. 3-c: rule in effect since 2025.



#### Put it into practice

A website charges you $150 for the EIN and $100 for the seller's permit. How much do you save by doing it yourself?
**Answer:** $250.



#### Your plan

Look up in CalGold (or your state's office) which licenses your type of business needs in your city.



### Learn more

- **Apply for an EIN** (IRS · English and Spanish): https://www.irs.gov | What to look for: "employer identification number".
- **ITIN** (IRS · English and Spanish): https://www.irs.gov | What to look for: "ITIN Form W-7".
- **Permits in California** (CalGold · English): https://www.calgold.ca.gov | What to look for: your city and type of business.

### Key words

- *EIN:* employer identification number: your business costs nothing number with the IRS.
- *ITIN:* individual taxpayer identification number, for people who can't get an SSN.
- *Seller's permit:* a California permit to sell products and collect sales tax.

### Sources

## M5 U03. Your federal taxes as an owner

**What you will be able to do:** Understand the two federal taxes a sole proprietor pays, which expenses are deductible and how much to set aside.

**To start:** Daniela made $14,000 in profit in her first year selling jewelry. In April she found out she owed the IRS more than $2,000 and hadn't set anything aside. In this lesson you'll see why and how to prepare.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### Two taxes on your profit

| Type | Description | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Self-employment tax | Social Security and Medicare. | 15.3% of almost all your profit. |
| Income tax | Based on your total income and family. | Can be 0% or more. |
| Business deductions | Ordinary and necessary expenses. | They lower your profit. |
| 20% deduction (QBI) | On business profit, with limits. | Lowers your income tax. |

> **Current fact:** self-employment tax is 15.3% (12.4% Social Security and 2.9% Medicare) on 92.35% of your net profit; you pay it if you earn $400 or more. The Social Security portion applies up to $184,500 in 2026. The 20% qualified business income (QBI) deduction became permanent starting in 2026. Accessed September 29, 2026 through the IRS and the One Big Beautiful Bill Act.

#### Daniela's self-employment tax

- Net profit for the year: **$14,000**
- Tax base: **92.35%**
- Rate: **15.3%**
- Self-employment tax (approx.): **$1,978**
Depending on her total income, she may also owe income tax.


#### A case in one minute

Daniela didn't know that as an owner she pays the full Social Security and Medicare. This year she sets aside 25% of each profit in a separate account and keeps her receipts to deduct materials, shipping and fees.

> **Key idea:** as an owner you pay 15.3% for Social Security and Medicare, plus income tax; set aside 25% to 30% of your profit and deduct your expenses with receipts.



#### Check your understanding

1. What two federal taxes does a sole proprietor pay on profit?
*Answer:* Self-employment tax and income tax.

2. From what profit do you pay self-employment tax?
*Answer:* From $400 of net profit a year.


#### Remember

- 15.3% self-employment tax.
- Plus income tax.
- Deductions with receipts.



### Go deeper (5 more minutes)

#### Commonly deducted expenses

| Expense | What you need |
|---|---|
| Materials, ingredients, merchandise | Receipts |
| App and card fees | Statements |
| Business miles (standard rate) | Mileage log |
| Part of your phone and internet | Estimate of business use |
| Home office (space used only for business) | Measurements and IRS rules |
| Business insurance, licenses, permits | Receipts |



#### Schedule C

As a sole proprietor, you report sales and expenses on Schedule C of your return (Form 1040) and figure self-employment tax on Schedule SE. Half of the self-employment tax is subtracted from your income.



#### No-cost or low-cost help

VITA programs help people with moderate incomes at no cost, although not all of them handle businesses with complex expenses. As your business grows, an accountant or a preparer with a PTIN saves you mistakes. Your state (in California, the Franchise Tax Board) also charges income tax.

> **Before you act, check:** your situation with a tax preparer; the rules change every year and depend on your family and other income.



#### Cases


**Case 1. Daniela's surprise**

Daniela owes the IRS more than $2,000 and didn't set anything aside.
- *What does she do from today on?* She sets aside 25% of each profit and keeps receipts to deduct.


**Case 2. Javier's receipts**

Javier doesn't keep his paint receipts because "the IRS doesn't ask for them."
- *What does he do?* He keeps them: without receipts he can't prove his deductions.


**Case 3. Lupita's small profit**

Lupita made $3,000 in profit for the year and thinks she doesn't have to file.
- *What does she check?* That with $400 or more of profit she must file and pay self-employment tax.


#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Not setting aside | IRS debt | 25% to 30% |
| No receipts | You lose deductions | Keep them |
| Thinking small profits aren't reported | Penalties | From $400 |
| Forgetting state tax | Double surprise | Check your state |

### Practice

#### Interactive activity

**What would you do? (H5P):** three situations from this lesson with Daniela, Javier and Lupita. Pick the best decision in each one; if you miss, you can try again. It earns experience points.



#### Quiz

1. What is the self-employment tax rate? a) 15.3% · b) 7.65% · c) 2.5%
2. You made $350 in business profit this year. Do you pay self-employment tax? a) Yes, always, even if you earn little · b) Yes, if you sold through apps · c) No, because it's under $400
3. What do you need to deduct an expense? a) To remember it · b) That it's for the business and you have proof · c) That it's over $600
**Answers:** 1-a: Social Security and Medicare. 2-c: the minimum is $400. 3-b: ordinary, necessary and with proof.



#### Put it into practice

Your net profit is $20,000. About how much is your self-employment tax?
**Answer:** $20,000 times 92.35% times 15.3%: about $2,826.



#### Your plan

Open an account for taxes and decide what percentage of each profit you'll set aside.



### Learn more

- **Self-employment tax** (IRS · English and Spanish): https://www.irs.gov | What to look for: "self-employment tax".
- **No-cost VITA help** (IRS · English and Spanish): https://www.irs.gov | What to look for: "VITA".

### Key words

- *Self-employment tax:* Social Security and Medicare paid by people who work for themselves.
- *Deduction:* an expense subtracted from your income to figure your taxes.
- *QBI:* a deduction of up to 20% on a business's qualified profit.

## M5 U04. Quarterly payments and 1099 forms

**What you will be able to do:** Know the estimated tax dates, the 1099 forms you may receive or send and how to protect yourself from fake "IRS" scams.

**To start:** Javier got a 1099 form from a contractor and didn't know what it was. He also got a call "from the IRS" saying he'd be arrested if he didn't pay with gift cards. In this lesson you'll see your calendar and how to protect yourself.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### Estimated payments

Since nobody withholds taxes for you, the IRS expects you to pay during the year with estimated payments if you expect to owe $1,000 or more. If you don't, you may pay penalties.

> **Current fact:** the estimated tax due dates for tax year 2026 are April 15, June 15 and September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027. Accessed September 29, 2026 through the IRS (Form 1040-ES).



#### The 1099 forms

| Type | Description | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| 1099-NEC | A business paid you for services. | From $2,000 for 2026 payments. |
| 1099-K | A platform or processor reports your payments. | Over $20,000 and over 200 transactions. |
| All income is reported | Whether or not you get a 1099. | Always. |
| "No 1099, no reporting" | Common mistake. | Not true. |

> **Current fact:** starting with 2026 payments, Form 1099-NEC is sent when a business pays you $2,000 or more in the year (previously $600). Form 1099-K is sent by platforms and processors when your payments exceed $20,000 and 200 transactions. All income is reported even if you don't receive a form. Accessed September 29, 2026 through the IRS and the One Big Beautiful Bill Act.

#### A case in one minute

Javier understood that the 1099-NEC only reports what he already earned. Now he pays every quarter with IRS Direct Pay using what he sets aside. The "IRS" call was a scam: the IRS doesn't call to demand gift cards.

> **Key idea:** pay your taxes every quarter and report all your income; the IRS never demands gift cards or threatens arrest by phone.



#### Check your understanding

1. When should you make estimated payments?
*Answer:* If you expect to owe the IRS $1,000 or more for the year.

2. Do you report income if you didn't get a 1099?
*Answer:* Yes: all income is reported.


#### Remember

- April, June, September and January.
- All income is reported.
- The IRS doesn't ask for gift cards.



### Go deeper (5 more minutes)

#### How much to pay each quarter

A practical approach: take what you set aside (25% to 30% of the quarter's profit) and pay it through IRS Direct Pay or your IRS online account. Your tax preparer can calculate it more precisely with Form 1040-ES. In California, the FTB also expects estimated payments of state tax.



#### Fake "IRS" scams

The IRS usually contacts you first by mail. It doesn't call to demand immediate payment, doesn't ask for gift cards, crypto or transfers to people, and doesn't threaten you with police. If in doubt, go to irs.gov yourself or call the official number.



#### Your Identity Protection PIN

The IRS offers an Identity Protection PIN (IP PIN) to stop someone from filing a return with your SSN or ITIN. Request it at irs.gov.



#### Cases


**Case 1. Javier's 1099**

Javier received a $6,000 1099-NEC from a contractor.
- *What does he do?* He includes it on his return with the rest of his income.


**Case 2. Don Ramón's call**

A call "from the IRS" demands that Don Ramón pay today with gift cards.
- *What does he do?* He hangs up and checks his account at irs.gov; it's a scam.


**Case 3. Daniela's sales**

Daniela sold $9,000 on a platform and didn't receive a 1099-K.
- *What does she do?* She reports those sales: all income is reported.


#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Not paying quarterly | Penalties | Pay every quarter |
| Waiting for the 1099 | You don't report income | Report everything |
| Paying with gift cards | Scam | Never |
| Answering "IRS" calls | Theft | Go to irs.gov yourself |

### Practice

#### Interactive activity

**What would you do? (H5P):** three situations from this lesson with Javier, Don Ramón and Daniela. Pick the best decision in each one; if you miss, you can try again. It earns experience points.



#### Quiz

1. Which is a 2026 estimated payment date? a) June 15 · b) December 31 · c) March 1
2. You didn't get a 1099-K for your platform sales. What do you do? a) Don't report those sales · b) Report only if you sold more than $600 · c) Report them anyway
3. Someone calls "from the IRS" to be paid today with gift cards. What is it? a) A scam · b) A real urgent collection · c) A new IRS method for small payments
**Answers:** 1-a: second estimated payment. 2-c: all income is reported. 3-a: the IRS doesn't collect that way.



#### Put it into practice

Your profit for the quarter was $5,000 and you set aside 25%. How much is your estimated payment?
**Answer:** $1,250.



#### Your plan

Put the four estimated payment dates on your calendar and request your IP PIN at irs.gov.



### Learn more

- **Estimated taxes** (IRS · English and Spanish): https://www.irs.gov | What to look for: "estimated taxes 1040-ES".
- **Tax scams** (IRS · English and Spanish): https://www.irs.gov | What to look for: "scams".

### Key words

- *Estimated payments:* tax payments you make each quarter on what you're earning.
- *1099-NEC:* a form that reports payments for services to independent workers.
- *1099-K:* a form that reports payments through platforms and payment processors.
- *IP PIN:* an IRS number that protects your return from identity theft.

### Sources

## M5 U05. Sales tax

**What you will be able to do:** Know which sales carry sales tax, how it's collected and how it's sent to the state.

**To start:** Lupita sells food at a market and at parties. Someone told her she has to charge sales tax; someone else told her food doesn't have it. In this lesson you'll see how it works, using California as the example.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### The tax isn't yours

Sales tax is collected from your customer and sent to the state. It's not part of your profit: set it aside as soon as you collect it. Each state has its own rules; some have no sales tax.

> **Current fact:** in California, the base statewide sales tax rate is 7.25%, and many cities and counties add local rates. Selling taxable products requires a seller's permit from the CDTFA, which costs nothing. Accessed September 29, 2026 through the CDTFA.



#### Is it taxable? (California, in general)

| Type | Description | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Products: clothing, jewelry, goods | Yes, in general. | Seller's permit. |
| Hot food or food eaten on site | Yes, in general. | Special rules. |
| Cold food to go | Often not. | Check your case. |
| Many services (painting, cleaning) | Generally not. | Check materials. |

#### Tax on one of Daniela's sales

- Price of a necklace: **$50**
- Total rate in her city (example): **9.25%**
- Tax she collects and sends to the state: **$4.63**


#### A case in one minute

Lupita checked with the CDTFA: her hot food at the market is taxable; cold tamales to go generally aren't. Now she sets the tax she collects aside in her tax account and files her return when the CDTFA tells her.

> **Key idea:** sales tax belongs to the state, not you; find out which sales are taxable, charge it separately and set it aside.



#### Check your understanding

1. Whose is the sales tax you collect?
*Answer:* The state's; you collect it and send it in.

2. What do you need in California to sell taxable products?
*Answer:* A seller's permit from the CDTFA.


#### Remember

- It belongs to the state.
- Depends on the product and the state.
- Set it aside when you collect.



### Go deeper (5 more minutes)

#### If you sell on platforms

In most states, large online marketplaces collect and send sales tax for you on sales made through them (they're called "marketplace facilitators"). Your direct sales through your own site or social media are still your responsibility.



#### Sales tax returns

The CDTFA assigns you a filing frequency (yearly, quarterly or monthly) based on how much you sell. File even if you had no sales in the period, if they tell you to.



#### Fairs and events

If you sell at fairs or temporary events, you may need a temporary permit or to report sales for the event location. Ask the organizer and the CDTFA.

> **Before you act, check:** which products are taxable and your city's rate on the CDTFA's site (or your state's); food rules are special.



#### Cases


**Case 1. Lupita's food**

Lupita doesn't know whether her food is taxable.
- *What does she do?* She checks with the CDTFA which sales are taxable and sets the tax aside when she collects.


**Case 2. Daniela's online store**

Daniela sells on a large platform and on her own site.
- *What does she check?* That the platform collects tax on its sales, but she must collect it on her own site.


**Case 3. Javier's services**

Javier thinks he must charge sales tax for painting.
- *What does he check?* That many services aren't taxable, and he checks his case with the CDTFA.


#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Thinking the tax is yours | You spend it | Set it aside |
| Applying it to everything | High prices | Check your product |
| Forgetting direct sales | Debt with the state | Collect it |
| Not filing | Penalties | By your frequency |

### Practice

#### Interactive activity

**What would you do? (H5P):** three situations from this lesson with Lupita, Daniela and Javier. Pick the best decision in each one; if you miss, you can try again. It earns experience points.



#### Quiz

1. What is California's base statewide sales tax rate? a) 16% · b) 0% · c) 7.25%
2. You sell $1,000 in products at a 9% rate. How much tax do you collect? a) $90 · b) $9 · c) $900
3. What do you do with the sales tax you collect? a) Use it to buy materials · b) Set it aside and send it to the state · c) Keep it as a business reserve
**Answers:** 1-c: plus local rates. 2-a: 9% of $1,000. 3-b: it belongs to the state.



#### Put it into practice

You sell $2,400 in the quarter at a 9.5% rate. How much tax do you owe?
**Answer:** $228.



#### Your plan

Check whether your sales are taxable in your state and, if so, get your permit.

### Key words

- *Sales tax:* a tax you collect from your customer and send to the state.
- *Marketplace facilitator:* a platform that collects and sends sales tax for the sellers on it.

CDTFA, accessed September 29, 2026.
