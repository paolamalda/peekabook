# Module 2. Costs and price

## M2 U01. Fixed and variable costs

**What you will be able to do:** Split your costs into fixed and variable and figure out how much each product or service really costs you.

**To start:** Daniela spends $6 on materials for each pair of earrings and sells them for $25. She thinks she makes $19 a pair. But she hasn't counted the online store fee, shipping or her monthly subscription. In this lesson you'll see what each pair really costs.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### Two kinds of costs

| Type | Description | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed | You pay them whether you sell or not: subscriptions, insurance, phone, permits. | Same each month. |
| Variable | They go up when you sell more: materials, packaging, fees, shipping. | Per sale. |
| Your salary | One more fixed cost. | Don't forget it. |
| Unsorted | You don't know what it costs to sell. | Sort them. |

#### Your cost per unit

Cost per unit adds what you spend on each piece (variable) plus its share of your fixed costs.



#### Daniela's earrings

- Materials: **$6**
- Packaging, shipping and fee: **$5**
- Share of fixed costs ($360 a month ÷ 120 pairs): **$3**
- Real cost per pair: **$14**
Daniela doesn't make $19 a pair but $11, and her taxes come out of that.


#### A case in one minute

Lupita figured out that each dozen tamales costs her $14 in ingredients and $4 as her share of fixed costs: $18. She sells it for $30. Now she knows she keeps $12 a dozen.

> **Key idea:** add to each product what you spend on it plus its share of your fixed costs.



#### Check your understanding

1. What is a fixed cost?
*Answer:* One you pay even if you don't sell, such as a subscription, insurance or your salary.

2. How do you spread fixed costs per unit?
*Answer:* Divide the month's fixed costs by the units you sell in a month.


#### Remember

- Fixed: sell or not.
- Variable: per sale.
- Cost per unit = variable + share of fixed.



### Go deeper (5 more minutes)

#### Your monthly costs

| Cost | Fixed or variable | Monthly amount |
|---|---|---|
| Rent or commercial kitchen | Fixed | |
| Phone, internet, subscriptions | Fixed | |
| Insurance and permits (divided by 12) | Fixed | |
| Your salary and helpers | Fixed | |
| Materials or ingredients | Variable | |
| Packaging and shipping | Variable | |
| Card and app fees | Variable | |



#### Services: your hour has a cost

If you sell services, like Javier, your main cost is your time. Divide your fixed costs and salary by the hours you actually bill in a month: that's your cost per hour. Add materials, miles and tool wear.



#### Cases


**Case 1. Daniela's earrings**

Daniela thinks she makes $19 a pair because materials cost $6 and she sells them for $25.
- *What is she missing?* Packaging, shipping, fees and her share of fixed costs.


**Case 2. Javier's hour**

Javier charges $35 an hour, but doesn't know if it's enough.
- *What does he calculate?* His cost per hour with his fixed costs, salary and billed hours.


**Case 3. Don Ramón's insurance**

Don Ramón pays the food truck insurance once a year and never counts it in his costs.
- *What does he do?* He divides it by 12 and adds it to his monthly fixed costs.


#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Counting only materials | Low price | Add fixed costs |
| Forgetting your salary | You work without pay | It's a fixed cost |
| Ignoring yearly payments | Surprises | Divide by 12 |
| Not counting time | Cheap service | Cost per hour |

### Practice

#### Interactive activity

**What would you do? (H5P):** three situations from this lesson with Daniela, Javier and Don Ramón. Pick the best decision in each one; if you miss, you can try again. It earns experience points.



#### Quiz

1. Which of these is a variable cost? a) Your online store subscription · b) The packaging for each order · c) Your phone plan
2. Your fixed costs are $600 and you sell 200 pieces a month. How much fixed cost goes to each piece? a) $3 · b) $30 · c) $600
3. Is your salary a business cost? a) No, it's profit · b) Only if something is left at the end of the month · c) Yes, it's a fixed cost
**Answers:** 1-b: it goes up with each sale. 2-a: $600 divided by 200. 3-c: without it you work without pay.



#### Put it into practice

A cake costs you $18 in ingredients and $2 for the box. Your fixed costs are $400 a month and you sell 50 cakes. What is the cost per cake?
**Answer:** $18 plus $2 plus $8 ($400 divided by 50): $28.



#### Your plan

List your monthly costs, split them into fixed and variable and figure out the cost of your main product.



### Learn more

- **Calculate your startup costs** (SBA · English): https://www.sba.gov | What to look for: "calculate your startup costs".

### Key words

- *Fixed cost:* an expense you pay whether you sell or not, like a subscription or your salary.
- *Variable cost:* an expense that goes up with each sale, like materials or packaging.
- *Cost per unit:* what it costs you to make or sell one piece, dish or service, counting variable costs and a share of fixed costs.

## M2 U02. How much should you charge?

**What you will be able to do:** Set a price that covers your costs, leaves a margin and makes sense for your customers and against the competition.

**To start:** Javier charges the same as other painters in his area because "that's the going rate." But he has insurance, a truck and new tools. In this lesson you'll see how to set your own price.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### Three ways to look at your price

Check your price from three sides: **your cost** (the floor, never charge less), **the competition** (what others charge for something similar) and **the value to your customer** (speed, quality, guarantee, insurance).



#### Price with a margin

- Real cost per pair of earrings: **$14**
- For a 30% margin on price: **0.70**
- Minimum price: **$20**
Daniela sells at $25: her margin is 44%. She has room for promotions without losing.


#### Your price against others

| Type | Description | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Cheapest of all | Lots of sales, little profit. | Risk of losing. |
| Similar | You compete on service and quality. | Show why you. |
| More expensive | You need to explain the value. | Insurance, guarantee, speed. |
| Below your cost | You lose on every sale. | Never. |

#### A case in one minute

Javier figured out his cost per hour is $32. He charged $35 and barely kept anything. He raised it to $45 and his estimates now say he has liability insurance and a 30-day guarantee. He lost one customer, but earned more with the rest.

> **Key idea:** your cost is the floor; your margin lets you grow and pay taxes; competition and value tell you how high you can go.



#### Check your understanding

1. What is the floor for your price?
*Answer:* Your cost per unit: you never charge less.

2. What is margin?
*Answer:* The part of the price left after covering the cost.


#### Remember

- Cost = floor.
- Margin = growth.
- Competition and value = ceiling.



### Go deeper (5 more minutes)

#### Margin and markup are not the same

If you add 30% to a $100 cost, you charge $130 and your margin is 23% ($30 divided by $130). For a 30% margin, divide the cost by 0.70: $143.



#### Shipping, fees and sales tax

Shipping, platform fees and card fees are variable costs: add them to the cost or charge them clearly. Sales tax is not part of your price: it's charged separately and belongs to the state (M5 U05).



#### Raising prices without losing customers

Give notice ahead of time, explain why (materials, quality) and keep a budget option if you can.



#### Cases


**Case 1. Javier's price**

Javier charges the same as other painters, even though his costs are higher.
- *What does he do?* He figures out his cost per hour and sets a price with margin, explaining his insurance and guarantee.


**Case 2. Daniela's sale**

Daniela wants to drop her earrings to $15 to sell more.
- *What does she check?* That $15 covers her real cost of $14 and leaves margin for taxes.


**Case 3. Lupita's price increase**

Masa and meat went up and Lupita's tamales barely make money.
- *What does she do?* She recalculates her cost, tells her customers and raises the price with notice.


#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Copying prices | You don't cover your costs | Figure your own |
| Selling below cost | You lose on every sale | Cost = floor |
| Counting sales tax as profit | You owe the state | It's separate |
| Raising without notice | Upset customers | Give notice and explain |

### Practice

#### Interactive activity

**What would you do? (H5P):** three situations from this lesson with Javier, Daniela and Lupita. Pick the best decision in each one; if you miss, you can try again. It earns experience points.



#### Quiz

1. Your cost is $35. What is the minimum price for a 30% margin on price? a) $45.50 · b) $35 · c) $50
2. When should you never sell? a) Below your cost · b) Higher than the competition · c) With a written guarantee for your customer
3. Your materials went up. What do you do? a) Use cheaper materials without telling your customers · b) Recalculate your cost and announce the new price · c) Wait for prices to drop on their own
**Answers:** 1-c: $35 divided by 0.70. 2-a: you lose on every sale. 3-b: with notice and clarity.



#### Put it into practice

A service costs you $120. You want a 25% margin on price. How much do you charge?
**Answer:** $120 divided by 0.75: $160.



#### Your plan

Check the price of your main product: does it cover your cost and leave a margin? Adjust it if not.



### Learn more

- **Pricing** (SBA · English): https://www.sba.gov | What to look for: "pricing".

### Key words

- *Margin:* the part of the price left after covering the cost.
- *Floor price:* the minimum price that covers your cost.

## M2 U03. Your break-even point

**What you will be able to do:** Figure out how much you need to sell each month to cover all your costs.

**To start:** Every month Don Ramón pays for his food truck spot, insurance, the county permit, his salary and his helper's: $6,000. He wants to know how much he must sell not to lose money. In this lesson you'll see how to calculate it.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### What it is

The break-even point is what you need to sell so your sales cover all your costs. Below it you lose; above it you earn.



#### The formula

Divide your **fixed costs** by what each dollar of sales leaves after variable costs (your contribution margin).



#### Don Ramón's food truck

- Monthly fixed costs: **$6,000**
- What each sale leaves after ingredients and fees: **60%**
- Minimum monthly sales (about $400 a day, 25 days): **$10,000**
If he sells less than $10,000, he loses; if he sells more, he earns.


#### A case in one minute

Don Ramón averages $480 a day: $12,000 a month. He is $2,000 above his break-even point: that leaves him $1,200 of profit (60% of $2,000) on top of his salary, before taxes.

> **Key idea:** know your minimum daily sales; it's your target so you don't lose.



#### Check your understanding

1. What happens if you sell less than your break-even point?
*Answer:* You lose money that month.

2. What do you need to calculate it?
*Answer:* Your fixed costs and what each sale leaves after variable costs.


#### Remember

- Fixed ÷ contribution margin.
- Your daily target so you don't lose.
- Above you earn, below you lose.



### Go deeper (5 more minutes)

#### In units

If you sell one main product, calculate in pieces. Lupita has $500 in fixed costs and each dozen leaves her $16 ($30 minus $14 in ingredients): she needs about 32 dozen a month, about 8 a week.



#### Your break-even point

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Monthly fixed costs (including your salary) | |
| What each sale leaves (%) or each piece | |
| Monthly break-even point | |
| Daily target (divided by working days) | |



#### How to lower it

Lower fixed costs, raise the price or improve what each sale leaves (buy better, reduce waste, negotiate fees).



#### Cases


**Case 1. Don Ramón's spot**

Don Ramón's parking spot goes up $400 a month.
- *What does he do?* He recalculates his break-even point and daily target.


**Case 2. Lupita's tamales**

Lupita sells 6 dozen a week and her break-even is 8.
- *What does she do?* She looks to raise sales or price, or lower costs, so she doesn't lose.


**Case 3. Daniela's shop**

Daniela wants to rent a small shop for $900 a month.
- *What does she calculate first?* How much more she would need to sell to cover the new rent.


#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Not knowing your minimum | You lose without knowing | Calculate your break-even |
| Leaving your salary out of fixed costs | False break-even | Include it |
| Not recalculating | Old targets | Every cost change |
| Renting without calculating | Debt | Calculate first |

### Practice

#### Interactive activity

**What would you do? (H5P):** three situations from this lesson with Don Ramón, Lupita and Daniela. Pick the best decision in each one; if you miss, you can try again. It earns experience points.



#### Quiz

1. Fixed costs of $3,000 and each sale leaves 50%. Break-even point? a) $1,500 · b) $6,000 · c) $3,500
2. You sell less than your break-even point. What happens? a) You earn a little · b) You're even · c) You lose money
3. What lowers your break-even point? a) Lowering fixed costs · b) Renting a bigger place in a better area · c) Giving customers more credit
**Answers:** 1-b: $3,000 divided by 0.50. 2-c: you don't cover your costs. 3-a: you need to sell less.



#### Put it into practice

Your fixed costs are $1,200 and each piece leaves you $8. How many pieces do you need to sell each month?
**Answer:** $1,200 divided by $8: 150 pieces.



#### Your plan

Calculate your break-even point and write your daily sales target where you can see it.



### Learn more

- **Break-even point** (SBA · English): https://www.sba.gov | What to look for: "break-even point".

### Key words

- *Break-even point:* minimum sales to cover all your costs: you neither earn nor lose.
- *Contribution margin:* what is left of each sale after paying its variable costs, to cover fixed costs.

## M2 U04. Inventory and waste

**What you will be able to do:** Buy what sells, reduce what gets lost and avoid tying up money in merchandise.

**To start:** Don Ramón throws out meat and tortillas every week, and Daniela has boxes of a necklace style nobody buys. In this lesson you'll see what it costs them and how to reduce it.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### Inventory is money

Your inventory is money turned into products. If it doesn't sell, it's money sitting still; if it goes bad, it's money thrown away. What gets lost is called waste.



#### Three kinds of products

| Type | Description | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Fast movers | Sell every day. | Never run out. |
| Slow movers | Sell now and then. | Buy a little. |
| Stuck | Don't sell. | Clear it out and don't rebuy. |
| Waste | Goes bad. | Measure and reduce it. |

#### How to reduce waste

1. For one week, write down what you throw out or don't sell.
2. Buy perishables more often and in smaller amounts.
3. First in, first out: use the oldest first.
4. Sell at a special price before it goes bad, if health rules allow it.



#### A case in one minute

Don Ramón wrote down his waste: $350 a month. He started buying meat twice a week in smaller amounts and prepping for his busy days. He cut waste to $120.

> **Key idea:** buy what moves, measure what you throw out and don't leave your money sitting still.



#### Check your understanding

1. Why is stuck inventory a problem?
*Answer:* It's money you can't use for anything else.

2. What is first in, first out?
*Answer:* Using or selling the oldest first so it doesn't go bad.


#### Remember

- Inventory = money.
- Measure your waste.
- Buy what moves.



### Go deeper (5 more minutes)

#### Your weekly waste

| Product | Amount lost | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| | | |
| **Total** | | |



#### Volume deals

"Buy 10 cases and get one at no cost" only pays off if you sell all 11 before they go bad or before you need that money.



#### Inventory and taxes

The cost of the merchandise you sell is subtracted on your return as cost of goods sold. Keep track of what you buy and what you have at the end of the year.



#### Cases


**Case 1. The deal for Don Ramón**

The supplier offers Don Ramón a no-cost case if he buys 10 of a product that sells slowly.
- *What does he do?* He buys only what he can sell before it expires, even if he loses the no-cost case.


**Case 2. Lupita's ingredients**

Lupita throws out chiles and husks every week.
- *What does she do?* She writes down what she throws out and buys less, more often.


**Case 3. Daniela's necklaces**

Daniela has 50 necklaces in a style that didn't sell.
- *What does she do?* She offers them at a discount to recover the money and doesn't rebuy that style.


#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Buying because of a deal | Money tied up | Buy what moves |
| Not measuring waste | You lose without knowing | Log one week |
| Keeping what doesn't sell | Takes space and money | Clear it out |
| No inventory records | Wrong tax return | Log purchases |

3. When does a "buy more" deal pay off? a) Always · b) If the supplier is trustworthy and gives you a good price · c) If you sell it all before it expires
**Answers:** 1-b: money thrown away. 2-a: money sitting still. 3-c: otherwise it's expensive.
