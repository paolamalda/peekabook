# Module 4. What you left in the United States

## M4 U01. Your last paycheck and your account back there

**What you will be able to do:** Recover the wages you're owed and decide what to do with your U.S. bank account.

**To start:** Don Rafa was detained on a Thursday; Friday was payday. His boss owes him a week, and 1,400 dollars were left in his Texas account. He thinks he lost them. In this lesson you'll see that's not necessarily true.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### Your wages are yours

What you worked must be paid to you, even if you're no longer in the U.S. You can ask your boss to deposit it into your account there or to send a check to someone you trust. The Mexican consulate can guide you if the boss doesn't pay.



#### Your U.S. account: options

| Type | Description | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Keep using it | With the bank's app or website. | Check for inactivity fees. |
| Send the money to Mexico | Transfer or send it to your account here. | Compare the cost. |
| Close it | Ask for the balance by transfer or check. | Do it in writing. |

#### Don't leave it forgotten

An account with no activity can charge fees and, after a few years, the money goes to the state as «unclaimed property». It can be recovered, but it takes more paperwork.



#### A one-minute case

Don Rafa logged into his bank's app from Mexico and transferred 1,350 dollars to his FINABIEN card. His nephew in Houston picked up the check for his last week and deposited it for him.

> **Key idea:** your last paycheck and your account back there are still yours; decide what to do with them soon.



#### Check your understanding

1. Do you lose your last paycheck if you were sent back?
*Answer:* No: it's owed to you; you can ask your boss for it.

2. What happens to an account forgotten for years?
*Answer:* It charges fees and the money goes to the state as unclaimed property.


#### Remember

- Your wages are owed to you.
- Decide about your account.
- The consulate can guide you.

## M4 U02. Your U.S. taxes

**What you will be able to do:** Check whether it's worth filing your U.S. tax return to recover taxes that were withheld.

**To start:** Lupita had taxes withheld from every check at the restaurant in Houston. She never filed because «what for». In this lesson you'll see there may be money waiting for you.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### Withholding

If you worked on payroll in the U.S., taxes were taken out of every paycheck. When you file your yearly tax return, you sometimes get part of it back: that's your **refund**.



#### What you need

| Type | Description | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Your number | SSN or ITIN. | The one you worked with. |
| W-2 forms | The ones your employer gave you. | One per employer. |
| The year | Each return is for one year. | You can file past years. |
| Your address | So the check reaches you. | It can be in Mexico. |

#### The refund can reach Mexico

The IRS doesn't deposit into Mexican accounts, but it can mail a check to your address or to someone you trust in the U.S. You generally have up to three years to claim a refund.



#### A one-minute case

Lupita asked her former employer for her W-2 forms. With help from an organization linked to the consulate, she filed two years. A check arrived at her sister's house in Houston.

> **Key idea:** if taxes were withheld, file your return; there may be a refund for you.



#### Check your understanding

1. What is the refund?
*Answer:* Part of the withheld taxes that you get back when you file.

2. Until when can you claim it?
*Answer:* Generally up to three years.


#### Remember

- Taxes were withheld.
- Ask for your W-2s.
- You have about three years.

#### Watch out for people who «get you more»

Some preparers promise huge refunds and charge a percentage. If they make up information, the problem is yours. Look for help from free-of-charge programs or through the consulate.



#### Cases


**Case 1. «What for»**

Lupita never filed.
- *What does she do?* She asks for her W-2s and checks whether she has a refund.


**Case 2. No W-2**

Don Rafa doesn't have his forms.
- *What does he do?* He asks his employer, or the IRS with his number.


**Case 3. The miracle preparer**

Memo is offered «double the refund» for a 30% fee.
- *What does he do?* He looks for no-cost help and doesn't make up information.


#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Not filing | You lose your refund | Check |
| Waiting many years | It expires | About three years |
| Making up information | Penalties | Only real information |
| Paying high percentages | You lose your money | No-cost help |

## M4 U03. Your U.S. retirement: 401(k) and Social Security

**What you will be able to do:** Check whether you left retirement money in the U.S. and who to ask so you don't lose it.

**To start:** Memo worked five years at a warehouse with a 401(k). A friend told him that if he didn't take it all out now, «the government keeps it». In this lesson you'll see what to ask before deciding.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### Your 401(k) is yours

If your employer had a 401(k) plan and you contributed, that money is yours. You can leave it, move it or withdraw it. If you withdraw it before age 59½, there's normally a penalty and taxes are withheld.



#### Before withdrawing, ask

| Type | Description | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| How much do I have? | The plan balance. | Ask the plan administrator. |
| How much is withheld? | Taxes and penalty. | In writing. |
| Can I leave it? | Keep the money invested. | And withdraw it later. |
| How do they pay me? | Transfer or check. | In your name. |

#### U.S. Social Security

If you worked with a valid Social Security number, your contributions were recorded. With 40 credits (about 10 years) you could be entitled to a pension when you reach the age. The rules for collecting it while living in Mexico depend on your case: ask the Federal Benefits Unit at the U.S. Embassy.



#### A one-minute case

Memo called the plan administrator. They explained how much would be withheld. He decided to leave the money invested and not touch it until he has a steady job.

> **Key idea:** your U.S. retirement is still yours; ask how much is withheld before taking it out.



#### Check your understanding

1. What happens if you withdraw your 401(k) before age 59½?
*Answer:* There's normally a penalty and tax withholding.

2. Where do you ask about your U.S. Social Security?
*Answer:* At the Embassy's Federal Benefits Unit.


#### Remember

- Your 401(k) is yours.
- Ask about withholding.
- Social Security: ask about your case.

#### Don't take it out out of fear

Taking everything out at once out of fear of losing it can cost you a good part in penalties and taxes. Get informed first; the money doesn't disappear because you live in Mexico.



#### Cases


**Case 1. «The government keeps it»**

Memo is told to take it all out now.
- *What does he do?* He asks the administrator how much would be withheld before deciding.


**Case 2. Ten years in construction**

Don Rafa worked 10 years with a Social Security number.
- *What does he do?* He asks the Embassy whether he's entitled and how to collect it.


**Case 3. The WhatsApp advisor**

Someone messages Lupita offering to «move her retirement» if she gives her information.
- *What does she do?* She gives no information; she talks directly with the plan administrator.


#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Taking it all out of fear | Penalty | Ask first |
| Forgetting the plan | Lost money | Write down the administrator |
| Giving information on WhatsApp | Theft | Only with the plan |
| Thinking you're not entitled | You don't ask | Ask about your case |
