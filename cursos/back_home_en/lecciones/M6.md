# M6 U01 | Think in pesos: your return budget
objetivo: Make a budget in pesos for your first 90 days, with what really comes in and goes out.
gancho: Memo earned 18 dollars an hour in the U.S. In Puebla he's offered 2,400 pesos a week. He does the math in dollars and feels «it's not enough for anything». In this lesson you'll see how to do math that actually helps.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-exchange | Switch your mindset
Comparing your pay there with your pay here only discourages you: prices are different too. Do your math in pesos, with the prices in your community.

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | Your 90-day budget
* fa-arrow-down | What comes in | Wages, odd jobs, support, savings you brought. | Only what's certain. | igual
* fa-home | The basics | Food, housing, transportation, health. | First. | igual
* fa-file-text-o | Papers | Certificates, copies, travel. | One-time expense. | igual
* fa-shield | A little set aside | For the unexpected. | Even if it's small. | igual

--- paso | fa-list | Three months, not one
In the first months, less money almost always comes in than you expect. Plan for 90 days and count only the money that's certain, not the money that «might» come.

--- paso | fa-user | A one-minute case
Memo wrote down what he spends in Puebla: shared rent, food and bus fare add up to 7,600 a month. With 2,400 a week he brings in about 10,400. He has 2,800 left for papers and his little savings.

> **Key idea:** do your math in pesos and for 90 days, with what's certain.

--- comprueba
1. Why not compare your pay in dollars with your pay here? || Because prices are different too; what helps is doing the math in pesos.
2. What money do you count in your budget? || Only money that's certain.

--- recuerda
- Math in pesos.
- Plan 90 days.
- Only what's certain.

* Thinking in dollars | You get discouraged | Math in pesos

# M6 U02 | Welcome-home obligations
objetivo: Set kind limits on the spending and loans people ask of you when you return.
gancho: When he arrived, Don Rafa's family threw him a welcome party that he paid for. Then his compadre asked to borrow money «since he's back from over there». In two weeks, half his savings were gone. In this lesson you'll see how to say no without a fight.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-users | «You came back with dollars»
Many people believe that whoever returns brings money. Sometimes it's true; often it isn't. Your savings have to last until you have income.

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | Three answers that help
* fa-comment | «I can't right now» | Without over-explaining. | Short and kind. | igual
* fa-clock-o | «When I have a job» | Change the when. | Without promising an amount. | igual
* fa-handshake-o | «I'll help you another way» | Time, contacts, skills. | Not only money. | igual

--- paso | fa-calculator | Decide an amount ahead of time
If you want to help, decide ahead of time how much you can give without putting your 90 days at risk. Give it as a gift, not as a loan that will hurt to collect.

--- paso | fa-user | A one-minute case
Don Rafa decided he could give his mother 500 pesos a month and nothing more. He told his compadre: «Not right now; when I have a job, we'll see». His savings lasted three months.

> **Key idea:** your return savings have to last; decide ahead of time how much you can help, and say no kindly.

--- comprueba
1. Why take care of your savings when you return? || Because they have to last until you have income.
2. How do you help without throwing yourself off balance? || You decide an amount ahead of time and give it as a gift.

--- recuerda
- Your savings have to last.
- Decide an amount.
- Say no kindly.

== profundiza

--- ecuacion | fa-calculator | What was gone in two weeks
= 8,000 | Don Rafa's savings
- 2,500 | Welcome party
- 1,500 | Loan to his compadre
= 4,000 | Half was left

--- tema | fa-heart | It isn't selfish
Taking care of your savings also takes care of your family: if you end up with nothing, someone will later have to help you.

--- casos
### Case 1. The party
Don Rafa's family wants a party he would pay for.
? What does he do? || He suggests something simple and sets a cap.

### Case 2. The compadre
His compadre asks for 1,500 «since he's back from over there».
? What does he do? || He says «not right now; when I have a job, we'll see».

### Case 3. Lupita's mother
Lupita wants to help her mother every month.
? What does she do? || She decides a fixed amount that doesn't put her 90 days at risk.

--- errores
* Paying for everything out of obligation | Savings disappear | Set a cap
* Lending without thinking | Hard to collect | A gift or nothing
* Over-explaining | They talk you into it | Short answer
* Feeling guilty | You give in | Protecting your savings protects everyone

== practica

--- actividad
**What would you do? (H5P):** three situations from this lesson with Don Rafa, his compadre and Lupita. Choose the best decision in each one; if you miss, you can try again. You earn experience points.

--- quiz

respuestas: 1-b: short and kind. 2-a: ahead of time. 3-c: without expecting it back.

--- ponlo
You have 9,000 in savings and need 2,500 a month to cover your expenses. How much can you give away over three months without falling short?
respuesta: 1,500 pesos.

--- plan

# M6 U03 | When the remittances stop
objetivo: Reorganize household spending when the relative who sent money comes back or stops sending.
gancho: Chayo received 300 dollars a month from her husband. Now he's back and there's no remittance. The bills and school costs keep coming. In this lesson you'll see how to adjust the household together.

== esencial

--- paso | fa-home | The household's numbers change
If the household depended on the remittance, you need a new budget with what comes in now. In 2025, remittances to Mexico fell 4.6%: many families are in the same situation.

--- tarjetas | fa-th-large | Four steps as a family
* fa-list | What comes in today | Wages, support, sales. | From everyone. | igual
* fa-scissors | What gets cut | What isn't basic. | Together. | igual
* fa-check | What isn't touched | Food, school, health. | Priority. | igual
* fa-calendar | Monthly review | How we're doing. | As a family. | igual

--- paso | fa-gift | Check for support programs
Some families are entitled to scholarships or programs they haven't applied for. Check with your municipality and the Bienestar programs.

--- paso | fa-user | A one-minute case
Chayo and her husband added up what comes in: her sales and his odd jobs. They cut cable TV and the expensive phone plan. They signed their daughter up for the middle-school scholarship. It's enough, just barely.

> **Key idea:** if the remittance ends, make a new budget as a family and protect the basics.

--- comprueba
1. Which expenses aren't touched? || Food, school and health.
2. Besides cutting, what can they check? || Support or scholarships they're entitled to.

--- recuerda
- A new budget.
- The basics first.
- Check for support.

== profundiza

--- ecuacion | fa-calculator | Chayo's household
= 6,000 | Chayo's sales
+ 4,500 | His odd jobs
= 10,500 | What comes in today

--- tema | fa-users | Talk without blame
The remittance didn't end because of anyone's fault. Talking with numbers helps you decide together instead of fighting.

--- casos
### Case 1. No remittance
Chayo no longer receives the 300 dollars.
? What does she do? || She makes a new budget with her husband.

### Case 2. Cable TV and school
There isn't enough for everything.
? What does she do? || They cut what isn't basic and protect school.

### Case 3. The scholarship
Her daughter could get a scholarship and they haven't applied.
? What does she do? || She checks the requirements and applies.

--- errores
* Spending the same as before | Debt | A new budget
* Cutting school or health | Greater harm | Protect them
* Blaming each other | Fights | Talk with numbers
* Not checking for support | Money that doesn't come | Get informed
