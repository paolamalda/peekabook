# Module 7. Work or a business with what you know how to do

## M7 U01. What you know how to do has value

**What you will be able to do:** List your skills and identify how to prove them to get a job.

**To start:** Don Rafa knows how to install drywall, read blueprints in English and run a crew. In the interview he only said «I do a bit of everything». They didn't call him. In this lesson you'll see how to tell what you know.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### Your experience is your capital

Your years in the U.S. left you trades, English, discipline and customer skills. That's valuable here, but you need to say it clearly and, if possible, prove it.



#### Your list of what you know

| Type | Description | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Trade | Construction, cooking, cleaning, mechanics. | With years. |
| Language | Spoken or written English. | In high demand. |
| Leadership | Coordinating people. | Supervision. |
| Proof | Photos, letters, certificates. | What proves it. |

#### Where to look

The Servicio Nacional de Empleo (national employment service) has a job board and serves repatriated people. Also, CONOCER can certify what you already know how to do, even if you have no diploma.



#### A one-minute case

Don Rafa wrote: «12 years in drywall, I read blueprints, I coordinated crews of 8, I speak English». He brought photos of his work. At the next interview they hired him as foreman.

> **Key idea:** make a list of what you know how to do and bring it with proof.



#### Check your understanding

1. What did your years in the U.S. leave you?
*Answer:* Trades, language, discipline and customer skills.

2. Who can certify what you know without a diploma?
*Answer:* CONOCER.


#### Remember

- Make your list.
- Bring proof.
- SNE and CONOCER.



### Go deeper (5 more minutes)

#### Your list

|---|---|---|
| | | |
| | | |

#### English opens doors

Call centers, tourism and exporting companies look for people who speak English. If you speak it, put it first.



#### Cases


**Case 1. «I do a bit of everything»**

Don Rafa doesn't say what he knows how to do.
- *What does he do?* He writes his list with trade, years and proof.


**Case 2. Memo's English**

Memo speaks English like a native, but doesn't put it on his application.
- *What does he do?* He puts it first and looks for jobs that require it.


**Case 3. No diploma**

Lupita cooked for 10 years in a restaurant, but has no paper that says so.
- *What does she do?* She looks into certifying her experience with CONOCER.


#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Saying «everything» | They don't remember you | Be specific |
| Hiding your English | You miss opportunities | Put it first |
| Thinking it's worthless without a diploma | You don't get certified | CONOCER |
| Going without proof | They don't believe you | Photos and letters |

3. You speak English. What do you do? a) Hide it · b) Mention it only if they ask · c) Put it first
**Answers:** 1-b: specific. 2-a: the SNE. 3-c: it's in high demand.



#### Put it into practice

Write one sentence about your experience with your trade, years and what you coordinated.
**Answer:** Example: «8 years in a restaurant kitchen, I prepared 200 dishes per shift and trained two helpers».



#### Your plan

Fill in your list with three things you know how to do and gather one piece of proof for each.

### Key words

## M7 U02. Compare offers: wages and benefits

**What you will be able to do:** Compare a formal job and an informal one by adding up what you don't see in the wage.

**To start:** Memo is offered two jobs: one for 2,400 a week with IMSS and another for 2,700 «in cash, no paperwork». Which to take seems obvious to him. In this lesson you'll see what else counts.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### The wage isn't everything

A formal job includes benefits: IMSS (health), Afore, aguinaldo (year-end bonus), paid vacation and Infonavit housing credit. An informal job pays only the wage.



#### What a formal job adds

| Type | Description | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| IMSS | Doctor, sick leave. | For you and your family. |
| Afore | Retirement savings. | Adds weeks. |
| Aguinaldo | At least 15 days' wages. | Every December. |
| Vacation | Paid days. | By law. |

#### Do the full math

A doctor's visit and medicine without IMSS can cost more than the difference in wages. And every formal week brings you closer to a pension (Module 5).



#### A one-minute case

Memo added up the aguinaldo and what he'd spend on doctors. The formal job was the better deal. He also started adding weeks.

> **Key idea:** compare the wage plus benefits, not just what you're paid each week.



#### Check your understanding

1. Besides the wage, what does a formal job include?
*Answer:* IMSS, Afore, aguinaldo, vacation and Infonavit.

2. What is the minimum aguinaldo?
*Answer:* 15 days' wages.


#### Remember

- Wage plus benefits.
- Aguinaldo: 15 days.
- Weeks toward your pension.



### Go deeper (5 more minutes)

#### Memo's aguinaldo

- Daily wage (2,400 divided by 7): **343**
- Days: **15**
- Minimum aguinaldo: **5,145**


#### Ask to be registered with IMSS

If the job is formal, the employer must register you from day one. You can check your registration on the IMSS website with your CURP.



#### Cases


**Case 1. The 300-peso difference**

Memo compares 2,400 with IMSS against 2,700 with no paperwork.
- *What does he do?* He adds up benefits and health costs before deciding.


**Case 2. «I'll register you later»**

Don Rafa's boss says he'll register him in three months.
- *What does he do?* He asks to be registered from day one and checks it.


**Case 3. No aguinaldo**

Lupita is told that at her job aguinaldo «isn't a thing».
- *What does she do?* She knows it's a right and asks PROFEDET.


#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Looking only at the wage | You compare wrong | Add up benefits |
| Accepting registration later | No IMSS and no weeks | From day one |
| Not checking your registration | You don't know if you're contributing | Check it |
| Giving up your rights | You lose money | Ask PROFEDET |

### Practice

#### Interactive activity

**What would you do? (H5P):** three situations from this lesson with Memo, Don Rafa and Lupita. Choose the best decision in each one; if you miss, you can try again. You earn experience points.



#### Quiz

1. What does an informal job NOT give you? a) A wage · b) IMSS and Afore · c) Pay for the hours you work
2. You earn 300 pesos a day. What is your minimum aguinaldo? a) 4,500 · b) 300 · c) 9,000
3. From when must you be registered with IMSS? a) From day one · b) After three months, once you pass the trial period · c) After a year
**Answers:** 1-b: there are no benefits. 2-a: 15 days. 3-a: from the start.



#### Put it into practice

You earn 2,100 a week. What is your daily wage?
**Answer:** 300 pesos.



#### Your plan

If you have two offers, write down for each one: wage, IMSS, aguinaldo and vacation.



### Learn more

- **Labor rights** (PROFEDET · Spanish): https://www.gob.mx/profedet — **What to look for:** «aguinaldo». Phone 800 911 7877.
- **Check your registration** (IMSS · Spanish): https://www.imss.gob.mx — **What to look for:** «semanas cotizadas» (contribution weeks).

### Key words

- *Benefits (prestaciones):* what you receive besides the wage.
- *Aguinaldo:* a yearly payment of at least 15 days' wages.
- *IMSS registration (alta):* the record of your job for social security.

### Sources

Ley Federal del Trabajo (federal labor law), aguinaldo and vacation; PROFEDET; IMSS; checked October 5, 2026.

## M7 U03. A small business without risking your savings

**What you will be able to do:** Test a business idea on a small scale before putting in all your money.

**To start:** Lupita wants to open a taco shop «like the ones in Houston». She got quotes for a space, a griddle and tables: 85,000 pesos, all her savings. In this lesson you'll see how to test before betting everything.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### Test first

Before renting a space, sell on a small scale: orders from neighbors, a stand on weekends, made to order. That way you see whether people buy and at what price.



#### Three numbers you need

| Type | Description | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | What it costs you to make each product. | Ingredients, gas, packaging. |
| Price | What you charge. | Higher than the cost. |
| Sales | How many you sell. | Per week. |

#### Keep business money separate

Use an account or an envelope only for the business. If you mix household money with business money, you don't know whether you're making or losing money.



#### A one-minute case

Lupita sold tacos to order for six weekends. Each taco costs her 9 pesos and she sells it for 18. She sells about 300 per weekend. Now she knows her idea works and how much she can invest.

> **Key idea:** test on a small scale, know your cost and your price, and keep the business money separate.



#### Check your understanding

1. What do you do before renting a space?
*Answer:* You test on a small scale whether people buy and at what price.

2. Why keep business money separate?
*Answer:* To know whether you're making or losing money.


#### Remember

- Test on a small scale.
- Cost, price, sales.
- Keep money separate.



### Go deeper (5 more minutes)

#### Lupita's tacos

- Price: **18**
- Cost: **9**
- She keeps per taco: **9**
- Tacos per weekend: **300**
- She keeps per weekend: **2,700**


#### To keep learning

If your idea works, the Tu Negocio, Tu Dinero, Tu Futuro program goes deeper into pricing, cash flow, SAT taxes and credit to grow.



#### Cases


**Case 1. The full taco shop**

Lupita wants to invest all her savings at once.
- *What does she do?* She sells to order first to test it.


**Case 2. The price**

Lupita doesn't know how much each taco costs her.
- *What does she do?* She adds up ingredients, gas and packaging per taco.


**Case 3. All in the same pocket**

Don Rafa uses the money from his jobs for the household without writing it down.
- *What does he do?* He keeps the business money separate in an account or envelope.


#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Investing everything at once | You could lose your savings | Test small |
| Not knowing your cost | You lose without noticing | Calculate it |
| Mixing money | You don't know if you profit | Separate it |
| Copying prices from there | It doesn't sell | Prices from here |

### Practice

#### Interactive activity

**What would you do? (H5P):** three situations from this lesson with Lupita and Don Rafa. Choose the best decision in each one; if you miss, you can try again. You earn experience points.



#### Quiz

1. What do you do before investing your savings in a business? a) Test it on a small scale · b) Take out a loan · c) Rent the biggest space
2. A product costs you 25 and you sell it for 40. How much do you keep per piece? a) 65 · b) 15 · c) 40
3. Why keep business money separate? a) Just because · b) Because the law requires it of every business, even small ones · c) To know whether you're making or losing money
**Answers:** 1-a: test first. 2-b: 40 minus 25. 3-c: to see if you profit.



#### Put it into practice

You sell 120 pieces a week and keep 12 pesos per piece. How much do you keep per week?
**Answer:** 1,440 pesos.



#### Your plan

If you have an idea, write how you'll test it on a small scale, its cost, its price and where you'll keep the business money.



### Learn more

- **Starting a business** (CONDUSEF · Spanish): https://www.condusef.gob.mx — **What to look for:** «emprendedores» (entrepreneurs).

### Key words

- *Cost:* what it costs you to make or buy what you sell.
- *Price:* what you charge.
- *Profit:* what you keep after the cost.

### Sources

CONDUSEF, guides for entrepreneurs; checked October 5, 2026.
