# Module 6. Your first 90 days

## M6 U01. Think in pesos: your return budget

**What you will be able to do:** Make a budget in pesos for your first 90 days, with what really comes in and goes out.

**To start:** Memo earned 18 dollars an hour in the U.S. In Puebla he's offered 2,400 pesos a week. He does the math in dollars and feels «it's not enough for anything». In this lesson you'll see how to do math that actually helps.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### Switch your mindset

Comparing your pay there with your pay here only discourages you: prices are different too. Do your math in pesos, with the prices in your community.



#### Your 90-day budget

| Type | Description | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| What comes in | Wages, odd jobs, support, savings you brought. | Only what's certain. |
| The basics | Food, housing, transportation, health. | First. |
| Papers | Certificates, copies, travel. | One-time expense. |
| A little set aside | For the unexpected. | Even if it's small. |

#### Three months, not one

In the first months, less money almost always comes in than you expect. Plan for 90 days and count only the money that's certain, not the money that «might» come.



#### A one-minute case

Memo wrote down what he spends in Puebla: shared rent, food and bus fare add up to 7,600 a month. With 2,400 a week he brings in about 10,400. He has 2,800 left for papers and his little savings.

> **Key idea:** do your math in pesos and for 90 days, with what's certain.



#### Check your understanding

1. Why not compare your pay in dollars with your pay here?
*Answer:* Because prices are different too; what helps is doing the math in pesos.

2. What money do you count in your budget?
*Answer:* Only money that's certain.


#### Remember

- Math in pesos.
- Plan 90 days.
- Only what's certain.



### Go deeper (5 more minutes)

#### Memo's month

- Comes in (2,400 a week, about 4.3 weeks): **10,400**
- Shared rent: **2,500**
- Food: **3,600**
- Transportation: **1,500**
- For papers and savings: **2,800**


#### Your 90 days

| Item | Month 1 | Month 2 | Month 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Comes in | | | |
| Food | | | |
| Housing | | | |
| Transportation | | | |
| Papers | | | |
| Set aside | | | |



#### Cases


**Case 1. The 18 dollars**

Memo compares his pay there with his pay here.
- *What does he do?* He makes his budget in pesos with Puebla prices.


**Case 2. The promised job**

Don Rafa is promised a job «in a month».
- *What does he do?* He doesn't count it until it's certain.


**Case 3. Everything in the first month**

Lupita wants to spend all her savings furnishing the house.
- *What does she do?* She spreads her savings over the three months.


#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Thinking in dollars | You get discouraged | Math in pesos |
| Counting uncertain money | You fall short | Only what's certain |
| Planning one month | Savings run out | 90 days |
| Forgetting the papers | Surprise expenses | Include them |

### Practice

#### Interactive activity

**What would you do? (H5P):** three situations from this lesson with Memo, Don Rafa and Lupita. Choose the best decision in each one; if you miss, you can try again. You earn experience points.



#### Quiz

1. How far ahead do you plan when you return? a) One week · b) 90 days · c) One day
2. You're promised a job «in a month». Do you count it? a) Yes, all of it · b) Half, in case they do hire you · c) No, not until it's certain
3. How do you do your math? a) In pesos with prices here · b) In dollars · c) With prices there, to compare better
**Answers:** 1-b: the first months. 2-c: only what's certain. 3-a: in pesos.



#### Put it into practice

9,000 comes in a month and you spend 7,200 on the basics. How much is left?
**Answer:** 1,800 pesos.



#### Your plan

Fill in your 90-day table with the money that's certain and the basics.

## M6 U02. Welcome-home obligations

**What you will be able to do:** Set kind limits on the spending and loans people ask of you when you return.

**To start:** When he arrived, Don Rafa's family threw him a welcome party that he paid for. Then his compadre asked to borrow money «since he's back from over there». In two weeks, half his savings were gone. In this lesson you'll see how to say no without a fight.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### «You came back with dollars»

Many people believe that whoever returns brings money. Sometimes it's true; often it isn't. Your savings have to last until you have income.



#### Three answers that help

| Type | Description | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| «I can't right now» | Without over-explaining. | Short and kind. |
| «When I have a job» | Change the when. | Without promising an amount. |
| «I'll help you another way» | Time, contacts, skills. | Not only money. |

#### Decide an amount ahead of time

If you want to help, decide ahead of time how much you can give without putting your 90 days at risk. Give it as a gift, not as a loan that will hurt to collect.



#### A one-minute case

Don Rafa decided he could give his mother 500 pesos a month and nothing more. He told his compadre: «Not right now; when I have a job, we'll see». His savings lasted three months.

> **Key idea:** your return savings have to last; decide ahead of time how much you can help, and say no kindly.



#### Check your understanding

1. Why take care of your savings when you return?
*Answer:* Because they have to last until you have income.

2. How do you help without throwing yourself off balance?
*Answer:* You decide an amount ahead of time and give it as a gift.


#### Remember

- Your savings have to last.
- Decide an amount.
- Say no kindly.



### Go deeper (5 more minutes)

#### What was gone in two weeks

- Don Rafa's savings: **8,000**
- Welcome party: **2,500**
- Loan to his compadre: **1,500**
- Half was left: **4,000**


#### It isn't selfish

Taking care of your savings also takes care of your family: if you end up with nothing, someone will later have to help you.



#### Cases


**Case 1. The party**

Don Rafa's family wants a party he would pay for.
- *What does he do?* He suggests something simple and sets a cap.


**Case 2. The compadre**

His compadre asks for 1,500 «since he's back from over there».
- *What does he do?* He says «not right now; when I have a job, we'll see».


**Case 3. Lupita's mother**

Lupita wants to help her mother every month.
- *What does she do?* She decides a fixed amount that doesn't put her 90 days at risk.


#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Paying for everything out of obligation | Savings disappear | Set a cap |
| Lending without thinking | Hard to collect | A gift or nothing |
| Over-explaining | They talk you into it | Short answer |
| Feeling guilty | You give in | Protecting your savings protects everyone |

### Practice

#### Interactive activity

**What would you do? (H5P):** three situations from this lesson with Don Rafa, his compadre and Lupita. Choose the best decision in each one; if you miss, you can try again. You earn experience points.



#### Quiz

1. People ask to borrow money when you arrive. What helps you? a) Lending everything · b) A short, kind answer · c) Borrowing so you can lend to your family
2. When do you decide how much to help? a) Ahead of time, based on your 90 days · b) When they ask, based on what each person needs · c) Never
3. If you help with money, it's best to give it as… a) A loan with interest · b) An advance · c) A gift you can afford
**Answers:** 1-b: short and kind. 2-a: ahead of time. 3-c: without expecting it back.



#### Put it into practice

You have 9,000 in savings and need 2,500 a month to cover your expenses. How much can you give away over three months without falling short?
**Answer:** 1,500 pesos.



#### Your plan

Write down how much you can give each month and your sentence for saying no.



### Learn more

- **Family finances** (CONDUSEF · Spanish): https://www.condusef.gob.mx — **What to look for:** «prestar dinero a familiares» (lending money to relatives).

### Key words

- *Cap:* the most you'll spend.
- *Obligation:* an expense you feel you're expected to make.
- *Limit:* how far you can go without harming yourself.

## M6 U03. When the remittances stop

**What you will be able to do:** Reorganize household spending when the relative who sent money comes back or stops sending.

**To start:** Chayo received 300 dollars a month from her husband. Now he's back and there's no remittance. The bills and school costs keep coming. In this lesson you'll see how to adjust the household together.

### The essentials (5 minutes)

#### The household's numbers change

If the household depended on the remittance, you need a new budget with what comes in now. In 2025, remittances to Mexico fell 4.6%: many families are in the same situation.



#### Four steps as a family

| Type | Description | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| What comes in today | Wages, support, sales. | From everyone. |
| What gets cut | What isn't basic. | Together. |
| What isn't touched | Food, school, health. | Priority. |
| Monthly review | How we're doing. | As a family. |

#### Check for support programs

Some families are entitled to scholarships or programs they haven't applied for. Check with your municipality and the Bienestar programs.



#### A one-minute case

Chayo and her husband added up what comes in: her sales and his odd jobs. They cut cable TV and the expensive phone plan. They signed their daughter up for the middle-school scholarship. It's enough, just barely.

> **Key idea:** if the remittance ends, make a new budget as a family and protect the basics.



#### Check your understanding

1. Which expenses aren't touched?
*Answer:* Food, school and health.

2. Besides cutting, what can they check?
*Answer:* Support or scholarships they're entitled to.


#### Remember

- A new budget.
- The basics first.
- Check for support.



### Go deeper (5 more minutes)

#### Chayo's household

- Chayo's sales: **6,000**
- His odd jobs: **4,500**
- What comes in today: **10,500**


#### Talk without blame

The remittance didn't end because of anyone's fault. Talking with numbers helps you decide together instead of fighting.



#### Cases


**Case 1. No remittance**

Chayo no longer receives the 300 dollars.
- *What does she do?* She makes a new budget with her husband.


**Case 2. Cable TV and school**

There isn't enough for everything.
- *What does she do?* They cut what isn't basic and protect school.


**Case 3. The scholarship**

Her daughter could get a scholarship and they haven't applied.
- *What does she do?* She checks the requirements and applies.


#### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Spending the same as before | Debt | A new budget |
| Cutting school or health | Greater harm | Protect them |
| Blaming each other | Fights | Talk with numbers |
| Not checking for support | Money that doesn't come | Get informed |

### Practice

#### Interactive activity

**What would you do? (H5P):** three situations from this lesson with Chayo and her family. Choose the best decision in each one; if you miss, you can try again. You earn experience points.



#### Quiz

1. The remittance ended. What do they do first? a) A new budget with what comes in today · b) Take out a loan to keep spending as before · c) Keep going the same
2. Which expense is protected? a) Cable TV · b) School · c) The expensive phone plan
3. What else can they check? a) Nothing · b) Online betting to make up for the remittance · c) Support or scholarships they're entitled to
**Answers:** 1-a: with real numbers. 2-b: it's basic. 3-c: there may be support.



#### Put it into practice

10,500 comes in and the basics cost 9,200. How much is left for everything else?
**Answer:** 1,300 pesos.



#### Your plan

Sit down as a family and fill in together what comes in today and the basics. Set a date to review it every month.



### Learn more

- **Programas para el Bienestar** (Government of Mexico · Spanish): https://programasparaelbienestar.gob.mx — **What to look for:** «becas» (scholarships) and «requisitos» (requirements).

### Key words

- *Remittance:* money a relative sends from another country.
- *Cut:* an expense you remove or reduce.
- *Scholarship:* support to study.
