Calculation practice

Short exercises to practice the course calculations. The answers are at the end.

Solve each exercise showing the procedure, the result, the period and the limits of the calculation. The answers are at the end.

Code Lesson Exercise
P01 M1 U02 Calculate 7% of 600 and explain the conversion to a decimal.
P02 M1 U02 A rate goes from 2% to 3%. Tell apart percentage points and relative increase.
P03 M1 U02 Principal 1,500, 8% yearly simple interest, nine months, no fees. Calculate interest and balance.
P04 M1 U02 Principal 1,000 at 5% a year, compounded yearly, three years, no other activity. Calculate the balance.
P05 M1 U02 A 12% nominal yearly rate compounded monthly. Calculate the monthly rate and the effective yearly rate.
P06 M1 U02 A price index goes from 250 to 265. Calculate the inflation for the period.
P07 M1 U02 Inflation drops from 6% to 4%. Did prices go back to their previous level?
P08 M1 U09 A made-up scale: the first 1,000 at 10%, the next 500 at 20%. Tax and effective rate on 1,500.
P09 M2 U08 3,600 pesos are needed. Exchange rate 18 and a fee of 5, no other charges. How much do you pay?
P10 M3 U01 Debt payments 600 and gross income 3,000 a month. Calculate the ratio and explain what it doesn't prove.
P11 M3 U05 Balance 800, 2% monthly interest, a 100 payment at the end of the month. Interest, principal paid and balance.
P12 M4 U06 Reserve 900 and basic expenses 1,800 a month. How much does it cover?
P13 M5 U02 Principal 1,000, final value 1,050 and a 30 cash payment not reinvested. Total gross return.
P14 M5 U02 Same case, with 20 in fees and 10 in tax for the year. Net return.
P15 M5 U02 Net return of 5% and inflation of 4%. Exact real return.
P16 M5 U02 An investment of 1,000 goes up 10% and then down 10%. Final value and an explanation of the average.
P17 M5 U02 Principal 1,000 ends at 900 and paid 50 in cash. Total gross return.
P18 M5 U02 The balance goes from 2,000 to 2,300 with a 250 contribution. Gain and the missing fact for the rate.
P19 M5 U02 1,000 grows to 1,440 in two years with no activity. Cumulative rate and compound yearly rate.
P20 M5 U06 You contribute 40 a month for 10 years with no return. Total contributed; is it compound interest?
P21 M3 U09 A tanda of 10 people, 80 a week, turn 4. How much do you receive and how much did you contribute before receiving?
P22 M5 U10 You sell 900 a month, spend 380 on supplies and work 26 hours. Profit and profit per hour.

Answers

See the answers
  • P01: 42. 7% = 0.07; 600 × 0.07 = 42.
  • P02: it went up 1 percentage point; the relative increase is 50%.
  • P03: interest 90; balance 1,590 (1,500 × 0.08 × 9/12).
  • P04: 1,157.63 (1,000 × 1.05³). With simple interest it would be 1,150.
  • P05: 1% monthly; 12.68% effective yearly.
  • P06: 6% ((265 − 250) ÷ 250).
  • P07: no. Prices keep going up, just more slowly.
  • P08: tax 200; effective rate 13.33%; marginal rate of the last bracket 20%.
  • P09: 200 for the transfer plus a 5 fee = 205 dollars.
  • P10: 20%. It doesn't prove you can pay after your basic expenses or that you'll be approved.
  • P11: interest 16; principal paid 84; balance 716.
  • P12: 0.5 months. Don't include money you can't access.
  • P13: gain 80; return 8% (3% from the payment and 5% from the price change).
  • P14: net gain 50; net return 5%.
  • P15: 1.05 ÷ 1.04 − 1 = 0.96%.
  • P16: 990; a 1% loss even though the average is zero.
  • P17: −5%. Receiving cash didn't prevent the loss.
  • P18: gain 50. You need to know when the contribution was made to calculate a comparable rate.
  • P19: 44% cumulative and 20% compound yearly (1.2 × 1.2 = 1.44).
  • P20: 4,800 contributed. There's no interest: contributions aren't a return.
  • P21: you receive 800; before receiving you contributed 240 (3 weeks × 80), and in total you'll contribute 800.
  • P22: profit 520; 20 an hour before taxes.
