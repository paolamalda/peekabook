The essentials

5 min

Compare what's included, not only the price

One person advertises their service at 50 and another at 80. You still don't know which is cheaper: maybe the first charges extra for what you need.

Compare the scope of service: what's included, what isn't and what it all costs.

Three steps to choose

  1. Explain what you need: federal and state, self-employment, ITIN, dependents.
  2. Ask what's included and the total cost, in writing.
  3. Check who will do the work and their credential.

Credentials in California

PTIN
IRS number every paid preparer must have.
Required for everyone.
CTEC
State registry for preparers who aren't CPAs, EAs or attorneys.
Check it on their website.
CPA, EA or attorney
Licensed professionals who can represent you before the IRS.
Check them in the IRS directory.

Warning signs

Walk away if the preparer:

  • promises "the maximum refund guaranteed" before seeing your papers;
  • asks you to sign blank pages;
  • wants the refund sent to their account instead of yours;
  • suggests making up expenses or dependents;
  • doesn't sign the return as the preparer.

Key idea: never sign something you haven't read. You are responsible for what your return says.

A case in one minute

Mar is looking for someone to do her tax return. An office in the neighborhood charges 60, but won't give her a copy. Another charges 150, signs as the preparer and is registered with CTEC.

Mar asks at a VITA site and finds out she qualifies for no-cost help. She brings her papers and gets a copy of everything.

She learned that the lowest price isn't always the best, and that the no-cost option is sometimes the safest.

Before you act, check: always ask for the total price in writing and a complete copy of your return.

Do it this week

  1. Ask two preparers what their price includes and the total cost.
  2. Check their registration with CTEC or in the IRS directory.
  3. Check whether you qualify for VITA, which costs nothing.

Check your understanding

What must every paid preparer have?

An IRS PTIN.

You're promised a guaranteed refund before they see your papers. What do you do?

You don't sign, and you look for an option you can verify, like VITA or a registered preparer.

Remember

  • Compare what the price includes, not only the price.
  • Check the PTIN and the CTEC registration.
  • Never sign blank pages or let the refund go to another account.
