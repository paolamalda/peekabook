Don't subtract twice

If you already set aside savings in another account, don't subtract it again from your balance.

If the remittance is already on your calendar for the month, don't count it again as an extra expense.

Income that changes

Don't make a permanent commitment based on a bonus or an unusual month.

Agree on what information will lead you to review the amount: for example, if your hours are cut or your rent changes.

Good to know: if you're paid every two weeks, some months have three paychecks. They're good for sending ahead a foreseeable expense, like the start of school. Not for raising your regular amount.

If several siblings help

When several siblings send money to their parents, it helps to agree on how much each one puts in and on what date.

That way nobody carries it all alone and the parents know what they can count on each month.

An envelope for sending

If you send money every month, open a digital envelope or a separate account just for sending.

Move part of each paycheck into that envelope. When the sending date arrives, the money is already there and you don't have to decide in a hurry.

If there's some left one month, keep it for the next one or for a foreseeable family expense.

When the emergency is yours

Your own emergency counts too. If you lose work hours or have a medical expense, it's okay to send less that month.

Let them know in time and go back to the usual amount when you recover.

Your sending calendar

Add your sends to your Module 1 money calendar, with their date and amount.

That way you can see at a glance if rent and a transfer fall in the same week. You can move one of them before it becomes a problem.

Review the calendar each time your work hours or your family's needs change.

Cases: think first, then check

Case 1. Rent and the send

Alex gets paid three days after paying rent. The month ends positive, but if Alex sends money before rent, that day comes up short.

What does Alex decide?

To send after payday.

Case 2. Mar's 120

Mar has 900. She needs 650 until her next paycheck, 80 for her tax savings and 50 for the savings she agreed on.

How much can she send?
  1. If she sends 200, she creates an 80 shortfall.
What can she do?

Send 120 now and the rest when she gets paid again, letting her mother-in-law know.

Case 3. Rubén's brothers

Rubén and his brother, who lives in Texas, support their mom in Oaxaca.

What do they agree on?

Rubén sends on the 15th and his brother on the 30th. That way their mom receives money twice a month and neither of them is squeezed.
