Who owns the account

Type Who can use it Keep in mind
Individual account Only you You decide everything
Joint account Two or more owners Any of them can withdraw everything
Authorized access Someone uses it without being an owner Ask what they can do

Sharing your password isn't the same as giving formal authorization. Before adding someone, understand who can withdraw, close the account and receive notices.

If you deposit cash often

Besides the monthly fee, compare:

  • where you can deposit cash and what it costs;
  • which ATMs cost nothing;
  • when the money you deposit becomes available;
  • deposit limits.

Some apps charge 3 to 5 dollars for each cash deposit at stores. Some banks don't charge to deposit at the teller or at their ATMs.

An account for your business

A second account helps keep personal and business money apart, but it doesn't create a company or replace your records.

Using a personal account to receive business payments may be limited by the contract. Ask first.

Good to know: according to the FDIC, a common reason people don't have a bank account is high or unpredictable fees. Bank On accounts were created to solve that.

Use the program's comparison chart

The program has a chart comparing more than 60 accounts, apps and services: monthly cost, accepted documents and cash deposits.

Each product shows the date it was checked. Always confirm the conditions on the official website before opening.

Accounts with no overdraft

Some accounts simply decline a payment if you don't have the balance, instead of covering it and charging an overdraft fee.

For many people that's better: they'd rather have a payment declined than pay a 35-dollar fee. Bank On accounts don't have overdraft.

Ask whether your account has overdraft and how to turn it off.

Cases: think first, then check

Case 1. Alex's "at no cost" account

Alex's account costs nothing with payroll direct deposit. Alex gets paid by check and deposits it at the teller.

How much does it cost?

12 a month, 144 a year.

What can Alex do?

Ask the employer for direct deposit or switch to an account with no fee, like a Bank On certified one.

Case 2. The joint account

Mar's cousin asks her to add him to her account "to help him out."

What's the risk?

Her cousin could withdraw the whole balance.

What does Mar do?

She looks for another way to help without giving him access to her account.

Case 3. Rubén's cash

Rubén gets paid through payroll, but his roommates pay him their share of the rent in cash.

What does he compare?

Where he can deposit cash at no cost near his home and his work.

Common mistakes

Believing "no fee" without reading the condition
You pay a fee every month
Ask what avoids the fee
Confusing the opening deposit with the minimum balance
You get charged even though you opened with what they asked
Ask about both amounts
Adding someone to your account to help them
They can take all your money
Help in another way
Not asking about cash deposits
You pay every time you deposit
Compare where it costs nothing
