The essentials

Only two can give you immigration advice

In the U.S., only these can give you advice about your immigration case:

  1. Licensed attorneys, and
  2. accredited representatives of the Department of Justice (DOJ), who work at recognized organizations.

Nobody else: not notarios, not "helpers," not social media "advisors."

A "notario" in the U.S. is not a lawyer

In much of Latin America, a notario is a kind of lawyer. In the U.S., a notary public only certifies signatures.

In California it's illegal to advertise as a "notario" to offer immigration services.

Who can do what

Licensed attorney
Advises and represents you.
Check them with the State Bar.
Accredited representative (DOJ)
Advises and represents you from a recognized organization.
Check the DOJ list.
Immigration consultant
Only non-legal tasks: translating or filling out forms with your information.
Must be registered and give you a contract.
"Notario" or social media helper
No immigration advice at all.
A red flag.

Watch out for "express asylum"

Watch out if someone promises you a fast work permit with an asylum request, even if you don't qualify.

A false request to USCIS is a serious crime. It can lead to a deportation case that can't be undone.

Key idea: always confirm your case with a licensed attorney or a DOJ-accredited representative.

Three steps before hiring help

  1. Ask: "Are you an attorney or a DOJ-accredited representative?"
  2. Verify their license or accreditation at the official source.
  3. Ask for a written contract with the total cost. Never pay for guaranteed "arrangements."

A case in one minute

Rosa wanted to help her granddaughter with some papers. On social media she found an "advisor" who charged 800 dollars.

Before paying, she looked up the name with the State Bar and on the DOJ list. It wasn't on either. She called a recognized organization in Fresno and got a no-cost appointment.

Do it this week

  1. Find a recognized organization near you on the DOJ list.
  2. Save its phone number and address.
  3. Share this information with your family.

Your consulate also protects you

The Mexican consulate isn't only for passports or the matrícula. Its protection team helps you at no cost if:

  • you or someone in your family is detained;
  • you aren't paid for your work, you're treated badly at work or you get hurt on the job;
  • you live with violence at home;
  • a "notario" or someone pretending to be a lawyer scams you;
  • you have a problem with your children's custody;
  • a relative dies and you need help to take them home;
  • you're looking for a relative you haven't heard from.

If you don't know which consulate to go to, call CIAM (Information and Assistance Center for Mexicans): 1 (520) 623-7874, 24 hours a day, every day. The consulate can also put you in touch with lawyers who work with it.

Watch out for these mistakes

Trusting a "notario" or helper. You lose money and put your case at risk.
Look for an attorney or accredited representative.
Paying for a guaranteed outcome. It's a scam.
Nobody can guarantee it.

Check your understanding

Can a notary public give you immigration advice?

No. They only certify signatures.

Will ICE or USCIS ask you to pay by phone with gift cards?

No. No government agency charges that way.

Remember

  • Only attorneys and accredited representatives can advise you.
  • Always verify the license or accreditation.
  • Nobody can guarantee the outcome of a case.
