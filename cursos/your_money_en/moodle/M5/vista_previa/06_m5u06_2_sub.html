What to check for each source

Source What to check
Social Security Your statement on my Social Security; contributions are only credited with a valid SSN
401(k) or 403(b) Employer contribution and when it becomes yours
CalSavers Whether you can join, how much is deducted and how to withdraw
Traditional or Roth IRA You need work income; there are yearly limits
AFORE Your balance with your CURP (M5 U07)

Traditional or Roth

The difference is when you pay taxes:

  • Traditional: you don't pay taxes now, you do when you withdraw.
  • Roth: you pay taxes now, not when you withdraw (if you follow the rules).

Neither is always better. It depends on your situation; ask a professional.

Scenarios, not forecasts

Contributing 100 a month:

Scenario 20 years 30 years
No return 24,000 36,000
Hypothetical return of 5% a year 41,103 83,226
Same 5%, in today's dollars with 3% inflation 22,757 34,288

The 5% is hypothetical. Inflation reduces what you'll be able to buy.

If you change jobs

Before taking money out of a plan when you change jobs, check the transfer options, the costs, the taxes and the penalties.

Cashing out before the allowed age can cost up to a third of the money in taxes and penalties.

Good to know: CalSavers is required for many California employers that don't offer a retirement plan. If your employer enrolls you, you can choose how much to contribute or opt out.

Your housing and your retirement

Housing is one of the biggest expenses in retirement. Getting there without a mortgage, or with rent you can afford, changes a lot how much you need.

Some people plan to retire in Mexico, where the cost of living may be lower. If that's your case, check access to health care, the cost of sending money and how you'd receive your U.S. income.

Write down where you plan to live and what it would cost. That number makes your plan more real.

Cases: think first, then check

Case 1. Andrés's job change

Andrés leaves his job and is offered to cash out his 401(k).

What does he do first?

He asks about transferring it to an IRA or to his new employer's plan, and what taxes and penalties he'd face if he withdraws it.

Case 2. Daniela's CalSavers

Daniela's employer has no retirement plan and enrolls her in CalSavers.

What does she check?

How much is deducted, how to change it and how to opt out if she needs to.

Case 3. Rosa's retirement

Rosa worked in Mexico with IMSS and here she's paid in cash.

What sources does she need to check?

Her AFORE in Mexico (M5 U07), her own savings and whether she ever worked with an SSN in the U.S.

Common mistakes

Waiting to have a lot before starting
You lose years of growth
Start with a little
Not taking the employer contribution
You leave matching money behind
Contribute at least what your employer matches
Cashing out your 401(k) when you change jobs
You pay taxes and penalties
Transfer it
Treating a projection as a promise
You plan with unrealistic numbers
Use several scenarios
