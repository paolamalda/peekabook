# Your Money, Your Family, Your Future

Program manual · Version 3.4 · Desarrolla Talento · September 30, 2026

Financial well-being program for Latino immigrants in California: money, remittances, credit, protection and future.

## Characters

### Alex and Mar

A couple living in Los Angeles with their two children, ages 7 and 4. Alex's mother lives in Michoacán and they send her money every month. Alex is paid biweekly and sometimes does jobs with his truck; Mar is paid weekly, gets most of her tips in cash and sometimes sells food to order.

### Luis

He's 24 and came from Oaxaca two years ago. He shares an apartment in Los Angeles, works in a warehouse, is paid weekly and sends money to his mother. He has no credit history and pays almost everything with apps.

### Daniela

She's 37 and from San Salvador. She lives in San Diego with her 10-year-old son, works by the hour and has a small custom-cake business she wants to formalize. She sends money to her sister.

### Rosa

She's 61 and from Guadalajara, where she worked 15 years with IMSS coverage. She lives in Fresno with her daughter and grandchildren, cares for other children in her building and is paid in cash. She worries about her health and retirement.

### Andrés

He's 44 and from León, Guanajuato. He lives in Sacramento with his wife, Carmen, and their 15-year-old daughter. He has a job with benefits and a retirement plan, wants to buy a home and supports his brother in Mexico.

These characters are fictional; any resemblance to real people is coincidental.

## Operator manual


This manual is for the team that runs the program: coordination, facilitation, assessment, support and data. Participants do not need to read it. It contains the program profile, the editorial guide, assessment with answer keys, metrics, the comparison matrix, state expansion, and program records.

### 1. Program profile

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Academic name | Personal finance and economics for non-financial learners |
| Public name | Your Money, Your Family, Your Future / Tu Dinero, Tu Familia, Tu Futuro |
| Format | 100% online, self-paced, with question support and optional live sessions |
| Platform | Moodle 3.10 with lesson books, H5P activities, self-assessments, Level Up, badges and Custom certificate |
| Cost to participants | At no cost: content, assessments, retries, support, companion tool and certificate |
| Audience | Immigrant adults in the U.S., with an SSN, an ITIN or neither; wage earners, self-employed, platform workers or mixed income; with dependents and ties to Mexico or other countries |
| Geographic coverage | Federal core and state module. Pilot in California; then Texas, Illinois, New York and Florida |
| Estimated duration | About 13 hours on the platform (5 to 10 minute lessons, activities and self-assessments), plus time for the integrative cases and the plan; to be adjusted with pilot data |
| Structure | 5 modules, 59 lessons, 59 "What would you do?" H5P activities, 5 self-assessments (177 questions), 5 integrative cases and 22 calculation practices |
| Certificate | A badge per module and a verifiable certificate of completion after passing the five self-assessments; not a license or official accreditation |

**Overall objective.** By the end, participants build, justify and adjust a plan that connects income, expenses, taxes, financial services, remittances, credit, protection and goals. They compare alternatives, recognize risks, verify information and seek the right help.

**Educational problem.** Decisions are often made under urgency or with fragmented information. People confuse a balance with available money, a monthly payment with total cost, an app with a bank and a remittance with an isolated expense. The program builds the ability to connect these decisions and anticipate their consequences.

**Professional scope.** Education and guidance to make decisions and prepare consultations. Tax, legal, immigration, investment, insurance or insolvency cases that require an individual recommendation are referred to authorized professionals. **The program never provides immigration advice.**

**Final result.** A portfolio with a diagnosis, calendar, tax preparation, services comparison, remittance plan, credit pathway, debt plan, protection, family preparedness plan and goals. It can be completed with simulated data.

### 2. Editorial guide

#### Fixed lesson structure

Each lesson is a chapter in its module's book. The cover page offers two paths: the essentials only (5 minutes) or the full lesson (10 minutes).

1. An everyday **question** as the title, an **objective** in one sentence and a **hook** with one of the characters.
2. **The essentials** (5 minutes, 350 to 500 words): steps, cards or a simple calculation; **check** (2 questions with answers) and **remember** (3 points). It is enough on its own to act.
3. **Go deeper** (full lesson of 800 to 1,200 words): topics and tables with data, **3 cases** with the characters and **4 common mistakes**. Facts to confirm go in a "Before you act, check" box.
4. **Practice:** the "What would you do?" H5P activity, a **quiz** of 3 questions with 3 options, a **practice exercise** with its answer and **for your plan** (a concrete action, without asking for real amounts).
5. **Learn more:** official resources with "what to look for" in each link; the lesson's **key words** and **sources**.

#### Tone

- Informal "you" (tú in Spanish). Short sentences. Clear verbs.
- No blame: describe situations, do not judge people.
- Legal cautions go in the "Before you act, check" box, not repeated in every paragraph. The content is made robust through dated sources and professional review.
- Characters are fictional and diverse in income, documents and family situation.

#### Readability

Readability measures **how the text is written**, not how much the reader knows. An adult who has never studied finance can understand a complex topic if the text uses short sentences, common words and explains each technical term when it appears. The content stays complete; only the form changes.

| Criterion | Target |
|---|---|
| Sentence length | Average of 15 to 20 words |
| Technical terms | Explained in the same sentence or under "Key words" |
| INFLESZ index (Spanish) | 65 or higher ("fairly easy") in "Lo esencial"; 55 or higher in "Profundiza" |
| Flesch-Kincaid (English) | Grade 8 or lower in "The essentials"; 10 or lower in "Go deeper" |
| User test | 5 to 10 immigrant participants read a lesson and explain the key idea in their own words |

Up-to-date, comparable information (fees, requirements, products) does not go in the lesson text but in the **living comparison matrix** (section 6), so it can be updated without rewriting the manual.

#### Professional review by topic

| Topic | Reviewer |
|---|---|
| Taxes (M1 U09 to U12) | CPA or Enrolled Agent with ITIN experience |
| Public charge and immigration fraud (M1 U13, M4 U02, M4 U10) | Immigration attorney or accredited representative; only to confirm content boundaries |
| Credit, collections, remittances and housing | Consumer protection attorney or specialist |
| Insurance and health | Licensed agent or insurance specialist |
| Investing and retirement | Registered professional (CFP, AFC or other) |
| Accessibility and language | UX specialist and bilingual reviewer |

Each lesson records: reviewer, verified credential, date, comments and resolution.

#### Updates

- Full annual review of the manual.
- Review of M1 U09 to U13 before every tax season.
- Extraordinary review when laws, products or programs change (for example, public charge, Medi-Cal, the remittance tax).
- The comparison matrix is reviewed every month (section 6).

### 4. Assessment

#### On the platform

| Instrument | Use | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Lesson quiz | 3 questions with 3 options on the *Practice* page, with answers | Self-check, not graded |
| "What would you do?" H5P | 3 cases per lesson, graded, with retries | Lesson completion and points |
| Module self-assessment | Bank of 177 questions with three options and feedback; 70% to pass, unlimited attempts | Module badge |
| Course completion | All five self-assessments passed | Complete plan badge and certificate of completion |

#### Application assessment (with facilitation)

For facilitated cohorts and for the indicators in section 8. It does not change the platform certificate: it measures learning and application.

| Instrument | Use | When |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline | Items 01, 03, 06 and 09 from each module (20 total, section 5), without showing keys | At enrollment (T0) |
| Closing bank | 10 items per module (plus alternates), section 5 | At module close (T1) |
| Integrative case | E1 to E5 in the support book, scored with the rubric | At module close |
| Calculation practices | P01 to P22 in the support book | During the module |

These are original instruments without external psychometric validation. When repeated items are compared between baseline and closing, report the possible practice effect. Open responses and cases require an evaluator.

#### Scoring

- **Items:** 0 (incorrect or unsafe), 1 (main idea correct but incomplete; in calculations, correct method with a calculation error), 2 (sufficient answer and reasoning).
- **Knowledge** = sum ÷ 20 × 100.
- **Integrative case:** four-dimension rubric scored 0 to 3; sum ÷ 12 × 100.
- **Module mastery** = 40% knowledge + 60% integrative case. Mastery requires 80 out of 100, at least level 2 on every case dimension and correction of critical errors. Retries with feedback are allowed at no cost.

**Simulated example.** Knowledge 16 of 20 = 80; case 10 of 12 = 83.33. Result = 0.40 × 80 + 0.60 × 83.33 = 82. Mastery only if every case dimension is at least 2 and critical errors have been corrected.
#### Rubric

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Interpretation | Does not recognize the situation or uses incompatible data | Recognizes part, misses an essential fact | Identifies need, data and limits | Also identifies an uncertainty and how to verify it |
| Calculation and use of information | Invalid method or evidence | Partly correct method with an error that changes the decision | Correct calculation or correct use of conditions and evidence | Also checks units, assumptions or a variation |
| Decision | Unsafe or unrelated action | Incompletely justified proposal | Feasible action considering needs, costs and risks | Compares alternatives and anticipates a contingency |
| Documentation | No next step recorded | Vague step without date or support | Records action, date and source or assumption | Adds follow-up and an adjustment trigger |

#### Critical errors by module

| Module | Critical error |
|---|---|
| M1 | Counting a loan as earned income; spending an unavailable monthly average; suggesting the use of someone else's or a made-up ID number |
| M2 | Claiming coverage without checking it; financing a transfer without recording the repayment; using a remittance company without verifying its license |
| M3 | Promoting a false dispute or promising to erase accurate information |
| M4 | Sharing access codes; not verifying a suspicious request; recommending a notario or consultant for immigration advice |
| M5 | Counting one reserve toward several goals; guaranteeing an uncertain return |

#### Assessment record

Participant ID · module and version · knowledge score · case dimensions · mastery · errors corrected · reviewer · date.

### 6. Living comparison matrix

The matrix replaces the fixed tables in the manual. It lives on the platform and in the app, with a visible date.

#### Required fields per product

ID · category · brand · legal entity · regulator and verification method · partner bank (if an app) · availability in the state · languages · documents accepted (SSN, ITIN, consular ID, passport, none) · opening channel · opening deposit · fees and waivers · cash deposits (where and cost) · APR or APY by product · risks and restrictions · applicable protection · official source · verification date · verified by · next review date · commercial relationship (none, referral or sponsorship).

#### Extensions

- **Remittances:** corridor, budget or amount to receive, fee, exchange rate, reference and time, additional charges, applicable tax by payment method, amount received, delivery time, delivery method, recipient access, promotions, DFPI or NMLS license and complaint route.
- **Credit:** net amount received, APR, total cost, collateral, which agencies it reports to, early payment.
- **Insurance:** premium, deductible, limits, exclusions and the profile used for the quote.
- **Retirement:** eligibility, costs, available assets.

#### Rules

1. Unknown data is marked "to be confirmed"; it is never made up.
2. Quotes in a comparison are taken in the same time window with the same profile.
3. Monthly review of active records; a record past its review date becomes "pending" and does not support recommendations.
4. **Editorial independence:** the order is calculated from the cost for the user's profile. No commercial relationship changes the order. If a commercial relationship exists, it is shown.
5. The methodology is published.

#### Verifiability rules

- Evidence levels: **V0** self-report; **V1** dated activity or record; **V2** document with sensitive data hidden or authorized professional review. Report the share at each level.
- For each value: definition, instrument version, period, numerator, denominator, missing data, source and person responsible.
- No response is missing data, never zero or failure. Lack of resources to act is recorded as a barrier, not as lack of learning.
- A before-and-after without a comparison group shows association, not causation.
- Compare with national benchmarks: the CFPB scale and questions comparable with the FDIC National Survey of Unbanked and Underbanked Households and the FINRA National Financial Capability Study.

**Fictional reporting example.** 50 started M2, 40 were assessed and 34 passed: 68% of those who started and 85% of those assessed. If 24 responded at D90 and 15 documented a comparison, report "15 of 24 respondents" and "follow-up coverage 24 of 50."

### 10. Privacy, data and operations

#### Data never collected

SSN, ITIN, passport number, immigration status, passwords, full account or card numbers.

#### Minimum data dictionary

random participant_id · cohort_id · language · state · start date · module_id · unit_id · instrument_version · assessment date · attempt · score · rubric_dimension · rubric_level · critical_error_resolved · evidence_level (V0, V1, V2) · completion_date · certificate_id · followup_anchor · followup_day · response_status · consent_version. Contact details are stored separately from responses.

#### Consent and retention

- Separate consents for follow-up, testimonials and any referral.
- Proposed retention: identifiable follow-up data up to 12 months after D180; then deletion or anonymization. Validate with legal counsel before operating.
- Aggregate reporting only; do not publish small identifiable groups.

#### Follow-up survey

1) What action did you try? 2) When? 3) What optional evidence can you record without sensitive data? 4) What changed? 5) What did not change or got worse? 6) What other event mattered? 7) What barrier did you face? 8) What help do you need? 9) Do you authorize follow-up contact? 10) Would you like to share a testimonial separately?

Curriculum coordination (scope and versions) · subject review (finance, taxes, protection, immigration) · bilingual review · support (answers and refers) · assessment (rubrics) · data lead · partnerships coordination (directory, matrix and conflicts of interest).
