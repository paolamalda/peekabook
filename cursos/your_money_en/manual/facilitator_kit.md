# Facilitator kit · Your Money, Your Family, Your Future

One 60-minute session per module, in person or online, for groups of 8 to 20 people. Everything comes from the module's lessons: key ideas, cases and practice. Use it after the group has worked through the module or as a review.

## Rules for every session

- No one shares personal data, account numbers or real amounts: we use made-up examples.
- We don't recommend institutions or products: we teach how to verify and compare.
- We don't give personalized tax, legal or immigration advice: we point to where to go.
- Talking about money is hard: thank every contribution and don't judge.

## Stress signs and where to refer

Watch for someone who misses sessions often, withdraws, mentions skipping food or medicine, borrowing from lenders or apps for basic expenses, threats over a debt, or not sleeping because of money. Talk privately, without pressure, and share these options:

- 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text, English and Spanish)
- CFPB: consumerfinance.gov (complaints about financial companies)
- 211 (local help with food, rent and bills)
- National Domestic Violence Hotline: 1-800-799-7233
- 911 (emergencies)

## Module 1. Understand your money and organize your finances

**Outcome:** An 8-week money calendar, a budget that also works in a tough month, a tax folder and a 90-day plan.

**Materials:** phone or projector with the module book, printed pages of the support book, case cards, pens.

| Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | Welcome and opening question: “What step did you take with your money this week?” |
| 5–15 | Key ideas of the module (below). Ask someone to explain one in their own words. |
| 15–35 | Cases in groups of 3 or 4 (below). Each group decides and explains why. |
| 35–50 | Practice: solve the exercises (below) and compare. |
| 50–57 | My commitment: each person writes one step with a date and tells their group partner. |
| 57–60 | Close: remind them of the module self-assessment and the next meeting. |

**Key ideas**

- M1 U01 · Is all the money I receive money I earned?: a loan gives you money to use today, but it doesn't make you richer.
- M1 U02 · Why doesn't the same money go as far anymore?: compare your own prices. The inflation in the news is an average; your rent or your food can go up more.
- M1 U03 · What do I need to take care of first?: there are no "good" or "bad" expenses for everyone. There are decisions that protect what matters to you.
- M1 U04 · Is getting paid every week the same as getting paid every month?: don't take on a new fixed commitment just because you had a very good month.
- M1 U05 · Why is my deposit smaller than what I earned?: in California your employer must give you a pay stub with the details of your pay. Keep all of them.
- M1 U06 · Can a small purchase add up to a lot?: adjust where the change is real and lasting, not where it makes you feel guilty.
- M1 U07 · Why am I short on money if I earn enough this month?: write down each payment only once, on the date the money actually leaves.
- M1 U08 · How do I decide what to use my money for?: there is no percentage that works for every household. Use your real numbers.
- M1 U09 · Does filing taxes mean I'll get money back?: 
- M1 U10 · Is everything I earn on my own for spending?: if nobody withholds taxes for you, set money aside from each payment from day one.
- M1 U11 · What tax benefits are there if I file with an ITIN?: 
- M1 U12 · How do I choose someone to help me with taxes?: never sign something you haven't read. You are responsible for what your return says.
- M1 U13 · What should I know before I apply for public benefits?: rules can change. A proposal isn't a rule until it's published as final and takes effect.
- M1 U14 · How do I turn what I learned into something I can actually do?: a plan that adjusts is a plan that works.

**Cases**

- **M1 U01 · The advance from the boss.** Alex gets a 400-dollar advance on wages. That week the account shows more money than ever. Mar wants to use it to buy a refrigerator. *What should they consider?* — Expected answer: The advance will come out of the next paychecks. It isn't new income.
- **M1 U02 · Alex and Mar's rent.** General inflation for the year was 3%, but their rent went up 8%. *Which inflation matters more to them?* — Expected answer: The inflation of their own expenses. Rent weighs more than the national average.
- **M1 U03 · The two subscriptions.** Mar pays 15 a month for a music app she uses every day and 9 for a video app she forgot she had. *Should she cancel the more expensive one?* — Expected answer: Not necessarily. What matters is the value she gets. She doesn't use the video app: that's the one worth canceling.

**Practice**

- **M1 U01.** This month you received: 600 in wages, a 200 loan, a 100 transfer between your accounts and a 40 refund for a purchase. How much is new earned income? — Answer: 600. The loan has to be paid back, the transfer only moved your money and the refund reverses a purchase.
- **M1 U02.** You save 1,000 and a year later you have 1,030. The prices of the things you buy went up 5%. Did your balance go up? Did your buying power go up? — Answer: Your balance went up 3%, but your buying power went down: 1,030 ÷ 1.05 = 980.95 in last year's dollars. The example doesn't include taxes or fees.

## Module 2. Understand the financial system and plan your remittances

**Outcome:** A map of institutions, accounts compared by how you use money, three comparable remittance quotes, a sending calendar and a family agreement.

**Materials:** phone or projector with the module book, printed pages of the support book, case cards, pens.

| Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | Welcome and opening question: “What step did you take with your money this week?” |
| 5–15 | Key ideas of the module (below). Ask someone to explain one in their own words. |
| 15–35 | Cases in groups of 3 or 4 (below). Each group decides and explains why. |
| 35–50 | Practice: solve the exercises (below) and compare. |
| 50–57 | My commitment: each person writes one step with a date and tells their group partner. |
| 57–60 | Close: remind them of the module self-assessment and the next meeting. |

**Key ideas**

- M2 U01 · Who takes care of my money and who provides the service?: being registered with a regulator shows that a company exists and is authorized. It doesn't mean every offer it makes is good for you.
- M2 U02 · Is a money app always a bank?: compare by how you use your money, not by the company's label.
- M2 U03 · What ID works for financial paperwork?: if one bank says no, ask another. Credit unions and Bank On accounts often accept more combinations of documents.
- M2 U04 · What do I need to open an account, and how much does it cost to keep it?: the opening deposit and the monthly fee are different things. Meeting one doesn't remove the other.
- M2 U05 · Is all the balance I see available to spend?: treat instant payment apps as if they were cash. Only send to people you know and trust.
- M2 U06 · How do I support my family without deciding at the last minute?: separate what's regular from what's an emergency. Not everything urgent is an emergency.
- M2 U07 · How much can I send, and when?: speak up before, not after. "My usual support is between 150 and 180. If my hours are cut, I'll let you know before the date."
- M2 U08 · Does "no fee" always mean more money arrives?: if you pay for your transfers in cash at a store, compare with paying from an account. You save the 1% and, often, fees.
- M2 U09 · What do I check before sending, and what do I do if something goes wrong?: canceling, fixing an error and reporting fraud are different requests. Say clearly what you're asking for.
- M2 U10 · What happens when I borrow to send money?: verify each alternative. None of them should be made up, but there's almost always an option before borrowing.
- M2 U11 · How do I say how much I can help without promising too much?: rushing and secrecy ("don't tell anyone") are signs of a scam.
- M2 U12 · How do I save for something that will be paid in pesos?: before a big commitment, check whose name it will be in and get a document that says so.
- M2 U13 · How do I bring my account and my remittances into one plan?: compare the full path of the money, not each piece separately.

**Cases**

- **M2 U01 · The pharmacy card.** Rubén bought a prepaid card at a pharmacy. A charge he doesn't recognize shows up. At the pharmacy they tell him they can't help. *Who should he call?* — Expected answer: The issuing bank shown on the back of the card, at the number printed there.
- **M2 U02 · "We work with banks".** An app tells Daniela that her money is protected because it "works with banks." *What does Daniela do?* — Expected answer: She looks for the bank's name in the terms and checks it on BankFind.
- **M2 U03 · Rubén and the branch account.** Rubén has a passport and a consular ID, but no SSN or ITIN. He calls two banks and a credit union. *What does he find out?* — Expected answer: One bank accepts his combination at a branch, the other requires an SSN, and the credit union accepts it if he becomes a member.

**Practice**

- **M2 U01.** For each financial service you use, write: brand, responsible company, what it does and how you checked it. — Answer: For example: "Payment app X · partner bank Y · holds my money · I checked it on FDIC BankFind on September 28." A logo isn't enough.
- **M2 U02.** An app says "we work with banks." What four facts do you need to know whether your money is protected? — Answer: The name of the bank, that the bank is insured, how your account is recorded (in your name) and the terms of the agreement between the app and the bank.

## Module 3. Build your credit and manage your debts

**Outcome:** Your credit path, a reviewed report, two financing options compared, a debt inventory with a strategy and a payment calendar.

**Materials:** phone or projector with the module book, printed pages of the support book, case cards, pens.

| Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | Welcome and opening question: “What step did you take with your money this week?” |
| 5–15 | Key ideas of the module (below). Ask someone to explain one in their own words. |
| 15–35 | Cases in groups of 3 or 4 (below). Each group decides and explains why. |
| 35–50 | Practice: solve the exercises (below) and compare. |
| 50–57 | My commitment: each person writes one step with a date and tells their group partner. |
| 57–60 | Close: remind them of the module self-assessment and the next meeting. |

**Key ideas**

- M3 U01 · If they lend me money, does that mean I can pay it back?: a good score doesn't make the monthly payment for you.
- M3 U02 · How do I know what information is used about my debts?: use only AnnualCreditReport.com. Other websites charge or ask for your card information.
- M3 U03 · How do I start if I have no credit history?: a product that doesn't report to the credit agencies doesn't build your history, even if you pay on time.
- M3 U04 · Does improving my credit mean erasing everything?: nobody can guarantee you a number of points or erase correct information.
- M3 U05 · Why can a small payment end up costing more?: compare by total cost, not by what you pay each month.
- M3 U06 · Which debt do I start with?: the best strategy is the one you can keep up. Both work if you pay all the minimums on time.
- M3 U07 · What do I do if I know I won't be able to pay?: court papers are never something to leave for later.
- M3 U08 · Can signing to help someone leave me with a debt?: a family promise doesn't change the contract. If the contract says you're responsible, you're responsible.
- M3 U09 · Does a tanda help me build credit?: don't put money you need for the basics into a tanda.
- M3 U10 · What's my next step with credit?: asking for help in time is a skill, not a failure.
- M3 U11 · Is a paycheck advance money at no cost?: an advance isn't extra money, it's your own pay earlier; add up a year of tips and fees, and ask your employer first.
- M3 U12 · Is pawning a good way out?: pawning doesn't affect your credit, but it's expensive and you can lose your item; get the fees in writing, keep your ticket and note the deadline.

**Cases**

- **M3 U01 · Andrés's car.** Andrés earns 2,500 net a month. His expenses and commitments add up to 2,250: he has 250 left. The car payment would be 220, plus 90 for insurance. *Can he afford it?* — Expected answer: No. He'd be 60 short every month, even though he was approved.
- **M3 U02 · Someone else's card.** Andrés's report shows a card he never opened and a real late payment on a phone. *What does he do about the card?* — Expected answer: He disputes it as possible fraud and freezes his credit.
- **M3 U03 · Rubén's secured card.** Rubén deposits 300 in a secured card and buys 40 in gas. *Does the deposit pay those 40?* — Expected answer: No. Rubén has to pay the 40 according to his statement.

**Practice**

- **M3 U01.** The lender offers you 500 more than you asked for. Should you take it to "make the most of it"? — Answer: Not necessarily. Compare the need, the cost and the payment. Additional borrowed money is an additional obligation.
- **M3 U02.** Your report shows a 900 balance on an account your closing letter shows at zero. Write the request. — Answer: Identify the account by its last digits, describe the difference, attach a copy of the letter with your sensitive information covered and ask them to investigate and correct it.

## Module 4. Protect your money, your identity and your family

**Outcome:** Your pause rule, a fraud response protocol, a review of your coverage, a staged reserve and your family preparedness plan.

**Materials:** phone or projector with the module book, printed pages of the support book, case cards, pens.

| Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | Welcome and opening question: “What step did you take with your money this week?” |
| 5–15 | Key ideas of the module (below). Ask someone to explain one in their own words. |
| 15–35 | Cases in groups of 3 or 4 (below). Each group decides and explains why. |
| 35–50 | Practice: solve the exercises (below) and compare. |
| 50–57 | My commitment: each person writes one step with a date and tells their group partner. |
| 57–60 | Close: remind them of the module self-assessment and the next meeting. |

**Key ideas**

- M4 U01 · Why is someone pushing me to act right now?: knowing your name or part of your account number doesn't prove they're legitimate.
- M4 U02 · Who can really help me with an immigration case?: always confirm your case with a licensed attorney or a DOJ-accredited representative.
- M4 U03 · What protects my accounts besides a password?: protect your email and your phone number first.
- M4 U04 · What do I do if I already shared information or see something strange?: be wary of anyone who promises to "recover all your money" in exchange for an upfront payment. It's the second scam.
- M4 U05 · Is talking about money the same as controlling someone?: recognizing it doesn't mean you have to tell your private life. To pass this course you don't have to confront anyone or share your story.
- M4 U06 · What is setting aside a little money for?: using your savings isn't failing. That's what it's for.
- M4 U07 · Does having insurance mean I won't pay anything?: compare the cost over a year with and without an incident, not just the premium.
- M4 U08 · Is this medical document asking me to pay?: if two papers describe the same service, they aren't two debts.
- M4 U09 · How do I pay for the essentials if I can't work for a few days?: don't count help that isn't confirmed. A payment from insurance or the government is only a resource once it's confirmed.
- M4 U10 · What happens to my family and my money if I can't be there?: this course doesn't give immigration or legal advice. For your options, go to an attorney or an accredited representative (M4 U02).
- M4 U11 · How do I know if my protection plan works?: a plan that only works for one emergency needs adjustments.
- M4 U12 · How do I make sure child support reaches my kids?: child support is your children's right; your state agency helps request it and record payments, and the paying parent must pay with proof.

**Cases**

- **M4 U01 · The job with a check.** An "employer" sends Alex a check to buy equipment and asks Alex to transfer part of it to a "supplier." *What does Alex do?* — Expected answer: Verifies the company independently and checks with the bank before moving any money.
- **M4 U02 · Mar's neighbor.** A "Notaria – Inmigración" office promises Mar's neighbor "papers in 6 months" for 5,000 dollars. *What does Mar do?* — Expected answer: She goes with her neighbor to an organization with accredited representatives, which reviews her case at no cost.
- **M4 U03 · The same password.** Alex used the same password for email and bank. The one from an online store leaked and someone got into Alex's email. *Is changing the bank password enough?* — Expected answer: No. The email was still compromised. Alex changed both and turned on two-step verification.

**Practice**

- **M4 U01.** A message says your bank will close your account in 20 minutes if you don't give a code. What do you do? — Answer: You don't share the code or use the link. You call the bank at a number you know. If you already shared it, follow the steps in M4 U04 right away.
- **M4 U02.** Write three questions to ask before hiring help for an immigration case. — Answer: Are you an attorney or a DOJ-accredited representative? What is your license number or recognized organization? Will you give me a written contract with the total cost and what it includes?

## Module 5. Build wealth and prepare your future

**Outcome:** Your goals with amount, date and priority; savings, investment, housing and retirement decisions with full costs; your binational map; and an integrated financial plan.

**Materials:** phone or projector with the module book, printed pages of the support book, case cards, pens.

| Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | Welcome and opening question: “What step did you take with your money this week?” |
| 5–15 | Key ideas of the module (below). Ask someone to explain one in their own words. |
| 15–35 | Cases in groups of 3 or 4 (below). Each group decides and explains why. |
| 35–50 | Practice: solve the exercises (below) and compare. |
| 50–57 | My commitment: each person writes one step with a date and tells their group partner. |
| 57–60 | Close: remind them of the module self-assessment and the next meeting. |

**Key ideas**

- M5 U01 · How do I turn a wish into a goal?: a goal has an amount, a date, a currency and a priority.
- M5 U02 · Does having more money mean my investment made money?: having more dollars doesn't always mean being able to buy more. Check inflation.
- M5 U03 · Does opening an account mean I already invested?: nobody can guarantee you a high return with no risk.
- M5 U04 · Rent or buy? What rights do I have as a tenant?: keep your lease, your receipts, photos of the home when you move in and all your messages with the landlord.
- M5 U05 · Is the advertised price all it costs?: always compare with the same list of costs.
- M5 U06 · What will I live on when I work less or stop working?: a small, steady contribution is different from a promised return. Nobody can guarantee how much it will grow.
- M5 U07 · What happens to what I saved in Mexico?: update your beneficiaries in your AFORE. If you don't, your family may have to go through a long process to receive that money.
- M5 U08 · What happens to my family if something happens to me?: if you're worried about your immigration situation, an attorney or a DOJ-accredited representative can guide you on a family preparedness plan.
- M5 U09 · How do I plan when my income changes every month?: in the good months, prepare for the bad months.
- M5 U10 · How do I formalize my business?: start simple and grow when your business can pay for it.
- M5 U11 · What's my financial plan?: it's not about doing everything. It's about taking the next step, with a named goal, a witness and a reminder.
- M5 U12 · What happens if I inherit or leave something across two countries?: in the U.S., POD and TOD beneficiaries and a transfer on death deed avoid court; inheriting pays no federal tax except on huge estates, but foreign inheritances over 100,000 dollars are reported on Form 3520. In Mexico, inheriting pays no ISR, but transferring the title does cost money.

**Cases**

- **M5 U01 · Daniela's course.** Daniela wants a 600 baking course in 12 months, with no debt. She already has 180 set aside just for that. *How much does she set aside each month?* — Expected answer: 420 is missing: 35 a month.
- **M5 U02 · Rosa's trip.** Rosa needs 1,000 in three months. *Does she invest in something that can go up or down?* — Expected answer: No. If it drops 20%, she wouldn't have enough for her trip. She uses a savings account.
- **M5 U03 · The IRA in cash.** Andrés opened an IRA a year ago and his money is still in cash because he never chose any investments. *What does he do?* — Expected answer: He asks his plan administrator what options he has and chooses based on his time frame and risk.

**Practice**

- **M5 U01.** Write three goals with an amount, date, currency and priority. Separate the one that needs money soon from the long-term one. — Answer: For example: 1) A 500 savings cushion in 5 months (dollars). 2) A 600 course in 12 months (dollars). 3) A 20,000-peso roof in 18 months (pesos). The first two are short term.
- **M5 U02.** For one goal, write down the time frame, how much you need available, how much you could lose without affecting your life and the costs. — Answer: For example: "Down payment in 5 years; I won't need it before then; I could handle a 10% drop one year; I'll look for fees under 0.5%."
